JohnFP Sun Jan 15, 2006 1:44 pm #2
Well, first of all I assume you meant a holographic plate and not a photographic plate. You need holographic film.

Put the plate back into the plateholder and shine the laser as you did in your exposure but remove the object. You should be able to look through the plate and see the object where it was originally.

MichaelH Sun Jan 15, 2006 11:42 pm #4
Who is the manufacturer of the film you're using?

What processing methods are you using?

Where did you get your instructions for exposure and processing?

How long did you expose the film?

How long did you put the film in the developer?

How black did the film go before you took it out of the developer?

There are lots of other questions I could ask but there's a good start.
Kukuri Tue Jan 17, 2006 3:21 am #5
It worked.. ^^;; Turns out that I needed a white piece of cardboard behind my photographic plate in order to view it. The image I saw was really faint but I managed to make out the object I was trying to make a hologram of.
JohnFP Thu Jan 19, 2006 11:20 am #6
Could you tell me how a white card behind a tranmission hologram helped you see the hologram? I am not sure I follow you. In viewing a transmission hologram the light needs to be going through the plate from the opposite side you are viewing. And usually a black card is better then a white card to darken the area. Unless of course you had the plate flipped around and were viewing the actual projected real image onto the card.
