Ed Wesly Mon Apr 25, 2005 6:56 pm #3
No problem using either one. If the JD-2 bleach is yellowy, then it's a dichromate based reversal bleach and will yield shorter wavelength shifted colors in reflection holos.

If the JD-4 is greenish blue, then it's a copper sulfate based rehalogenating bleach, and will yield laser color reflection replay.

Check my miscellaneous ramblings on this forum about CWC2 and Pyrochrome for more details.

Can the JD-2 and JD-4 bleaches be used interchangeably?

(if anyone has tried it, which combination of developer and bleach gives the best results, or how do they differ?)
Tony Wed Apr 27, 2005 12:44 am #7
JD-2 and JD-4 bleaches are both effective but are very different bleaches with different results. JD-2 is a reversal bleach which will result in a color shift. JD-4 bleach is a rehalogenating bleach which will result in little color shift.

For a basic silver material such as PFG-01 and D-19 developer, both bleaches will act as a bleach; one would give a shifted color reflection hologram, the other unshifted.
Tom B. Wed Apr 27, 2005 4:47 am #8
> eric343: Can the JD-2 and JD-4 bleaches be used interchangeably?(if anyone has tried it, which combination of developer and bleach gives the best results, or how do they differ?)

I've tried mixing and matching both, and found that the JD2 developer and (dichromate) bleach work best together and are good for medium grain films like PFG-01. Use of a rehalogenating (copper or ferric) bleach as in JD-4 with this film seems to produce excessive additional noise probably due to excess grain growth.

For finer grain films like PFG-03, etc. a JD4 type of combination seems to work best - use of dichromate bleach here produces noticeably dimmer results than the JD4 rehalogenating bleach.

Your mileage may vary.
