Jeffrey Weil Sat Jul 09, 2011 4:06 pm #2
There's no need to rush things. By the time you've mastered single color work you'll know what to buy. Besides, by that time there might choices not available now.

Get single color reflections working, then split beam work including h1 to h2 image plane transfers and at least a few other types. Do some experiments, make a bunch of holos. If you go right into full color work not only might you get the wrong lasers you'll also be dealing with so many variables, all of them new, that you might never get good results without stepping back to the basics first.

I wish my laser was only 1000 bucks. That's not a lot for a laser that's appropriate for full color or most other forms of advanced holography. Other than small He/Ne's everything is going to be a grand or more, sometimes much more. That could also change by the time your ready for full color.

Single beam stuff is relatively inexpensive. If you want to do full color you should really have a full size table, tons of mounts, plenty of optics, etc....It will not be cheap. Starting with the basics will also give you time to save up money and acquire equipment cheaply as deals come up.

Jeff Weil

NorthBeach Holography Inc.

Jeffrey Weil Sat Jul 09, 2011 7:48 pm #4
Hey There,

Your still way ahead of yourself. How powerful a laser do you need? The 10 dollar one won't cut it one day. Wow, that's impossible to answer right now. Check this small sample of stuff out.

What emulsion are you using, how fast is it?

What is the curve compared to how much power you have in your other lasers

3 separate exposures or one

(here's a monster one) how big is the hologram you want to make

(another monster) how big is your H1

what kind of chemistry did you use and how bright is your H1

what's your technique like, how far do you like to spread out your beams

See where I'm going, I could go on and list many more variables. You have to reverse engineer everything from the final result that you want. For example if you want to make 8x10 holograms, that takes 4 times as much power as a 4x5 hologram. Plus there's all that other stuff, and plenty more.

Making more simple holograms will give you what you need to answer those questions. Unless you want to hire a professional consultant to build you a lab and teach you how to do full color this is really the only way to get there, by making basic holograms first and learning.

If you want to make full color holograms I suggest you get a nice big He/Ne and do pseudo color with swelling the emulsion. It might not be "true" color in the technical sense of the word but it can sure look that way. Most of the best multi-color and full color silver holograms made have been done that way. Probably well over 90%. And most of the 3 laser "true" color holograms made have been single beam techniques. Almost none have been made image planed.

So what is the Cadillac of display holography? Many would say a full color image planed reflection hologram. In that case almost 100% of the ones out there are done with a single red laser and pre-swelling the emulsion. It's a very developed technique that's been highly published. You should have an easy time of learning it since your so dedicated.

So, do some simple stuff with the kit. Then buy a nice He/Ne. The beam quality, coherence and power will take much more money to match using diode lasers right now. Of course that could change. The He/Ne is still the best bang for your buck. Using that you can do simple basic stuff all the way up to the best display holography in the world.

Plus doing single red will give you the best selection of materials. Even ones you can make yourself pretty easily. Full color emulsions are much more restrictive. Much less choice and much harder to make.

Jeff W

Ok coming in a little late here but here's my two cents anyway, The small investment made for the holokit will likely help you to determine if holography will become your "newest hobby".

For your red beam stick with a HeNe as far as i can tell the red diodes cant compete (yet) the coherent length of these laser will be limited but the beam quality will be unsurpassed (15-40Mw).

Your green beam is a DPSS laser,by Coherent #315,#215, and most JDS green units as well with an almost unlimited coherent lenght, try get the most Mw you afford (10-200mw). These all work great for DCG holography too!

The blue beam will shurely cost you the most and the longer you wait on this one the better as the prices are dropping like flies! , the Coherent Sapphire Compass units seem best for your dollar, be sure to inquire if its operating SLM otherwise you can chance it ( i have had a number of these and all have worked for display holography) ,(20-200mw), the 488nm diode lasers will make holograms but the beam quality suffers greatly!

Good luck w/your new kit, be sure to post us some pictures and WE will help you progress Quickly !

When you purchase a laser on ebay be sure to inquire that it indeed works for holography, and that it has some guarantee to work as well. I regularly sell complete units on ebay (and here too). Outside of Phil and myself (holokidd) one rarely sees sellers who guarantees their units to operate SLM for holography.

The controller is a very necessary piece of a complex laser, it does a fine balance between temperatures, stabilized output, and ambient temperatures. with out it your laser would multi-mode and and output would vary greatly, to have an optional variable output on these lasers (DPSS), greatly complicates things as a small adjustment in output changes all the other parameters, temps, power consumption etc...
