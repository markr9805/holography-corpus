Ed Wesly Mon Feb 09, 2009 9:32 pm #1
In addition to all my other talents, I am the History of Photography teacher at Harrington College of Design’s Digital Photography Department. As part of the “Demo or Die” philosophy, I make a variety of light-sensitive coatings as part of my class.

But last semester I made a big boo-boo with my silver nitrate baths. For lack of organizational reasons, I was carting around more than one container of silver nitrate solution. Since the bottles were getting low, I decided to marry them into one.

But the bottles were all full of stains, and it turns out that one of them was not AgNO3 but developer! (Note to self: mark bottles better!) So as you might suspect, the Dektol reduced the silver, precipitating what I believe to be silver sludge at the bottom of a tray.

I would much rather have silver nitrate than silver sludge, which is not very pretty, just looks like mud. So I think if I poured some nitric acid on top of this, I should get back to what I want. Any advice from the hard-core chemists before I do something foolish?
John Klayer Mon Feb 09, 2009 9:52 pm #2
I wouldn't just pour the nitric on top of it. I'd pour off the liquid and wash several times with distilled water, then add the diluted nitric with stirring. I miss the good old days when I worked in a chem lab and nitric acid was right there on the shelf.
Hans Tue Feb 10, 2009 3:12 pm #3
I think that adding nitric acid to metallic silver is very dangerous due to toxic gasses being released. Please be careful.
