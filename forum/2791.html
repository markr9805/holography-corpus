Ed Wesly Thu Oct 14, 2004 11:16 pm #2
Spectra-Physics used to offer an etalon option on its classic Model 125, and Jodon did too on their HN-50.

These things can still be found kicking around, although the last ones were made about 20 years ago! Don Gillespie of El-Don was supporting them, but I don't know if he's still kicking around. Other surplus laser sellers might have them.

The 30 mW you mentioned might have enough gain to support an etalon, but the optic needs to be mounted between the tube and a mirror, so if it's an integral mirror design tube you're out of luck.

The ones I might still have in my basement were parallel plane-polished, uncoated, about an inch thick, if you are interested. You'd have to extend the cavity to fit the optic, which means grafting a couple of inches to the resonator. The etalon would have to be placed in something like a mirror mount to tilt it in two axes.

Hope this helps.

Ed Wesly Fri Oct 15, 2004 10:52 am #4
I don't own an S-P 125, just used to use two at Lake Forest College. The tubes were about 1.5 m long, Brewster windows at both ends. The mirror mounts were separate from the tube. It was a "cold cathode" design where the cathode was in an arm that branched off from the main capillary, not like the typical contemporary coaxial cathode hot dog shape.

The longer the tube, the more chance for other modes to pop up. Smaller He-Ne's show less spikes on a scanning Fabry-Perot Spectrum Analyzer than these without the etalon. I don't think that there would be any difference with an integral mirror design versus external cavity as far as coherence length.

These big beasts were capable of 60 - 70 mW without the etalon, and around 50 mW on a good day with etalon. In addition to the usual DC excitation, there was a Radio Frequency Exciter built into the power supply, and switiching it on would excite the gas into emitting a few more milliWatts, kind of like a Turbo. These fire-breathers were the acme of '60's laser engineering.

Dinesh Wed Oct 20, 2004 4:56 pm #25
Regarding etalons. Is it possible to make an external etalon. It'd be bloody tricky to do, but the coherence length of a laser is simply dependant on the purity of the wave. An etalon is just an interference filter such that the distance between the mirrors is lambda/2 of the desired wavelength. Of course, adjusting this ain't easy and maintaing the seperation still less so. Couldn't the etalon be housed in it's own container and maintained by a seperate controller. This is obviously speculative, but with people making their own 20 micron pinholes, it seems that external etalons are within the realm of possibility, albeit not ver good ones. Perhaps an increase from 4" to 20" is possible.

Regarding the cyclical nature of the coherence length, the coherence function is cyclical but drops. It's basically a sinusoidal function modulated by a slow exponential:

exp(-alphL)cos(f(L))

where L is the tube length, I'm dragging this up from some research I did fifteen years ago, so I can't swear on the accuracy. I was chosen to do some research for NSF and the only way I could make it work was to exploit the cyclic nature of coherence. Basically, the coherence of the laser falls as you depart from strict equal paths, however, at integral multiples of some function of tube length, the coherence rises again, but not to the same level. It then falls, rises, again not to the same level, and falls again. The fringe modulation:

I = ( I_max-I_min)/(I_max + I_min)

will be cyclic.

You can see this if you have a very long table. Make a Michelson, seperate one mirror and the fringes will eventually lose contrast and finally all contrast is gone. However, if you continue the seperation, at some point the fringe contrast reappears, but not with the same contrast. Of course, I'm referring to temporal coherence.

Unfortunately, as I discovered, you also need exceptional good Bragg selectivity to actually make it work in practice. A thin film emulsion will not be able to exploit this.
