I use JD-4. It works fine if you make white light reflection holograms, but I have noticed that when viewing the finished hologram under laser light parts of the image are black. I figure this is because of the notorious tendency for this emulsion to shrink. I never had this problem with JD-4 and 03M. I do get good results for Denisyuk holograms.

My development time is <3 minutes.

walschuler Fri Dec 30, 2005 3:33 pm #7
This question applies to o3c and more generally:

Has anyone tried preexposure bathing the emulsion in formate (formula =

OCHO, I believe)? Jacqueline Belloni, prof at U. Paris/Orsay published a note, mentioned also in LASER FOCUS several years ago, that formate would increase the quantum efficiency of any emulsion. It is said to speed up any emulsion by a factor of 2, without affecting grain size. If memory serves, you would soak in 10% aqueous solution for 2 minutes, then dry. Since o3 emulsions are very soft, this may carry some risk. It is on my list to try but I have not gotten there yet.

Tommy Sun Mar 12, 2006 6:49 pm #13
I have been doing H1H2 reflection holograms with PFG-3C and a 315M.

I have had my best results (sofar) with 600 micro-watts/cm^2 of light (measured with a meter calibrated for 632nm, NOT 532nm, so take that number with a grain of salt). Then JD-4 developer for about a minute (Photographer's Formulary documentation says 30 seconds... I've been meaning to try a greater exposure, since Slavich says more like 1200 micro-watts/cm^2), and JD-4 bleach till clear (takes 30 seconds to a minute) plus 30 seconds. Wash in distilled water after developer, and bleach, then wash in running tap water for 10 minutes, then 30 seconds to a minute in 1 percent acetic acid (to reduce/prevent printout), and dry.

I have observed printout occuring with my overhead floresent lights on during the 10 minute wash... (So keep the light subdued until after the acetic acid)

They take a very long time to dry, on the order of an hour, about twice what VRP-M takes.
