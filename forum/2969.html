Difficult to say without more information.

[quote:6954fb67c2]The photopolymer is irradiated by two coherent 524nm laser at same incident angle.[/quote:6954fb67c2]

At the same incident angle on the same side of the plate (transmission geometry) or opposite sides (reflection geometry)? If reflection geometry, then are the beams on either side of the normal, on the same angle or along the plate normal. In other words, are they, for example, are they at 30 degrees and 210 degrees or are they at zero and 180.

[quote:6954fb67c2]The 'O' is diffraction beam and transmission beam spot.

[/quote:6954fb67c2]

There are two O's seperated at either end of a diameter. Are these both tranmssion and diffraction beams or is one transmiossion and one diffraction?

Some possibilities:

There is internal reflection inside the emulsion causing two sets of gratings. The zero order ("transmission") beam is along one of the first order ("diffraction") bemas and so seems to be just a zero order, while the other seems to be the first order diffraction.

The zero order beam is itself internally reflected, so that there are two zero order beams, while the first order diffracted beam is too weak to produce a visible internally reflected beam.

An optic is imaging, as Colin suggests, or is holographically encoded due to stray reflections.

I'd suggest not recording symetrically. If you take one of the beams at a slightly different angle you may not get these artifacts, which are pretty normal for two beams that are symmetric due to the reasons I stated above.
