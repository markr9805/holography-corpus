Martin Mon Aug 04, 2008 12:07 pm #3
In a contact copy regime you can do fairly good transmission gratings with a filtered LED. Making high quality reflection holograms is much trickier though.
BobH Mon Aug 04, 2008 1:33 pm #4
Peter miller did some nice work making image-planed reflection contact copies with a filtered mercury arc lamp while employed at the RCA. He was working with Nick Phillips, and their work was published in one of the Holographics International issues.
zipsister Tue Aug 05, 2008 12:31 am #5
Way back in the mid 1980's while doing my Masters I took a cement paver, inner tube and holo plates and set up the lot on a street corner in the middle of the night and made some crude laser-like in-line transmission holograms (subject of of my degree work; 'In-Line Holography as an Art Technique') using street lighting. I was careful to pick a corner where there was no multiple interference from more than one light. For an object I used clear persex with scratched images on one surface placed up against the hologram emulsion. Then the police pulled over and questioned me about what I was doing loitering around street corners in the dead of night with a cement paver on top of a floaty. I got as far as "...the coherence of Sodium street lights creates crude interference between..." before they were back in their patrol car and half way back to the station.

zipsister
walschuler Thu Aug 28, 2008 8:07 am #6
As I mentioned in a post to the Lippmann part of this site earlier tonight, I did some images using a sodium safe lamp, and a reflective mylar film in fair contact with a holographic emulsion. I estimate the coherence length was in the neighborhood of a millimeter. I got clearly 3-D images of the hills, valleys and dimples in the mylar, created by my imperfect handling of the film and its imperfect contact with the emulsion.

Martin Thu Aug 28, 2008 12:55 pm #8
> jeff-blyth: I am curious to know what you mean by a “sodium safe lamp”.

I guess he meant a low pressure sodium vapor lamp - the kind that used to serve as safelight for panchromatic photographic materials.

These lamps have two strong and very narrow lines close to 589nm – see [http://www.lambdasci.com/download%20pdf/LLE-2.pdf](http://www.lambdasci.com/download%20pdf/LLE-2.pdf)

However, these lamps are not particularly good at making a point light.
walschuler Thu Aug 28, 2008 1:44 pm #9
Yes, it was a low pressure sodium vapor lamp, used in my school's darkrooms as a safelight. I have the specs somewhere, but I think it was about 150 watts. Eventually I bought my own tube and built a diffuser box and used it first to create a field of pure color, just aiming at the diffuser, and then used it to illuminate objects in a darkened room. I photographed using a 4x5 Speed Graphic with old plate holders or film holders. All this was in aid of my Lippmann research.
