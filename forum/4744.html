Joe Farina Mon Apr 11, 2005 3:06 pm #1
Although I haven't done it myself, I understand it's possible to have a laser that's not attached to the table, and have it go through a spatial filter on the table. It seems like alignment would be pretty tricky, though.

For color holograms with 3 beams, will it be possible to have the lasers not attached to the table? Has anyone tried it? I think it would be difficult to get all of them through the spatial filter, even when the lasers are attached. Doing it with them unattached seems like a nightmare.

I remember in Lake Forest, when they made 3-color holograms, all the lasers were on top of the table. They managed to mount the water-cooled argon laser in such a way that no injurious vibrations were caused by the flow of the cooling water. These were single-beam Denisyuk holograms, and the lasers were outside of a thick styrofoam box (which rested near the center of the table). The object and plate were inside they box.
JohnFP Mon Apr 11, 2005 3:36 pm #2
Joe, my laser is off the table and I have done single beam and split beam hologams. The first geometry uses one spatial filter - the latter uses two spatial filters. When I first set up the table, for about a week, I had to reallign the spatial filters each day. But now I fire up the laser and they are alligned. I also use one line in the Argon, allign the spatial filter and can dial down to a different line. The spatial filter is only off by a small margin. I use the transfer mirror just after the laser on the laser table and off the isolation table to tune the whole set up, including the spatial filter back in with the second line.

I would suggest that if you use the laser off the isolation table, add a transfer mirror just after the laser on the laser table not the isolation table. I have found it very useful hitting the first mirror on the holgoraphy table if something moves a little. Case in point. If you don't have an active dampening table but a passive one like I do (inner tubes), then moving a heavy optic to a different part of the table causes some misallignment. But I found with the transfer mirror on the laser table (again not on the isolation table) that stears the beam to the holography isolation table, I can adjust it slightly and the entire set up comes back in.

If you want the lasers off the table then with three colors I would do the same thing. Have each laser off the table and a transfer mirror just after the laser stearing the beam to the beam combiner. This way you could tweak in the set up one line at a time, but no matter how you look at it, this would probably be a challenge.

A Deniyuk can be made with vibration introduced into the system as long as the plate and object to not move relative to one another. Glue the plate to the object and I bet you could make the hologram on a train ( I am not suggesting you try this). Of course there will be a point where too much vibration or just the right kind will ruin the hologram. I am only trying to point out that a water cooled laser on a table making a Denisyuk is different then a water cooled laser on a table making a split beam. There are many holographers that have water cooled lasers on the table and are making split beams, but I imagine the table needs to a pretty good at moving as one system.

I guess microscope objectives are chromatially corrected, so the foci would be very close. I rember spacially filtering 514, 476, and 633 quite nicely, and got a clean white beam. But the lasers were mounted on the table. It's important to plan the set-up carefully. There are many degrees of freedom, and if you get one wrong, you won't be able to compensate.

What I mean, is make absolutely sure the two or three beams are collinear, and precisely superimposed before they get to the lens. They should not be coming in at different angles, as then the foci will be separated laterally.
BobH Mon Apr 11, 2005 4:34 pm #6
I'm passing three colors (633, 532, and 473nm) through my spatial filter at home. The objective is achromatic, but if the pinhole is 25 microns, all three go through well. It's important that they all have roughly the same beam diameter and divergence to make it all work best. Keeping the optics clean before the spatial filter allows the use of larger holes, and makes alignment with the lasers off the table easier.

Along with two mirrors to keep the beam aligned with the system on the table, I like to have the beam reflecting off the mirror on the table go down the length of the table and through two iris diaphrams (one next to the mirror and the other at the far end). I can then always quickly re-align the beam to the table.
