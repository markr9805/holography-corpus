I did manage to get a few holos shot while being sick... (just had to scratch that dcg itch..)

I will hopefully post some pictures in the next day or so when I get my camera back from a comercial shoot. here's a description and some results, also some some probably mostly incorrect theories ideas about dcg... (mind wonders when cold medicine/nyquill collide... )

Plates 10-30-250 (HomeMadeDCG)

Age: 7 days

Exposure: 100mw/cm2 @ 532nm w/C315M @ 100mw

• Wait 4min

• Fix/Harden in Kodak rapid fix (mixed 700ml water – 100ml fixer – 10ml hardner) until bluish tint in dcg (~1min)

• Soak in water 4min @ 23deg/c

• Soak in 50% ISO 1min @ 23deg/c

• Soak in 100% ISO until image appears bright (1-3min) @ 23deg/c (also at 50deg/c) which produced a brighter image (more broadband)

• Dry w/heat gun until image is nice and bright

• Seal w/epoxy and another glass layer

Results: extremely bright images, clear plate, cooler iso yielded a green/blue holo and hotter iso resulted in a orange/red wider bandwidth holo.

(I could see detail in the flat black table the object was laying on)

observations/theories/NyQuill induced hallucinations…ect..

2. Post exposure hardness related to gelatin yield, the harder the gelatin in post exposure the more resistant it is to hotter water and ISO temps for processing to a greater bandwidth. (Before dcg becomes milky.)

3. holographic clarity related proportionally to dcg bandwidth and depth, also edge distortion is related to depth, ex, narrowband dcg yields greater depth, and broadband yields less depth yet brighter image.

Using the spring anology from ([http://cabd0.tripod.com/holograms/id4.html](http://cabd0.tripod.com/holograms/id4.html)) it would appear that water temp is the stretching force on the gelatin (spring), and temp of the ISO is how quickly one releases the spring force. In a frustrating game of tug of war.
