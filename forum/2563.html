It's usually standard practice to have an H1 for rainbows as long and narrow. You could have a 7 x 2.5 for the H1 and a slit width of less than 2.5 in. along the '7' side, ie a slit 7 in long and about 1/2 in wide. The problem is that once you've made an H1 with these dimensions, you can't ever use it for anything else but rainbows. If you want the flexibility of using the H1 for anything at all, use the full 5x7. If you know that the H1 is only going to be used for rainbows, use te 7 x 2.5.

> 1 should i mount my h1 film To glass?

If you're just trying to see if you can make one, just use a glass sandwich to hold the film. If you're creating a master that you'll want to use over and over and/or last a long time, either use a glass master or pre-mount the film to glass before H1 exposure. You'll get much better results.

> 2 sould the rainbow h1 be slightly larger than the h2 let say h1 5x7 h2 4x5

The general rule is yes, make your master larger than your copy. It depends on what you want in your final result. Because of the geometry, most H2 transfers end up with a smaller viewing angle than the master. This is often compensated for (somewhat) by using a larger master so you end with a greater field of view.

> 3 has any one done a h2 refection narrow apature like 7x2.5 for a h1 and 4x5 for the h2?

Yes, several of my H2's use a similar size. My standard master so far is a 5x7" plate and I often play with lighting the master with varying slit widths using a small cylindrical lens.
