JohnFP Tue May 13, 2008 3:44 pm #2
The easiest and most cost effective way I can foresee, it so make a mass produced embossed hologram which could be put on the same material as the tax stickers. This separates the holography procedure from the actual license plate making and allow a sticker to be put on which cannot be peeled off.

I am not sure which state you are from but if room on the existing plate would allow it, it would be more cost effective and environmentally friendly for the state to simply issue the stickers instead of an entirely new plate. And the supporter can place them on their plate. If there is no room, a new plate can be designed such that there is room for the "sticker", which would be placed on the plate during manufacturing.

Justin W Wed May 14, 2008 12:07 pm #7
carol - Here is a thought from a guy that barely knows holography and knows zilch about embossed holograms...

How much of a license plate is one legally allowed to cover with a sticker?

Is there any merit in suggesting a license-plate-size holographic sticker with the plate numbers die-cut from the sticker....?

The state of issue could be printed in ink (in a color that boldly contrasts the holographic background).

Seeing as how you say you'll be issuing new plates, the plate-sized holographic sticker shouldn't be burdened with trying to adhere atop registration stickers from the previous years - or - on that note, one could (and should) design said stickers with a stock hole die-cut to allow for that box on the plate where the registration sticker is to be affixed...

All in all, this would result in an entirely holographic license plate, minus the tear-out numbers and tax sticker hole.

Again, this depends on legality of such things and available budget.

Just a thought from the dimmer bulb in the chandelier
