Well, I received some plain unsensitised film and finally got around to playing with it.

[http://www.filmotec.de/English_Site/Pro ... _40_e.html](http://www.filmotec.de/English_Site/Products/GF_40_e/gf_40_e.html)

Here is the holographyforum thread that introduced me to the film, thanks Kris.

[http://www.holographyforum.org/phpBB2/v ... php?t=4315](http://www.holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4315)

I took the gelatin coated film (not glass) and put it in a solution of AmDi (about 3.5 %) for 1 minute and anther for 1.5 minutes. I then let air dry standing up vertically. Both seems to sensitive very well. I cleaned the back and then sandwiched between two pieces of glass. I made two single beam reflection holograms.

In processing as normal the first one, I heated with a hot air dryer as I normally do with glass (hot and heavy). I got a pretty nice hologram, not too bright, but it deformed the plastic film. Haha, I didn't even think of that until it happened. On the second hologram, I processed as normal and dried with the dryer on cool. The hologram did not deform but still was not very bright.

Jean have you played with the film yet? Has anyone else used it for DCG or Silver sensitising?

I will try to do more testing (I still have 8 more sheets) but I am working on and H1 to H2 that I want to complete first.

Hans Fri Nov 03, 2006 7:47 am #8
Yes, I loaded the plate with AmDi, with a little bit of glycerine. I accellerated the dark reaction with few minutes on a hair drier. The hologram was super bright and sharp. The downside of the film is that it is very thin, which makes it hard to index match it. Even the smallest speck of dust bends the film when it is index matched. This shows up as a distortion on the hologram.

Because of the thinness of the material and the high cost, I decided to continue experiments at making my own emulsions.

Right John that is beginning to sound plausible and OK and a challenge worth a go . If you did our glycerine and 100C bake system it would make things better still. Unless you have an amazing domestic oven you

probably won’t be able to get a 50 x 60 cm glass sheet in. However a piece of film that size would perhaps be possible in a 50cm long roll lying diagonally across. It should be OK at 100C but not any higher.

When it comes to mounting the film to make a long exposure

it could be done with an additional bonus you would not get from coated glass. This is that you mount the dried film with lamp oil (ie. in UK “white spirit”) with the GELATIN side against the clean dust free glass carrier sheet,

putting considerable pressure with roller if possible to make the oil layer as thin as possible and to exude any bubbles of course.

The bonus then is that if you leave this sheet to stabilize in the dark for a while then when you give it your long exposure time the gelatin will not creep by changing its thickness out of the plane of the sheet because it is now locked in.

Then you may achieve the largest area single exposure DCG and send it in to the Guinness Book of Records eh? ----Sounds quite exciting.
