Ed Wesly Mon Feb 09, 2009 10:08 pm #1
Back in the heyday of Agfa film, there was the debate of polyester versus (tri)acetate. Agfa started manufacturing the film on the dimensionally stable polyester, but holographers who were transferring reflection film copies from film masters would complain about image dropouts because of the birefringence twisting the polarization vectors out of alignment. So later runs of film, and contemporary manufacturers use acetate.

Turns out acetate is not such a good idea from an archival standpoint! I recently had a show and pulled out a piece from storage where I had squished a piece of acetate between a piece of glass and a mirror to turn a transmission holo into a sort of reflection/embossed deal. Basically to front light it.

But I couldn’t hang this piece because the film had puckered, for lack of a better word. It looked like what happens to a piece of paper that gets wet, then air dries, but doesn’t go flat again, growing ripples on it.

So I figured that this thing had been around the block a few times, got hit with harsh lighting, not stored archivally, etc., so I had screwed it up. So I asked my friend who had received a copy of this piece for his wedding 10 years ago to loan me his for the duration of the show.

Even though this one had been in storage for a decade, it too had the pucker! And just this weekend I talked to my friend Don Barnhart, who said he had 10 30 by 40 cm holos that had done the same thing, blaming himself for the disaster!

So it looks like that the polyester, in spite of its birefringence, was actually a smarter choice for a holographic substrate than triacetate! Anyone else seeing this effect? Film manufacturers take heed for the next generation of holographic products! (Kodak always polyester, dubbing it Estar, but you could never really make reflection holos with it anyway!)

And of course the punch line: any good (not necessarily quick) fixes? I am not so sure if this pucker can be ironed out by a laminator.
