Ed Wesly Fri Sep 16, 2005 10:52 am #2
Certainly I am not an experienced user of this stock, but the Rosetta Stone comparing this film to something that I could latch on to is at [http://www.2spi.com/catalog/photo/maco-TEM-film1.shtml](http://www.2spi.com/catalog/photo/maco-TEM-film1.shtml). It says that it is a drop in replacement for Agfa Scientia 23D56.

Good old 8E75 and 10E75 were originally known as Scientia products, then they started calling them Holotest. In the Agfa nomenclature, the first digits refer to sensitivity (which implies resolution to some degree). From my experience, 10E needs about 1/16th of the light that 8E needs, or 4 stops in photo talk. So 23 is much higher sensitivity, with attendant grain size.

The letter gives the contrast, E being very high, then the D would be a bit less contrasty, but still high, considering that D-19 is the recommended developer.

56 means sensitivity out to 560 nm, but the Kodak lit says that SO-163 is blue sensitive, and I didn't see a spectral sensitivity curve on the Kodak site, since it's designed for electron exposure, so its suitability for green exposures would need to be verified experimentally. But it's probably fine for blue lasers.

It looks like what these TEM cats are doing are making shadow grams, kinda like X-rays, and then enlarging them to see details. I gleaned that from the size of the film, the traditional lantern slide format, 3 1/4" by 4".

So it might be possible to make what Gabor was looking for with this material with the right kind of object! And if I were to pursue characterizing this material, I would start off with a Gabor type scheme with a blue laser. Or if I had green, the same thing, with an far ranging exposure series. An alternative would be to start with low frequency gratings.

I remember seeing in some antique Agfa lit that 23D was useful for photographing holograms. Not having any experience with it, I'm guessing that this makes it similar to the late, lamented Kodak Technical Pan film, which was capable of recording low frequency gratings, but I mean really low frequency, like fringes visible under a magnifying glass.

All this is speculation on my part. One of the sites I visited had contact people posted for the Microscopy Suite at U of I in Urbana (my alma mater!), so why not give him a jingle! [http://www.itg.uiuc.edu/ms/contacts.htm](http://www.itg.uiuc.edu/ms/contacts.htm)
