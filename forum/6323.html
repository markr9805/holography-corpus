Hello experts holographers!

I just purchased the holokit from intergraf and as luck would have it, I've had no luck. After 6 pretty much feeble attempts, I thought I would seek some help from the pros, if you wouldnt mind.

The setup-

Using the laser with the kit, glass plates (small 2x2) and the JD4 that was included. I started with the 'standard' coins for a subject, all silver and bright, the laser spread covered the entire area and was roughly 10" from the laser that was elevated and isolated in sugar. The setup was on a marble vanity in the basement, using a green colored night light, turned off the air, the sump pump and it seemed very quiet. I made my first exposure at 10 seconds long, I tried 12 the next attempt then 8. Now I understand its hard to troubleshoot without a picture, let me first ask this-

When developing, the plate turned dark almost immediately, less then a second. Is this an indication of over exposure or possible underexposed?

Most of the plates came out where you could just barely make the outline of the coin, if that helps.

Thanks in advance for any thoughts!
Colin Kaminski Sun Jul 01, 2007 3:23 am #2
It should take about 60 seconds for the plate to turn dark. When adjusting exposures use factors of two. If you think 10 seconds is over exposed then try 5 then 2.5 etc. Or the other way, if you think 10 seconds is underexposed try 20 then 40 etc. Once you are getting an image you can make fine adjustments to try to make a brighter image.

Tom B. Sun Jul 01, 2007 6:58 am #4
Do you get any hologram at all? Do you know what to expect and are you looking at the plate from all angles with a good light source like the sun or a small halogen light?

I have noticed plates turning dark pretty quickly with PFG-03M and JD4, but usually it takes at least 10 to 20 seconds. If they go black instantly, there is a problem - either the plates are bad (chemical fogging) or they have been exposed to bright light. You could check your safelight with a diffraction grating to see if there is any red in it (often there is and more green filtering is needed) but this is unlikely to be your problem.

If you are willing to sacrifice a plate, take an unexposed one out in the dark (or as close to dark as is practical) and develop and rinse it (also in the dark) If it turns black, you have bad plates (known to happen sometimes with Slavich) and should request replacements.
