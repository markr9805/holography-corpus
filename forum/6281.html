Are you talking about 10E as in your title or 8E (56HD)?

If you are talking about 10E then your only choice is to empty out all the AgBr and refill it by the diffusion method. I will not go into that unless it is relevant to you.

However the 8E56 material is a different matter. The problem when it was

NEW was that it was no good for reflexion holograms using the Denisyuk single beam system. So your lack of success was probably due to this inherent problem and not much to do with it being old. It was capable of making in its day good bright reflection H's but ONLY by using split beam

methods requiring much more laser power and good stability than the Denisyuk system. The problem being that this material contained so much dye that it killed too much object beam light. You can get some improvement by giving the plates a good long soak with agitation in alcohol/water solution (say 80% ethanol or methanol). This can remove some of that dye but not enough perhaps to really do a good hologram using single beam. Probably some of the best known display holograms produced in exhibitions in the 70's and 80's used 8E56HD made always by split 514nm beam from large expensive Argon ion lasers . It would probably be a challenge worth trying to you to do a split beam from a modern 532nm source.

Jeff

Hi Floh,

No on 2nd thoughts I am afraid that the dye is too much locked into the AgBr grains to be extractable with alcohol.

I have got amazingly good results with emptying out old 10E material,

(8E would be the same) using ~20% sodium thiosulfate or "hypo" without hardener, then rinsing then removing the dyes with 80% alcohol (the dyes come out pretty easily once they are unlocked from the AgBr which has gone then of course. I then did the diffusion system on the gelatin coating

as per

[http://cabd0.tripod.com/holograms/id3.html](http://cabd0.tripod.com/holograms/id3.html)

I was surprised to find that the 10E gelatin seemed to have retained no memory of having held much bigger grains for 30 years or so!

You can also get reasonable 532nm sensitivity using the pinacyanol dye

but 514nm I am not sure about.

Jeff
Ed Wesly Tue May 29, 2007 7:04 pm #6
I beg to differ about the much maligned 10E Agfa materials. Although definitely unsuitable for reflection holos, they make fine transmission ones.

We used miles of 10E75 with a Ruby laser for Bubble Chamber Holography, developed in Kodak D-19 with 1.5 g phenidone in it. This was before I discovered SM-6. But you might say it's an amplitude holo, and it doesn't need to be too bright.

But anyhow I used the PyroChrome process on some leftover bubble chamber film, and got results comparable to 8E! The zero order in some gratings and zone plates is nearly depleted!

For any doubting Thomases out there, PM me courtesy of this forum, and I will send you an address to where you can send me a stamped, self-address envelope and I will send you back some samples!
