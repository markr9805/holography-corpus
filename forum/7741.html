Do transmission holograms suffer any from potassium dichromate bleach?

JohnFP Wed Feb 18, 2009 12:00 pm #3
Back in the day when I was producing Silver Master to copy on resist, we used PyroChrome process (pyro dev and KDi) and got very little shift. Of course this was in the old Agfa emulsion I believe.

I think of transmission fringes as modulated hills and valleys. That is, hills and valley with hills and valleys on the hills and valleys. This is why Resist and finally embossed holograms need to be tranmissions (relief hologram) if you want to be able to "press" the fringe structure into a plastic.
Ed Wesly Wed Feb 18, 2009 1:59 pm #4
Another way to think of the transmission fringes is like Venetian Blinds. When the emulsion shrinks, it's like closing the blinds a bit, and like Colin mentioned, the replay angle shifts a bit. The more shrinkage, the more tilt.

Walter Spierings created quite a controversy with the first publication of the Pyrochrome process in holosphere, as conventional wisdom thought that there were two distinct processing schemes for reflection and transmission mode. This proved them wrong.

Using the hardening action of the pyrogallol based developer minimized the shrinkage effects of the removal of material by the bleach. The removal of material dropped the noise level compared to a scheme where there was development followed by rehalogenating bleach without fixing. The tan color of the processed plate might be a problem for green laser users, but usually green laser users have plenty of power to burn (holes in wall, eyes, etc.)!
jeff-blyth Wed Feb 18, 2009 2:16 pm #5
Yes potassium or ammonium Dichromate bleach can give you relative freedom from print-out and a good absence of scatter provided you use de-ionized water carefully as discussed in the Holowiki here:

[http://www.holowiki.com/index.php/Reversal_Bleach](http://www.holowiki.com/index.php/Reversal_Bleach)

When it comes to the shrinkage problem then my guess is that it can be counteracted by using the citric acid post -exposure swelling technique that pretty much evolved right here on Colin’s forum from an original posting by our Swedish colleague DJ and then by members posting in their results of citric acid tests.

[http://www.holographyforum.org/phpBB2/v ... f=2&t=5224](http://www.holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?f=2&t=5224)

These were all examples on Denisyuk holograms which enabled us to get an immediate handle on the amount of swelling , whereas with transmission holos the effects would have been more difficult to demonstrate in postings.

I cannot think of any reason why the citric acid system should not work well on transmission holos, ….it is conceivable that there might be an increased tendency to print-out overtime, but I would guess that the relatively high acidity of the system will help to avoid that…… But I don’t know for sure.

Jeff
