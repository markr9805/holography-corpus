JohnFP Sat Dec 22, 2007 5:40 pm #7
Waming the water softens the gel and allows the gel to expand more.

And think of heating the alcohol as causing that particular concentration to work faster.

Changing the temp of either will affect the final outcome.

> Say for example a hologram is made with a warmer water bath, and a cooler alcohol bath. Then, in another test, this was reversed by making the water cooler, and the alcohol warmer. Would the end result be the same?

No, cooler alcohols gives a narrower band holograms and warmer baths gives broaderband holograms. But this can be slightly offset by the time in the baths. Longer pushes toward narrower band and shorter pushes toward wider band. Now if you put the hologram in warm water first and soften it, and then try to process it with cool long baths you are kind of working against yourself. Same with the reverse.

I try to keep my water the coolest of all baths including the alcohols and either keep the subsiquent bath the same or warmer depending on what I want.

Example. If my water is 78F the each bath after that is the same or warmer. If I want to use cool, 68F alcohols, then I use my water at 68F also.

Oh, this also includes the fixer temps since it is the first bath. It is always the coolest or same.

Martin Sun Dec 23, 2007 8:52 am #14
> Joe Farina: I did some tests today, and was wondering if there is any reason to heat the subsequent alcohol bath(s), instead of the initial water soak. It seems to me that it essentially does the same thing. I figured that if the alcohol didn't need to be heated, it would be a good thing.

Speculating about the effect of hot (vs. cold) alcohol, it'll dehydrate the gelatin much more quickly.

And in the context of the “void theory”, hot alcohol might

1) significantly heat up the layer (when the water soaked layer encounters 100% alcohol);

2) form larger voids;

3) more diverse (polydisperse) void size which might lead to the large broad bandwidths so typical to DCG. My guess would be that the largest voids will build up on the surface.

Moreover, hot alcohol may be analog to hot (silver halide) developer. So it would be likely to be much more active. The amplification of the latent image will become more enhanced and hence it might require less light exposure to form an image.
