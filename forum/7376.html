walschuler Sat Jun 14, 2008 3:39 pm #5
The Olsons are an excellent reference for making the original portrait hologram(s). But a morph would require at least 2 exposures, and some way of combining them to get the morph. The Olsons may be able to double-expose a plate, or copy two masters onto a copy master. If you want a smooth morph, you will need multiple exposures, usually made with an ordinary 2-D movie camera, and then you would need someone else to transform them into strip holograms side-by-side, which is then called a multiplex stereogram (Google!). In either case you would see the morph as you tilt the image, and it will be like a short movie. It can be full color. One color is cheaper.

An older technique called a lenticular stereogram (Google) would give a similar effect, usually based on a stereo pair of stills (or maybe 3 or 4), 2-D, sliced into strips and mounted left-right, left-right, behind a special sheet of triangular profile lenses that give each eye its respective views. Again, if it is two different views you can get the morph. You may well have seen this type of image on someone else's record or magazine cover. They are around. If you run your finger across the lens sheet you can feel the lenslet ridges, similar to the ridges on a vinyl record.
