I've been experimenting of late and have come up with a new developer that has caused me to throw out the Pyro solutions and all others. Its use has cut my exposure times for Agfa film by a factor of 5 producing bright images for both reflection and transmission holograms; dubbed Zip-1. Yet to try it on Slavich film but anticipate equally good results. My first attempt with normal exposure, as if for a 2 minute development in Pyro solution, had the hologram turn black within 3 seconds, and when dried it was an ultra bright reflection hologram...mighty powerful stuff.

potassium hydroxide 30gr

ascorbic acid 10gr

metol 1gr

hydroquinone 1gr

sodium sulphite 30gr

water 1000ml

then wash, then bleach in normal potassium dichromate/sulph.acid bleach till clear, then wash.

zipsister

No Max it does not neutralize. You just need to look up the formula weights in the chemical suppliers' catalogues to see this.

Taking the weights given and dividing by the formula weights we have here

KOH 30/56 = 0.54 equivalents

Ascorbic acid 10/176 = 0.057 equivalents

Hydroquinone (acts as a weak acid too) 1/110 = about 0.01 equivalents

So you still have about 88% of the KOH unneutralized. This makes the whole formula extremely alkaline .

You would get the same result with 10 gram NaOH because although NaOH is slightly less strong than KOH you would have 0.75 equivalents instead of 0.54 for KOH.

Although sodium sulfite is routinely used to reduce arial oxidation it is also a weak solubilizer of AgBr and if you can keep the oxygen out fairly well by using the close fitting floating plastic dish method then zipsister would do even better not to include sodium sulfite. This is because the bleach you are using is a "reversal" bleach. i.e. All the developed up black silver metal gets REMOVED into solution and you are viewing the hologram via the diffraction of the undeveloped up AgBr in the dark fringes. But by using Na sulfite in your developer you are going to remove "some" of your precious AgBr that you need to give you your finished diffraction. However the very high alkalinity of this developer might allow an under -15 -sec development time which is good and can

limit the amount of AgBr loss . However much longer development time than this and the difference in final brightness between a dev with and without

(30/126) 0.24 equivalents of Na sulfite will be quite noticeable.
