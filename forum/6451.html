Tom B. Sun Sep 23, 2007 7:34 am #6
From the example you posted it's hard to tell if it's a squeegee problem or not... You usually see streaks where the squeegee slipped and large blobs at the edge where it missed entirely, but drops are unusual. When squeegeeing I can usually spot any streaks and missed spots by inspecting the reflections from a safelight about 4-6 feet away and give the plate another swipe if needed before drying . You could experiment by repeatedly soaking and squeegeeing a dud plate under various lighting conditions and then under the safelight to see what to look for. I think a fairly distant light source is best to show up the wet spots.

Another cause would be that the gelatin has hardened unevenly in some areas so the swelling is uneven. I had had edge defects that look similar to this with plates from various manufacturers, but fortunately they have been infrequent. If you are confident that your soaking and squeegeeing technique is OK, then it's likely a plate defect. Plan your compositions to allow generous edge cropping
