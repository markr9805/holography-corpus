I decided to start all-over, purchased the Integraph kit, set up a basic sand table and tried again.

Would developing be also influencing lack of brightness (using JD-4)----> It could be over or under exposure. After development and before bleaching the plate should be dark (density about 2.0 or 1% light transmission), but not darker. It's not all THAT critical. It could also be due to vibration during exposure washing out the fringes or laser instability. or an object that's not very bright or too far from the plate or...2- Horizontal streaks migth indicate movement, but the image, even though dim is clear and focused, so I am leaned to think these are due to light entering the top edge of the plate (I forgot to cover it). Do you agree?----> The latter is the most likely explanation.3- Just to be on the safe side, I'd like to set the laser beam at Brewster angle.... Is diode polarization fixed according to the beam ellipse axis, or depends on the particular diode I am using (I mean, is light polarized along any of the axis for every diode or is this diode dependent).?----> All diodes that I've seen have been polarized the same way - I think the long axis is S (for Skips) polarization and the short axis is P (for Passes). If you are shining the laser on the glass plate you want the P polarized light to be hitting at Brewster's angle. If the plate is vertical and the laser is shining down on it, you want the long axis to be horizontal. If you put a white card in front of the plate (perpendicular to it), you can view the reflections and see where the minimum is when you tilt the plate or change the laser angle.Thanks. I hope I will be able to show some results soon!Arturo
