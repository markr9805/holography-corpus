> JohnFP: Are the reflections off of a Reflection hologram specular or diffuse? Or a combination of both?

These are simple questions John. Take some time and think about them. A hologram is not a point source so therefore is is not specular. It does not reflect light from any direction into random directions so it is not diffuse. It is a "hologram".

> JohnFP: How about the reflections from a junction between two materials with different indexes of refraction?

That is the same as a mirror only with a lower efficiency. If the surfaces have a radius then they are specular.

> JohnFP: Does the type of reflection change with different delta n?

No.

BobH Thu Nov 24, 2005 3:48 am #4
> Are the reflections off of a Reflection hologram specular or diffuse? Or a combination of both?

The reflections off the hologram are specular because the surface of the hologram is smooth.

I think you're really asking: Is the image reconstructed by a Reflection hologram specular or diffuse? If the "object" of the recording was a point source, the image will be specular. If the "object" was diffuse, the image is diffuse. Just like in the real world!

Like Bob says, since the hologram is the optical equivalent of the object, the reflections are specular or diffuse depending on whatever was holographed.

Going a little deeper, if we go back to the fundamental principle of what the fringes are shaped like in a simple SBR holo, each object point has its own set of convex mirror fringes. So it might be said that the reflection mechanism inside the hologram is specular!
