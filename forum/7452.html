Glenn Thu Jul 31, 2008 10:20 pm #7
More so-called technology I am not impressed by.

The idea is to take an SLM (LCD display) and project light through it. The LCD is displaying a simplified diffraction pattern, called a HOGEL (the LCD may be replaced my a micromirror device (there are billions of companies that make these MEMS devices). The patent I cite for reference here calls for an LCD solution). This image coming out of the LCD is then shined on a lens array. There are shutters on each lens.

The basic idea is that the LCD (assume a resolution of 1,000 x 1,000 pixels) shines this diffraction pattern on the shutter/lens array.

One specific shutter (corresponding to one lens) is opened, and only that shutter is opened (assume the specific shutter to be the upper left corner shutter of the lens/shutter array).

The 1K x 1K image from the LCD then goes thru that one lens (because that lens's shutter is opened), and gets magnified down to a much smaller image of the 1K x 1k LCD image.

This much smaller, magnified image gets aimed at a specific point on an EASLM (assume the specific point to be the upper left corner of the EASLM).

Then that one shutter that was opened now closes.

The next image of part of the diffraction pattern is generated on the LCD. This new and next image is shined on the lens/shutter array. The next shutter is opened (the shutter right next to the shutter that just closed), and the image from the LCD shines thru theat next lens, to a part on the EASLM right nest to the spot on the EASLM that was just exposed.

This next shutter now closes.

This process is repeated until the entire EASLM is full of the magnified, consecutive diffraction images of the LCD.

As you might well assume, you can take a simple, 1,000 x 1,000 LCD and from it create an image on a screen that has resolutions in the billions of pixels.

This idea is covered under a patent, and is called Active Tiling--a method of dynamic video holography created by a British defense and aerospace company called Quintiq. You can find the patent (patented in like the early 2000's) by googling active tiling holographic device

favalora Mon Aug 04, 2008 3:41 pm #13
@kfliu: I've always preferred the DMD to any LCD-based technology, but I haven't used anything yet for direct-view holography. Of course, the biggest challenge right now is figuring out how to take a spatial light modulator with fairly wide pixels and use it to make a 3-D image which is simultaneously large and having a wide viewing angle. In its favor, the DMD gives you an extra "design-space dimension" because it is very fast, and you could map that excess speed into other areas that are problematic, such as pixel count.

I recall hearing problems about using DMDs as modulators for film holograms, but I don't remember why. Something about repeatability, perhaps, or having to use different electronics to get a true off-state.

Anyway, once you dig in to the literature you'll see a bunch of attempts to make holographic video using ordinary SLMs, and the images are either small, blurry, or too restricted in viewing zone.
