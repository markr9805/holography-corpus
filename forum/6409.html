JohnFP Wed Aug 22, 2007 12:41 pm #2
Try to illluminate the text, a transparancy or such and use that as the object in a standard transmission set up. The only thing you want to do is now cover all the plate except a small area with a opaque mask. Move the mask and shoot using the next page. Do not focus the object with lenses or anything else.

Then flip the plate and illuminate with a laser beam and the real image will be focused automatically at a distance the original image was.

Remember, in a standard transmission hologram each point has the entire image of the object recorded but fron the view point of that point. So you could actually illuminate a standard transmission hologram with a dot (raw laser) of light and see the entire object (real image) projected on a screen from the viewpoint of that spot. Move the illuminating spot right to left on the hologram and watch the projected image magically change perspectives....

Hope this helps.

I wanted to do the bible (or parts of) in this way such that you could have a small laser pointer and a small hologram pendant on your keychain and you would be able to slide the pendant in the slot over the laser pointer and pick the page you wanted and project that page of the bible on a screen.

This is what I got from Wikipedia....

Microtexts or Nanotexts

Dot Matrix Technologies allows implanting into hologram microtext of various sizes. There are three types of microtexts in holograms: high contrast microtexts of size 50 – 150 microns; diffractive grating filled microtexts of size 50 – 150 microns low contrast microtexts. To distinguish microtexts of sizes smaller than 50 microns, it is called nanotext. Nonotexts with sizes of less than 50 microns can be observed with a Microscope only.

CLR (Covert Laser Readable) image

This feature permits one to use a simple laser device to control the authenticity of the hologram. Computing CLR images is a complicated mathematical task that involves solving ill posed problems. This feature is only possible in hologram originated with Dot Matrix technology. There are two types of CLR which we can offer. Firstly, Dynamic CLR is a set of CLR fragments that produce animated images on the screen as the control device moves along the hologram surface. Secondly, Multigrade CLR images produce certain images on the screen of the controlling device, which differ in the first and minus first orders of laser light diffraction. As a variant we can create a hidden image, which is negative and positive in plus one and minus one order respectively.
