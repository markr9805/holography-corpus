Sorry, wrote that post in a hurry.

I think that what's holding up the mass usage of display holography is computing power.

Static images are unlikely to be popular in a mass way until they can be produced and sold in such a way that they are in the dollars-per range instead of in the 10's of dollars per and don't require special lighting to see them.

Imagine a hand-held polaroid version of our tables that produces a 4x5 which anyone can make and includes a top layer that only lets through light from one angle but lets out light from all angles.

Lasers are getting cheap and powerful enough that I can imagine a table-top direct write device being made today with the computing power to process a CCD-style capture within a few years. Hand-held units would follow given time.

Art holography like many of us aspire to is shown in galleries around the world but is anyone besides curators buying?
