Ok, these things are so easy to use I had to post a couple of photos.

These are the magnetic bases that do not come with standard 1/2" rods. Not sure what the rod diameter is but the clamping capability of the Blox allows easy interfact with my gimbals. No machining needed.

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Blox/BloxBases.jpg)

The new 8" diameter collimated mounted with the Blox. This one is used on the table for side lighting.

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Blox/BloxCollim.jpg)

Here I was able to simply mount the overhead collimator such that I can adjust height (via the enlarger mount) and tilt (via the Blox). Just a little turn of the two set screws and the collimator easily adjusts tilt angle.

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Blox/BloxCollimTilt.jpg)

Side view of the ovehead collimator.

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Blox/BloxCollimTilt2.jpg)

These Blox are so easy to work with it's not even funny.

JohnFP Fri Jan 19, 2007 1:39 pm #3
They are expensive but well worth it because they are reusable. The ones in these photos were being used for a self contained Michaelson (rail). I could just lay the michaelson on the table and feed the beam though it. I used it to find out exactly what the best tweak was for the etalon. I used it for months. Then I dismantled the Michaelson and used the blox in my static mounts. Then disassembled them so I could use them for the magnetic mount. They are priceleess.

I got them from [http://www.lenoxlaser.com](http://www.lenoxlaser.com) under web sites then Joeblox.

JohnFP Tue Jan 23, 2007 5:00 pm #6
Here are some more applications.

Here is the first rail interferometer I made solely with the Blox. It allowed me to place the entire rail into the beam path and tweak my etalon and study the stability of the laser without worrying about table vibrations. It was extemely stable. Of couse this interferometer was used to insure I had a good fringe contrast with a path length differenc of a couple of feet (Long coherence with the etalon tuning).

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/TheLab/OldLab/RailInterferometer.jpg)

Here is a couple of views of my first 4"x5" Denisyuk "camera". In actuallity, this camera could be used with no vibration isolation table. The rigidity needed would only be from the plate to the object. You can see the versatility of the block. In the first photo, the near opics can be moved in 5 directions (x,y,z, pitch and roll).

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/TheLab/OldLab/HoloCamera.jpg)

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/TheLab/OldLab/SingleBCamera.jpg)

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/TheLab/OldLab/SingleBCamera2.jpg)

This best thing I like is I can configure almost anything without any machining. And I can take it appart and reuse them. Actually both the rail interferometer and Denisyuk "camera" no longer exist. The Blox have been used elsewhere in the new lab.
