Hello all. This idea just came up in my mind and I have some unanswered questions.

It is a known fact that we can still view the whole image of the hologram even if the original plate/film is broken. The viewer of course will have some sort of constraint in viewing the image, it's like we are viewing the backyard from a partially opened window.

My question is, is it possible to reproduce the whole image of a hologram if the original hologram is broken? What I mean for this is if I have a transmission hologram, and for some reason it got broken to pieces and only about 1/4 of the original plate is left, the rest maybe scratched or just too small to be viewed, can I still make the remaing part of the hologram as a master and basically project the real image to a new plate and reproduce the entire image with all its 3-D feature? Has anyone actually tried this? Or maybe the answer is too obvious that it doesnt need to be experimented at all. Nevertheless, im very interested to learn if it can be done.

If you've lost part of the hologram, you have lost the information regarding how the object looks if looked at from that angle. There is no way to get this back.
Ed Wesly Fri Aug 04, 2006 4:02 pm #3
You could certainly make a transfer with whatever was remaining, but of course the parallax will be limited. To see 3-D you are going to need at least a chunk of holo 2 1/2" wide.

Emmett Leith in his keynote address at the first ISDH at LFC talked about how they set up and shot the first hologram of a three-dimensional object and although all parameters were OK, it didn't look 3-D "We scratched our heads for a few days and realized that because we had uses 2" by 2" plates both eyes weren't seeing the scene at the same time!" (Maybe not verbatim, but close enough!) They were successful after they got a box of 4 by 5's. (They were using Kodak 649-F Spectroscopic Plates)

When making masters for rainbow holograms, the real image is projected using only a slit, sometimes as thin as 1/4". As long as you have something that is wide enough for both eyes to see the scene simultaneously, (or reassemble a couple of chunks separated by that intra-ocular distance), and about a laser beam tall, you should be able to reconstruct the object for a usable transfer, albeit without a lot of parallax.
