MfA Sat Aug 01, 2009 1:37 pm #3
AFAIK there are generally 3 forms of digital holograms.

Multiplex holograms : record a 2D image from multiple angles.

"Hogel" holograms : records for small "holographic pixels" the incoming light from a discrete set of angles one pixel at a time (the end result is essentially equivalent to multiplex holograms but it's much easier to record lots of angles with full 3D parallax). AFAIK [this thesis](http://arxiv.org/PS_cache/arxiv/pdf/0902/0902.0551v1.pdf) describes the basic method.

Fringe based holograms : compute the sub-micron level fringe and find some way to print/display it.

The first 2 are in commercial use ... the last only is only used in research labs (using either custom printers or semiconductor lithography tools for display holograms and SLMs for real-time holographic displays).

For the enthusiast amateur (ie. people who have decent sized optical tables in their basements) multiplex holograms should be possible with some metal shop and EE knowledge ... [it's been done before](http://www.bobdbob.com/~protius/multiplex/).
