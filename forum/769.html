With an undersized pinhole in my H2 reference beam spatial filter, I have made my own by arcing a 1000 microfarad electrolytic charged to 6 volts from the finest needle in my Wife's sewing basket' sharpened even more with 400 mesh wet 'n dry, on to the surface of a piece of 0.02mm thick aluminium kitchen foil clamped and glued between two 16mm steel washers. A fine screw thread drives the needle forward until the discharge is monitored on the current meter of the digital power supply. The capacitor is constantly trickle charged through a 47 ohm resistor.

Set-up in the spatial filter the Airy rings look clean and even and from measurements taken of the 1st Airy ring diameter (Saxby 3rd Edn. P95) the hole diameter calculates out at 22 microns. For my X10 objective I'll wind up the PSU voltage to see if I can get nearer to 50 micron

The photo shows the 'sharp end'

![Image](http://s1139.photobucket.com/user/holo197/media/1693ed.jpg.html?sort=3&o=0)

A few days later…..the original 18mJ discharge into the 0.02 mm thick aluminium foil produced a smaller hole than I wanted, so I doubled power to 36mJ by increasing the charge voltage across the electrolytic to 8.5 volts - I could nearly crawl through the hole produced, it was also very ragged and the Airy Rings were hideous and fragmented.

The next change was to sharpen the needle to an even finer point by rubbing it quite hard and at a very narrow angle on 400# wet 'n dry paper. Through my microscope on X75 it became visibly sharper. I increased the charge voltage to 6.2 V (19mJ energy discharge) and bingo! A nice almost round hole which calculates out from the 1st Airy Ring diameter as 50 microns - just what I want for a X10 microscope objective. In the spatial filter a nice even red reference beam and continuous round Airy rings during the filter’s adjustment.

I repeated the exercise with a slightly lower voltage of 5.8v and again produced a hole diameter of 20 microns, slightly oval in shape and I think that this is probably about the smallest size that this technique will produce, as a test with a lower voltage than 5.8 did not produce enough stored energy in the capacitor to penetrate the foil and produce a hole

The holes are not all perfectly round but in the spatial filter they produce continuous concentric Airy rings and a smooth and even pool of red light on the reference plate holder

I think this is a viable way of making a pinhole providing the required size is not <20 micron and with only 0.02 thick foil, it won't cope with too much power but seems to work fine with my 10 - 20 mW HeNe
