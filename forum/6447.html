I was reading the new question/answer session on G307 by Michael and John, and I think it was John who made the interesting comment that CrVI is orangish, while CrIII is bluish.

On some of my recent tests, I noticed that some of my processed DCG layers, the really bright images, are definitely bluish, while the dimmer ones came out clear. That is, if I place the finished holograms on a white surface, and light it so that I don't see any diffraction from the image (just the light bouncing back through the emulsion, at a glancing angle) it is definitely bluish or purplish. Has anyone else noticed this?

It turned out that ageing the plates for only one day was not enough in my case. I stored some coated plates in the refrigerator for two weeks, and made two exposures (using these) last weekend. Much brighter holograms were produced, for some reason, compared to the same batch of plates that aged for only one day. Don't know why.

Hey Joe, not sure if your following the alcohol thread but I have come to the conclusion that the there are hardly any voids in the crosslinked areas and the amount of voids and their size there is completely dependeng on the amount of crosslinking in that area. I would assum many to be quite close to the size of a molecule or two of water. But in the unexposed and much less crosslinked but crosslinked to some extent because of dark reaction, fixing/hardening, post baking and the such, there is much more room for larger collections of water molecules and it is in this unexposed region that the voids and cracks are formed that everyone talks about and has electron photographed.

For some reason this just makes more sense to me.

PS: I thought I was wong once, but I was mistaken....LOL.

But lets look at this logically;

There has to be something in the unexposed regions (layers in a reflection) to keep the exposed regions from collapsing down on one another.

 It would not make sense that the higher index of refraction exposed layer has air voids in it. That would lower it's index of refraction.

* It does make sense the the expose area has a higher concentration of gelatin and thus is higher index of refraction but the unexposed area has the voids and cracks of air which lower is index of refraction.

The real key in a very bright hologram, as I have been pushing, it so get as least dense as possible with the most air in the unexposed regions to give a greater delta index of refraction between it and the dense exposed gelatin layer.
