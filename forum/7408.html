Let me express my appreciation to the members of the Russian section of the forum, particularly Gall and Holos, for providing a very interesting paper on DCG. Holos sent the original paper in Russian, while Gall did a very throrough and painstaking translation of the complete paper into English. Gall and Holos, please accept my heartfelt thanks.

The paper relates to the use of citric acid to increase the diffraction efficiency of DCG. The authors feel that, to increase diffraction efficiency, a larger amount of CrV needs to remain in the layer, without being being washed away by water. They believe that citric acid helps to "lock" the CrV into place:

"We believe that, in order to increase the diffraction effectivity of holograms, one has to create an increased concentration of Cr(V) compounds in the layer. The fact that they are extremely unstable in solutions, especially in water solutions, is evidence of the unregulated waste of some of Cr(V) compounds in the wet layers. However, it is also known that Cr(V) ions may be stabilized even in water solutions in the form of hydroxy acid complexes, i.e., with citric acid."

If succinic acid has a similar effect, it might be possible to combine this idea with the color-control method as described by Tony.

If anyone has an interest in this paper, I will be happy to forward the .pdf English version that Gall provided.

I believe this link has been provided before (here on the forum), but it contains some interesting information regarding gelatin, specifically about the "collagen-like" helical structure which was stressed in the paper by Courts:

[http://albumen.stanford.edu/library/c20/kozlov1983.html](http://albumen.stanford.edu/library/c20/kozlov1983.html)

If, as Courts suggests, gelatin becomes more rigid after treatment with citric acid (i.e., becomes more collagen-like by assuming a helical-coil structure as opposed to a random-coil structure), then this might fit well with the observations made by Sherstyuk et al. That is, if they think that CrV is being "locked" into place by the citric acid treatment, this might be easier to imagine if the gelatin was becoming more rigid at the same time. So the change in structure (i.e., helical and more rigid) might be conducive to keeping the CrV locked into position.

It should be rather simple to find out if the idea proposed by Sherstyuk is practical. After exposing a DCG hologram, place it in a citric acid bath, then fix, then dehydrate. Then do a control without the citric acid step, and compare the results. It would be preferable to use succinic acid in place of citric (for DCG) if this is possible, as DCG/citric holograms definitely seem prone to vanish. Tony was doing the succinic acid step near the end of the process, and when he gets back from vacation, I will suggest that he try it near the beginning, and see what happens. First, to see if there really is an increase in diffraction efficiency (by supposedly locking the CrV in place to be subsequently reduced to CrIII by the fixer), and perhaps at the same time casuing a red shift that can be brought back down towards the blue by heating (i.e., the "normal" succinic acid method that he described).
