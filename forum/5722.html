You can never be too clean. If you can, run a HEPA filter at all times. Blow off your glass with compressed air after cleaning and right before laying down the polymer. Blow compressed air across the polymer before taking off the cover sheet. Clean everything in the lab periodically with a HEPA vacuum. Don't lean over the glass plate after cleaning it unless you're wearing a dust cap on your head.

JohnFP Tue Jul 25, 2006 1:39 pm #11
I don't think I should make specifics to particular films public but I could offer some generalizations from the papers. Remember these are papers from their old testing and it may be the film you have acts differently. But this may be a good place to start.

The films can be use with or without cover sheet in place as the coversheet has low birefringence. With cover sheet in place sandwich film between two pieces of glass with index matching fluid. Without cover sheet, laminate tacky polymer side to glass. For reflections, an Anti Reflection plate should be used on both sides using an index matching fluid like Xylene.

The Mylar base is birefringent and needs to be oriented with its optical axis parallel to the recoding laser polarization to minimize the rotation of the laser polarization. This can be determined by placing the film at the recording angle between cross polarizers and finding the film direction which minimizes the transmitted light. Then use this orientation for subsequent exposures.

Producing holograms in the film involves three basic steps:

1. Exposure

2. UV cure – about 100mJ/cm^2 using 340 -380nm light

3. Bake - 120C for about 2 hours

> HOW CAN I KNOW MY SETUP BEAMS ARE S-POLARIZED? FURTHER,IF I RECORD IN ANOTHER ANGLE,IS IT NECESSARY TO ADJUST MY SETUP BEAM ON OTHER POLARIZING DIRECTION(IF THERE ARE EXISTING),AND HOW?

First, please do not use all caps. There's no reason for it. Now, do a search on this forum for polarization. Coherence, polarization & stability are the three main ingredients for holography. It's a real shame that "The Holography Handbook" for example, doesn't even mention polarization! But I digress.

When using p-polarized beams, fringe visibility depends upon the angle (inside the medium) between the beams. It's maximum at 0 degrees, and 0 at 90 degrees. When using s-polarization, you get maximum fringe visibility regardless of interbeam angle.

With a filtered liquid emulsion, is possible obtain a <5um particle free emulsion for sandwish coating but the glass must be optically clean.. it gives a very interesting HOE production method.
