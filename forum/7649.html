Joe Farina Tue Dec 23, 2008 8:22 pm #4
> Colin Kaminski: If I am making a hologram to keep I use it on the glass side only. I would bet with DCG you don't have that luxury and have to spray the gelatine.

As a side note, there is an alternate drying procedure for use with DCG. In the last alcohol bath, a cover plate is slipped into the bath and pressed against the DCG plate (while still submerged in the alcohol). The sandwich is pulled out, with a thin layer of alcohol trapped between the cover plate and DCG hologram. Then the sandwich is heated, and the alcohol liquid changes to vapor, which escapes through the narrow gap between the plates.

I've done this a number of times, and it actually works very well. I used a standard hair drier, so it took a long period of time to evaporate the alcohol, maybe something like 20 minutes, with the hair drier on maximum and held about an inch from the surface of the cover plate. Unfortunately there is another (more serious) problem with this method: if there is the tiniest fragment of loose gelatin that makes its way into the sandwich, it wreaks havoc in that area of the hologram. But if there is no foreign substance that causes that kind of problem, the results can be superb.

Joe Farina Tue Dec 23, 2008 9:19 pm #6
100C might be fairly close, it was just the maximum a standard hair drier could do. Regarding the "speed" of the final air drying to remove alcohol (in general), I honestly don't know. I wouldn't even venture to say that faster is better. But the results in the sandwich method were equally bright (without question) compared to normal drying. If you try it, you will see a definite "boundary" within the sandwich during drying (i.e., the areas which have turned to vapor vs. the areas which are still liquid). I have to admit it's slow going, and requires patience. When I tried it the first time (on 2.5" X 2.5" plates) it seemed like it was taking forever. Maybe an ordinary oven or heat gun would speed things up considerably, if you can avoid cracking the glass.

One thing I didn't like about the heat gun is the lack of air flow. It was very minimal, almost non existant in the one I had tried. And it was hard to contol as if I got to close it would crack the glass, to far away and it did not heat at well as the hair dryer.

I don't think you would get brighter results with a heat gun. The rapid drying, if you desire the broadband, happens mostly in the alcohol baths. Higher temps, less baths (35%, 90%, 100% or just 90% and 100% instead of using a few in between) and a shorter time in each bath (except for last 100%) is what really causes the "rapid drying" that affects the hologram the most. From what I understand the outermost layer forms a sort of crust and the water inside the hologram is trapped and pushed down into the fringes causing them to expand more the further from the surface you go. Then as the water is dried out those fringe crust into their expanded state. Thus you have narrower fringes at surface and they get increasingly wider the deeper you go. This allows more of the illuminating light's wavelengths to be reflected and thus produces a brighter hologram (more light is reflected).

Now off to the void discussion; You would think that all loose water and most of the loosly bonded water would be out already in a hot 100% alcohol. Remember, we are processing and trying to remove the water mostly from the unexposed areas. There is much more water and much more going on during processing in the unexposed areas then in the exposed areas. I believe because there must be a point of drying where the alcohol evaporates so fast that the gelain cracks in that area and does not have time to move slowly that increasing that drying temp does not help. And I believe you can achieve that threshold easily with a 1500 watt hair dryer set on high temp and high flow.
