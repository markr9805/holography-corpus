Martin Sun Dec 19, 2004 9:42 am #3
> Tom B.: I expect that microwave energy would heat up the water and drive it out, but would it have any interesting effects on dry bromide laden gelatin?

Very likely it depends on the amount of water loaded into the layer. In a wet layer microwave radiation will lead to boiling the water inside the emulsion. Unless the layer is strongly hardened, this might disrupt/damage the gelatin.

In my opinion applying microwaves on a "dry" layer (which still contains enough water, strongly linked to the gelatin) looks much more favorable.
Dinesh Sun Dec 19, 2004 3:56 pm #4
It's an interesting question perhaps to see what the effect of microwaves is on gelatin alone. Microwaves heat water because water H-O-H has a strong absorption band in the microwave region used by microwave cookers. However, since the gelatin molecule is full of H-O-H bonds and also H-N-H bonds, it seems that these molecular subgroups should also have an absorption band in mu_waves. It may be that the energy levels are either distroted due to the presence of the other bonded elements or the kinetic energy from the mu-waves is not strong enough to dislodge the subgroup from the molecule, but could the mu-waves generate rotational energy? In other words, could, say, gelatin, be induced into birefringence by exposure to microwaves?

Also, can the frequency of the klystron be altered in a microwave by altering the drive current? If so, can a frequency be found in a microwave oven that does heat gelatin alone? I'm surprised that all you electronics hackers out there haven't taken apart a microwave and played with the klystron.
