I just got some Bone 200 Bloom, Hide 200 bloom, Hide 175 Bloom, Hide 150 Bloom and have in storage some Bone 250 bloom. I am doing some testing with each.

Here is some more info from Mr. Busher from Great Lakes per our conversation.

The gelatin I use (food, bond and hide) does not contain the triple helix molecules I have been reading. The "gelatin" or collagen made to have the triple helix molecules is a special process that uses cold water and acetone. It is not food or photo gelatin.

The gelatin for photo (and food) has the amino acid strands which are linked to one another in long chains and are not found in the triple helix structure. When you heat the gelatin in water the strands break free from one another and themselves and are suspended in the water as they are negatively charged. As the gelatin cool the strands start to link together again to make long chains as found in the tissue of the body. The higher blooms have a higher concentration or densities of these strands. Crosslinking causes a solidification of the gelatin
JohnFP Wed Nov 02, 2005 1:38 pm #8
Ok, take some pasta (spaghetti) and boil it in water till it is soft. Then take the pasta and drain the water off and put the pasta back into a bowl and cool it off with running cold water. Drain again. Then put it in the refrigerator and let it chill. Take it out and observer it. It is all stuck together in a tanglement of strands going every which way. It is springy and you can squish it slightly and pull it slightly and it goes back to its normal shape. If you squish or pull it too much the strand break and it does not return to its original shape. If you put the unbroken clump back in hot water the strands separate and the process can be repeated. If you put the broken and deformed (stressed) clump back in the hot water and repeat the process the chilled mass does not take up the volume. Smaller broken pieces fit more tightly together, thus the mass is not as springy. Too much brokenness and the mass becomes more like a solid mass then a spongy material.

Now, do the same thing but after you take it out of the refrigerator take some water proof epoxy and glue some strands that are close enough to glue (Corsslinking). Now put the mass back in the hot water. All the glued pieced retain their bonds but the rest of the mass floats freely. If you screen out the loose strands and then recool what's left, you will find only the epoxied parts are left holding their shape and the non epoxied spaghettis have been removed (wash process).

Now do the epoxying in rows to simulate fringes but remember the rows are three dimensional. That is epoxy a row a couple of inches wide and 1 inch deep, leave a row unepoxied one inch deep, epoxy the next row one inch deep, repeat. Then go back and (use a syringe) to epoxy a row from one inch deep to two inches deep below the unepoxied row. Do this for each unepoxied row. Repeat the heating in water, straining loose strands and cooling process above. You should see a three dimensional matrix with glued (crosslinked) areas where the spaghetti is more dense and unglued (uncrosslinked) areas where there is hardly any spaghetti.

Stressing this mass in the boiling water (the second time after gluing (crosslinking)) will cause mainly the glued areas to break apart thus allowing more glued spaghettis to be washed away in the process.

Well there you have it, my interpretation of what's going on. All those years as a cook in a restaurant finally paid off for holography.
