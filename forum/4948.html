> what is a hologram?

...An optical device which, under certain conditions, is capable of reconstructing the light wave patterns which emanated from an original scene, providing the visual cues which give the observer the impressions of a three dimensional reconstruction... (others will do better than I on this one)

> why do you need laser light?

...You need to be able to create a reference when making the hologram, and to reproduce the reference when reconstructing (viewing) a hologram. Lasers are the easiest way to do this, though holograms have been made using electron beams and street lamps as well...

> what kind of objects can you take a hologram of?

...depends on the laser. For small affordable lasers, the answer is usually 'small dead things', i.e. things that don't move even a fraction of a wavelength of light over the exposure time. For big expensive pulse lasers, the answer is more things are included like people...

> what kind of stuff do you need?

...depends on how sophisticated you want to get. For a minimum, you need a laser, a lens, an object, and a type of holographic 'film'... Mirrors, beamsplitters, spatial filters, optical tables can be added for better results...

> can anyone do it?

...NO. But 5th graders, high school students, college students, and even PhDs can do it, so it's not that hard!

It all depends if you want to learn about holograms (what they are? etc) or if you want to make one. If you want to make one, you don't need to know anything about how and why it works. You simply get the right equipment, follow a few easy steps and you got yourself a hologram! De Freitas has an easy guide ( [http://www.holoworld.com/shoebox/index.html](http://www.holoworld.com/shoebox/index.html) ) and there's the kit from Integraf (link above this page).

If you want to understand how and why it works, it may get bit complicated. The level you want to understand it depends on your level of science knowledge. You can get the "it's all done with mirrors" explanation or you can go deeper. There's a bunch of books, net resources (Edelbrp mentioned Wiki, there's also sites like these ( [http://www.holostudios.com/holohelper/faq.htm](http://www.holostudios.com/holohelper/faq.htm) ). Jason Sapan is an old hand and knows his stuff backwards!
