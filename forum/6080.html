Hans Wed Feb 28, 2007 9:02 am #2
In a way, all single step holograms are negatives. This is just a result of the photographic process. If you were to make a photographic copy of this hologram, you would get a positive. But the difference would only be that all the dark fringes have swapped place with the light fringes: about 0.3um difference in place. If you could inspect the hologram under a microscope, it would look as if the fringes have moved this distance to the side. Other than that, there would be no difference between the two holograms.

If you are thinking about needing a holographic negative to be able to make a contact copy of the original hologram? Sure you can. With certain types of hologram that is. Saxby writes about this in his most excellent book "Practical Holography"

ErichRose Wed Feb 28, 2007 10:04 pm #6
When making a hologram you are almost always recording at true, or actual, scale. When you see something that has been reduced or enlarged the hologram is probably a multiplexed image (stereoscopic effect visualized through a holographic "technique") or was computer generated and never really existed as a real object. Holographers also will work with scale models.

There are some scale shifts that occur as a result of change in playback wavelength or a "focused" image hologram may be slightly changed. But generally you get pretty much exactly what you shoot.

I've never seen an "enlarged or shrunk" hologram. I guess it could be done with some lenses between a master (H1) and a copy (H2) but there would be real compromises in the final image.

I would also make the point that recording a hologram is closer to shooting a Polaroid photo. There is no inherant need for a "negative" at all. The various discussions of H1s and H2 and masters and such are all just techniques toward a particular end. H1s or masters are as much finished holograms as the H2s or copies made from them.
