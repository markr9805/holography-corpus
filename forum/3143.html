I have to agree with Michael, what are you looking for? If you read some of the posts here and the older archived posts you will find tons of different ideas. It sounds like you are doing a Denisyuk hologram and may be looking for additional ideas with the components you already have. A fun thing to do with what you have would be to make a two channel hologram. Now I am not sure if you are making your hologram with an overhead reference beam or a side lit reference beam, but I am going to assume it is an over head for the following description. As it seems you have a good handle on holography, adjust as necessary for side illumination (which may prove a little more difficult). First find two objects that are related. Take the first object and make a Denisyuk hologram of it with about 2/3 of the exposure you normally use. Have the plate and object not only at the 56 degrees or so angled from the reference beam but also twisted such that the light is almost coming in from one of the upper corners. Then set up for the second hologram with the same, undeveloped plate but have the reference beam angle, again not only at the 56 degree overhead but twisted such that it comes in from the other top corner of the plate. Make that exposure about 2/3 of normal exposure. Process as normal. What you should have is a two channel hologram. When you set it up for viewing with a nice light and move from your eyes from left to right the hologram changes from the one object to the other. You many need to test exposure times and take into consideration the different reflectivities and nature of each object. But it will be fun to do.
