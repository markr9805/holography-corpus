MichaelH Wed Jul 12, 2006 8:54 pm #2
One caveat to include is that the ratio depends on the film, subject and processing regime.

For PFG-01 film processed with CWC2 and bleached with EDTA I use a 2:1 ratio for my transmission holograms and sometimes post treat them with 1-2% Ascorbic acid to increase contrast.

For reflection H2s using the same film and chemicals I measure light from each source in order to figure the approximate exposure time but use a piece of plain white paper in my plateholder and adjust the ratio until I can just see the H1 image from the reference side of the H2 plateholder. That works well where there's high contrast areas in the H1. If the brightness of the projected image is approximately uniform across the H2 preview paper, I'll actually measere both to get a ratio of close to 1:1.

BobH Tue Aug 08, 2006 7:24 pm #13
> I have wondered if I should or if I'd get better control over the final brightness in replay.

The effect would be to essentially change the zero set point of your metering system. The fact that most holographers either don't do what you suggest, or do some variation of it, is one of the reasons we get our best results at unique beam ratios. Same thing as whether we account for reflections or not.

You could record the hologram with a polarizer in front of the object. This makes a brighter, cleaner image for a given beam ratio because the gratings formed by the extra object light are prevented. It means you can decrease the beam ratio, making the hologram even brighter (until the previous signal to noise level is achieved). Of course, this usually isn't very practical unless you have lots of light to use.

Ron Michael Tue Aug 08, 2006 10:09 pm #15
Colin,

Also to point out that beam ratios may be confusing if not qualified. Most of this discussion is related to as measured at the film plane which takes in all setup issues and the reflectivity of the object and the reference energy density. Some use an average object values while some use the maximum value from the specular highlight. But in split beam holography, the beamspitters, illumination size and loss, and object reflectivity affect setup beam ratios which can be controlled by variable beamsplitters or by a polarizing beamsplitter and half wave plates. So from the setup position, object beams generally are the brigher source. This may be obvious to most holographers but not to a novice. In my pulse work I refer to the setup ratios. Typical for my setup are: 5:70:14 for ref:obj1:obj2. I use two photodiodes meters and a digital storage oscilloscope to measure the actual film ratios which by the way generally are around 1.5:1 Ref:obj when shooting a subject. But additonally the meters are used to measure also the energy density on the subject as well, regarding irradiance safety calculations and film exposure levels.
