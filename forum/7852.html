DJ Mathson Sun Aug 16, 2009 11:15 pm #9
Thanks for your input.

> Colin Kaminski: So you can magnify the beam with a single prism but you can not control the magnification without re-grinding the prism.

Wouldn´t it be possible to control the magnification by just rotating the prism? The transmittance would be less, when not using the prism at Brewster´s angle, but it would let you change the beam diameter.

I read in the Melles Griot paper [Characteristics of Efficient Diode Laser Collimators](http://www.mellesgriot.com/pdf/effdlcol.pdf) that:

> A simple and economic way of transforming the elliptical laserbeam cross-section emerging from a collimating lens into a circle, is to use a pair of identical wedge prisms, as shown in Figure 3.This result could also be accomplished with a single prism, however, the prism pair has the advantage of directing the beam back along its original direction, and also allows larger amounts of ellipticity to be corrected.

So this kind of answers my first question in this thread. Citation bolded by me.

Melles Griot also have another interesting paper on beam circularization, [Diode Laser Circularization](http://www.mellesgriot.com/pdf/d.l.cir.postscript.pdf).

Regarding transmittance through an anamorphic prism pair it says:

> Again, the total transmission is the product of the individual transmissions and for a pair of identical prisms it is just T2. This expression is useful because it gives an idea of what happens to the transmission of a prism pair with changing magnification. For a magnification of 2 the transmission through a pair of identical uncoated BK 7 prisms is 88 percent, whereas for a magnification of 6 it is only 41 percent.
