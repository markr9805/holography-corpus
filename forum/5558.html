Ed Wesly Sun May 21, 2006 9:06 pm #2
Here's what I usually do:

For exact same laser replay color in reflection holograms, CWC2 developer and PBQ rehalogenating bleach.

For H1's, transmission or reflection, ditto

For color shifted reflection holograms, (single beam or H2), Pyro or CWC2 developer, dichromate reversal bleach.

For transmission H2's, either the first choice, but in a somewhat radical move use develop, fix, rehalogenating bleach. This can boost the signal to noise ratio compared to the latter, since whatever crystals aren't developed in the shadow areas (where only the reference beam falls) are removed, lowering the population density of scattering sites.

Another case is for low frequency gratings, use develop reversal bleach or develop fix rehalogenate, for if the fringes are too far apart the "swim" of the materials from one fringe to the other is too far and not much modulation occurs. I discovered this when I made a low spatial frequency grating (the fringes were about 30 line pairs per mm, as you could see them with a strong magnifying glass) and the transmission grating was weak, but the single beam reflection holo of the back side of the glass (the dreaded woodgrain) was decently bright! I switched to develop/reversal bleach and painted the back of the plate back to fix things up.

Ed Wesly Mon May 22, 2006 11:25 am #5
All my knowledge was gained on Agfa emulsions, 8E75 and 56. When Ilford came out with their stuff, I added the trick of using a pyrogallol based developer with a rehalogenating bleach to counteract the effects of Ilford's Built-In PreSwell agent to preserve wavelength rendition in reflection mode. I used this trick also on the BB-640 plates because they were not replaying exactly laser red with their recommended developer.

PFG-01 works just like the Agfa, but it seems PFG-03M is a different animal, so I use their recommendation. I would guess Fuji would be the same as Agfa. I haven't used any Gentet material yet.

I also used this system on some Bulgarian Academy of Sciences HP-490 plates, instead of the recommended colloidal processing, and it worked fine.

One other thing I forgot was that the higher speed emulsions, Agfa 10E75 and Kodak SO-253 film/Type 131 plate respond best to the PyroChrome processing, making acceptable transmission holograms and gratings.

Maybe when a blue laser falls out of the sky I'll try some color plates.
