edelbrp Thu Feb 10, 2005 2:46 am #9
> Colin Kaminski: What is the gravity of pure alcohol? I guess I should know this for my job as well.

Isopropyl is 0.8000 @ 25C (I think)

Take a look here: [http://www.simetric.co.uk/si_liquids.htm](http://www.simetric.co.uk/si_liquids.htm)

Divide #'s by 1,000 to get [specific gravity](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_gravity).

Notice that spirit hydrometers are calibrated for ethanol at 60 degrees F (increase slightly if hotter), and NOT for isopropyl. You need to make a miinor adjustment. If I did the math right, pure isopropyl with read as 98.1% on a spirits hydrometer.

Oh, last note: be careful which kind of hydrometer you use. There is a much more common hydrometer used for fermentation which has a % alcohol scale on it. It's actually measuring the amount of /potential/ alcohol by measuring the amount of sugar in solution that the yeast will convert to alcohol. Spirit hydrometers use a smaller weight on the end. They are also very fagile, so be careful. (I broke one just a few months ago trying to tap the clinging bubbles off.)

Colin Kaminski Thu Feb 10, 2005 4:38 am #10
Well, I am a brewer ([http://www.downtownjoes.com](http://www.downtownjoes.com)) and knowing the specific gravity of Ethyl Alcohol is a good thing. I use hydrometers calabrated in Plato everyday. A note about bubbles. Fill the sample jar to the top. Insert the hydrometer slowly to where you think the reading will be. This will overflow the sample and wash out all surface bubbles. Then spin the hydrometer to dislodge any remaining bubbles. If you just drop in the hydrometer it will sink below the reading and the wieght of the wetted surface above the meniscus will make the sample read light. Very important: Read from the bottom of the meniscus! This take practice.

If you need a more precise method then you will need a pictnometer and a scale. These are tricky to use but about 100 times more accurate than a hydrometer. If money is no object there is a device, who's name excapes me, that is a U shaped tube. You fill the sample and it measures the resonance of the tube. Know this frequency in can calculate the exact wieght of the volume inside the resonating structure. I used one when I got to visit UC Davis's brewing department on a consulting gig. Really nice!
