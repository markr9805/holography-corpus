Most objects, being diffuse reflectors, de-polarize light anyway so it's kind of a moot point. But there will be interference. I've seen holos come out just fine with unpolarized or randomly polarized lasers, but like the above never have done a side by side comparison. I doubt if there would be a significant difference, the big advantage of the polarized beams is in minimizing the dreaded woodgrain.
Colin Kaminski Tue Nov 08, 2005 4:47 am #8
> Ed Wesly: the big advantage of the polarized beams is in minimizing the dreaded woodgrain.

Since we have a few new people, now would be a good time to review woodgrain. If you make a hologram that looks like there is a "woodgrain pattern" in the film plane then you need to rotate the laser 90 deg and/or make the reference beam angle about 56 deg. This has to do with low frequency fringes being created from the reflection from the glass. 56 Deg. is the brewsters angle for plain glass to air interfaces. Search the forum for brewsters angle for more information.
