There seem to be far more sources for fs mirrors than for ways to acutally use them. I have some loose 17mm mirrors, no breadboard or magnetic table, and am looking for a simple method of mounting/ using them. For the time being I am just using single beam techniques and I just picked up some mirrors for fill and misc beam bouncing fun. I saw some parts for a setup using 1/2" posts, but that seems a bit much for 17mm mirrors and I couldn't tell if my mirrors would fit in the lens/mirror mounts (is there a difference?) anyway. Should I be using larger mirrors? Is there a good source for mini tripods or small stands? Can I glue or blue tack them to small post? I don't mind spending some money if I have to, but I'm just starting to play around with all of this and I'd like to start out with a simple setup if I can before I dive off the deep end.

Thanks,

Colin Kaminski Tue Mar 21, 2006 10:41 pm #2
You can hot melt glue them to anything!

Hot melt glue it to some 1/8" x 1" steel and stick it to a magnet. There is an easy way to use two magnets and some small pieces of steel to make a 3-d translation mount for pennies but I don't have a photo handy.

Bill Ashuler has some and they were quite easy to use and very stable.

You could make a sand table and glue them to pvc and stick them into the sand.

Easier is to stick it to a popsicle stick and push it into a glass of sand.

Be creative!

Wow, that brings back memories from long long ago. When I was very young, maybe 8 or 9 years old, I was facinated with light, mostly sunlight. I remember getting a bunch of mirrors (just regular mirrors) and setting them up in my bedroom directing the sunlight all over the room. I drew the curtains except for a small opening where the first mirror was then aimed it to another mirror, etc. It must have been 10 mirrors or so set up. Then for weeks I could go to my room at a certain time of day, turn off the lights, light and extinguish a candle and see the sunlight come into the correct angle and bouce around each mirror for a few minutes. Then it would leave as silently as it came. I got such a kick out of that. I would invite a family member or friend to come to my room, knowing in a few minutes the "thing" would happen. Then when we got there, bam... light reflecting everywhere. I got some great reactions.
