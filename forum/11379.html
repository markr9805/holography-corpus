It's important to illuminate the film as uniformly as possible. The reason is that the material (polymer) reacts to the energy it's "given" by the beam, if the beam is non-uniform, some areas of the film - and so the object - receive more light than other areas, and the polymer "receives" more energy. This may cause some areas of the image to be darker than others, because some areas are over-exposed. It's a common error to see a Single Beam Denisyuk image to be dark, almost black, in the centre because the beam just covered the film area causing over-exposure in the centre due to the Gaussian nature. On the other hand, if you only illuminate the film, and expose as per the beam intensity at the centre, so the corners receive too little light, the corners will be under-exposed, and may be dim.

As a general rule of thumb, you should expand the beam so that the centre 1/3rd of the beam covers the exposure areas. If your film is 4in x 4in (10cm x 10cm), the centre 1/3rd of the beam should have a diameter of about five and a half inches ( 14 cm) - this is the length of the diagonal. So, the beam should expand to have a diameter of about 12 - 15 in ( 30 - 37cm). This is, as I say, a rule of thumb, but, if you can't expand by this much, you should "overspill" the beam around the film to get it as uniform as possible on the film.

I had a great first result. A few things I learned over the next five exposures

2) I doubled the distance to the film and got about 10 cm. The exposure was 6 minutes.

3) I asked Geola about the exposure latitude and they said it was nearly impossible to overexpose. So I did a 10 minute exposure.
