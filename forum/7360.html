I learnt something amazing today, and thought I'd pass it on to all you folks out there who coat your own plates and may have no knowlwdge of this.

Float glass has two sides- one of which is in contact with molten tin, and the other which is exposed to air, during manufacture. Simple enough so far.

The surface which had been contact with the tin ('tin side') has different surface properties from the surface which was exposed to air ('air side').

It turns out that 'air side' has properties which are far more conducive to good gel coatings than the tin side. In order to obtain good quality gel coatings, one has to be able to identify the 'air side' and coat on it.

The 'tin side' can be identified by the flourescence when it is illuminated with short wave UV. See for eg-

[http://www.freepatentsonline.com/y2007/0002313.html](http://www.freepatentsonline.com/y2007/0002313.html)

More information on the identification technique as & when I get it.

So.............which side do you coat on?

Yes I have had over the past few years 2 lots of 0.75mm thick glass as free samples from Pilkingtons for our research purposes. (They were about 60 cm square so they were as breakable as you could possibly imagine. You had to move them about slowly , if you even walked along too fast the air resistance would break them.) Anyway these sheets were carefully annotated as to which was the "tin side". In using them for gelatin coatings, and polymer coatings of many types I never could find that it made the slightest difference which side I chose.

JohnFP Mon Jun 02, 2008 10:33 am #5
I can instantly tell which side is which when I clean the glass. The last step is to do a final dry with a paper towel. The side that is the float side (tin) is much much more smoth. The air side definitely is rougher. I have tried coating both sides and never really saw a difference. But i bet the air side will hold the gelatin layer better for those of you having lifting problems.

Ed Wesly Mon Jun 02, 2008 4:23 pm #8
I learned this coating trick from Steve Provence: When washing the glass with a sponge, see which side squeaks louder, and use that side. We were using photo-resist, and it stuck well after the plate was coated with HMDS. There is no need for the Ferroxo backing for adhesion, and we simply used Krylon Ultra Flat Acrylic enamel spray as the anti-halation layer, which removes easily with water, if it isn't too old. (like weeks old)

Hope this helps the gelatin coating!
