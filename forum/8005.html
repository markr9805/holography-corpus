a_k Fri Oct 02, 2009 8:44 pm #17
John, it might be a bit more involved than that as the refractive index of gelatin (1.55-1.25 - before/after exposure) is not necessarily constant throughout the thickness of the coating. Rallison proposes a gradient of the refractive index of the gelatin along the thickness of the coating. According to his proposal at the interface glass/gelatin the refractive index is high with low variations and the refractive index decreases while its variations get larger across the film.

[http://www.xmission.com/~ralcon/dcgprocess/p2.html](http://www.xmission.com/~ralcon/dcgprocess/p2.html)

Ed Wesly Mon Oct 05, 2009 2:20 pm #21
Is the chirp of the grating to give a broadband effect related to the fringe contrast? At the top of the emulsion, the reference beam dominates since it hasn't been attenuated by the coating, while the object light has been through the whole emulsion so it is at its dimmest. But at the bottom of the emulsion, where the reference beam has gone through all of the thickness, it is at its weakest while the object beam has just entered the coating and will be at its strongest.

It's similar thinking to the above, I don't know if this helps the understanding, but this question/problem has been around for a while!
