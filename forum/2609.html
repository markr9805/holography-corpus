Tom B. Thu Jul 08, 2004 4:17 am #2
Presensitizing is absolutely required. The recommended plain water soak did not work at all for me. Use at least 3% TEA pretreatment. This will still need a fairly intense exposure - I seemed to need at least 1 milliJoule/cm^2 to get reasonable density. More TEA reduces exposure requirements. I think at 20% TEA exposure was down to a few hundred microJoules/cm^2.

If the color shift is not wanted, or if you have no TEA (triethanolamine), you could try presoaking in 1% ammonia solution. (Dinesh's suggestion) I just diluted household ammonia (not BLEACH!) with distilled water. Use an airtight container - the stuff is very stinky. I found it gave sensitivity equivalent to TEA with no color shift, but with slight fogging (darkening of unexposed areas). As a TEA alternative, you could experiment with ammonia + something else (corn syrup or sugar?) to preswell the emulsion.

I think the JD-4 will work fine - I use the same developer, but a slightly different bleach - ferric rather than copper based. Re density, I went to the same density as usual for reflection holos - approx. 2.0 (1% light transmission). This did not seem to be too critical.

Dinesh Thu Jul 08, 2004 7:07 pm #4
I've never used BB640, but I have used BB520 and the new blue sensitive plates (BB420?). I can agree with Tom about the 3% TEA pre-treatment and the subsequent ~1mJ exposure, but I've never tried 20%. With 3% TEA, I soak for 2 minutes, then a 1 minute wash in water to wash out the TEA, then a quick dunk in photoflo for a few seconds - basically, dunk it in and shake it around. I lay it flat on paper, emulsion side up and squegee twice. My technique is to dip my finger in the photo-flo/water and wipe it across the blade of the squegee to "wet" the edge before I apply the squegee. I then wipe the back (non-emulsion) side with a dry paper towel and stand upright in a dark environment. It usually dries within about 10 minutes.

I usually shoot straight away. Once I pre-sensitized the previous evening at about 6pm and shot the next day at noon with no change in propertties. However others on this forum have more experience about the effect of delayed shooting after pre-sensitizing.

Tom B. Fri Jul 09, 2004 12:41 am #6
I've never tried washing out the TEA - I presume this gives a sensitivity increase without the wavelength shift due to swelling. I had naively thought that the TEA had to be present in the emulsion for it to work. Now that I think about it, I've never seen an explanation for the sensitizing effect of TEA.

I mix the distilled water, TEA, and several drops of foto-flow all together in a rectangular sealable plastic food storage container, soak for 2-3 minutes, remove plate, stand it on edge on paper towel to let most of the liquid drain, then squeegee per Dinesh's method. I take care to wipe (with a damp towel) all traces of TEA that cling to the non-emulsion side of the plate, and polish until clean with a dry towel. Note: TEA is a bit slow to dissolve in water- mix under a good light until the faint "stringyness" caused by differences in refractive index is gone. The TEA solution can be re-used several times, but eventually develops a chemical smell, I think from preservatives and inhibitors dissolved out of the emulsion, and I throw it out. Drying afterwards I use a hairdryer on cool setting for a minute or so. I actively dry the plate to minimize the time the emulsion is sticky and attractive to dust, and don't use heat because it's not needed and it would take some time for the plate to thermally stabilize after heating.
