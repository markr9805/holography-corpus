john kaufman Wed Mar 12, 2008 10:07 pm #2
I, too was sad to hear of Harriet's passing. Years ago we worked at my studio in Pt. Reyes on reflection transfers of nudes. For me, she was a pleasure to work with; an artist who knew what she wanted but who could still adapt to the necessities of the medium.

I am also angered. In looking at her obituary in the Boston Globe, and in an earlier article, the implication is that after decades in the same studio, she was forced out and the January move broke her health and led to fatal pneumonia.
Jeffrey Weil Thu Mar 13, 2008 1:46 pm #3
I met Harriet probably a half dozen times. She was great. I would always spend some time talking to her at Lake Forest or wherever we might meet up. Big ones for sure, she took no s**t from anyone!

One time she showed up for a symposium dressed in some lingerie like Madonna would wear, she was probably over 60 at the time, that takes some guts! And she had them.

She believed more in her own work than most artists do in there own. She would show us slides of pulsed holograms of deformed and overweight people and then lecture the audience about true beauty if someone winced. She was always ready for a fight and I've seen her have more than one on stage. She was always ready to stand up for what she believed in.

I'll miss her. If anyone here on the board has a chance to meet any of the classic women artists in holography don't miss the chance. I love them all, Harriet, Margret Benyon, Marie Andree Cossette, etc.....

Jeffrey Weil
