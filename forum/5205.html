I was having a problem getting this hologram to come out bright due to it's natural color. I tried it with the Dual Reference SBR and it came out much brighter. Too long an exposure or second light from below too intense and burn in is visible with top reference viewing but not when hologram is turned over and viewed with bottom reference beam. Believe it or not it seems to be a delicate balance with these two variables which I have not mastered yet. Notice the shadows from the bottom reference beam.

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Test_Research/DualRef/BothLeftOverRight.JPG)

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Test_Research/DualRef/BottomL.JPG)

Here is one of the tests. I tried a few varying the ratio of the two reference beams and changing exposures. This one was the best. These two variables are very sensitive in controlling burn in and yeilding maxium brightness for the overhead reference beam lighting. The first view is two beam pseudo view (I had the plate emulsion facing the wrong way) thus viewing with bottom reference beam with both beams on left and only below reference on right.

![Image](http://www.holograms3d.com/Test_Research/DualRef/DualRef4.jpg)

Here the hologram is turned over. You can see the dual beam side is considerably brighter.
