Colin Kaminski Sun Nov 16, 2008 1:56 am #2
I still have not found the minim conditions. A bathroom with a Electro Luminescent Green night light would be fine. Finding the stability is going to be your concern. You should be able to do a 4x5" hologram if the plate is touching the object. The point of contact will be sharp no matter what. But the background will only be sharp if you are stable. If you get more involved I can give you a teble idea that should work better but would take a couple of hours of wood working.
BobH Sun Nov 16, 2008 7:32 am #3
I recommend it. I've just made my first pictorial hologram in four years; shot on the floor of my garage and processed in the bathroom as you're considering. First one worked! Dim, but it's all there!

I'm using a 25mW He-Ne as old as the hills, a Denisyuk geometry, 3 year old PFG-01 plate I had left from a HOE project, a couple of Optiled LED lamps with gels in front for safelights, processing in 4x5" trays on a temporary shelf I installed over the toilet (that's removed when not in use), and washing in a small tub in the sink. I wash the trays out in the tub. The bathroom window is covered with a piece of solar shield nylon material (white on one side and black on the other side) taped to the edges of the window. Working at night means I don't need to seal everything in the garage very well to get it good and dark in there.

I have to say I felt plenty of that old juice when I saw color in the first plate as I dried it, then that sense of amazement that hooked me in the first place as I walked quickly out to see it in the sodium vapor street light.

I know it will only get better from here. Everything I'm doing can be better with the longer coherence length of a proper diode laser. I have to say it's just as satisfying making a hologram this way as it is with all the stuff. For me, it's the process. Big or small, bright or dim, red or green, art or HOE ... it's still the process. There is something a little extra when it's pictorial though. Go ahead and do it!

Colin Kaminski Wed Nov 19, 2008 3:35 pm #5
The kit comes with trays and a laser. The laser works very well but is only 5mw. Take the lens off and you have a diffraction limited beam spread with no other optics. The diode is quite eliptical. The last one I was using was 3 to 1. It all fits into a box slightly larger than a shoebox.

Hi,

I have been using Integraf kit for about 3 months now and into my second set of 30 plates now. Ultra-dark room is not needed. I have made some hologram in twilight hours when little bit of light was leaking around the newspapers that I had stuck in the window. Got the results alright. Also I made a safelight using a 10mm green LED. It works fine.

My initial holograms were made by keeping the object and plate on a mouse pad as recommended by Integraf. I got the results. The mouse pad was kept on the floor and not on the table.

Then for experiment's sake I switched to an iron plate put on a inflated tube inner. The results have improved. So i am sticking to this make-shift optical table.

Hope this helps in some manner.

Cheers!
