wler Wed Mar 09, 2005 7:10 am #2
Energy never disappers, of course. Either there will be zones of

constructive interference around your black spot, where the intensity

is doubled. That will be the case because your beams are not parallel.

In other words, you cannot combine two non-parallel beams such that

they fully annihilate; they will always create zones of constructive

interference as well.

For parallel beams, energy is reflected back to the source (the

latter is how a dielectric mirror works - there is destructive

interference such that no light passes, but it does not just disappear

but rather goes back to where it came from).
Dinesh Wed Mar 09, 2005 3:26 pm #3
The question of: "where does the energy go in interference?" has been asked many, many times. To me, it's always a sign of a bright student, but I have heard optical engineers with many years experience ask it! Wler's explanation is the standard one, the energy is redistributed amongst the many constructive and destructive sources. Also due to interference within the reflective materials. The "constructive" zones occur within the medium. One small point, wler, the energy distribution is dependant on the beam ratio, the constructive areas are not always quadrupled (I assume you were confusing A for I). I only say this because I've heard of "perpetual motion" optical machines based on this apparent quadrupling of the constructive region by people who get their information from TV or google. However, regarding the dielectric mirror, I suppose you can always get total destructive interference with a glass mirror.

There is another way of looking at it. This involves the concept of "work". The electric field from one wave is going the opposite way to the electric field of another wave. Thus the net electric field is zero, since the field is a vector quantity. The electric field causes forces which drive motion in charged particles and so, with no e field present, there's no force and so no work is done. It's probably a bit like climbing a hill and then descending. The net energy is zero because what you lost going up you regained coming down. Or perhaps two springs pulling on a ball in opposite directions. Both springs contain energy but the forces generated are in opposite directions and no work is done on the ball. As an astronomer, you probably know that this is why planets don't lose energy and fall into the sun - no work is done on the planet despite the fact it's whizzing around at a gazillion mph.

Bear in mind, this is pretty theoretical. In a real situation, you can't get complete destructive interference over an area. As Wler says, you'll always get constructive and destructive interference in non-collimated beams and it's impossible to get a beam with a completely flat wavefront. Diffraction effects through the air will diverge the beam. If it appears to be completely black, the constructive areas are probably too small to be detected except with very fine equipment.

I've see this myself. One thing to consider is that any of illumination (or lack of) on the wall is caused by some magnification system and all magnification systems have apertures. It's possible, by aperturing off the side areas to make the central black spot appear to be alone. So when you use lenses or mirrors to expand and collimate, you're creating an entrance and exit aperture. At some point the beam is expanding and being cut off by the optical system, even if it appears not to be. It's possible to have an interference structure in which 90% of the inner ring is black and 10% of the outer ring is light. The standard sinc function (sin^2(phi)/sin(phi)) is such a function. In "standard configuration" 96% of the light is in the large central region with side lobes dropping off to about 4% at the next maximum and then lower still at further maxima., However, by placing a phase plate in the system, it's possible to invert this. In this case, the large central area will be dark with the intensity distribution being spread over the smaller side lobes to infinity. If the aperture of the system cuts off these side lobes, then it will appear as if you've cancelled out all the light. All you've in fact done, is to transfer the enrgy to the higher order Fourier terms and then "spatially filtered" them out.
