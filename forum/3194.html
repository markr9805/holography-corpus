If working with green sensitive film, a red laser pointer is great to use as a safe light and very inexpensive. But check your film spectral sensitivity.
Dinesh Tue Mar 08, 2005 3:37 pm #2
Wouldn't you need to expand and diffuse the beam first? I don't see how a laser can illuminate a dark room. I just use standard 25W bulbs with solid, as opposed to transparent, glass. It works well enough with Slavuch VRP. A safer technique is to house the bulb in a concave, movable lamp fitting. This way, if you're unsure, you can always point the lamp up and away from your working area.
MichaelH Tue Mar 08, 2005 4:04 pm #3
> Dinesh: Wouldn't you need to expand and diffuse the beam first?

Nope, at least not with a 5mW green pointer. Aim it at a white wall or ceiling and it lights up the whole room. Red should work similarly but will appear slightly dimmer at the same power level.
floh Fri Oct 14, 2005 8:29 pm #4
my safe light is a 7W low-energy (or whatever they called in english in german we say "Energiesparlampe") fluorescent bulb in a white plastic box where are two or three layers of green or red foil (depends on the film i currently use) on the inner side. check my gallery (on my home page) for a picture. It works really good. a test piece of holographic film that lays on the table for more than 30 min never get black in the developper bath.

the costs for this safe light is low. It goes not hot because of only 7W and it makes enough light to work comfortable. the foil is good enough that three layers completely block a 200mW laser beam

if i watch the green one thru a grating there is a lot of green a very little blue but no red. the red has a little orange but no yellow or other colors.

Ed Wesly Tue Nov 29, 2005 10:19 pm #8
I don't know if this belongs here or under Dumb, Dumber, Dumbist (which is even more funny since it's spelled dumbest).

I was doing some photographic printing and it appeared that the paper was fogged, as the white border was turning grey. I added more and more Benzotriazole (Kodak Anti-Fog No.1) to the developer and it didn't improve matters.

I looked at the glow of the safelight on the darkroom ceiling (I have the traditional Kodak 10 by 12 inch fixtures pointed for indirect safelighting) and although there was a nice orange, upon closer examination it turned out that it was a low color temperature orange from the funky Wratten No. 3 Green that had developed some cracks. I had forgotten to swap out the filters! Plus this green is no longer usable for holo, since it's passing a broadband emission!
