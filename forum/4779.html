I suspect your diode has failed due to static electricity. One zap can blow off a facet, and then the resonator is toast. Usually this also results in reduced output as well.

I've had this happen several times to small diodes, even when the driver circuitry was attached. I have a drawer full of dead ones.

That's one reason why I switched to Henes. Heck, If a Hene doesn't start right away, I scrape up some static electrity and give 'em a 1/4 inch zap! -starts em every time!

Colin Kaminski Sat Apr 23, 2005 10:51 pm #7
The size of the fringes relates to the geometry of the interferometer. Try to align the two beams so that they are perfectly in line and you get a bullseye pattern for the most accurate results.
JohnFP Sun Apr 24, 2005 12:00 pm #8
> Colin: Try to align the two beams so that they are perfectly in line and you get a bullseye pattern for the most accurate results

Yes, and the largest fringes. Although ususally when I get the bulls eye in my set up I only see a fringe or two.

If your fringes are running up and down move the right to left tilt of one of the mirror a very little bit. If the fringes get smaller then go the other way with the same adjustment. You should see them get bigger. At some point, like tweaking a spatial filter they will probably change to diagonal or across, where you would need to move the top to bottom mirror adjustment.

Play and you will see.
