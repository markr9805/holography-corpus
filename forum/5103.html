Bill Jensen Thu Oct 27, 2005 3:20 pm #4
Hi there,

Here is a really cheap way to make faint transmission holograms. It is based on [http://www.holokits.com/holography_kit.htm](http://www.holokits.com/holography_kit.htm) which also sells a great kit with the stuff. It also has good directions in the articles and FAQs:

Get red sensitive PFG-01 film or 10 year old 8E75 is OK

Get a red diode module laser Digikey VLM-650-01LPA-ND $30.80 and use 2 pliers to unscrew the lens off it.

Power laser with 2 D cells directly. Red lead is +6 volts. Black is ground.

Clamp it in a small vise (or anything sturdy), on a steady floor.

2 plates of glass and binder clips to hold it together.

About 15" away put you plate on the floor at about 45 degrees, and the objects sidelit on the opposite 45 degree.

Expose 5-30 seconds

Develop in regular DEKTOL black and white developer 1-2 minutes to half black. Mine is 10 years old and all browned.

rinse 1 minute

Use normal FIXER for black and white 1 minute.

rinse 1 minute

Air dry

This creates an amplitude transmission hologram. Put it back in the same place with the laser shining on it as before. Remove the objects, Look through the hologram to see the objects magically appear there.

I can send you the film, laser, and chemicals for free if you need it. My film and opened chemical powders are 10 years old and they all still work. That's about as cheap as it gets!

Sincerly,

Bill Jensen
DJ Mathson Thu Oct 27, 2005 5:27 pm #5
> Sharzady: It is my understanding that PFG-01 needs more exposure time then PFG-03...is this correct, and if so, what range should both the exposure times be? (assuming the above info?)

No, PFG-03M is less sensetive to light than PFG-01 and therefore needs a longer exposure time. According to Slavich to get a density of 2 during development you need ca 105 microJoule/cm2 for PFG-01 and 1500 microJoule/cm2 for PFG-03M. That means you need 10 to 15 times longer exposure for PFG-03M. With a 3-4 mW red laser you need approximately 10 sek exposure for PFG-01. But it is good to make test exposures with different times, for example exposure slits with 5, 10, 20 and 40 seconds.

> Sharzady: Does the double concave lens have to be a certain strength or just as long as it gets good spread it's fine? (book says -6 to -12 focal length).

As long as the spread laser beam covers the plate it does not matter what focal length the lens has.

> Sharzady: Finally, I'm trying to figure out what all is in the "JD-4" developing chemicals kit and in what concentrations, because I have a chemistry lab to my disposal and can make my own. Do the developing times vary from PFG-01 to PFG-03?

At [http://www.slavich.com](http://www.slavich.com) you can see some processing formulas.

JD-4 contains:

Part A

Distilled water 700 ml

Metol or Elon (p-Methylaminophenol sulfate) 4 grams

Ascorbic acid (powder) 25 grams

ADD Distilled water (40 C/104 F) to make 1000 ml

Part B

Distilled water, (40 C/104 F) 700 ml

Sodium Carbonate, anhydrous 70 grams

Sodium Hydroxide 15 grams

Add Distilled water (40 C/104 F) to make 1000 ml

Bleach

Distilled water (40 C/104 F) 700 ml

Copper Sulfate, Pentahydrate 35 grams

Potassium Bromide 100 grams

Sodium bisulfate, crystal 5 grams

Add Distilled water (40 C/104 F) to make 1000 ml

Be careful to take safety precautions. Especially beware of Sodium hydroxide and metol.

Colin Kaminski Sat Oct 29, 2005 1:51 pm #10
If you can afford it, the easiest and a very pleasing size is 4x5" plates. Also 2.5 x 2.5" plates can be used. Film is a little harder but after about 20 tries it is not too bad.

Learning the right density to develop to takes some practice. Don't worry too much about it in the beginning. If you really have to know, the easiest method is to buy a step wedge from Photographer's Formulary and tape it to your safe light. When you film matches the correct step you are right. I often just use the seat of the pants method.

Once you have used a particular chemistry a few times it will not be such a mystery. If you look at the exposure times to make a trial posted above you will notice that each trial doubles in time. You will find there is not a great deal of difference between successive times but there will be one the is better than the others. This will become the new starting point.

Don't waste too much time thinking about all of this stuff before you have made some exposures. You will learn more in the first night of shooting about being a holographer that you can learn by reading for a month.
Ed Wesly Sat Oct 29, 2005 2:07 pm #11
Instead of relying on how dark the plate should get, try exposing several different times on one plate and keeping the developer time constant. This is more typical of photo-sensitive materials processing techniques.

Details are given in my articles at the beginning of the forum pages, plus I have another one that isn't posted with pictures of the 4-up exposed plates, available upon request via the PM on this forum.

Also not that the JD-2 and 3 developers are the CWC2 formulations. The -2 uses a dichromate-based reversal bleach, and the improvement in -3 is the copper sulfate rehalogenating bleach of Jeff Blyth.
