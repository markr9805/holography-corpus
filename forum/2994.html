I am working with BB-640 plates and the pyro developer to make reflection holograms. As a bleach I have selected the EDTA-bleach. However, I've noticed that the bleach wasn't able to clear the plate completely. I estimate that the density after the bleach is approximately 0,3. Is this normal with this type of bleach?

Colin Kaminski Thu Dec 30, 2004 10:14 pm #2
Use distilled water, heat the water to 100F, mix in the chemicals on a stir plate if possible. Only add one chemical at a time and make sure it is stired in completely before adding the next chemical.
Tom B. Fri Dec 31, 2004 7:29 am #3
I have not tried the pyro developer/ferric bleach combination with BB-plates. I believe that pyro is a tanning, staining developer, and perhaps the EDTA bleach is unable to clear the stain generated during development. Bjelkhagen mentions that a later version of a different bleach (PBQ-2) had the advantage of clearing the pyro stain due to its incorporation of citric acid - perhaps the Brilland bleach (recipe at end) might have a similar advantage?

From experiments with other emulsions, I found the JD-4 (metol + ascorbic acid) developer seemed to work best for me with ferric bleaches and fine-grained film. Results are clear, with sometimes a slight yellowish stain.

I would modify Colin's suggestion to include mixing chemicals in the exact order specified in the recipe (sometimes very important), and (for mixing 1 liter), heating 700 ml distilled water in a microwave until it is quite warm to the touch (about 1 minute), dissolving the chemicals, then topping up with 300 ml of cold distilled water to cool it down faster. I often notice some precipitate with ferric sulphate, but not what I would call a whole lot.

Another good low noise bleach is Ferric Brilland - Fe3 sulphate 30g, Citric acid 30g, KBr 30g to 1 litre water. It seems to me at least as good as the EDTA version, and as mentioned earlier might be worth a try to clear or reduce pyro stain.

Dinesh Fri Dec 31, 2004 7:39 pm #5
Never tried pyro with the ferric/edta bleach. I believe this is mainly a transmission bleach, where the film is not taken so dark, so maybe you need to increase the bleaching component (FeSO4) in the formula. I also agree it may be the tanning due to the pyro. The standard pyro, process is pyro developer and Van Raness bleach (Pot Dichromate, 4g/l and concentrated sulphuric acid, 4 ml/l). This is, however a reversal bleach and not the rehalogenating bleach you're using. There are various differences to do with fixing, noise etc.
Martin Sat Jan 01, 2005 9:38 am #6
The question whether a bleach removes the pyro stain does not only depend on its pH value and concentration. It also relates to the oxidative power of the bleach (and hence to the oxidative number of the particular chemical agent involved). For the latter reason I assume it will be difficult to remove the pyro stain by means of a ferric bleach. Incidentally, when those ferric bleaches (ferric nitrate, ferric sulfate, FeEDTA) came up, it was believed to be of great benefit that they did not remove the pyro stain – since it was found that on those large grain emulsions (namely Agfa's 8E75 and 8E56) it greatly helped to suppress noise (actually those stain layers seem to have high absorption at short wavelengths).

To get along with that stain problem on today's ultra-fine grain emulsions I see two different approaches:

increase the amount of sodium sulfite in the pyrogallol developer;

use a different bleach.
