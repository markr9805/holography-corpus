a_k Sun Oct 13, 2013 5:51 am #2
Hello Joe,

The diffusor in front of your RGB might be disturbing when used to illuminate holograms. Other high power LEDs have their emitters exposed and come closer to being ideal point light sources. You can get kits with the remote + IR receiver/driver for RGB and a more suitable RGB LED. Or a set like yours, but with a clear bulb with reflector like this one:

[http://www.ebay.com/itm/NEW-3W-4W-E27-1 ... 5d44e121a0](http://www.ebay.com/itm/NEW-3W-4W-E27-16-Color-LED-RGB-Magic-Light-Bulb-with-Wireless-IR-Remote-Control-/400587563424?pt=US_Light_Bulbs&hash=item5d44e121a0)

The millions of colours (2^24 usually) are generated by modulating the intensity of each of the 3 colours, not by actually changing the emitted weavelengths. What you have are 3 bands with a width of about 20-30nm each.

These are a few photos of the LED lighting a Russian full-color (sandwich of two plates) SHSG hologram. This LED allows adjustment to the amounts of R, G, and B. It's obvious that this LED is very effective for multi-color holograms. It can correct a multitude of sins with improper color balance in the original hologram. In fact, with the adjustments possible, this hologram got "new life" and looked more colorful than ever before. It makes a big difference to be able to just sit there with the remote and play with the RGB ratio.

The only downside I can see with this LED is that if you project the spot on the wall, the color balance is far from uniform. It's quite bad, in fact. I thought that since the emitters were so close together, the colors would "fuse" together, but this is not the case. When I was lighting the hologram, I didn't notice it.

I also took a photo (not included here) with just blue (the max. number of photos allowed in a post is 5) but this was quite dim. Maybe this is due to the blue wavelength (unknown for this LED, but I can check it with my little spectroscope). The blue always seems quite dim on the holograms I've tested so far.

I think this is essential for any color holographer, if only for analyzing the replay colors. It would also be good for display, but it needs better color uniformity in the output. Every holographer should have one, they're so cheap.

Joe Farina Wed Oct 23, 2013 1:55 pm #20
A note about the spectral distribution of the 4W spotlight. I don't have the proper equipment for this, just a cheap diffraction-grating spectrometer with a wavelength scale. It's tolerably clear, though, that each emitter in the ~2mm reflector cup emits a fairly broad range of wavelengths. For example, with only the green emitter on, I was seeing blue, green, and red. The other two (blue and red) were not this extreme, but they were still broader than I expected.

I assume the wavelengths emitted from a LED are entirely dependent on the phosphor coatings. They can have single or multiple coatings (from what I've been reading) for a broad range of wavelengths, to emit warm white for example.

It was hard to judge emission peaks from my cheap spectrometer, but it seemed that red was around 620nm, green about 530 (but very broad and difficult to judge), and blue seemed to be emitting throughout the range of 440 to 480, possibly peaking somewhere in the middle (~460?).

Overall, the peaks seemed fairly well in line with what we would expect for good color rendition in holography (i.e., ~633. 532, 458).
