Hi -

I've been trying to learn about advances in computational photography. One of the simpler aspects of it is "synthetic aperture photography," in which, for example, you can computationally simulate having a large lens by analyzing the data generated by a video camera moving on a linear track. One benefit of this is the ability to refocus on whatever depth you'd like, at home, on your computer.

If you're curious about this, feel free to check out my recent blog post. I filmed a portion of Mass. Ave. (of Paul Revere riding a horse up the street fame) while my wife drove the car up the street and I held a camera out the window. Later, I produced an image of a building that had been blocked by trees.

Yes, this is simple stuff, really, but it's a window into a deeper collection of research happening around the world. Computer graphics and optics and holography continue to get closer.
