From a July post: "If the color shift is not wanted, or if you have no TEA (triethanolamine), you could try presoaking in 1% ammonia solution. (Dinesh's suggestion) I just diluted household ammonia (not BLEACH!) with distilled water. Use an airtight container - the stuff is very stinky. I found it gave sensitivity [roughly] equivalent to TEA with no color shift, but with slight fogging (darkening of unexposed areas). As a TEA alternative, you could experiment with ammonia + something else (corn syrup or sugar?) to preswell the emulsion."

I soaked for 2-3 minutes. The household ammonia concentration was not known, so I assumed it was 30% and diluted 30:1. I now know it is more likely to be in the range of 5 to 10% ([http://www.csupomona.edu/~jskoga/Aquariums/Ammonia.html](http://www.csupomona.edu/~jskoga/Aquariums/Ammonia.html))

so I was really only using a 0.15% to 0.3% ammonia soak. Probably even less will work OK, which might reduce fogging. Also washing before development might help with this, though as noted, fogging is not very severe anyway.

Dinesh Thu Dec 16, 2004 5:57 pm #8
I've done it with Agfa 8E75, but never with Slavich or BB. With Agfa, I soaked in about 6% TEA for about 5 minutes, then squeegeed. Unfortunately going from red to blue really strains the emulsion almost to breaking point. Another problem may be with grain density, as opposed to grain size. If the grain density is low then expanding the emulsion depth may take the grains too far apart to take the higher spatial frequencies. As a rough calculation shows, blue needs a fringe seperation of 200 odd nm, which translates to 5000 l/mm.
