wler Tue Mar 28, 2006 10:45 am #1
Highly reflective surfaces are usually not recommended to make holograms of. Nevertheless I took up the challenge and made a few shots of some polished-metallic object (made from a magnet kit), and some H2 reflection copies from it. It was really hard - the intensity ratio between brightest and average spots on the H2 image plane was more than 20 or so. Thanks to the high dynamic range of the Ultimate emulsion it came out not too bad at the end, after overexposing the brightest spots by a factor of 2-3 and having the max beam ratio set to close to one.

However, taking pictures of it with my camera never succeeded well due to the high contrast. At any rate, below an image and a movie of the best H2 copy; the forefront seems to stick out almost 10cm from the plate:

Colin Kaminski Sun Apr 02, 2006 3:22 pm #6
If you put a polarizer at the film plane aligned to the polarization of the unaltered object beam so you can see the reflections and place a 1/2 wave plate in the object beam you will see the relative brightness of the reflections dim as you rotate the polarization off axis.

Rotate the 1/2 wave plate so the reflections and the diffuse light from the object have the "desired" brightness.

Also, make sure that any bright spot is not exactly on the film plane when making an H2 or it will burn out.
