While searching the recent abstracts on SciFinder, I found the following:

Title: "Modified dichromated gelatin for increasing moisture resistance"

Authors: Romero-Arellano/Solano/Martinez-Ponce

Journal: Revista Mexicana de Fisica (2006), 52(2), 99-103 (in Spanish)

Translated abstract: It is presented a comparison between the diffraction efficiency in holographic phase gratings induced in films prepared with two different mixtures based on dichromated gelatin. The traditional mixture (DCG) is modified incorporating glycerol (DCG-G) before deposition. Both kinds of film were dried under different humidity conditions. It is seen that optical recording on DCG-G films is independent on the moisture settings. Also, the induced refractive index modulation is larger than the one obtained with DCG.

*

I don't have access to this journal, nor would I feel comfortable in translating the Spanish, but the last comment regarding the increased index modulation is interesting. It isn't clear what is meant by "increasing moisture resistance" but maybe it means that sensitivity variations (during exposure) due to moisture content are reduced (?) Does anyone have experience with (or know of references to) glycerol in DCG? I know nothing about it. Jeff's new green sensitive DCG uses it also.

Joe Farina Mon Jun 19, 2006 2:23 pm #7
The so-called "increased index modulation" in that abstract is perhaps misleading. Maybe all it really means is that diffraction efficiency is increased due to a better differential of hardened/unhardened gelatin, when using their DCG system containing glycerol. In other words, better "sensitivity."

To quote from Jeff's new paper:

"The small amount of glycerol in our sensitizer formulation appears to play a crucial role in enabling the transformation of the light-produced Cr(V) to take place much more rapidly at 100 degrees C. Without glycerol the process seems to take more than a half hour to harden sufficiently. So it may be true to say that hot glycerol catalyses the reaction Cr(V) to Cr(III)."
