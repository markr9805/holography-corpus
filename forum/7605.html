Hologram Basics:

Here are a few terms you should familiarize yourself with if you have an honest interest in creating a hologram.

OBJECT BEAM - one of two laser beams necessary to create a hologram - this is the expanded laser beam that shines upon your object you are hologramming and scatters from there to fall on the surface of your film.

REFERENCE BEAM - one of two laser beams necessary to create a hologram - this is the expanded laser beam that shines directly on your film.

When these two beams mingle on the surface of your film in the correct proportion, an interference pattern will be generated. If left undisturbed by air currents or vibration for long enough for your film to record, this submicroscopic pattern of fringes will be stored in the photosensitive grains of the film. You then develop the film and after it dries, you have a hologram.

REFLECTION HOLOGRAM - a basic type of hologram - this kind of hologram is recorded by exposing your film with object and reference beams falling on opposite sides of the film. This type of hologram is viewable with white light.

TRANSMISSION HOLOGRAM - a basic type of hologram - this kind of hologram is recorded by exposing your film with object and reference beams falling on the same side of the film. This type of hologram is generally only viewable with laser light of the same wavelength it was recorded with.

No matter which type of hologram you may choose to create, you are strongly advised to do so on a table that isolates your laser beams, optics, film and object from the ambient vibration that is always present.

It can be a very involved process and bear in mind that it is likely that your first few attempts at creating a hologram may not work. Practice makes perfect.

Good luck. If you have any further questions, feel free to ask.
