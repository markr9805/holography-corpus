Hi there guys. I'm really new to Holography methods and theory, last week I made my first attempt at creating a hologram using a 5mW diode laser, aswell as PFG-01 plates and JD-2 purchased from Integraf.

Unfortunately I keep running into the same problem: When I try to develop the plates, they do not turn black at all. At first I thought this was due to under exposure but I have now done multiple tests using some old PFG-01 film I managed to get a hold of. I made sure this film was not under exposed by illuminating some with flourescent light and even a few with direct sunlight for a few seconds. I believe this should cause the film to turn black almost instantly when submerged in the developer. This is not the case; even when leaving the film submerged for 15min or longer there is only a marginal change in opacity, it's not even be noticable unless you have undeveloped film to compare it to.

I've tried a few things to fix this so far:

1) I suspected the water I was using was not de-ionised properly but a rudimentary conductivity test showed it to be far less conductive than tap water. I know distilled water is recommended but from what I've read this shouldn't be too much of a problem.

2) I tried mixing a new developer kit just in case I did something incorrectly the first time or the kit was bad. Unfortunately I still get the same results with the newly mixed developer.

I do live in a high humidity region but the kits are store in a cabinet in an air-conditioned room so I doubt this is the cause. The plates have been refridgerated when not in use.

Any help with this would be appreciated, I'm sure I'm doing something wrong somewhere along the line.

> Unfortunately I keep running into the same problem: When I try to develop the plates, they do not turn black at all. At first I thought this was due to under exposure but I have now done multiple tests using some old PFG-01 film I managed to get a hold of.

PFG-01 film keeps very long, even when not refrigerated. When film does get old, it usually fogs in the developer (turn dark even without any exposure). I don't think that the problem is with your film.

> 1) I suspected the water I was using was not de-ionised properly but a rudimentary conductivity test showed it to be far less conductive than tap water. I know distilled water is recommended but from what I've read this shouldn't be too much of a problem.

The water purity in the developer can have an effect on the quality of the hologram. But I don't think that less pure water would prevent any developing action.

> 2) I tried mixing a new developer kit just in case I did something incorrectly the first time or the kit was bad. Unfortunately I still get the same results with the newly mixed developer.

I would suspect the developer also. Are you sure that you followed the mixing instructions correctly? JD-2 consists of an A and B part. Before use, you need to take a little of developer A and mix with a little bit of developer B. The mix can be used for about two days. if I remember well, normally exposed plates start to darken after about 45 seconds.

> Another funny observation is that when I go through the entire developing process(ie developer->stop-bath->bleach->stop-bath) the plate or film turns a dark yellowy colour. I haven't been able to isolate when this occurs as I only do this in dark room conditions but I'm pretty certain its not in the developer. Funnily enough it seems to happen in the first stop-bath.

JD-2 bleach does give the plates a yellowy colour, that's normal.

Hans Thu Jan 24, 2008 3:04 pm #7
Are both A and B parts more or less colourless? Usually the A developer (the one that does not contain te alkaline) has a faint yellowy tint. The other one should be colourless transparent (if I remember well).

It is very easy and a lot cheaper to make your own developer. Look in the holoWiki for the TJ1 developer. It works much better than JD-2 and you can buy the individual chemicals cheaply. The chemicals needed for te B part of TJ1 developer can often even be found in drug stores. NaCO3 is washing soda. And for KOH you can use caustic soda. Caustic soda is the stuff that people use to unclog their kitchen sinks (in Holland that is, because garbage disposers are forbidden here in our sinks). Always use eye protection and gloves when you use these chemicals.

Hans Fri Jan 25, 2008 8:01 pm #13
Important tip about TJ1:

Ascorbic Acid + Metol like to oxidise when they get the change. So exclusion from air is important. Luckily, you only need about 10ml part A plus 10ml PartB to develop a small plate.

So, to keep my solutions in good condition before mixing, I always make a stock 600ml of developer A. I then store this developer PartA in ten syringes of 60ml. When I need some, I squeeze some out of a syringe. When I run out of developer, I make another 600ml. Voila, never any air with the developer.

Use developer only for one plate if you want the brightest and sharpest results. You can use as little as possible. Just enough to soak the plate with developer. It helps to submerge the plate in some water before putting it in the developer.
