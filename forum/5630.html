Dear Holographers,

I am exploring options in doubling the frequency of the 1064nm laser to 532nm green for holography. I post my findings for others to know. Please correct any errors since I am just learning about it.

I have a 5mm diameter beam from the SSY-1 laser. I have made holograms from a 2mm x 2mm KTP, 4mm x 4mm KTP, and recently 5mm x 5mm KTP. The bigger the crystal, the more energy I am getting which allows me to take better and clearer holograms of larger areas. Still, there is a lot of improvement needed.

In all these smaller crystals of KTP, there is clipping of the 5mm beam which gives rise to what appear to be diffraction lines from the edges of the KTP. I put an aperture just before the KTP, and that seems to help clear up the expanded beam profile a bit. But there is some other source of distortion going on (maybe inconsistent bleached transparency of the dye passive q-switch, or dirty optics?)

With the PFN1 (standard SSY-1 pulse forming network) we inject 15 Joules into the flashlamp. It produces about 150 mJ of 1064nm radiation over 4ns for a 5mm beam diameter. The peak power over area is ((.15 J / .000000004 sec)/0.25 cm^2) = about 150 Megawatts / cm^2. Damage threshold of KTP is 500 MW / cm^2 so we are OK.

With my PFN2 (enhanced pulse forming network) in either single laser or as the amplifier stage, we inject about 50 Joules of light to get about 450 mJ of 1064nm. The peak power is about 450 MW / cm^2 Which is really pushing the KTP towards destruction. If you can warm the KTP crystal, it really helps keep the KTP from damaging at the higher powers. Right now I am not heating them since I don't have the stuff to do it. My "hot air" is not enough to do it.

There are two types of growing techniques of KTP. Flux-grown is the cheapest method, and the crystals are not able to withstand quite as much power. But they are pretty cheap compared to the other type of KTP growing technique. The other is Hydrothermally Grown crystals which have a great damage threshold, but are really really expensive.

The cost of large >5mm x 5mm KTP crystals becomes phenomenal! I have scraped up enough money to buy a larger 10mm x 10mm KTP ($500) so that I can rule out the problems of edge diffration from the observed expanded beam distortion. It comes later this week. Can't wait. It is Type II ( which maximizes green output by adjusting the angle of incidence, mine is cut for maximum for 1064nm incident normal to the surface) It is also the more inferior but cheaper flux grown type. Will let you know the results here. Ktp's conversion efficiency is high compared to other SHG (single harmonic generation) crystals.

KDP doubling crystals are also a possibility. High damage threshold, even cheaper to make, hydroscopic so it needs to be encapsulated in a housing. KDP encapsulated 7mm x 7mm ($400). It is complicated to find out exactly which type you want.

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen

Ron Michael Thu Jun 15, 2006 4:15 pm #3
TIP: All controllers use a feedback loop to regulate temperature with some designs using a thermister. Make sure you mount that thermister fairly close to the heat source to minimize thermal latency which otherwise generally creates larger temp swings that are hard to control. The gain on the feedback helps reduce this but this is not a controller fault as much as a response issue from the oven. Compared to analog circuits, microprocessor controllers can anticipate this thermal lag time.
Ron Michael Thu Jun 15, 2006 4:38 pm #4
Tip 2: if you find the controller is oscillating (temp swings) due to thermal latency, try lower the "thermal gain rate" or in other words use fewer watts of heating so that the thermal rise rate is slower. Balance this against your cooling rate which will determine your max operating temp obtainable based on specific ambient environment designed for.

Okay tip 3:

Try machining some insulation covers in front and behind your oven. Insulate the outer oven as well and use as small of a themal mass as possible in the design.

Ron Michael Thu Jun 15, 2006 10:53 pm #7
This is just one of many research papers regarding the KTP grey track problem caused by 532nm and I think this paper also puts a threshold of 80MW/cm2 when this begins.

[http://www.stanford.edu/group/fejer/fej ... 994/72.pdf](http://www.stanford.edu/group/fejer/fejerpubs/1994/72.pdf)

Other studies indicate that thermal annealing helps remove the gray track problem when the crystal is above 80 degree centigrade. This problem shows up quicker in quasi-cw mode but obviously is not limited to this.

OF course in non critical phase matching you generally temperature tune to reach the phase match condition but in angle tuning you have to let the crystal temp stabilize and then find it's angle at that temperature. Else if your temperature is not stable your output will vary from shot to shot due to the temperature-angle dependency. Some companies now offer a grey track resistant KTP I believe with a slightly less efficiency. KDP and others don't have this problem. With KTP you have better angle acceptance and higher deff which together gives a much better efficiency thant KDP. With larger aperture KTP costs coming down it's highly competitive to KDP and without the limitations of KDP's lower efficiency and smaller acceptance angle regarding your beam parameters.
