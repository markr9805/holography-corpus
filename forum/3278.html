If I understand well you got an interferometric region (supposing that the laser operates at few longitudinal modes) that is a multiple of 2L (L=cavity length), with this path length the temporal coherence can interfere with itself. Your laser coherence is defined as the path length difference for which fringe visibility in a Michelson interferometer is reduced to 1/e^2 points.

I have a similar laser (bought from Sam), 11mW, 17" long. It has so much conherence length that I almost stopped worrying about it entirely until I got caught by it the other night.

You say you can't afford to strip the table down. Is this necessary? Can you insert a mirror at some raw beam part of the setup, before the beamsplitter, say, to bleed some of the beam off. If space is the consideration and you have two beamsplitters, ie two more than the one you're using, can you use an "inline" Michaelson:

====beam====\(bs_1))=====|(bs_2)=====|(mirror)

The light goes through bs_1 (some of it is divertied "down", but we'll ignore that) and continues on to bs_2. From bs_2 some is reflected to bs_1 and goes "up" while some is transmitted through to the mirror. From the mirror it reflects back through bs_2 to bs_1 and also reflectes "up". The path difference is, of course, the distance between bs_2 and the mirror. You may want to slightly tilt bs_2 so as not to get ringing between bbs_2 and the mirror. This setup just needs an unused edge of the table so if the bled beam can be run along an edge of the table you can set up the "inline" Michaelson.

If you need the odd beamsplitter, let me know and I can ship overnight.
