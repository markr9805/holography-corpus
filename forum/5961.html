Sounds like an uneccesary, messy proposition. The heat will go into the mineral oil - then where? Why not put the heat sink in a box, and duct some fan-driven air thru it using dryer vent hose? Gary Deal Thu Jan 04, 2007 7:22 am #6
There's another option for using the cooling capacity of the heatsink that wouldn't be vibration-heavy. Basically, put the finned section in a box, with a "flue" on one side and leave the other side open. The warm air will find it's way into the flue and rise, sucking air through the fins. Any idea how much air you'll need to flow to maintain a desired temp? (I don't have any idea) It should be easy enough to test with some cardboard, tape, and a paper towel core. I've been told that the longer the flue/taller the chimney, the more draw you'll get into the fireplace.

If airflow in the optical area would be a problem, a couple more towel cores would provide external ductwork to outside the curtain. A final design could use flexible clothes-dryer ducting (cheap).

An alternate idea, anyway, the oil sounds scary.
Joe Farina Thu Jan 04, 2007 1:39 pm #7
I think the most important thing here is to accurately measure the temperature of the laser head for an extended period.

A couple of recommendations, Michael: if you don't have it already, I would suggest getting a copy of the 315M operator's manual. On page 2-5, is says that the maximum temperature for the laser head baseplate is 130F for the 315M-100, and 113F for the 315M-150. This is the stated maximum, probably you will want to keep it cooler than this.

Also, if you don't have one already, I would suggest getting an infrared non-contact thermometer to obtain accurate measurements. These have become very cheap recently. I think they are available for about $20 or less from Harbor Freight. These thermometers are very, very useful.

The heat sink in the photo already looks pretty good. If you need something better, I would suggest using 1/2 aluminum plate of whatever dimensions are necessary. Whatever you use, just monitor the temperature of the baseplate (not the heatsink, but the baseplate of the laser head itself -- the parts that stick out about 3/8 inch on the bottom of the front and rear). "If it works, it works."
