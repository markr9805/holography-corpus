I was reading a paper Joe sent me last night and they made an interesting point. Since a processed DCG hologram can be reprocessed it can also be resensitised and re-exposed to produce another hologram.

In other words. Expose the DCG and process as normal. Then resoak the already existant hologram in a sensitiser, let dry, expose and process again to get two holograms.

I assume you could use one concentration of AmDi in the original emulsion. Then another concentration of AmDi in the second sensitising bath. Together with different exposure time, one should be able to get a wide range of colors with a single wavelength.

JohnFP Thu Nov 16, 2006 3:04 pm #9
I am not sure the rules for reprocessing apply here. I simply don't know yet.

Let's say we have a blue hologram. After resensitising and exposing it is definitely going to have more crosslinking so an overall increase in hardness. Now, if after resensitising the dry (set) time is short and more moisture is still in the emulsion and it is dosed with light I assume the original blue might red shift as the emulsion has been hardened in a more swollen state (this alone would be a good test without a second hologram but using non-coherent light). But if the emulsion has had quite a while to dry and most moisture is out, then I assume a bluer shift. Also, I assume if you change processing parameters like hotter soak and bath you may cause a more broadband.

Again, the paper only mentioned it and never did any experimentation in the area. To my knowledge I have never seen this actuall technique tried and reported on. So it is all a new frontier to me.

I am setting my table up for the diffractbow hologram with the LCD. This would be easy to test in parallel as I only need a single beam and can simply block off the object beam for the transmisison diffractbow geometry. Hopefully I can get in the lab soon.
