Ed Wesly Fri Dec 02, 2005 12:34 pm #5
At one time Agfa made not only meter square plates but also 1 by 1.5m! The only hologram I had seen in a photo using one of those plates was shot by a guy named Tribillon (I'm at work so I can't look up his first name right now) of the Venus de Milo. (But I do remember getting drunk with him and somewhat able to follow his conversation with my high school French!)

Another French company called AP holography made good use of the meter square plates, who I think is responsible for the futuristic city hologram, which was featured on the cover of Laser Focus World many moons ago.

They stopped making those huge plates in the late '80's, because they said that the machine was almost 100 years old, and the main product they were coating with it was plates for map-making, which was a dead market. 50 by 60 cm might have also gone down the tubes then too.

Agfa film was available in 1.1 meter wide rolls, although the edges were a bit funky, and the producers of large holos, Advanced Dimensional Displays, now defunct, and Holographics North (who made a T-Rex skull) utilized it. So they coudl make them larger than 1 m in the other dimension.

I don't know if Dot Matrix images count exactly as holograms, but there was a machine that would ablate the dots into Kaptan, and they made some sort of record sized image, something like 4' by 6', just to show they could. This image was of a car. The machine was developed by Pennsylvania Pulp and Paper, and then bought by ITW, but who knows if it's still up and running, as its inventor as passed on.

I met the guy, and have a good mental image of him, but my danged Edzheimer's has got the better of me today! Wait, his last name was Monahon, not so sure of the spelling, again the first name escapes me.

I was part of a team that worked on recording gratings for packaging, and we spun photo-resist plates that were 42" by 42". The goal was to make a wide web tool that was 40" wide with a 40" repeat.

I think that Wavefront Technology (wft.bz) made a 60" square grating, but I couldn't find that on their site, which has been redone since the last time I went there. But they are making wide web grating foil with a 40" repeat!

Ed Wesly Sat Dec 03, 2005 4:03 pm #12
BobH said:

> I remember seeing a hologram that size of a skelaton, made by TJ I believe.

This one was still existing at LFC. It was on a 50 by 60 cm plate. Still pretty impressive. The way TJ and his partner at the time, David Wender, set it up, in a classical transmission style, was to have the skeleton look like it was holding the plate on the virtual side. This was recorded probably mid-70's.

Another quote:

> I also observed Steve Provence make his meter square cross gratings in photoresist. This was at our adjacent studios in Boulder Creek, around 1990.

I know that these Provence gratings were only 32" by 32", not exactly a meter. Oh, what's 6 or 7 inches on a side.

CFC bought out Provence's junk, and he was supposed to train us on how to make perfect gratings. He was only committed to doing what he had done, that size above. But what the management wanted was a tool for the 40" wide embosser, which Steve didn't have nor would commit to making.

The last I heard of Steve was he was out of holography, and going into the prosciutto ham business! I don't know if his first batch ever got cured, but he definitely was a character with a lot of wild stories! I wish him the best of luck!
