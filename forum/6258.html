Joe Farina Wed May 16, 2007 6:13 pm #1
I finally bit the bullet and bought one of these, the right beam is doing 205 and the left 185mW (@458nm).

I wanted to share a few things I've learned about these, in case anyone is considering buying one. Like any surplus or used laser, the thing that really determines price (at least for a knowledgeable purchaser) is the demonstrable output power. If you want a larger argon laser complete with etalon, doing "as new" power, I've found the price to be in the neighborhood of $6K. The same price applies to the Melles Griot 58-BLD-605 at specified power.

Of course, this is out of the question for the vast majority of holographers. (This includes me, but it so happened that a number of fortunate events came together that allowed me to buy it. It also took me about 7 years.) But the question arises as to how to get the price down.

I was really surprised when the seller (Dick at Anderson Lasers) told me that someone in his shop has replaced the diodes for this type of laser for under $1K. And Sam Goldwasser seems to offer the same thing (see his classified page) for $1.5K. So if someone is lucky enough to find one of these lasers that simply needs a new diode, it seems there are people out there who can install them.

I noticed on eBay that someone is currently selling one of these lasers in the price range of $1K to $1.5K (it's in a non-functioning state). The seller is of the opinion that the diode needs to be replaced. This may or may not be the case, however. But the potential to "get lucky" does exist.

It is always a risk to buy a non-functioning laser, as the money you spend may ultimately go out the window. But for those people who really want blue, this approach may be something to consider: look for a Melles laser that "seems" to need a new diode, and then have the diode replaced (by someone other than Melles, since they charge somewhere around 5K, I'm told). It's risky when you don't know the reason for the laser not functioning, but I believe diode failure is the #1 reason for this kind of laser. But even if you get one that's already near specified output, like I did (and pay a premium price), it's still an open question as to how long it will last.

These lasers are nice, though. Single-frequency, wall-plug electrical input, no water cooling. Just push a button to turn on, and push a button to turn off. The head and power supply each have fans, however, so off-table mounting is indicated. The beams are elliptical, and required additional beam-shaping optics to make them circular.

Joe Farina Thu May 17, 2007 1:41 pm #6
> Ed Wesly: I seem to remember that the two beams were polarized at right angles to each other, and maybe the polarization was even drifting?

Ed, both beams are linearly polarized, but I haven't checked the relation of one to the other yet. I have never heard of any polarization-drifting problems with this kind of laser. Back in '99 when I first inquired about these lasers (they weren't made by Melles back then, the original company was Laser Power), they sent me some literature, including a letter from one of the engineers at Perkin Elmer (in the holographic grating laboratory). He gave it a glowing review, and took some micrographs of the grating profiles (which were shown), and reported that these grating profiles were as good as those made from a large-frame argon laser.

I think the only reason we don't hear more about these lasers is that holography (embossed holography) was really going downhill when they came out (circa '99). Most of the people in the business didn't seem to stay in business for much longer after that, and they already had ion lasers to do the job. The Melles laser seems to really be a holographic laser in my opinion, given the extra trouble involved in making it single-frequency, and with the beams already split. I'm not sure if they're even still in production (which wouldn't surprise me, given the practically non-existent state of holography in this country). Maybe it's already gone the way of the dinosaur.
Joe Farina Thu May 17, 2007 2:49 pm #7
> floh: A friend of mine also has one but in broken state.It lases but only for a short flash when switching on. We checked the power supply, it is ok. Diode current as well as both TEC are running fine.We assume the diode is broken but why then it lases for a short flash? Any idea?

It sounds like the laser is detecting a problem and shutting down. My first thought is that the diode may be running in an overcurrent condition, but you've checked this. Does the "system fault" on the back of the power supply blink?

There was a laser like this on eBay a couple of years ago (is this the same one, offered by Outback?). It came on briefly, then shut down. One thing that I noticed with that deal is that the head and power supply were manufactured at different dates, the head in 2000 and the power supply in 2002. This suggests to me that the head and power supply are not a matched pair, and this is required for this type of laser. Each head/power supply combination is unique, and they are not supposed to be subsitiuted with other heads, or with other power supplies. They are optimised for each other at the factory.

If you cannot determine the problem, I would suggest sending it to Sam Goldwasser for an evaluation. This evaluation will not be cheap (see his classified page) but if it needs a new diode, I doubt that you would want to send it to Melles (due to the high cost).

If you haven't done so already, you can check with Amir at Melles Griot, and he will tell you if your head and controller are a matched pair (if you provide him with the serial numbers of the head and controller). He can also tell you what an evaluation from Melles will cost, if they are willing to do one. I would imagine the costs would be high, however, and that's why I recommend talking to Sam.
