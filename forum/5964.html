Tony Thu Jan 04, 2007 11:47 am #2
First, it sounds like your exposure is too long. The plate should darken, but not completely, after development but before bleaching. It should be about as dark as dark sunglasses.

Second, vibration is almost always the culprit in first hologram failures. Choose a rigid object, and have the plate actually resting on the object so there is no relative motion between the plate and the object.

Keep trying - you'll get it!
JohnFP Thu Jan 04, 2007 12:21 pm #3
Other then Tony's excellent suggestions I would like to add:

The rainbow you saw... sounds like it was a diffraction grating made from light entering the edge of the plate, especially if it was wavy. Cut a piece of black electrical tape to the thickness and length of your glass and stick it to the edge of the glass facing the laser light to block the light from entering into the edge of the glass.

This may not help in actually getting an image, but when you get one, you don't want this same rainbow showing up with your image.

Also, when the emulsion is wet it is very soft. Rubbing it in the bleach will actually rub the emulsion right off the glass.

Keep trying and keep posting your results. Have you read the holowiki located at the top of the forum page? It has a lot of great and helpful information.

Good luck!

JohnFP Thu Jan 04, 2007 5:58 pm #7
Ok, I will take a try at it.

Considering you want to conserve plates here is what I would do.

Slavich PFG-03M suggest 2mJ/cm^2.

Lay you plate directly on a piece of rigid dull aluminum (or other similar flat rigid - not too shiny material) the size of your plate. Bring the laser light in at a 56 degree angle (11 degrees off normal) so that you will be making a single beam reflection hologram of the aluminum plate.

Using a 5mW laser the approximate loss of a single lens will be 10% thus the plate will receive 4.5mW

Expand the beam, or more accurately, place the plate and aluminum such that the plate in the beam right at the plane where the beam is expanded to a 6" diameter.

6" (15.24cm) diameter beam = 148cm^2

This total beam = 4.5 mW thus

4.5 mW / 148cm^2 = .0304mW/cm^2 per second.

1/.0304 = 33 seconds thus

Your exposure should be around 33 seconds.

Now

Cover all the plate except a thin strip of the plate. For a 4" x 5" plate cover all but 1" on one side. Expose for 10 seconds. Then cover all of the plate except for a thin 1" strip right next to the plate and expose for 20 seconds. Do the same for the next three strips (total of 5") using 30 seconds, 40 seconds and 50 seconds.

So you should have a plate with 5 individual strips of different exposures.

Now after exposure cut the plate into 4 - 1" strips perpendicular to the strips. Each piece of cut plate will have the 5 exposures on it.

Now process each of these pieces with various developing times. The first one at the developing time to where you see the plate starting to turn a little dark. The second one develop until the plate goes very dark, almost opaque. Then take the time in between these two times and develop your last two plates evenly timed between the light and dark pieces. Record your times.

Rinse and bleach until clear.

Compare exposure time with developing time.

This should get you in the area of a good exposure time and developing time for future holograms.

You will have to keep track of which slices of exposure are which and which develping times are which but this should help you tweak in your processing.

This technique saves a lot of film/plates when getting started.

Hope this helps!

I think all the above discussion has value, especially the last suggestions by JohnFP. I have a question and a few things to add.

> I also tried a second attempt as i did earlier, but i shortend the exposure time to 60 sec, but had the same results. The plate has a light blue milky shade after developing. So for a test, i put the plate again in developer solution and amazingly it again turned black. So after rinsing in water i gain put it in Bleach , whihc made a clear glass. By enthusiasm, i rubbed the glass inside the bleach until it became a clear glass. And what to say, there in no image at all.

Was the second sentence accurate, or did you really mean "light blue milky shade after bleaching?" If after the first development and before bleaching, then the light blue meant you were underexposed, and the fact that the plate blackened after viewing meant the viewing light exposed the plate, not the laser, so no image would be seen. Your next sentence suggests it was seen black after development, in which case you did get a laser exposure.

I would modify JohnFP's procedure slightly:

1)Make a blocking cardboard larger than the plate and cut a strip of the size you want in it, so the plate will be shielded everywhere except in the open strip for each exposure. Check that the emulsion side is going to be in contact with the object by breathing on the plate; if that side fogs that is the glass side. If neither side fogs then the plate is too warm (India!?) Then dampen a clean uncallused finger and lightly touch a corner; the emulsion is slightly tacky, the glass side is not, and the emulsion side feels satiny while the glass side feels glossy if you slide a dry finger across it.

2)Do the exposures one after the other, in 10 sec increments, as JohnFP suggested, holding the slotcard in the air off the plate. Also, when you remove the shutter from its rest, lift it into the air but still block the beam for 30secs; that gets rid of "shuttershake" if it occurs.

3) Develop the whole plate for the recommended time, don't cut it unless you are good glass cutter in the dark. By the way, did you use a pre-devo hardener? (Or is it in the JD-4? I forget if JD-4 is self-hardening. PFG-03 is prone to come off the glass if not hardened, so that is a strong reason to use hardener or to switch to PFG-01 when you reorder plates; that emulsion is faster and needs no hardening. But if in the end you see any color in transmission, or that it turned black, or you see rainbow color in reflection, then the emulsion is still there.) If the emulsion stayed on the plate but was not pre-hardened it is possible the image recorded but the emulsion collapsed and shifted the viewing wavelength into the UV

4)After development rinse and dry the plate and look for an image. (The plate must be dry. If not the emulsion will be swollen and any image will be in the infrared. When dry the emulsion should look uniformly glossy in reflection.) Remember the viewing light should be sunlight or a halogen or a vertical linear filament incandescent or a white LED (to deal with possible emulsion shrinkage-color shift); the source should be behind you and about 50 degrees above the horizontal (if your ref beam angle was about 50 degrees), and you have to get the plate oriented so that the viewing beam has the same relation to it that the reference beam did originally. Assuming you shot as JohnFP suggested, the emulsion side should face away from you and the glass side towards you. If the plate looks dark gray in transmission but the image does not appear when you first look try rotating the plate while keeping the emulsion side away from you.

5)If you see an image then hold the plate half into the bleach so half of each exposure strip is bleached clear and half is only developed. Again, no image will appear unless the emulsion is dry. This is a less fine-tuned version of JohnFP's procedure but should get you into the ballpark of bleach-exposure combination. Then you can do another test and play with development time too.

First I would like to appoligize for a mistake I made in my last post. I stated

> Bring the laser light in at a 56 degree angle (11 degrees off normal)

when actually that is incorrect. It should be "Bring the laser light in at 56 degrees off normal".

You state you are using a tube light. The reason the image is blurry from using a tube light is each section of length on the tube is trying to create it's own image. Thus multiple images are being formed over the length of the tube. Most holograms like to be lit with a point source of light. Try a spot light (quartz halogen) a few feet away from the hologram and over your shoulder.

The reason the hologram looks green is because the emulsion has shrunk probably due to processing. If material is removed during processing then the fringes are more closely spaced and the result is that the hologram color is shifted towards the blue, in your case from the red recording color to green. Sometimes you can post shift this (green) hologram to get it closer to gold by soaking the hologram in some household sugar water (about 10%) for a few minutes, then squeegy and redry. The sugar left in the hologram will keep the fringes expanded a little and the hologram color should be red shifted (hopefully into the yellow). I did some experiements with sugar water (I did not have other chemical that do work better) and found I do get a shift but only about 5 to 10% at most no matter how high the concentration of sugar. If the sugar crystalized in the emulsion the then concentration is too high and the hologram can be reprocessed by first soaking in water for a few minutes then in a lower concentration of sugar water.
