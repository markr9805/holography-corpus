Dinesh Mon Dec 13, 2004 3:49 pm #4
The thing to look for in a laser are: power, coherence length and polarisation.

The power may or may not have to be as high as you may think, depending on the setup and surroundings. On the other hand, the more the better. Michael shoots on the second floor and gets pretty good results. The key is in making a good vibration isolation table, surrounding it with a screen to block out outside and airborne noises and having black cards to break up air currents on the table surface. Also note the traffic flow and aircraft activity in your area. Most holographers work late at night to minimise disturbance from traffic. Before you build your table, it's best to check various areas of the room you propose to build the table with a michaelson interferometer on the floor. While a Michaelson on a floor will never give steady fringes, it'll give you an idea of where your room is the most stable. There are lots of threads on the forum on getting mirrors and a home-made beamsplitter.

The coherence length determines the depth you can shoot. If you're making a single beam Denisyuk (object behind plate and beam hitting the front of the film), the coherence length needs to be the distance from the film to the back of the object and back to the film. When you go on to split beam setups, the coherence length just determines the deepest object you can shoot. I'm not too knowledgeable on DPSS laser and diode lasers, but I believe a well tuned DPSS can give you many feet of coherence, so this may not be the problem. Typically a coherence length of about 6 inches (15cm) is a good distance for simple Denisyuks. If and when you buy a laser, though, ask about the coherence lengh. You can check the coherence length of the laser with a Michaelson by varying the distance of the mirrors from the beamsplitter. As you move one of the mirrors in the interferometer away, the fruinges will eventually lose contrast and finally disappear completely. The distance at which the contrast falls by about 20% is a good measure of coherence.

The polarisatione of the laser will determine the exposure time. In order to get the best possible hologram the object and reference beams must be polarised in the same direction. This is only possible (in a simple setup) with a polarised laser. If the laser is unpolarised (sometimes this is called "randomly polarised"), the brightness wiill decrease from the best possible and/or the exposure times will increase. A polarised laser is not essential, but if you can get one within your budget, it's preferable. It's someting to ask about when you buy.

tpark Mon Dec 13, 2004 5:36 pm #5
Hi,

You can get excellent results with the diode that Intergraf provides - it's not super powerful, but if the stability requirements are met you can get great holograms. If there are problems with your setup, it won't matter how powerful a laser you have, you will still get terrible results.

I purchased a D&S kit, and got the thing assembled, however I had mixed results (mode hopping) with it initially. I modified the circuit so that the drive current could be adjusted, and used the photocell/audio amplifier setup to get it away from a mode hop zone, and I've had good results with that setup. I also have had good results with constant current sources (I bought mine from Ebay for about $30.00). Sealed laser diode modules tend to exhibit mode hopping - I've purchased several from Ebay, and although the device worked fine for producing a bright red spot, there were problems with mode hopping so the output was unsuitable for creating holograms. I've not found any green modules that produce good holograms - I think for that you have to go for a Real Man's laser such as the coherent 315m. I've purchased surplus ebay hene's and they've produced good holograms.

The output from semiconductor lasers tends to be much cleaner than that from the hene, so you can get away without a spatial filter for many setups.

The mounting for the lasers I purchased from a company called Optima optics. I bought a lot of stuff from Anchor Optical (mirrors and whatnot). If you buy the mountings from Optima, be sure you get the handy laser adjusting tool.

I agree that Intergraf is very helpful. The service that they provide is first rate.

Part of the problem with the D&S setup is that it doesn't account for variability between the semiconductor diodes. Mode hops can happen at almost any point in the power/temperature space.
