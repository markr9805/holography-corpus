Hans Sat Jun 10, 2006 2:39 pm #1
With Type A Pig Gelatin I have been getting consistant results so far. I have now been able to make some exposure tests ranging from 45 minutes down to 45 seconds. I will not be able to make accurate claims about the exposure energy until tomorrow when I have been able to calibrate my exposure meter. My D&S laser is running at about half power now, so about 25mW. I have been exposing an area of about 7x15cm on a 5x5cm plate. This should be an exposure energy of about 0.23mJ per centimeter per second.

EXPOSURE ENERGY:

Exposures longer than 2.5 minutes (35mJ) seem to have no effect on the final brightness of the hologram. When I expose less than 2 minutes (28mJ), the hologram starts to progressively re-play more broad-band at shorter exposure times, but brightness seems to not decay significantly down to exposure times of 45 seconds (10mJ). Coating thickness seems to have not much effect on the sensitivity.

DARK REACTION

I finally had a chance to explore the effect of dark reaction after exposure more carefully. I noticed no difference with dark reaction times up to 15 minutes, in comparison to just flipping the plate in the wash bath immediately after exposure. Tonight I will try to bake an exposed plate for some time in an oven to see if that has any effect.

MILKINESS:

One disappointing thing is that, though the results have been very consistant, I have not been able to make very bright holograms. At low pre-swell temperatures, the holograms are dim and transparent. When I process at slightly higher temperatures, the holograms become more milky with increasing temperatures until the gelatin has become so milky that a (very bright) reflection is only visible from the back side of the hologram (pseudoscopic of course). My first guess was that this was due to the initial gelatin hardness, so I tried several pre-exposures with diffuse light. The only result was that the pre-exposed area's became dimmer. Then I thought that my coatings were perhaps too thick. I tried the veil coating method, but the thinner area's show only a very slight decrease in milkiness.

Joe Farina Sat Jun 10, 2006 5:36 pm #3
Hans, the following paper may be of interest to you:

"Methods of increasing the sensitivity of methylene blue sensitized dichromated gelatin" by Kubota/Ose, in Applied Optics Vol. 18, No. 15 (1979), pages 2538-9. They used two different methods to increase sensitivity, dark reaction and heating. With regards to dark reaction, they said "the sensitivity becomes highest when the plate is developed several days after exposure, then it reduces with time."

With regards to heating, they seem to recommend doing it for a prolonged period of time (1 to 6 hours) at 60 degrees C. A quote from page 2539 of the paper (regarding the heating method): "...this method increases the sensitivity of the plate more than several times..." Also, "When the plate is heated to a higher temperature, say 90 degrees C, this method produces the reverse effect."

Of course, this paper is from 1979 and they are using a different MBDCG system, not Jeff Blyth's. I have always wondered why there is such little information on dark reaction/heating for MBDCG. I think this is the only paper I have which discusses it.
Hans Sat Jun 10, 2006 6:15 pm #4
> what about your processing steps?.... fixer w/ hardener?

MBDCG requires no fixer. The plates are put in water to remove the MB dye and the Chromate. After that the plates are pre-swelled with warmer water and dried in Isopropilalcohol. I have no hardener in house, but I have ordered the chemicals for color (MB+)DCG F5 fixer. So I can experiment with that when they arrive.

I do have Chrome Alum, wich has a hardening effect on gelatin, but Jeff suggested that this just increases the overall hardness of the gelatin and increase the required pre-swell temperature. I will give it a try anyways.

Concerning heating, I remember reading an old post from Jean that he heats his plates after exposure for increased dark reaction. I will also try this tonight. However, theoretically because in MBDCG the Cr(5) intermediate reaction does not exist, dark reaction should have no effect there. But perhaps a heated dark reaction will assist the Cr(3) in crosslinking with the gelatin.
Hans Sun Jun 11, 2006 9:20 pm #5
I visited Kris today. We made some MBDCG plates in his lab (sorry Jean, we tried to phone you to invite you also, but we could not reach you). One came out very bright and sharp, without any milky fogging. If it is not a one-off, there should only two variables left to try for me:

- Lower alcohol temperature: It was a very warm day (30C) in Holland. I was afraid that the alcohol might be too warm, so we pre-cooled the containers in a bath of cool (16C) water. This seemed opposite to my intuition because so many DCG articles promote warm alcohol baths.

- Lower pre-swell temperature: It never occured to me that this might have been the problem with my home exeriments. I always used a minimum pre-swell at 26C. Our very bright hologram was swollen at 25C and pre-washed at 24C.

- Relative Humidity in the lab was 35%. I am not sure, but this might account for the relatively high minimum exposure energy of 120mJ for the Hologram to come out bright.

I will make a new batch tomorrow and give it a try.
