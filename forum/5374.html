Yes, subbing might be something to try: bathing the plates in a 1% gelatin solution + a small amount of chrome alum and have them dry vertically.

Or, another option to improve gelatin adhesion on glass would be using lower gelatin concentrations. Of course this suggests changes to the coating method. In that case bar coating might be more convenient. By the way, using low viscosity solutions, Mayer bars are not even mandatory since we found simple threaded rods produced pretty nice layers.

Lower gelatin concentrations would imply 2 - 10% gelatin solutions.

Moreover, the particular gelatin quality might be paramount. With Aldrich's 300 bloom I could do things that were impossible with say, cooking gelatin. I was amazed to see that stuff stick on untreated PET film - and it even surived the fairly alkaline Neutol development + EDTA bleaching.
