meekan01 Wed Apr 27, 2005 4:18 pm #4
A trick we used to use to get bubbles out of PDMS for high resolution contact lithography was to put it under vacuum after casting. The reduced vapor pressure in the atmosphere pulls out the bubbles very, very well. A bell jar works brilliantly, but you don't really even need that high of a vacuum- just keep in mind that if you lower the pressure too quickly, the gelatin may boil and make a big mess. Do it gradually =)

Dave, yes I can usually get the few bubbles out of the beaker easily but thought the alcohol thingy was interesting and I should try it. If I see a bubble sliding down the glass during coating I hesitate continue to pour in that area until it is washed off the plate. I hold the plate by hand and I recapture the emulsion in a container that I hold the plate over during coating. This container is heated thus I can pour it back into the beaker and continue coating. Whenever pouring or coating to keep bubles from forming I found it is like pouring headless beer. Tilt and pour closely to surface.

I use double Mr. Coffee Filters to filter in a ceramic funnel shaped filter holder by gravity. I bought one of these and use the ceramic filter part but not the vacuum. I tried the vacuum and it bubbles the gel terribly. It came with the hand vacuum pump, hose, stopper, ceramic filter holder, beaker and some 5 micron filters. I only tried the filters once and the gel would not flow trough them too easily. I took a long time and the gel was hardening.
