HI, I was thinking about making a remote shutter using an old moving coil volt meter.

Is the idea of this to reduce the possibility of completely ruining a holo, or is the idea that all my holos will be that bit brighter?

Thanks, Rick.
JohnFP Fri Apr 15, 2005 10:06 am #2
rja4096, having a remote shutter allows you to be away from the table and laser during the time of exposure. If you are close to the table you may introduce vibrations and/or air currents. Depending on what type of hologram you are making these factor could possibly dim your hologram or cause it not to form at all. If one of these factors was causing your holograms to be dim and the remote shutter did away with that factor then the hologram should be brighter. But if it was something else causing the dim hologram, then obvioulsy the remote shutter will not help.

Usually making a remote shutter is very inexpensive and well worth the effort, in my opinion.

It depends a lot on your setup. As John says, it helps if you are not near your laser/table during exposure, since then you set up air currents and your body heat may dim a hologram. If you need long exposures or if your table can't take the exposures you need, then a remote shutter may help. The shutter itself needs to be carefully installed because it also causes movement. Anything waving such as the needle of a voltmeter will cause air currents. So you need to make sure that the exposure time is large compared with the "move" time of the voltmeter needle. Probably the exposure time should be about 10 times the "move" time, so if it takes 1/4 a second for the needle to pass the beam, you need exposure times of greater than 2.5 seconds to make it worthwhile. On the other hand, if the shutter is a long way away from the optics (assuming you have a large enough table) then this is not a issue.
