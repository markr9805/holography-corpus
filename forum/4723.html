Here was the e-mail anouncement:

THE ROYAL PHOTOGRAPHY SOCIETY HOLOGRAPHY GROUP

is pleased to announce a meeting on

Saturday April 30th 2005 between 2pm -5pm

at The Camera Club, 16 Bowden Street, Kennington, London SE11 4DS

Tel: 020 7587 1809

Directions to the Camera Club can be found at [http://www.thecameraclub.co.uk/page22.html](http://www.thecameraclub.co.uk/page22.html)

The speakers will be Sean Johnston and Pearl John.

Admission is free

Dr.Sean Johnston, who is Senior Lecturer in Science Studies at the University of Glasgow, Crichton Campus, is currently engaged in researching the history of holography. He will be talking on 'Scientists, artists and artisans - the making of holographic communities' and the following is a brief synopsis of his talk:

³Over the past 58 years, holography has been recast as a subject time and

again, and has been taken up by new communities. The word 'holography'

first came into common use in 1966, and the term 'holographer' began to

circulate soon afterward. But the definition of the holographer was

reshaped over the next decade by different environments and goals. While

Dennis Gabor and a handful of workers explored the possibilities of

wavefront reconstruction until the mid 1950s, it was workers in classified

laboratories that created more successful versions of the subject. The

explosion of interest following the work of Emmett Leith and Juris Upatnieks

at the Willow Run Laboratories in Michigan led to a wave of military funding

for the new technique. Until the late 1960s, holographers were largely

State-supported and academically trained. At that time, however, the

subject began to be appropriated by new communities: by a handful of artists working with academic and industrial scientists to explore the aesthetic possibilities of the medium, and by artisans self-consciously developing new methods. It was the interactions between these communities that created the more stable and popular subject of the 1980s and beyond.²

You can read more about Sean¹s research at

[http://www.cc.gla.ac.uk/research/johnst ... graphy.htm](http://www.cc.gla.ac.uk/research/johnston/holography.htm)

Pearl John has been teaching holography for the last ten years both here and in the States, where she helped develop an innovative laser technology programme for school children. She currently works for the School of Physics and Astronomy at the University of Southampton where her job is to promote the study of Photonics - the Science of Light - to pupils throughout the South-east of England, using a powerful laser light show to inspire student's interest in Physics. Pearl will be talking about her work in Holography Education and as a practising fine art holographer.

You can see more about Pearl¹s work at

[http://www.pearljohn.co.uk/](http://www.pearljohn.co.uk/)

If you are able to be in London on April 30, I do hope you will come to the meeting. It would be good to see you.
