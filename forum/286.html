Joe Farina Mon Aug 01, 2011 12:19 pm #1
In addition to the many problems involved in two-color holography , I've discovered a benefit which I would like to pass along.

It's often the case that an excess of laser power is available at 532nm, and a shortage exists at 633nm (or whatever red wavelength is being used). In my case for example, I have 100mW at 532 but only 30mW at 633. Assuming the recording material has a similar sensitivity at both wavelengths, the extra power at 532 is being wasted, because the minimum exposure time will be determined by the lower-power 633 laser.

The extra green light could be used for generating fringes for simple observation, or for fringe locking, by adding two dichroic mirrors as far downstream as possible in the reference and object beams respectively, picking off say 50% of the green from the combined green/red beam, but allowing 90% of the red light through. Then the two green beams could be used to generate a parallel interferometer, to monitor stability. These fringes could also be locked if needed. Such bright fringes would be a real luxury. They have some interesting dichroic mirrors on eBay which seem to meet the requirements for 532 and 633 when used in this manner, I have two on order.
Jeffrey Weil Mon Aug 01, 2011 12:36 pm #2
Unless your doing single beam deni stuff. There's no reason to use a fringe locker on that kind of a setup. It wouldn't even do anything as the "beam splitter" on single beam holo is the object and plate combo. With fringe locking you need to effect one "arm" of the interferometer. That's close to impossible with single beam stuff and that's the only kind of holography I've seen anyone on the board do with more than one laser freq.

I don't really believe in using fringe lockers for normal display work anyway. And I'm shooting resist, which means I always have very long exposures, not enough laser light and many many exposures in a single finished piece so the table has to be very reliable.

If your exposures are under 5 min and you need a fringe locker to get good results there's something wrong that needs to be fixed.

Dinesh Mon Aug 01, 2011 1:03 pm #3
> Joe Farina: Assuming the recording material has a similar sensitivity at both wavelengths, the extra power at 532 is being wasted, because the minimum exposure time will be determined by the lower-power 633 laser.

Don't you mean that the 633 will determine the maximum time? Assuming the same sensitivity (rare, in my experience of PFG-03C and BB Pan, have not tried other colour emulsions),and assuming the reflectivity of the objects are the same to give the same ratio, and assuming that the loss in the optics for both lasers are the same to give the same proportion of beam intensity at the plate (depending on the mirror material, there may be a wavelength-dependent scattering) , then the weaker laser will have the longer exposure time.

If the loss in beam intensity due to the optical arrangement is, say, 50%, then the power at the plate from the HeNe will be 15 mW and from the 532 will be 50. Assuming a 4x5 plate (125 sq cm), then the power from the HeNe is 15/125 = 0.12 mW and from the 532 is 50/125 = 0.4 mW. If the plate sensitivity is 150 uJ, the nthe HeNe exposure time is 150/120 = 1.25 seconds and from the 532 is 150/400 = 0.3 second. Thus T<532> < T<633> and the minimum power is dependent on the 532, while the maximum is determined by the 633.

Of course the beam diameter must be factored in. Here, I've assume the beam diameters are within 10% of each other, giving a 1% error in the calculation, due to the area factor being a square function.
