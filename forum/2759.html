How do you monitor the de of a hologram as it develops in the tray?

Well, this may sound simplistic.

But use a tray that is made of glass. Mount an LED that is of wavelength that your film is not sesitive to underneath the glass tray. Make a fixtur that mounts a photo diode above the tray which is lined up with the LED and has a meter attached to it. The hologram is placed in the tray and lays between the LED and the photodiode. As is darkens it starts to block the light which is measured on the meter. You would need to calibrate the DE with the readings on the meter.

Gary Deal Wed Oct 06, 2004 2:53 am #6
Let's see, to measure the diffraction efficiency of a hologram in the developer, I'd think you'd first have to figure out what wavelength the wet hologram would diffract while wet, then supply the wavelength in a more or less linear form through the hologram, then measure the diffracted and undiffracted light(?) and compare the two. That question mark is because what the hologram would diffract while it was wet would be probably be outside the visible spectrum.

Dinesh, that calculating fiend, says he's got a method that worked. Properly placed IR diodes and a good power meter?

I made a "wet" hologram a while back. I soaked the film in developer, clamped it between two glass plates, exposed it and watched. The film darkened a bit and I gave it some extra exposure, I then put it back in the developer (Kodak HRP 1:2) to finish developing. After the usual process I pulled it from the wash and took a look. Wet, the image showed. As it dried, I watched it change color and disappear as it passed beyond the visible spectrum. Once the image was gone I could bring it back by breathing on it, or better by re-wetting it, but I found limited novelty in an invisible hologram.

There may be practical uses for this in humidity (or water content) indicators, or for holograms that bees can see while we can't.
