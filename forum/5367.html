Here is an excerpt form the Amateur Scientist column of Scientific American (December issue 1969):

> "Calcium acrylate monomer is made into polymer by a combination of both photopolymerization and photocrosslinking. In effect units of monomer, each roughly in the shape of a T, are fastened together to form a ladder-like structure by chemical 'nails.' The crossbars of the T's are joined end to end to form the sides of the ladders. This is an example of polymerization. The rungs of the ladders-the crosslinks-are atoms of calcium."For the initial experiment put about 20 milliliters of monomer solution in a transparent container such as a cup made of clear plastic, add two drops of triethanolamine and two drops of methylene blue and swirl the container to mix the solutions. Hold the container a few inches from an incandescent lamp. Within seconds the clear blue mixture will become cloudy on the side that faces the lamp and will change into an opaque, blue-white film attached to the wall of the container. The film is a plastic: polycalcium acrylate. It will continue to grow in thickness as the dye absorbs light; within seconds it will fill the container with solid polymer. The mass can be dissolved by adding a mild acid. The resulting fluid is a solution of polyacrylic acid.

Perhaps it would be possible to dope a gelatine solution with this magic mixture to make a homemade photo polymer?

Fancy you digging this old reference out Hans! Yes it originated from the interesting work of Gerald Oster et alia.

Your intuition is right about it being suitable for mixing with gelatin to make red HeNe sensitive photopolymer capable of recording holograms.

I used this principle to make my own photopolymer holograms over 20 years ago and then made it the subject for an M.Phil at Wolverhampton Polytechnic with Graham Saxby as my internal examiner. I found Zinc acrylate (made simply from zinc oxide and acrylic acid) was better than calcium acrylate because it is has about the highest water solubility of the divalent metal acrylates (Magnesium was also good but magnesium polyacrylate had a lower R.I. that the zinc salt.)

I never understood why the Polaroid photopolymer system stuck to using the monovalent lithium salt with all the additional headache of getting insolubilization of lithium polyacrylate. Certainly their stuff could produce excellent high diffraction but apparently only when made "in house" .

However at the end of the day my zinc polyacrylate system was not more photosensitive than what I managed to achieve later with my simpler and cheaper MBDCG system .

Jeff

PS. As you seem to be interested in doing your own photopolymer,

I should point out on a practical level that what counts more than the list above is the water solubility of the acrylate monomer .

So because the solubility of Zn acrylate is higher than lead acrylate

you finish up getting a higher refractive index in zinc polyacrylate

than lead or Barium polyacrylate simply because you can pack much more of it into your polymer layer which can more than compensate for its lower RI taken on a mole for mole basis.

Also it is better often to have more concentrated acrylate groups in your starting material because then the free radical polymerization is easier

ie. more light sensitive. However this can go too far and the radicals actually need to be prevented from having their mean free paths exceed a fringe width , so it is good to have oxygen around as an inhibitor.
