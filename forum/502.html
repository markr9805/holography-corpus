zipsister Fri Mar 23, 2012 6:39 pm #1
I have been using my own developer for the last three years which is extremely active requiring much shorter exposure times than others (JD-2, JD-4, Pyro, etc). Used it for reflection and transmission on Slavich (especially VPR-M) and Agfa films and plates (8E75/56). Sometimes EDTA and sometimes Dichromates bleaches:

Zip1:

Metol 1gr,

Hydroquinone 1gr,

Phenidone 0.5gr,

Sodium Sulphite 30gr,

Ascorbic Acid 10gr,

Potassium Hydroxide 30gr,

water to make 1 litre

Dave
jsfisher Sat Mar 24, 2012 9:32 am #2
What sort of shelf life are you getting for it?
zipsister Sat Mar 24, 2012 4:29 pm #3
I usually have used it up after about 3-4 months and it does start getting darker/oxidizing by then, BUT it still appears to be working as effectively right to the end. What I like about this formula is that it is extremely active reducing my exposure times drastically while retaining contrast and brightness of my work. I wouldn't use anything else now and haven't for years. Used it on the kilometre long Hololujah hologram which required 2 minute exposures for 1.1x1 metre hologram panels.if you only occasionally make holograms then just make up smaller quantities. If your exposure time is 30 seconds with your regular developer then start with 2 seconds with this formula.

zipsister Sat Mar 24, 2012 8:05 pm #5
As safe as any other developer. I usually wear gloves regardless when mixing any chemicals just out of respect for my hands. Possasium Hydroxide will generate heat when the water goes in so I usually throw that chemical into the mix after the water has gone into my Pyrex container. But even if you don't the amount of water (1000ml) cools things almost immediately. These are all easily obtained chemicals, as well in an era when some are becoming hard to get or you need to take out another mortgage to buy them.
zipsister Sun Mar 25, 2012 5:23 am #6
Because I don't work like most in that I'm primarily working with very large holograms, even some of my test strips are metre square size then I tend to use this developer up fairly quickly and usually mix it up in four litre batches. But when making smaller holograms I would suggest placing some in your developer tray and using it over and over in the one session then dispose of what's left using fresh mixture for each session. Just a suggestion.

It also over comes the hassle of accidently getting stained fingers with the pyro developer if you forget to put the rubber gloves on. The mix of the Metol, Hydroquinone and Phenidone with the Potassium Hydroxide is quite an active combo. I'm not really surprised and certainly these chemicals are cheaper than pyro and catechol..
