Jeffrey Weil Tue Sep 10, 2013 6:08 pm #6
When I have a flat, I have a short table that can fit under the table and take the weight. I put a car jack on that and just lift up the flat side of the table with a bit of 2x4 between the jack and the table to protect it and disperse the load a bit. From there I can slide the bad tire out and replace it. Easy.

Since I'm using magnetic mounts and not tilting the table that far I don't need to remove any optics before jacking it up.

Place a bit of wood in the donut hole of the new inner tube before you inflate it. That way if it goes flat the tire's not crushed and you don't have to jack that far to remove it.

Jeffrey Weil Sat Sep 28, 2013 1:52 pm #20
Ok, here's the first proof of my hockey card. It's a bit dinged up from shipping, that's why the color changes on the right side in a few places. The proof is quickly embossed so the client can approve the image before things move ahead into full production.

[](https://holographyforum.org/forum/download/file.php?id=2409&mode=view)

It's a full color, 3 level, 2d-3d. The background which looks like cracked ice. A black and white Lord Stanley which looks pretty burned out. The art they gave me for him was many generations old and looked just like that. And then the rainbow colored Stanley Cup in real space.

Lord Stanley is black and white but not a true achromat. It's a full color mix of a black and white object. A true achromatic hologram would have been made with an open aperture master.

I've made many 2d-3d's over the last 25 years but this is the first time someone has asked for pure black to fade into transparency showing the layer behind it. The fade looks better in real life, more gradual. I like how it came out. I'll post the finished card after it's produced. The hologram will be cropped down to fit into the card properly. This image contains lots of bleed.
