Title: "Tropical Hot-Dog Night"

Ultimate red 70 nm preshift, 660 nm diode laser, ~200 uJ exposure. Single beam Denisyuk, lit for photo with with Optileds Amber LED, camera Canon A95. Stars are a mixture of split chromed plastic beads and sprinked fine glitter (pixie dust). Note the Southern Cross constellation to the left.

One of the palm trees moved during exposure so it's dim. Will have to reshoot when I get more plates and it's less windy Image from camera is unmodified and is more colorful than reality. The holo is more monochrome and the moon does not look so noticeably different in color visually.

Tom B. Tue Mar 22, 2005 5:26 am #8
Thanks for the kind comments. On Ultimate vs. BB, the jury is still out. I like the end results from both, but appreciate the convenience and sensitivity of Ultimate. The diode is a Panasonic LNCQ05 50 mw in a crude home-made setup (variable power supply + resistor), cylinder lens for divergence control, temperature control by attachment to an insulated thermal mass and operating in a very stable thermal environment.

I'd like to try the black transparency over backlit diffuser method sometime. Could make some very realistic star fields this way using a laser printed transparency. The glitter on black velvet method has its limits - most glitter bits end up at the wrong angle for forward reflection and the reflected beam width is too narrow, but the twinkling effect is nice. The metalized beads create nice point sources visible from a wide angle, like pearls except not white. Also my best black velevet is not quite black enough for good contrast with direct laser illumination when near the plate.

Tom B. Fri Mar 25, 2005 5:23 am #14
Yes, a hulagram is a hologram that incorporates Hawaiian elements, especially the hula dance and/or philosophy. A short description of the tradition here: [http://www.kaimi.org/the_hula.htm](http://www.kaimi.org/the_hula.htm)

In my case, I just focused on the superficial elements available as fridge magnets at the Honolulu airport

I do not claim credit for the invention of the word "hulagram" - I believe Western Union at one time sent out shivering grass-skiirted maidens from door to door to chatter out telegrams while shaking their hips. But maybe for holography it is a novelty?

I should also point out that the crescent moon really does look like that from equatorial latitudes - the suns sets almost straight down, and the horns of the crescent moon point away from the sun so it looks boat-shaped. Further south the dierection of the horns is reversed and the crescent moon looks backwards.
