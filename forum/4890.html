School of Holography @ Spatial

Spatial Imaging An established holography studio of some 15 years has

teamed up with award winning holographer/ artist Patrick Boyd to start

classes teaching holography at their studios in Richmond, London.

The courses will initially be aimed at beginners and will last for 5

days. During this time attendees will learn the basics theories of holography and will learn how to make a variety of types of hologram including pulsed holography. The primary aim of this course is to provide students with sufficient practical & technical knowledge to develop their own interest in whatever direction they choose.

The style of the teaching will definitely be ³hands on² with an

emphasis on learning by doing. Apart from a basic intro to each type of hologram, any theory needed will be handled in parallel to the teaching.

The courses are planned to start in June and will run monthly. It is

envisaged that there should be about 5 students per class. It is

anticipated that a large percentage of students will be from abroad.

Advanced & specialist courses will also be available at a later date.

Patrick Boyd has an MA in holography from the Royal College of Art and

has won many awards including a Fulbright Fellowship and exhibited in many countries worldwide.

Spatial Imaging is a pioneer in the field of holography with expertise

in many holographic processes. They are responsible for many world famous works and recently were a major component in making a holographic portrait of HRH Queen Elizabeth 2nd.

At present there are three holography studios at Spatial Imaging.

A pulse studio with a 10 joule ruby pulse laser. complimented by

dedicated pulse optics and a 50x60 cm collimator, masters up to 50x60 cm can be made.

A Continous wave studio. A 6x 4 foot isolation table with a 50mw HeNe

laser, a full set up of optics, collimators & plate holders, this

studio can be used to make CW masters or transfers.

The 3rd studio can be used either for basic CW work or as a dedicated

stereogram studio.

Confirmed dates of the courses are:

week of the 20th June 2005

week of the 25th July 2005

no courses in August

week of the 26th September.

The course structure!

Day 1. AM. Basic intro the the theory of holography.

Preparation for the following four days. Assessment of subject material

bought by students & discussion of projects. This will also been seen

as an introduction & preparation day for the other 4 days of the course

PM. Make a Denisyuk hologram! A simple hologram which is very easy to

make and easy to view. The object needs to be able to fit into a 4x5

inch space, of a reflective nature and fairly solid (not flimsy)

Day 2 AM. Brief look at art and holography.

Make a Michealson Interferometer. (a real time interferometry test which

will demonstrate the the importance of isolating the table and equipment

from all vibration).

PM Shadowgram & Pseudo colour reflection. This is a one step hologram

that produces bright results and is easy to make. This technique works best with transparent or translucent objects eg, glass, clear plastic,

photographs or drawings on film (acetate & not polyester) etc. Objects should be able to fit into a 4x5 inch space.

Day 3 Make a CW master & rainbow transfer. Our 1st master/transfer set

up. Rainbow holograms can be bright and easy to view when lit from behind. Again, the object needs to be able to fit into a 4x5 inch space, of a

reflective nature and fairly solid (not flimsy)

Day 4 Pulse Master. The pulse laser enables us to make holograms of

people. We will make a 10x 8 portrait type hologram.

Day 5 Reflection Transfer. Using reflection transfer camera, we will

make a 10x 8 inch image plane reflection copy of the pulse master.

Each day is closed with a question and answer session designed to clear

up any areas of doubt and uncertainty in the ground that has been covered

during the day.
