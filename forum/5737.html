Hans Tue Aug 01, 2006 10:56 am #2
Why do they need to be the exact same frequency? Is it to make the hologram brighter? Or do you want to make interferograms? In the case of interferograms, is it required to have this same frequency?

A fantastically dangerous way to do it would be to split the beam first, use beam #1 for the first exposure. A 20ns beam is about 6m long. So, if you would take two mirrors of very high reflectivity and bounce #2 beam back and forth between two mirrors about 3m apart at an offset angle, each double bounce would account for a delay of 20ns (exactly one pulse length). By adjusting the angle between the two mirrors, you can adjust the amount of bounces between the mirrors and thus the delay time of the second pulse.
Ron Michael Tue Aug 01, 2006 1:08 pm #3
For interferograms you don't need it the same frequency, you just need enough time between exposures to catch the movement and this is where active q switching come in. A pockel cell is the preferred method but an AOM is also useful.

If you don't want the interference, then the two pulses have to be very close together like say 50 or so nanosecs apart for a slow object. The problem with multiple pulses is not the frequency, it's the object movement speed. Due to spatial hole burning, even if you have a SLM first pulse, more than likely the next pulse will be a different SLM.

To prevent SHB,

You could setup a ring cavity with four mirrors rectangle with one as your partial reflector. both waves will emit at two different directions. 90 degrees apart, so use just one.
Ron Michael Tue Aug 01, 2006 3:18 pm #4
Ring cavities help with spatial burning hole issue (spectral issue), but not the temporal issue (SLM). You would need mode selectors like high finese etalons etc or better yet, have a very small yag crystal polished/coated in a way to make a micro ring laser. This can be diode pulse pumped and because of it's diode pump mode volume and small ring size it can be made slm and tem00, then this will seed your oscillator (regen amp) before going on to your power amps. But as you can see these techniques are more complicated and involve more time and money.

Keep in mind to design multiple pulses with the same L mode, you know that oscillator will require active control of the pulse timing, temperature, cavity length, etc.

ron olson Tue Aug 01, 2006 9:22 pm #6
What is it you're going to be looking at? You need to ascertain the required timing difference between pulses before you design the system. If the pulse separation is a few to a few tens of nanoseconds (this is rare) you can get by with an optical delay line; if the separation is from several to a few hundred microseconds (typical for studying things under stress) you'll need to double-pulse a Pockels cell; if the timing difference exceeds 0.5 milliseconds, you'll need two independently Q-switched lasers driven by a delay generator and seeded by a common source (assuming they need to be the same frequency). Frequency drift between two consecutive pulses under the same flashlamp curve has never, to my knowledge, been an issue for interferrometry using either ruby or YAG lasers.
