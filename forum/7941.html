Joe Farina Wed Aug 19, 2009 4:00 pm #1
Having done a few tests with my spatial filter on a vibration isolated table (i.e., a wobbly table), and the laser off the table, it turns out that the wobble transferred to the table (by fiddling with the screws on the spatial filter) does indeed make it difficult to align the spatial filter. For people who have spatial filters with micrometers rather than screws (and perhaps a less wobbly table) maybe it won't be a problem, but I'm trying to work on this issue at the present.

I asked Don Gillespie about it, and he had some suggestions. First, he said give all the screws a good cleaning with MEK. Then apply lanolin to them. (I hadn't heard about that before, so I did a little checking to see what lanolin is, and where to get it.) Also, he said to affix some kind of long rod to each of the three screws, and do the adjustments by turning those rods instead of turning the screws directly with my finger. By that, I assumed that the "long rod" might be very thin and/or flexible, so that the rod would absorb any twisting stress, so that it wouldn't be transferred to the screws (and then directly to the table). I'm trying to work on that also.

After really cleaning your hands from chemical exposure make sure to put lanolin on your hands to protect them from picking up trace chemicals. I use it often to protect myself from epoxies.
dave battin Thu Aug 20, 2009 12:32 am #3
The way i get around this problem (when doing one beam holograms), is to first send the beam to a X,Y adjustable mirror (off table) and then when doing the last tweaking befor the shot ,i would tweak the mirror's adjustments as the beam enters the spatial filter, my old table would drift a bit and i was able to get it nice and centered again without shifting the beam too far :wall:
BobH Thu Aug 20, 2009 1:11 am #4
I'd suggest you try a larger pinhole, or a smaller diameter beam going into the microscope objective. Or stiffen your isolation system. The trick Don G. mentioned is probably to use use socket head cap screws on the adjusters and a ball driver to drive them. That allows you to decouple the shaking of your hand from the component being adjusted. It's easier to put even pressure on the handle of the ball driver.

If you're using those big, spindly old Newport spatial filters, you might just be out of luck. The way they hold the pinhole is terribly unstable. The compact Newport device is the best! Thorlabs makes a spatial filter body that's also kind of big, but it has socket head screws as an option. But really, try a bigger pinhole. Clean your mirrors and see how big you can get away with! ![:pray:](https://holographyforum.org//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f64f.svg)
