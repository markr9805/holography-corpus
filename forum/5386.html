Ron Michael Sun Feb 19, 2006 5:18 am #2
page 114. H.I. Bjelkhagen. Silver-Halide Recording Materials. Springer. Vol 66 1995.

Agfa Gevaert holotest:

8e75hd 7um thick <5000 lp/mm gn size 35/44 nm 633nm 10uj/cm2, 694nm 20uj/cm2

10e75 7um thick <2800 lp/mm gn size 90nm 633nm 1uj/cm2 694uj/cm2.

8e56hd 7um thick <5000 lp/mm gn size 35/44 nm 442nm 25uj/cm2

Similar quoted in Pg 115. Unterseher, Fred. Holography Handbook. Ross 1982.

7um emulsion thickness.

at 521nm

10e56 1 to 1.5uj/cm2 3000lp/mm gn size 90nm

8e56hd 18.25uj/cm2 5000lp/mm gn size 35nm

at 627nm

10e75 .5 to .75 uj/cm2 3000lp/mm gn size 90nm

8e75hd 10uj/cm2 5000lp/mm gn size 35nm

Additionally the amplitude (density) transmission curves given where for the Agfa G-3p developer for 2mins at 20 degrees C and fixer g-334 for 2mins at 20 degrees C folloed by 5 miute wash.

8e75hd at 627nm showed OD 1 at 15uj/cm2 and OD 2 at 25uj/cm2

8e56hd at 521nm showed OD 1 at 25uj/cm2 and OD 2 at 50uj/cm2

In my case with pulse, using SM-6 I've be able to get to OD2 within 2min develop with about 30 to 45uj/cm2 exposure on 8e75hd at 694nm by processing within 5mins of a single pulse of 12-20nsecs.

An undated Agfa leaflet (# 21.8269), released around 1984 probably, emphasizes on an important difference between glass plates and films:

emulsion thickness on glass plates (8E75, 10E75 and 8E56) was said to be 7um, whereas on the corresponding films it was 5um only. Incidentally, I wonder how much that contributed to the far superior image brightness on film.
jeff-blyth Sun Feb 19, 2006 1:07 pm #5
> Martin: ...................................................... an important difference between glass plates and films:emulsion thickness on glass plates (8E75, 10E75 and 8E56) was said to be 7um, whereas on the corresponding films it was 5um only. Incidentally, I wonder how much that contributed to the far superior image brightness on film.

The interesting cause of this difference was that Agfa-Gevaert's big scale coating machinery required a large quantity of freshly made emulsion to be all coated without delay on 100 + meter lengths, 1 meter wide. One run then was enough for the small holo market for years apparently. Whereas with glass plates they were coating on much smaller scale and the emulsion once made was refrozen and then re-thawed coated and refrozen. We guessed at the time that that sad, bad grain growth occurred then, occasionally a batch of plates would arrive which were at the time really brilliant---better and brighter than film . I was pretty sure these good ones must have had new freshly made emulsion. Ordering plates felt like a sort of Russian roulette game with a 1 in 6 chance of getting a good batch. The small band of buyers who complained and sensed the problem were of course an irrelevance in the big business scheme of things.
