This is a followup to [this](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2165&postdays=0&postorder=asc&start=0) thread.

In the mean time I was designing a simple light meter and building

a few samples of it; a brief preliminary description is [here](http://argonholo.webhop.net/electronics/lightmeter.html). It

works as expected.

I will make them available to anybody just for cost of material and

shipping. Right now I have materials for like a dozen units. I

may acquire material and order PCB's for more, if there is sufficient

interest. So whoever is interested, please drop me a line, so that

I can gauge the demand.

There are several options:

A) just the PCB. It fits the common OPT101 sensor but also the

better OPT202 (no dark voltage).

B) PCB and OPT202 sensor plus most of the remaining components. You

must supply an MAX1044 or LTC1044 chip in SO8 package (hint: available as samples).

C) Ready to use and calibrated against my S20MM Thorlabs meter.

Obviously this is a lot of work and I can't do this on a larger

scale . I have in mind people who do not have a calibrated meter

around and are struggling to find the right exposure.

However, right now I can accurately calibrate it only at 532nm as

I have a stabilized laser only for this wavelength. For other

wavelengths an extrapolation is possible but that will make things

less accurate of course. But in any case the accuracy should be

more than adequate for holography applications. I may be able to

very accurately calibrate other wavelengths later this year.

I may have time during the weekend, but before I proceed, a little

feedback is asked for: should I calibrate to have a round number,

like 20mV/(uW/cm^2) (which makes exposure computations easier, but

it can be true only at one wavelength), or should I calibrate to

match the raw sensitivity claimed in the sensor data sheet? That would

allow an easier comparison with other's uncalibrated sensors;

the exposure can be determined via the curves on my web site.

It would be good to have a common standard for this. So please

give your vote which way you prefer!
