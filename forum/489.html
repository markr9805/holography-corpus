John Sonley Sun Mar 04, 2012 6:43 am #1
Since changing the gantry and overall set-up of my sandboxed based holography table to use most of the 6' X 5' surface area, I noticed when making H2s in particular, that the reference beam drifted off the plateholder slowly over a 12 - 24 hour period. It was still stable enough to make bright H2s - but I didn't like it as H2s would be brighter without ANY movement!

This drift was noticed first of all during some very cold weather we were experiencing in N. Yorkshire - about -8 deg at night up to about +5 deg during the day. My 1st thoughts were that the slight temperature fluctuations in my basement lab was causing the air in the 6 scooter tyres upon which my sandbox sits to change volume in some non-even manner and thereby distort the table. I installed a pulse proportional heater into the airspace below my box, skirted off the temperature controlled area and confirmed a temperature stability if circa 0.1 deg ..................and the ref. beam drift was just as bad!

After two weeks of detailed study into the instability of the ref beam, undertaking selective testing of temperature effects on a range of the optical components I use, I eventually homed-in on the source of the instability which has provided the solution - and it's simple really.

My 30 mW HeNe requires about 3 days to fully stabilise !!! - all the rest of the components - and the box are already beautifully stable over a tested temperature range in the lab of 12.8 - 16.8 deg

I've now had the ref beam centred on a white painted sheet of glass in the filmholder slot for 36 hours and the beam has not shifted one iota - QED

I had a good chat about the problem with Dave Battin on Skype a couple of days ago - I'm pleased to be able to give you some good news Dave!

I would be interested to hear from fellow members on how long you leave your HeNe 'on' to warm up before firing-off to expose a hologram

BRgds

John
BobH Sun Mar 04, 2012 11:04 am #2
You're talking about beam pointing stability. Is your laser mounted on its side? If so, that would increase the time to stability. He-ne lasers are generally pretty good as far as beam pointing is concerned. Are you certain it's not the mount for it that's heating up under the laser head, and bending a bit? I always use two transfer mirrors between the laser head and the first component on the table to be able to constantly adjust the position of the input beam. An iris diaphram after the spatial filters also helps in keeping the beams centered.
Jeffrey Weil Sun Mar 04, 2012 8:56 pm #3
If the beam is wandering a bit over 12 hours it won't be a problem. Any motion before the beam splitter won't matter as long as the wander doesn't cause your spatial filters to go out of focus.

You're not losing any brightness.

I used to let my He/Ne heat up for about an hour. Same with my He/Cd. Warming them up isn't about pointing it's about frequency stability. If the resonator is heating up, the frequency of the emitted light is going to change over time. If that's going on during an exposure your going to get "moving" fringes even without table motion. The pitch of the interference will shift with that frequency drift.

The resonator comes to thermal equilibrium quickly. It's close to or even part of the heat source.

The only time drifting over 12 hours would be a problem is if you had a 12 hour, or close to it, exposure and you lost either spatial filter lock or uneven exposure.

BobH Tue Mar 06, 2012 11:26 am #9
Perhaps in your focus on preventing air currents from getting at the recording system, you have cut off air flow that's necessary for proper cooling of the laser head. The set-up hates air flow. The laser head needs lots of it! It's not the plasma tube that's bending, it's the resonator. I recommend ducting outside air to flow around the head, and best if it is guided to enter the head from underneath and with fan assist. That has worked very well for me in the past, for getting the head to stay at a stable temperature after a short warm-up.

Joe Farina Tue Mar 06, 2012 3:37 pm #12
> Dinesh: John Sonley wrote:The HeNe's a 20mW Melles Griot, about 1 metre in length and it's mounted on a wooden plank on the top edge of the sand boxCould the wood be warping and/or expanding/contracting very slightly due to humidity variations? What kind of wood?

That's a very good observation and quite possibly the source of the problem. I don't have any data for expansion versus humidity variations for wood, but I would assume it's substantial. I do have some data for coefficient of thermal expansion, from "Building Scientific Apparatus" (Moore/Davis/Coplan, 2nd edition 1989), page 11:

Douglas fir (air-dried): 6 parallel to fiber (grain) and 35 across fiber

Oregon white oak (air dried) (probably similar to English oak): 5 parallel to fiber and 55 across fiber

As can be seen, the expansion parallel to the grain is remarkably good (by comparison, aluminum is 23, cast iron is 11, and invar is 1.3) but across the grain, it's not so hot. (Wood usually warps across the grain.) But this just addresses temperature, things could easily get worse when humidity fluctuations are thrown into the equation.
