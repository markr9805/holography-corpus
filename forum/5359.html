In a stereogram there is no depth information. Then why does the master have to be a hologram? This has been troubling me for quite some time and I have researched and tried a variety of techniques to understand this.

What I have found is the only thing that is needed in the H1 in a stereogram is the focusing ability of the slit and the direction of the focus.

What I propose, is to separate the image in the slit and the hologram part of the slit. I wish to make a hologram of the LCD but without an image on it, just wide open to light in each of the slits to record the light geometry of the LCD in relation to each slit. This will be the Slit HOE. Then record each LCD image onto a standard piece of Photographic film focusing it to a slit relative to the same distance and angle that formed the HOE slits. After the regular photographic slit master is done, overlay that master with the slit HOE and illuminate with a beam as to the original reference beam that was used to make the HOE and each slit image should focus to the same spot in space. At this spot place a piece of holographic recording material and a reference beam and make the hologram. I have some drawings and may post them if there is interest.

The benefit of this system is, one only has to produce the painstaking slit master once, which then can be used for many photographic masters. The photographic masters can be done very quickly and do not need a pulsed laser or stability requirements. And relative intensity from one slit to the other can be controlled much more easily.

I have the geometry worked out (I think) but foresee registration may be difficult to achieve as the photographs have to be nuts on to the focusing geometry of the slit HOE (not unlike a standard Stereogram master). I also realize I have not made a transmission hologram of any type in DCG yet, so that too will be a challenge.

This could also be done with a grid of images instead of slits for full parallax in both directions.
