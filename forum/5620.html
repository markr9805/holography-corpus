I am starting to collect information of a the difficulties of building a home built 473nm blue DPSS. The YAG and Diode bars are becoming less expensive. LBO must be used as the doubling crystal. The YAG must be cooled and temperature stabilized. The cavity would need to be a ring design. While this all adds up to lots of money and complexity soon it will be in the reah of a hologrpher.

Here is the first abstract I have found:

Diode-pumped laser amplifiers: application to 0.946 μm Nd:YAG

Norman P Barnes et al 2003 J. Phys. B: At. Mol. Opt. Phys. 36 879-891 doi:10.1088/0953-4075/36/5/308

Norman P Barnes1, Theresa J Axenson2, Donald J Reichle Jr1 and Brian M Walsh1

1 NASA Langley Research Center, Hampton, VA 23681, USA

2 Science and Technology Corporation, 10 Basil Sawyer Drive, Hampton, VA 23666, USA

E-mail: n.p.barnes at larc.nasa.gov and t.j.axenson at larc.nasa.gov

Abstract. A diode-pumped laser amplifier model is derived from first principles and applied to a Nd:YAG amplifier operating on the 4F 3/2 to 4I 9/2 transition at 0.946 μm. The effects of amplified spontaneous emission are included in the model and the addition of this effect is shown to produce better agreement with the data. The amplifier model includes effects of the transverse and longitudinal variation of the pump beam, transverse and longitudinal variation of the probe beam, and multiple passes of the probe beam. Experimental results obtained with a quasi four-level Nd:YAG amplifier operating at 0.946 μm are used to validate the model. The amplifier was evaluated as a function of the pump energy, the probe energy, the probe beam radius, the pulse repetition frequency and the temperature. For all of the experimental conditions, the experimental results and the model agree.

Print publication: Issue 5 (14 March 2003)

Received 17 September 2002, in final form 3 December 2002

Published 19 February 2003
