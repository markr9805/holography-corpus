What paint or material is out there does a good job at light sucking?

Light Absorbing Blackout Material

Velvet-like, adhesive or non-adhesive backing choices allow black-out material to be applied to any acceptable surface. Flocked material surface characteristics are consistent across product line, but higher paper numbers indicate thicker, more durable levels of backing for the material. The #40 backing is paper-thin (0.01" thick), the #55 adhesive backing is thicker (0.02" thick), but still very flexible, and the #65 backing has a texture similar to heavy construction paper (0.02" thick). Note that material with non-adhesive backing will have a slightly duller (less reflective) finish than the adhesive backed flock material.

I have not tried this product. I have however tried this one, for telescope and holography light blocking:

It's quite good for general blackening (and much better than paint) but for say H1 backgrounds I prefer black velvet, which is near-invisible when

properly oriented for minimum reflection. Unfortunately modern velvet available at fabric stores is all polyester and does not work

as well, so I try to keep an eye out for good old crushed velvet when it becomes available at second-hand stores and Ninja outfitters.
BobH Sat Jun 06, 2009 1:58 pm #5
I agree that the black velvet that's "all polyester" is not as good as the cotton based velveteen. That's also still available. Another good baffeling material is black anodized aluminum foil sold as "Blackout Film". Cinematographers use it to shield lights. One roll (I recommend the 24" wide version) will last you a lifetime as it's very thick and you can reuse the pieces many times.
