Some potentially(?) useful resources for other future beginners.

[Holographic Imaging, Spring 2003 course by Prof. Michael Halle & Prof. Stephen Benton, made available by MIT](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mas-450-holographic-imaging-spring-2003/pages/syllabus/)

The readings and lab pdfs have a lot of useful information. Since this is a course, you could attempt to follow the classwork as a self-directed learner. The reading list has a lot of books referenced. Presumably the early draft of Benton's mentioned in the syllabus is Benton's book Holographic Imaging. Which would be the book to follow for the assigned reading? This appears to me a very valuable resource available to the public currently for free. Any thoughts from other users? (labs not withstanding)

[Bigwords, a book search aggregate to find the cheapest copies of books available!](https://www.bigwords.com/) (does not search ebay afaik)

[Holowiki.org](https://holowiki.org/wiki/Main_Page)

I have questions that are not easily answered even after searching for articles on this wiki, however there is a wealth of knowledge when you can find it.

[Ed Wesley's list of 7 holography projects](http://edweslystudio.com/Pedagogy/7SBP/ListFrame7SBPWeb.html)

This blog has a lot of useful information if you navigate to the home index!

[International School of Holography](https://www.internationalschoolofholography.com/)

Paid courses relating to holography. Their yt channel has [3 videos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GtitPgDHXnI) of "holo"seminars, featuring individuals who have been active in the holography scene for decades.

[Holocenter, they offer a breif overview of holography.](https://holocenter.org/what-is-holography)

[laserboyholo](https://www.laserboyholo.com/), a hologram artist fairly active on social media, with useful videos on [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/@laserboyholo/videos), instagram, [tiktok](https://www.tiktok.com/@laserboyholo), and patreon.

As for getting started in holography, I would recommend a green (532nm) laser of at least 50mW, preferably 75 or 100mW (which is known to be good for holography), and Bayfol HX200 from Geola in Lithuania.

BobH Wed May 01, 2024 8:53 am #3
If ever in the Phoenix area, stop by "Vintage Lasers & Holograms" in Tempe, where you can see the history of holography and examples of all types. I could show you my home studio set up for silver-halide processing and work with photopolymer. I'd recommend away from Holography Handbook because of its dated information and focus on sandbox equipment (a hobby killer for certain).

A visit to any working holography studio will give you very practical knowledge not available in the books or online. Attendance at ISDH2024 in June in Lake Forest, IL would be a great opportunity to for a concentrated course in modern pictorial holography, and connect you directly and personally to those currently making holograms for a variety of applications.
Ed Wesly Thu May 02, 2024 5:38 am #4
Hello, nullvoyger, I appreciate your Bette Davis homage and your positive comment about my site! Any of the above books will help you get started, but to echo Bob, there is nothing like seeing holograms being made in the flesh. At the 2024 International Symposium on Display Holography at Lake Forest College, we have access to a well-equipped lab thanks to their Physics department to demonstrate the recording of various types of set ups and shoot them, if desired. Demonstrations already on the roster include tuning spatial filters, how to split beams with polarizing beamsplitting cubes and half wave plates, laminating photopolymers, casting chocolate holograms, and more! Plus there will be equipment and recording materials for sale, along with holograms!

Also included in the price of the conference is a set of Proceedings from previous ISDH's, 1982, 1985, 1988, 2009, all invaluable reading, and the accompanying art exhibition catalogs, plus 3 square meals a days and open bar during the evening sessions. Dorm rooms are available for $35/night/person. So if you or anyone needs a crash course in holography, this is it, like a graduate school colloquium, much more worthwhile than a virtual Zoom class!

Check it out at [https://isdh2024.org](https://isdh2024.org)
