Joe Farina Mon Apr 30, 2007 1:41 pm #2
Welcome to the forum. I hope you make rapid progress with MBDCG. I wouldn't use gylcerin instead of TMG. Instead I would follow Jeff Blyth's formula as closely as possible. As an important supplement to this, I would review the information on this forum with regards to MBDCG, especially the comments by Cristiano, who has shown how spectacular these holograms can be (using only modest HeNe power).

If the problem is acquiring TMG (and other hard-to-find chemicals), I will tell you how to do this (since you're from New Jersey, and I'm from Michigan). First, form your own company, and pick a name for it. (In other words, just choose a name like XYZ Holography or whatever.) Then go to the bank and open a business checking account. (Admittedly, this will involve a service fee per month, maybe around $10 in my neighborhood. But with DCG you've got to get serious, and this $10 really is inconsequential.) Then, call your potential supplier (always project a professional manner over the phone) and place your order. Then, pay by company check. It should be as simple as that.

> RaulBSP: I think that using new more available and cheap powerful lasers, we can overcome the lack of sensitivity. I think nowdays it is much easier to get a DVD writer laser than to acquire TMG. Only rest to solve laser stability questions at high powers.

That raises some interesting questions, Raul. If you remember the thread below concerning DCG sensitivity at 405nm, I would guess that it's highly sensitive, perhaps far more sensitive than John suggested.

I have a Lake Forest tutorial-manual on DCG by Rallison, and he makes the following comment:

"DCG is most sensitive in a hot moist environment. At 50% RH and 68 degrees F it may required 60 mj/cmcm of 488 nm light, while at 75% RH and 80 F 4mj/cm will do the same job. At 441 nm less than 1 mj is enough and at 514 or 532, 50 to 100 may be necessary."

The thing that catches my attention is that only 1 mj is needed at 441nm. If you look at the curve on Shankoff's paper, the absorption is huge at 405 as compared to 441. The difference between 633 and 441 is only about half* the difference between 441 and 405!

I'm not 100% sure how sensitivity relates to absorption, but I think there is a strong correlation between the two. Because of this, just plain DCG might have incredible sensitivity at 405nm.

Now the problem becomes the stability of the 405nm laser diodes (they seem to be easily/cheaply available now). But is there a possibility of using short-coherence length lasers for contact copies? I've never made a contact copy in my life, so I don't know. I recall reading somewhere that a short-coherence length laser can be used for contact copies, but I really don't know. But if it can, maybe silver masters can be made and contact copied on DCG using a 405nm diode. I guess the wavelength would still need need to be shifted upward, or maybe broadened.

Just some idle speculation to keep the conversation going....
