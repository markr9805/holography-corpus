TEA etc.

I also used to have considerable trouble squeegeing a TEA treated emulsion uniformly. For us the absolutely best way to achieve a nice pre-swelled layer proved to be like this: insert the plates in a rather large tray filled with TEA/water + some drops of Photoflo or alike. Still in that bath, slowly tilt the plate into a vertical position (avoiding bubbles). Pull it out and gently remove the liquid on the top etch and the glass side. You then put the plate on a piece of paper against a wall at say a 80° angle and let it air dry.

Slightly modified, that method equally worked for film material (Agfa). The Slavich materials might be a bit trickier to dry though - since they used to be coated on thinner carriers.

To speed up the whole thing you might consider adding ethanol to the solution (in which case the fastest way to get the emulsion to equilibrium was to soak it in plain water prior to the TEA/water/alcohol bath). And of course there is always the option of using a fan etc.

Has anyone tried this method of post TEA bath drying? I would of thought that this technique would leave streaks and drips of uneven swelling as the plate dried. More TEA would be concentrated near the bottom. I also see it would be important to dried the top edge to stop water from dripping down. Maybe I'll give it a go. Worth a try!
