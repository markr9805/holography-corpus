Colin Kaminski Wed Jan 02, 2008 4:50 am #1
I was reading about food science and I read a very interesting discussion about using gelatin for mousse. For those who have not had mousse it is based on making egg whites hold air by beating it. The whites polymerise and make a matrix that can trap air.

It turns out that you can make mousse with gelatin for the same reason! While I am not advocating the use of gelatin in mousse, as I think mousse should be lighter, anytime you trap air bubbles you will create a milky look. Perhaps this is one of the causes of milkiness in DCG recipies? This is a good argument to make sure your stir plate does not creat a vortex that sucks in air.

Any thoughts?
JohnFP Wed Jan 02, 2008 12:26 pm #2
It's a good thought but has merrit elsewhere then what you state. In mixing the gelatin, when it is finally disolved and filtered there should be no air voids and no milkiness at all.

But when you process the gelatin, you actually want air voids. It is how these voids are created that will allow you to have a clear or milky final hologram.

But back to mixing, I have my stirrer/heater on almost full spin with the votex thingy going on when the water is at room temp and I am adding my gelatin slowly. After all the gelatin is added I then lower the spin rate to a very moderate rotation but the gelatin thickens, and thus this needs to be monitored and altered as time passes. After the gelatin has reached 45C then the rotation can be slowed to just a slow mix. After filtering, the stirrer should again be adjusted to insure just a slow mix and no introduction of bubbles.
