JohnFP Thu Nov 23, 2006 2:17 pm #2
Welcome u01anm3.

> could it be something to do with one wavelength reaching the holographic plate early?

No.

If it is a narrow band reflection hologram then the fringes will only reflect a narrow set of wavelengths, ie. a narrow band green reflection hologram will only reflect wavelengths 500nm - 530nm. Thus if the hologram is illuminated with 488nm the light simply passes through the hologram, not reflecting the image.

With broadband or full color holography film as long as both sets of fringes exist (those that reflect blue and green) then you could get a hologram with each wavelength.

For transmission holograms I think Kaveh's explaination and visuals are the best at explaining this.
