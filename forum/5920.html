Colin Kaminski Thu Dec 14, 2006 6:07 pm #3
I have built three 3-D pantographs. All of wich have been in use for more than 15 years. Reducing requires the pantograph to be very stiff. I will look for a photo of one. They were mostly designed to make archtop guitars but I have used them for volin and cello tops and backs as well.

For your project I would use a pivioting carridge on linear bearings with a laminate trimmer as the cutter. Make sure the model is made from a isotropic material. Wood grain is problematic. There will be a fair amount of sanding involved and this is not an easy project. For me I would find it easier to simply carve one by using a sculpture textbook.

Cost of my pantographs was abot $1500 in parts.
Joe Farina Thu Dec 14, 2006 7:30 pm #4
I suggest using a different approach.

For reference material, take a number of photos of your head. Put your camera on a tripod, stand in one spot, then take as many photos as possible, gradually rotating the camera around your head (in the horizonatal plane) to get the different views. (Take some from other angles also.) It would be easiest to have another person take these photos, so you could stay relatively motionless as the photos are taken. Or perhaps you could rotate yourself slightly, and use the time-delay feature to make a series of photos, if no one is available to help.

Then find a sculpture material you are comfortable with, and make your sculpture using the photos for reference. Additionally, you could use a mirror if a question arises which is not answered by the photos. The photos can be squared-off to judge the proportions of your sculpture, and perhaps a piece of glass could be squared-off as well, and you could look at your sculpture through this squared-off transparency as you work, comparing this to the photos. Decrease the size of the squares on the transparency to make any size reduction you wish.
Gary Deal Fri Dec 15, 2006 5:16 am #5
Rick, you're missing an axis. You've basically drawn an artist's pantograph, which is fine for copying/scaling 2D art from one flat surface to another. You can pick those up cheaply on ebay. However, you want depth, so you need that third axis ("Z"). If you had a really ridgid 2D pantograph and could raise/lower both the master and the scaled copy separately in precise increments, it could be done that way. Finish work would be required. For a weird effect you could do 2D "layers" and cut them out and stack them.

There's industrial grade 3D pantographs out there that used to be the standard way of machining molds, dies, etc. They're a comparative bargain at todays prices of $1k - $3k. I'd like to have a Gorton P1-3 because it's designed to eliminate an inherent problem, but there's Alexanders, Deckels, and others. I own a Gorton P1-2 2D pantograph and did mil-spec engraving for eight years.

A simpler method would be to have someone who owns the right kind of machine and knows how to use it do the job, cutting a soft "engineering" type material like wax or "butterboard". I can ask around, but the shop time would probably run $150 or more.

Direct sculpting is a good option. There's also casting materials that are designed to shrink, the reduction involved would probably require several intermediate molds and castings but if you're handy you might be able to get through it for $100 or so. You could search for info on the "casting" list at yahoo groups, I've seen it mentioned there.

Of course, then there's 3D scanning and small cnc mills, but that's some money there too.

You've seen those little paperweight/display things with the 3D figures created inside with multi-axis lasers making a series of tiny bubbles? I've seen one that was a more or less instant 3D portrait, of a friend's daughter. A very good likeness, laser scanned and then burned into the block. I'll see if I can find out the name of the company, one of those might be a good item to start with and then image plane into an H2 - your dot-matrix smiling face poking out of the image.

Colin, if you're reading this, you might want to check out the low-tech 3D duplicator at [http://www.copycarver.com](http://www.copycarver.com) I'd build one if I had something to duplicate with it.

-Gary

I had a portrait done by:

[http://www.crystallaserconcepts.com/](http://www.crystallaserconcepts.com/)

I believe their equipment was made by:

[http://www.seaena.com/](http://www.seaena.com/)

The technology is described here:

[http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_engr ... .28SSLE.29](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_engraving#Sub_Surface_Laser_Engraving_.28SSLE.29)

The camera used a scanning 532nm DPSS. It took about 2 seconds to scan a face. The resulting file was a contour plot of my daughter's face. The CO2 laser then cut the block of glass/crystal in contour patterns until you could see the image.

It was quite important that you stay still for the 2 seconds. It was difficult for my 2 month old but would be easy for an adult.
