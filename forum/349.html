Ed Wesly Mon Oct 10, 2011 10:52 am #3
Yes, I am confused as to whether you have 180 field of illumination or need it. If you have it, what more do you need?

If you need more, and have the holographic light shaping diffusers, try putting some holographic diffraction gratings before or after them. That out to spread the beam out more. Or the lenticular screens that go on 3-D postcards, etc. A pair of those would give some wide spread. Or gang up some lenses before your diffuser.
Ed Wesly Wed Oct 12, 2011 6:42 am #4
Another one came to me - if you have a wide angle lens for a camera, like a 21 mm of less for your 35 mm film one, you could put an expanded laser beam into the exit pupil (the side that would be toward the film plane) and see if you could get 90 degrees or more coverage. If you had a 180 degree fisheye lens, you might be able to get that kind of a spread coming out of it!
Johnfp Wed Oct 12, 2011 8:05 am #5
John, cant you use a beam splitter then use two of those fiber optics to get the whole 180 degree of lighting?
John Klayer Wed Oct 12, 2011 8:48 pm #6
Thanks guys for your suggestions. The reason I want 180 degree illumination is because I'm doing overhead reference transmission holograms in caves. I want the finished hologram to have the full 180 degrees of viewing. Some of the holos I shot so far do so, the closer to the finished holo you get the more panarama you see. The best results I have so far came from using two 80 degree light shaping diffusers (engineered diffusers) spaced a small distance apart which gives about 110 degrees for object lighting. The multimode fiber provides the reference light and the near plate fill in object light, meaning that the reference cone is much wider than needed for the plate so the rest of it lights up everything else near the plate.

I'm kind of pleased with what I have so far -BUT- the holos are somewhat grainy. I'm thinking of substituting a space filter and a fast convex mirror for the fiber and some other way to spread the object lighting around near 180 degrees. I'm going to buy some holographic diffusers, but not sure if they are any better than the "engineered diffusers" or if they are both the same. I thought about a fish eye lens too but haven't tried. JohnFP, the idea about using a beamsplitter and two fibers I like, I may try it.
