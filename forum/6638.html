Hans Sat Jan 05, 2008 8:27 pm #2
I use yellow and red Philips Deco Leds. They are quite narrow band. Wavelength is outside the sensitivity curve.

You can use the yellow ones for panchromatic film as well. They come in versions that allow them to be used as replacement bulbs for normal fixtures.
Joe Farina Sat Jan 05, 2008 8:47 pm #3
I use John's bug lights for DCG, I have several in my lab, in clamp-on reflectors. One good thing about DCG as opposed to silver is that you can have a lot more light in the lab. These look like normal 60-watt light bulbs, but are yellowish. I forget why they call them 'bug lights" but that's how they are labeled, and how they are known (these are made by Sylvania and are easily available in the US).

JohnFP Mon Jan 07, 2008 12:20 pm #5
For green sensitive silve halide I use a few of those red LED keychain lights. I think they were about $1 US each at K-Mart.

Insects (bugs) mostly navigate and see in the blue and UV. Thus this "Bug Light" is sold so that you can put it outside and bugs are not attracted to it, so they say. But I have viewed these bug lights through a grating and for sure it does have some green and blue but a much smaller amount then a standard light bulb. The great thing about DCG, is it likes to be hardened a little and light does just that. Because it is so dumb to light longer then green, there is minimal effect from a bug light. I wouldn't leave it out in a bug light for a few days but surely a couple of hours has minimal effect on it.
