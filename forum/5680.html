Tom B. Wed Jul 12, 2006 4:59 am #4
I assume you are using a rehalogenating bleach such as ferric EDTA? Anyway, a treatment which greatly reduces printout is a final rinse in a mild (1%) acetic acid bath before squeegeeing and drying. This leaves a slightly acid environment which inhibits redevelopment, and someone also claimed that it dissolved the sensitizing dyes (not sure about that).

Cheap white vinegar is totally inorganic - it is 4-5% pure acetic acid (derived from acetylene gas) and can be used when diluted down to 1%. The vinegar makes squeegeeing more difficult (the rubber blade doesn't slide smoothly and squeaks) but don't use a wetting agent like PhotoFlow - it has been implicated in printout and doesn't work with vinegar anyway.

It's been a long time since I did sunlight tests - why not try the treatment on half of a plate, let it sit in direct sun for a few hours and report back on the results?
Steve M Fri Nov 24, 2006 1:07 pm #5
I had this same problem many years ago. Re-bleaching did remove the problem, but it just occurred again. My solution was to use a stop bath between the developer and bleach. You can go directly from the developer to the stop bath without washing and keep it in the bath for 2 minutes with agitation. I use Kodak's Indicator Stop bath with a 32:1 ratio of distilled water to stop bath solution (mixing directions are on the stop bath container). After the stop bath, I wash in running water for 5 minutes, then bleach. This approach essentially gives the hologram archival properties. Since using this approach, I've never had a hologram of any type of emulsion or developed in any type of developer turn brown on me again, even in bright sunlight.
