Hi John,

I think you may possibly have badly fogged plates for reasons quite different from normal photographic aging which with our ultra fine grain stuff means reduced photosensitivity (requiring a bit of aqueous TEA solution or vitramin C/sodium carbonate at pH 6-7 or even very dilute standard proprietary developer to reactivate them.) rather than chemical fogging. Somewhile ago many of us observed this fogging in Slavich plates and I have been told it was due to the wrapping paper they used which was giving off traces of formaldehyde. (I am sure this was discussed in Colin's earlier forum).

From experience with recovering fogged Agfa plates I found that

20 grams of ferric EDTA salt with 10g pot bromide per litre worked to clear out the latent silver nuclei but the time spent in this very mild rehal bleach was important. Too short and it was still fogging and too long and grain growth started to spoil diffractive potential. What was essential though was that after washing out the bleach you needed to reactivate the plate with the solutions given in the above paragraph ie. mild reducing solution to compensate for the mildly oxidizing treatment you had to give it. I was amazed to find that the plates were then almost but not quite as good as new. (Don't try using dichromate for the oxidizing solution though, or the photosensitivity can remain zilch).

Jeff
Ed Wesly Wed May 11, 2005 11:29 am #3
It wouldn't be a reversal bleach, but a rehalogenating bleach. I discovered this by accident (and I am sure many others) by getting mixed up in the dark. Just use your favorite, PBQ, Fe EDTA, Copper Sulfate, etc, in the dark of course, a nice rinse, photo-flo, and dry in the dark. Don't use anything dichromate based, as it ruins the sensitizing dye.

More details in "Recycling Holographic Plates", Proceedings of the Third International Symposium on Display Holography, Lake Forest College, 1988.

I think since John W. described the fogging of the unexposed Slavich plates as going black in the dev. it sounds severe. So I think my suggested formula :

"20 grams of ferric EDTA salt with 10g pot bromide per litre "

Should be 40g ferric EDTA sodium salt and 20g KBr. Probably CuSO4 should be at the same level. (If you have the ferric EDTA compound it would be much more preferable I think because the vitally important sensitizing dyes might form copper complexes whereas the iron salt is already complexed up completely, there is also the possible formation of low refractive index Cu(1)Br particles) .

(A fascinating fact [to me] about both these oxidants that I discovered is that without any bromide ion present neither of them are strong enough to oxidize silver metal nuclei/grains. Pure quite concentrated solutions will not bleach your holograms if they are left in for a year and a day!

The reason for this must be that the energy that comes from the formation of the AgBr nano-crystals, 'lattice energy' is vital to tip the balance. ie. no net energy comes from forming soluble Ag

sulfate or the EDTA silver salt. Not even 10g potassium chloride instead of the bromide work OK I found because the AgCl is about 10 times more soluble than AgBr so lattice energy is lower. If you double or quadruple the Br level the bleaching rate goes up hugely , particularly with CuSO4)
