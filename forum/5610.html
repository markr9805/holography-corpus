I wrote the original formula out for BB640 but that was for the times when

red lasers where the norm and green ones were comparatively rare.

Things are now rather different and I realized while I was adding to Colin's Holowiki that I should get stuck into getting a good system to give no shrinkage for Denisyuks shot with green and also for distortion free transmissions . I used to advocate using Copper sulfate/KBr for bleaching

because it gave a longer replay wavelength. Martin and I had a discussion on the forum a while back on why this longer wavelength occurred , (I will dig the old literature out that Martin found later).

It was to do with the co-deposition of cuprous bromide CuBr along with the AgBr. (CuBr is mono-valent form of Cu closely resembling Ag unlike the common usually very soluble divalent Cu salts) .

However I have now gone off using copper salts because I have now definite proof that the resulting diffraction efficiency is markedly less than you can get from the ferric edta bleach such as "60KB" on the Holowiki

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... ing_Bleach](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Rehalogenating_Bleach)

Ok, I took the easy way out. I used some JD2 developer and JD2 bleach that I had that was only a couple of months old (I figured what the heck, I might as well try it, it's paid for).

I am very pleased with the results. I sensitized the BB520 plate with just water and a few drops of Cascade Crystal Clear dishwasher additive. I then squeegied the plate and dryed the back with a hair drier very low and far away for just a minute or so. I then put the plate in the plate holder and allowed it to finish drying while settling, about 20 minutes. I did three test exposures masking 2/3 of the plate each time and exposed for 120uJ, 240uJ and 360uJ (2, 4 and 6 seconds with the laser power cut way way down to 1.5 on the scale knob of 10) The recommendation was between 150 and 300uJ. ( Jeff, I love that meter ) I then mixed A and B JD2 and developed for one minute (as suggested for their Vitamin C developer). Then rinse in running water for 30 seconds, then bleach in JD2 bleach until clear. I then squeegied both side and let air dry. I placed the plate in the plateholder and I am very pleased with the results.

No halo or noise, very bright and the brightest real image projected on a white card seems to be very nearly exactly the same angle as the recording angle.

So either this emulsion is very easy to work with and very forgiving or I got real real lucky. I like it.....

Additional notes:

I had to lower the power of my laser because I never had to use exposures in the low single digits and I am not sure I can be accurate down at the 1 second regime due to mechanical shutter and software.

I don't like the fact of having to use such a dim safe light. I will need to invest in some red Neon lights (hehe) for the lab if I continue this. I am spoiled by the amount of light I can use with DCD. A 5 mW HeNe laser in the table room and a 10mW HeNe in the processing lab is pretty dim even when expanded. (I know it's a shame I am using them for safe lights instead of making holograms)

I do like the fact of not having to make plates (2 hours) then waiting 4 hours before I can make a hologram (6 hours total minimum)

I don't like the cost of silver plates and if I decide to continue with silver masters I may need to fly Michael out here to show me how to work with film instead of plates (kidding). Index matching etc...

I know I will not like doing 4x5 copies from 4x 5 master so I would have to increase the size of the masters.

I like the ease of processing.

I like the fact the plate does not need to be as protected as DCG.

Averall I am very pleased with the BB plates. I got geat resuls with JD2 on my first attempts. I guess the proof in the pudding will be the DCG copies from these plates but the real image looks great!

I want to thank you all for you input giving me the courage to try what I had and saving me $100.

Uli Thu Jun 08, 2006 9:40 am #18
Hi John

The only thing is I can add is that for many years I was working with these emulsions, and I used a variety of developers, all based on vitamin C. They all had sodium carbonate for pH adjustment. The simplest one, with Ascorbic Acid (18 g/l) and sodium carbonate (60 g/l) worked very well, and adding Methol, phenidone, cathecol, or a combination of them did not increased the DE or the sensitivity in a significant amount. Pyro has the problem of tanning the emulsion, which looks like a good thing for decreasing the (already low) scatter levels in reflection, but it will also reduce the brightness of the image when you use it for transmission masters. Although with a powerful green laser that is not a big problem javascript:emoticon(':)')

Smile

Another think you can try is reversal R9 bleach (Pot. Dichromate 2g/l + Sulphuric acid 10cc/l). I got my brightest gratings with that, crystal clear (you are just leaving original unexposed grains in the plate), but I was doing 1000 l/mm symmetric – non slanted gratings, which means that, since I was recording interference planes perpendicularly to the plate, there is no effect if you shrink the emulsion. In a non-symmetric setup, as the one you are using for your masters, you will notice an angle shift, but I do not think it to be large, given the spatial frequencies you use for mastering, and the thickness of the emulsion.

Ed Wesly’s suggestion for Sodium Bisulphate (Sodium Hydrogen Sulphate) is a good suggestion. I was using it for years. The good thing about it is that you can keep it for months. I personally do not like PBQ. It is a nasty chemical, and last time I used it was more than 15 years ago.

Water for presensitising is ok. TEA will make the plate about 1.5 times more sensitive, but since you have power for DCG, you have more than enough for this emulsion with water. TEA will also pre-swell the emulsion, so more shrinkage after processing, and a bit extra angle shift.

I really think that this emulsion is very easy to work with. If you are regularly working with DCG, I do not think the good result you have got is just luck.
