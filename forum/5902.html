MichaelH Sun Dec 03, 2006 10:55 pm #2
Motive?

Shine your laser on the gelatin and see what happens. Also use some polarizing film (sunglasses work fine) to see what it does to the polarization.

The only real problem I can think of that's all that likely is movement in the gelatin due to evaporation but if you cover it that should be minimized.

Give it a shot, the results could be interesting.

(btw, if the goal is things flying in space you could achieve similar results by supporting the objects from behind)

You could do quite well by using a casting epoxy. I don't think it will change the polarizations but you will want to test it in between crossed polarizers.

I have had very interesting results making a wavy appearence by passing the reference beam through a wavy optic before mastering.
