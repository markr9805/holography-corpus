Hans Wed May 23, 2007 8:21 pm #1
Thickness of gelatin can be measured with a very simple spectrometer (the $15 dollar mail order variant). The problem with using this method is that the index of refraction of gelatin is nearly the same as that of normal glass.

In order to measure the thickness of a flow coated gelatin layer, I coated a first surface mirror with gelatin. Now the dark lines can easily be seen with the spectrometer and the lines can easily be counted. Very cheap and suitable first-surface mirrors can be found in the USA at car-parts stores. They are sold as replacement side-view mirrors for trucks and cars. If you buy one, make sure that it is not the wide-view curved variant.

In order to do the test, look at the reflection of a single halogen lamp, with the lamp a few meters away from the mirror. Look into the reflection of this lamp with the spectrometer. You will see a number of dark lines throughout the visible spectrum. Count the number of black lines between two known wavelengths. Here is the formula that gives the thickness of the gelatin:

t= mL1L2/(2n(L2-L1));

m = number of black lines seen between known wavelengths L2 and L1.

n = index of refraction of gelatin. About 1.55

If you count 7 black lines between 500nm and 600nm, the gelatin is 7um thick.

If the black lines are very sharp and contrasty, the gelatin is smooth. If the lines are vague, the thickness varies.

Also, a simple visual test can be done. Because you have such good reflection of both the gelatin and off the first surface mirror, you can easily see the interference patterns when you look at the reflection of a diffuse light source. This time without a first surface mirror. Each time you see the rainbow pattern make a complete cycle and become sort of only purple rainbows, the thickness has varied over one complete visual spectrum. If the rainbow reverses orientation, the gelatin is back at its previous thickness.

Hans Tue Jun 05, 2007 7:55 pm #8
The procedure to count the amount of black lines through the spectroscope is a little cumbersome because they move as you move the spectrometer. And taking a photo of them is even more difficult as it is difficult to find the correct focus.

Luckily you only have measure only at one spot on the plate (=front surfaced mirror). You can then simply visually inspect how the thickness varies across the plate. Just look at the reflection of a diffuse object.

Here's how to do it:

![Image](http://hologramhans.com/pictures/NewtonRings.jpg)

Suppose that the thickness measured at spot 1 was 8um. As you can see the colours shift to blue as you move your eye towards spot 2 (red lines to the right of the green lines). This means that the gelatin becomes thinner here. But from spot 2 to spot three, the fringes revert back to green again (red lines left of the green lines). You can see that the gelatin becomes thicker again because the red lines point towards spot 2, just as they did in spot 1.

Towards spot 3 the plate becomes thicker again and after spot 3 thinner again, etc...

If the orientation of the red versus the green bands is different: I.e. before and after a series of blue lines, the red lines are both to the right of the green lines. This means that the thickness continue to change towards a bigger or smaller value.

This plate was puddle coated. In the vertical direction, the thickness hardly varies at all. I guess this means that the plate was not perfectly horizontal when I poured the gelatin.
