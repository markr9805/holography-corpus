My understanding is that a circular photographic polariser, the kind that you fit over the lens in a camera, allows light that's polarised along a particular direction through and block all other light. The axis of polarisation blocked can be varied by rotating the polariser. This is not the same as a half wave plate which alters the polarisation rather than block polarisation components.. A half wave plate alters plane polarised light by twice the angle of the optic axis. So If horizontal light hit a vertically orientated photographic polariser, no light would come through. If this same light hit a half wave plate whose optic axis was orientated at 45 degrees, the half wave plate would alter the polarisation of the light so as to make it vertical.
