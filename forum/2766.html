JohnFP Wed Oct 06, 2004 4:18 pm #1
If a laser puts out not a single frequency but multiple frequencies (linewidth) then are the fringes of the peak frequency modulated to carry those other frequencies. In other words is there more then one hologram being made in the emulsion? It seems most technical discussion assumes we are talking about a single frequency not a range of frequencies together (linewidth). As from my example of interfering two different wavelengths, 514 adn 488, I can tell that the side frequencies when interfering with the peak frequency actually makes fringes that are very far apart and dim thus not effecting the hologram that much. But it is something to ponder about and thus reinforces the statement that two differnt wavelengths will interfer.
Tony Wed Oct 06, 2004 7:54 pm #2
I think the answer is yes, you are making a hologram at more than one wavelength, but the wavelengths are so close together it doesn't matter much.

If my math is correct, the frequency of a 633nm Hene is 473 THz. That's 473,000,000 MHz. Stabilized Hene's can have 1 MHz frequency stability, common Hene's have mode spacing of ~300 MHz. So the variation in frequency is relatively minute. Hence the variation in fringe spacing across the bandwidth of the laser is also minute - 1 ppm or so.
