JohnFP Tue Jan 22, 2008 4:04 pm #3
Seems you have a manual embossing press. You need the holographic shim, which may or may not be included with your press and some mylar. The shim has the groves of the hologram in it and the mylar is the "plastic" that you will stamp the groves of the shim into.
JohnFP Wed Jan 23, 2008 5:08 pm #4
Sorry, more specific to you question,

You need mylar that has a foil or reflective backing. You will press the groves into the mylar side.

Do a Google search for "Mylar" and you should be able to find some to try. You could also try as a test to see if the die in your press is actually a holographic "shim" die is to try to press into some clear mylar. Then view the pressed sheet as a transmission hologram with the light source on the opposite side of the sheet then your viewing side. The reflective side it so the transmission hologram can be viewed with the light source on the same side you are viewing. The light goes though the transmisison hologram, does not reflect a hologram, the bounces off the reflective coating and hits the hologram from the opposite side simulating the light source coming from the opposite side and the hologram is created. You could take that clear piece of stamped mylar and place it on a mirror and then view it as a reflection hologram to simulate the effect.

Then of couse if you wanted to apply the final hologram to another surface, you need to get a reflective backing mylar with an adhesive side, the adhesive being on the reflective side.

I am currious, does this press have to be plugged in for heat? If not, you may have to experiment with preheating the mylar sheet prior to pressing. Go low first. To high and the mylar might melt to your holographic shim. When we built our embosser we had to control heat as well as pressure.

If it does have a heater, then again start with low heat and low pressure and work your way up slowly with both.
