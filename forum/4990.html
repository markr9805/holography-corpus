To combine several laser beams with different colors you can use dichroic filters. Is it possible to use a prism instead? Any obstacles with this idea?

![Image](http://www.eyedream.se/bilder/holografi/prism.gif)
Dinesh Sun Aug 21, 2005 11:09 pm #2
Theoretically, I can't see any objections. Practically, it may be difficult to get a pure white beam because the beams have a bandwidth. This would cause the beams to spread out slightly at the exit. If a pure, collimated 514 (say), 488 and 633 were launched into the prism at exact angles, each would come out at a specific angle that could be calculated and mix. However, a "514" beam is really 514 +/- <bandwidth>. Since the deviation at each face is a sinusoidal function of the input angle and the index varies linearly with wavelength it would come out slightly divergent. Same for the other two beams. It may be possible to shape the wavefronts just so that the phases of the varioous components going at different speeds "catch up" causing the wavefront to flatten.

Ed Wesly Mon Aug 22, 2005 2:04 am #4
The big problem with this scheme is that the beams will not be exiting the prism with circular profiles, but will become ellipses.

I did a similar thing with a diffraction grating, it was mentioned in a paper called "HOE for Holography" in a SPIE Practical Holography paper co-authored with TJ. The "white" beam came out along the normal, and the RGB's were incident at the appropriate angle. But the output was elliptical. (A photo of this set up graced the cover of Laser Focus World.)

Usually prisms are used to circularize elliptical beams. See Optics Guide by Melles Griot in their diode laser section. Here the situation is reversed.
