Colin Kaminski Thu Jan 25, 2007 11:28 pm #2
When I was doing a lot of 2.5" by 2.5" holograms I had some small round developing trays. They had a dimple in the center. I would often forget which side should be up and wear a hole in the gelatin by rubbing it on the dimple. The developing station was only 2 feet from the plate holder. You think I could keep it straight for 5 seconds...

Not really a mistake so much as a gotcha. While viewing a transmission hologram i realized that one of the objects needed to be moved slightly; so i reached around the plate and found myself plucking at thin air. Doh!!! The really sad part is that it has happened more than once.
BobH Fri Jan 26, 2007 4:01 am #4
... like the time I poked a 12x16" transmission hologram out of its plateholder, on the way toward adjusting the object beyond it.

I used to place an 18" long half inch diameter post on the edge of my table near the plateholder, to protect critically placed cards from being bumped in the dark. One time, I was setting up for a transmission hologram and thinking only about how I was going to light the sideways mounted object. Without thinking, I bent over to align my head with the object and slammed me skull right into that freaking post!

My full weight was behind it. It hit right on the boney side of my right eye socket and I lucked out. I've always thought what a grisley sight I would've made had it been a few millimeters more.
Colin Kaminski Fri Jan 26, 2007 4:27 am #5
Before I had any experience with lasers over 25 mw or so I was invited to visit a lab. I was asked to help with a H1 transmission mastering setup and without thinking I put my head in the plane of the beam and started aligning optics with a 1 watt beam.

Fortunately the person helping me dropped a card into the beam quickly and gave me a lesson on how to aim optics.

Moral: Don't look into beam with remainng eye.

Hi there,

my recent holo mistake: I take a PFG Plate out of the box, expose it and develop. I wonder, why I dont get any darkening of the plate, but some kind of strange pattern. So, I continue to wash, bleach, and when I finally turn on the light, I see that I used an already developed holo! And the labeling with black pen had partly dissolved in the developer.

After one PFG-box was empty, I would put the final holos into this box, just mixed it up in the dark. Fortunately it wasnt a good holo.

jean Mon Jan 29, 2007 10:50 am #9
I made some tests of large MBDCG plates (8*10), mold coating.

One night, I've settled, exposed (more than 90 minutes), warm hardened, rinsed, processed a very nice and clean uncoated plate of glass I used as mold (rain-x treatment).

An other one, I've prepared a mbdcg batch and I added some drops of agepon (photoflo) as last step to help curtain coating. Well... it was not agepon, it was fixer (same bottle design). Of course, I coated 10 plates before see what was wrong (methylen blue turned green)
