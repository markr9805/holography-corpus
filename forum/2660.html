> BobH: I think The whole "reference beam at Brewster's angle" is waay overplayed as well. It's academically true, but it presumes collimated reference beams. This is OK for the master, but the reference beam for an "H2" transfer should be convergent.Nobody will ever reconstruct the transfer with collimated light, and the distortions caused by improper reconstruction is NOT acceptable (contrary to popular belief), unless the composition is done to accomodate the effect. Although the optics to converge the reference beam for an "H2" are a hassle to procure (or make), they are necessary to make images that look real. This is one of the reasons Dennisyuk recordings look so much more "real" than image-planed holograms. Subtle, but significant.

> Dinesh: Abso-o-l-u-tely agree about the Brestwer's angle thing!This is OK for the master, but the reference beam for an "H2" transfer should be convergent.Even for a master, there's a feeling that you've "got to have a collimated reference". Not true, all you need is the conjugate of the original H1 reference. The only reason a collimated ref is used is because you don't need to create a conjugate when you flip the plate. The conjugate of a collimated beam is also a collimated beam. In fact, you can use this to create a ref for H2 by bleeding off the H1 recon. If you make a divergent H1 ref, you need to converge the H1 recon. You can bleed off a little of this, since the H2 is smaller than the H1 anyway, and divert it for the H2 ref. Assuming, of course, you have the coherence.What would you suggest?Index matching. I hate the stuff!

This is one of the reasons Dennisyuk recordings look so much more "real" than image-planed holograms. Subtle, but significant.It has an effect, but not nearly as much as the H1. It's the H1 that has to be absolutely spot on. The size distortions and aberrations in an image are in general proportional to the distance of image points from the hologram plane. In an H1, the distance is large. In the H2, the points are usually much less. So changing the reconstruction beam from a diverging to converging or vice versa will not have a huge effect. In the case of very shallow image-planed images, you will see no difference.

I think the practice of squeezing the depth of image possible with holography out is partly to blame for the demise of pictorial holography thus far. It's taken to the extreme by the embossed holography industry, and "dot-matrix" imagery essentially threw the baby out with the bath water. The true magic of the technique is full parallax, "window with a memory", ultra-realism. It's unfortunate, but white light point sources (and even tri-chromatic point sources) are still way too expensive for pictorial holography to fulfill the promise in the real world. All the compromises; including the image-planing technique, monochromatic exposure, horizontal parallax only, aberrated imagery and reconstruction by extended sources, while bringing holography into the public eye (mostly in the '80s) have whittled the magic away.
