JohnFP Fri Feb 16, 2007 3:26 pm #5
Antony, yes you can use lower wavelengths from an argon laser with an offset that exposure times will be longer for resist.

Not really, the silverizing I used to do was an atomizing 2 part technique. The technique is used for resilvering mirrors.

[http://ourhouse.ninemsn.com.au/ourhouse ... 09/984.asp](http://ourhouse.ninemsn.com.au/ourhouse/factsheets/db/intheworkshop/09/984.asp)

You simply had a air sprayer that had two bottles and the sprayer mixed the two parts in the nossle as it was being sprayed.

Usually the nickle plating process is done at least twice. Once for mother shims (you only get one from one resist plate) and a few times after that in creating child shims (you can get a few child shims from a single mother shim). This allows multiple shims to be had from a single hologram thus increasing the total number of embossed holograms (the shims wear out).

That's the way I do it, with a silvering kit from Peacock Labs. The kit contains four bottles of liquid chemicals. First one tinns the surface, second is mixed with the third, then the resulting mixture is poured over the plate as the deposition happens. The final step can happen in a tray or in a spray gun.

Ron Michael Sat Feb 17, 2007 6:30 am #9
Not sure if this helps you:

Diffraction Gratings I help setup where done directly by using the 351nm beamline from an argon 7watt laser. It yielded single frequency 300mw at 351nm. The setup has two 45 beam steering mirrors and 1 spatial filter toward a 6 inch spherical mirror that reflects to a 10 inch lloyd's mirror (long mirror from a miltary periscope) on a rotation stage to vary the grating period. The grating to be made was mounted normal to the lloyds mirror (black mount next to the mirror). Exposure given to photoresist spin coated plate, etch developed, then finish coated. This setup produced high quality gratings but unfortunately, SP charged 42K for the beamlok laser alone.

I always felt it could be done cheaper than this. I wanted to use either a 2nd harmonic ruby laser or 3rd harmonic yag without table but there would have been other issues. So this cw setup was actually easier and better quality without all the high energy issues.

I found this link on a 351nm laser for sale, I'm sure you could find better.

Ron Michael Sun Feb 18, 2007 4:30 pm #11
No, it is a table I help setup at a university. As a consultant, I helped the grad engineering students with their projects. I spec'd and ordered the 351nm laser, procured the parts, and helped them setup the table. Since the lab had other opto work going on, we took an abandoned room and setup for this specific project. I designed and built their spatial filter and helped them troubleshoot their grating imaging issues. For example, I setup an interferometer so they would have real time data on the table during exposures. Adjusted their setup so they get good even illumination (SF project a 12" beam dia but only use the center 6") and examining their grating results and making adjustments for them. They did the work of grating making and operation.

The professors told me they wanted a simple setup that the students could maintain and operate, so the lloyd's mirror was chosen over a two beam approach that would have given them more capability in different types of gratings.
