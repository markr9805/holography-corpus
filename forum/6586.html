JohnFP Mon Dec 10, 2007 6:17 pm #5
This may work for a hologram of a transparency but in thinking it through for a stereogram, it may not be advisable. The reason is, each of the slits will have a different object to reference ratio due to the nature of using a single beam to illuminate both. The center of the beam will have to be directed at the intersection of the prism and diffusing screen. Thus the light passing through the prism will be less instense at the apex.

If there was a lot of light to "waste" then the center of the beam could be directed at the center of the prism which would work toward a more even ratio from slit to slit. Then the overflow light could be used for the transparancy.
JohnFP Mon Dec 10, 2007 6:22 pm #6

JohnFP Wed Dec 12, 2007 11:50 am #8
Raul, you will want to try to achieve a collimated reference beam so that when you go to copy the master to an H2 it will be more practicle.

I also envision that if you send the object beam of the tansparancy through the prism at that angle you will get some distortion, thus the reason I thought it better to keep the object dead straight and parallel to the plate.

Yes, you can get make a large collimating lens to use as a collimator and I think Vidar has made a few and uses them. It would be quite difficult to make a round single large collimating lens but you could make two large cylindrical lenses and mount one standing up and one on it side to effectively produce the product of a spherical lens.

walschuler Thu Dec 20, 2007 6:11 am #10
Raul,

It occurs to me that you can play with the last setup you showed for the H1 to do two things:

1) build out the object beam side of the prism so that it is vertical (in your drawing) and you can again have your diffuser and transparency in contact with the prism,improving transmission.

2) Doing that likely means adjusting the apex angle of the object side of the prism to make the beam hit the plate appropriately. While thinking about that, it occurred to me that as the exiting object beam hits the rear face of the prism, if its angle of incidence approaches 45 degrees the internal reflection will rise as you approach the critical angle. So that angle will control not only the polarization of the transmitted beams but potentially the object/reference beam brightness ratio. Perhaps that is obvious?
