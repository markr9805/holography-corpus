I finally got around to doing it. Thank you Ed Wesly for the tips and the encouragement to try it.

[http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewt ... microscopy](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=3729&highlight=holographic+microscopy)

Split beam; First beam - to ant mounted on a pinhole next to hole in a spatial filter. Light was brought in from side. Light off ant went through objective backwards to plate. Second beam - overhead reference beam to plate. Exposure - 3 minutes. 4"x5"-DCG.

The first one I did I only used the objective for magnification and the viewing window was way to small, you can imagine I could bearly find the ant. This one I took a 4 inch lens and put it after the objective to give a much larger window.

Illunination light needs to come in from top right corner beause I had overhead reference beam and the magnified object beam came in from side.

I used the reflectiveness of the pinhole for silhouetting the ant.

Ed, I see exactly what you mean about how the focus gets stretched, excellent learning experience.

If I ever do it again I will put object beam on axis and try to paint the ant white..hahaha... Maybe I can find an albino ant. Overall it was very fun to do.

JohnFP Sat Oct 15, 2005 2:38 pm #4
Dave, yes, pretty tight. You can imagine a 4 inch diameter master to make a 4x5 inch plate about 8 - 10 inches away. I think I was too close to the plate when photgraphing. If I stand further back and view the hologram the background color is all one color not two as in the photo and it rainbows as you move from lower left to upper right (along axis of reference-object beam bisection).

I was also thinking on the next go around to try the 12" square fresnel lens or my 8" collimator in closer proximity. Not sure how all lens geometry would work out, that is if I can get the object back far enough to allow the focused image to be relatively close to the fl of the lens. The fresnel might work as it has a very short fl. Time to play with all kinds of lenses and mirros. The set backs are noise from the optics after the ojbect.

Also what I have not figured out yet is I think the image is being cast on the plate pseudoscopically. That is the head of the ant is closer to the object and further from the plate then the rest of the body. Thus if I do get a large enough lens window that allows more parallax, I may have to turn the ant upside down thus using a reference that comes in from the bottom and view the reflection hologram from the back side coming in from the top.
