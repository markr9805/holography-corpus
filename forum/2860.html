I have been making a bunch of H.O.E and gratings with small bits of VRP cut to fit between microscope slides. I have managed some nifty effects by playing with the geometry, but I notice that the effects are always across the plate horizontally. I am wondering how you could make these effects have depth, like looking at a rainbow extending back into the plate instead of across it. Any ideas?

Mark
JohnFP Mon Nov 01, 2004 2:37 pm #2
What is your desired effect. A rainbow for reflection/transmission display or an HOE application?

I am not sure if this is what you are looking for but what if you took a piece of frosted glass and place it where and how you wanted the light to smear, or rainbow.

That is make a transmission hologram of a piece of frosted glass laying at a 45 degree angle from plate to away from plate. When the final transmission is lit from behind with a white light source you will get the rainbow effect going back into the plate.
Dinesh Mon Nov 01, 2004 3:21 pm #3
Any of the "broken glass" methods will work. If you want to go into the z direction, get a solid, stiff plate like a piece of long metal. Shatter a piece of glass and "spray" the shattered pieces of glass along the plate. One way is to spray paint a piece of metal, about 2" x whatever your coherence length is, with a spray glue. Then sprinkle glass shards, metal dust, anything that glistens along the metal. Now place the metal plate at some angle, or even normal, and use a single expanded beam to illuminate the shards. The single beam will illuminate all the shards and each shard will act as a reference for all the others. You'll get a myriad of gratings gong into the plate. If you did a double exposure in the volume of the metal plate, the object will seem to have dancing colored lights inside it. Although to truly limit the dancing colors inside the object you'll need to have the glass shards or whatever only inside the object volume.

Another way: I remember some years back there was a toy that was basically a two part plastic. You embedded something inside the mould and added the two parts. After a while, you got a lump of plastic with something embedded inside it. If such a thing is still available, embedd tiny glistening objects inside a transparent plastic of, say a Buddha. Shoot it single beam. Spray the Buddha flat white and shoot again on same plate. Don't disturb the setup between the two shots. Now develop and hopefully you'll get a very colorful, if not enlightened, Buddha.
