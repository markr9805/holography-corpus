Hi,

Could someone please explain to me whether it is possible and if so how, to make a single beam reflection hologram that contains a left and right image – i.e. a 2 channel stereogram. So if you view from the left or right, then you see the left or right image accordingly, but if you view exactly straight on, then your left and right eyes see the different views – thus creating the 3d illusion.

Is this possible? Or am I over simplifying?

Thanks, Rick.

Tom B. Fri Jun 30, 2006 4:48 am #6
Yes it is possible - I have done so. The technique is described in Graham Saxby's book, unfortunately less clearly in the current edition than in the first. Essentially you first make a double exposure master from the two photos, masking off one half of the plate for the left eye photo and the other half for the right eye photo. Each photo is centrally placed about 250 mm from the plate for each exposure. Then make an image plane H2 transfer. When viewed, "the real images of the master apertures will be side by side, standing out in front of the hologram, and if you stand approximately in the plane of these images of the apertures you will see the two images autostereoscopically" (quote from Saxby, 1st edition p.247)
BobH Fri Jun 30, 2006 6:27 am #7
There are many many ways to make a split-beam 2-channel holographic stereogram, but the question was about a single beam 2-channel stereogram.

Here's another idea. Make a single beam reflection hologram of the left channel photograph, with the subject offset to the right. Make another single beam reflection hologram of the right channel photograph, with the subject offset to the left. Maximize the distance between the object photographs and the holograms.

Next, mount both holograms next to each other, and reconstruct their real images so that the subjects overlap properly. Finally, make a single beam reflection hologram at the plane of the overlapping photographs.
Tom B. Fri Jun 30, 2006 7:41 am #8
At the borderline between single beam and split beam techniques is the method dubbed by Saxby the "single beam bypass" method, which is what I used to make the reflection H1 master and the reflection H2. This uses one half of the disk of the spread beam for object lighting and the other half (with optional ND filter) for the reference beam. The beam is "split" by the setup geometry rather than optically by a beamsplitter.
