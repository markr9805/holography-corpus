Ok, so let' think about this. Sliced bread holograms show that the coherence length is exactly the distance between the fringes in the z direction. So if you had a laser of 1' coherence, you would find the image would be bright in the beginning, as you shift the difference in leg lengths the image gets dimmer until it goes black. If you keep shifting the leg lengths the image will get bright again! This is how sliced bread works. Sliced bread is simply a coherence length of about 1/4" or so.

Can't comment on sliced bread since I've never used a diode. I've always assumed it's along the x-y direction (the face of the hologram) and so it's a sort of beat frequency due to two distinct wavelengths from the laser. However, Colin's reply seems to indicate the slicing is in the z direction. If so, I accept Colin's explanation. However, focus has nothing to do with coherence.

The sharpness of a holographic image is a property of the reconstruction source and/or aberrations. If you're talking about a reflection hologram viewed in white light, it's the spread of the light you're using to view it. If you're reconstructing an H1 with laser light, it's aberrations - usually reconstruction by one wavelength of a hologram shot by another wavelength. This is because the the wavelength mismatch causes coma and/or astigmatism and so it's impossible to get a sharp edge focus. Coherence mismatch would result in a reduced fringe contrast. Since the efficency of a hologram depends on the fringe contrast, as the coherence length dropped, the fringe contrast would also drop and so you'd get dimmer images, all other things being equal. If this happens with H1's in diode lasers, it may well be because the diode is giving out a multiple of wavelengths. The multiple wavelengths would create multiple, slightly displaced, images causing a blurring when reconstructed. Also this reduces the spatial coherence of the laser source and so broadens the reconstruction source. Do you get blurred out of focus images when viewing an H1 with a diode that's been made with a gas laser ?
