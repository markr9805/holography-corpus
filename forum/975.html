Joe Farina Mon Dec 08, 2014 11:19 am #1
I have a 100X microscope objective (oil immersion type) and was doing some spatial filter tests. The first thing I noticed was the very high loss. Without even going through the pinhole, it only had about 33% transmittance of the laser beam. It is a rather old (but expensive) achromatic objective bought from Edmund.

I was wondering if others have seen such high losses with the 100X objectives. Some of these are designed for "oil immersion" meaning that the front end is immersed in a drop of oil, linking the end of the objective to the microscope slide. The output from my 100X objective is odd looking, consisting of many small spots or dots, rather than the swirls and patterns often seen in the output of other objectives.
Dinesh Mon Dec 08, 2014 1:03 pm #2
Yes, I can confirm. We have a couple of those objectives. The problem is that the higher the magnification, the closer is the focus to the end of the lens, and, the closer the focus, the greater is the heating effect of the laser on dust particles on the end of the lens. This causes the dust to "burn" into the lens face. There's more energy deposited closer to the end of the lens face. We got a new one of those and within one day, these "dust spots" already began to appear.

Joy says, "Cleaning is key, and so is putting it away at the end of the day so as not to expose it to dust in the air."
