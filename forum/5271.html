JohnFP Sat Dec 17, 2005 8:58 pm #3
zeljkomrsic, You have to be patient for answers sometimes here.

The Green or Red laser work good for holography.

A red laser can produce red or green holograms.

A green laser can produce red or green holograms.

Things to look for in a laser are:

Stability (no mode hopping, drift etc...)

Coherence length - the longer the coherence length the deeper your holograms can be. (also called linewidth)

Power (not too important for begining)

Polarization (the higher the polarization the more usable holography laser light)

I am sure if you give it time others more knowledgable can add to this.

This page is interesting, and $350 for a 100mW green DPSS laser would not be at all a bad price. Though I'd like to know where these things are coming from - he writes that it's not "cheap China junk", but what is it then?

At any rate, unless a laser is specifically designed for stable single mode operation, it is quite uncertain whether it can be used for holography or not. Some simple modules work well, others not. If the thing is well stabilized, there is a better chance that it works, but it's no guarantee. It's a gamble and you may be lucky or not.

So if you want to be on the safe side, get a proven kit like the one that was mentioned in this thread. Or, how about asking the the vendor you mentioned, whether he allows you to retun a laser if it doesn't work for you. Another possibility would be to look on ebay for a polarized HeNe Laser in the 5-10mW range, those won't be expensive and are almost guaranteed to be suitable for holography.

tpark Mon Dec 19, 2005 9:41 pm #12
I've used both PFG-03M and PFG-01, both with good results.

The PFG-03M takes much more energy to expose. The emulsion is very soft when wet, so you must treat it very carefully during the developing process. Contamination in the development process can lead to small colored blobs on the hologram, so it's helpful to have an extremely clean environment. The development time is very fast, but the plates take a while to dry unless you use a hair dryer, which can lead to imperfections in the finished product.

I've had good results with PFG-01. Development takes longer, but the emulsion dries faster.

I never bought the student kit (I bought the standard kit) but I bought the PFG-01 and the appropriate chemicals seperately.
