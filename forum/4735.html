> Dinesh: Cloth has a constantly shifting weight...

That's too broad. I'd agree that creased cloth when suspended such that the creases were "settling" would probably show motion. Creased cloth where the creases are running perpendicular to gravity most likely would not move due to gravity as long as the suspension is reasonably stable. Granted, this does depend on the cloth and very fine cloth is more likely to move during an exposure but that's most likely going to be from air currents than anything else.

Also, if your laser is close enough to be causing thermal effects across your table, what the ^$%# is it doing that close? Are you mad?? Put some baffling between the laser and the table if you can't move it farther away but either way, you've got a broken table that needs fixing.

I submit that the problems people have with cloth have less to do with the cloth than it does with their shooting environment.
MichaelH Sun Apr 10, 2005 1:46 pm #4
> Dinesh: Some materials do seem to move "on their own". This is why so many have tried making a hologram of a rose and going through all sorts of gyrations to stop it moving

Roses don't "seem to move", roses do move.

They're alive and light sensitive. When put in darkness, the petals do relax. Combine that with the fact that they don't really like to be cut from the parent plant and can't get the nutrients they need and you end up with even more motion.

One way to work around this problem is to keep them as cold as possible and use hot developer, as used by Lon Moore.

BobH Sun Apr 10, 2005 5:27 pm #9
I've had pretty good luck making holograms of black velveteen ... unfortunately! I'm usually trying to prevent recording of the background, or supports for the object. I find that I have to take special steps to make the fabric move during the exposure to make it disappear. It may be because I secure everything well to prevent the fabric from drifting into a beam somewhere and wasting an exposure that way.

I've used black crepe paper instead of fabric. If it's allowed to hang, it'll probably move. Also, black velveteen (cotton based) is better than black velvet (rayon based) because it doesn't have a "sheen" to it. Another good source of a black background is retroreflective paint. It will send the light that hits it (object lighting) back toward its source instead of toward the recording plate.
