Does anyone know if somehow 3-Aminopropyltriethoxysilane has a bad influence on Silver Iodide? When I coat Silane treated plates with an emulsion that is a mix of Silver Iodide + Silver Bromide I often see a severe milkiness after drying in some area's. These milky area's look as if a milky liquid has been applied to the emulsion with a sponge: streaks are visible. It made me suspect that the milky area's are where there are thicker traces of Silane left on the glass.

When I coat plates that have been treated the same way with just AgBr emulsion these milky patches never show up.
jeff-blyth Wed Dec 10, 2008 2:38 am #2
So what may need to be taken on board here is the fact that the number of silane molecules needed to give you a monomolecular coverage over a typical clean sheet of glass is in the nanogram range. If we bother to work it out for say a 10cm square plate with most of the molecule sticking up perpendicular to the glass and only the Si-OH bit occupying the glass surface . If this covered an area of say 0.1 sq nm, then with a molecular wt. of 221g. per mole and using Avogadros No. and a density 1 g per cc I get to a value of 20ng only to cover a 10sq cm area!!! This seems ridiculously low but even if my calc. is out by a factor of 1000 it is obviously going to be an extremely small amount and more than 99.99% of the stuff we routinely use is just forming umpteen multilayers and the bonding of one single silane layer to another seems to be less strong than the FIRST silane layer to the glass.

However I have gathered that the bonding to glass is dependent on a hydrolysis reaction taking place ON the glass surface rather than before it reaches that surface. If you go making up a homeopath's type concentration of say 0.0001% silane solution in acetone then maybe it completely hydrolyses in solution in a very short time if you do not put it down on the glass instantly.
