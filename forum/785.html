Hello all,

I've been slowly collecting equipment to begin making holograms again, since my early attempts back in 1978. I was a senior in high school, and the only person I knew who had a laser (a Metrologic he ne pumping out 2 big milliwatts).

My first holograms were crude, but I was amazed that they even came out at all, since I made them my hallway at home, on a piece of plywood.

35 years later, I'm gearing up to do it again, with a bit more knowledge, and better hardware. So joining Holoforum is the next step. While the basic principles of holography haven't changed much, techniques have, and so questions abound.

Let me start by giving a little background and a partial equipment list. I'm a professional illustrator and graphic designer, with a lifelong love of science. In fact I believe that science is just as creative as art, and holography is an ideal blending of the two. I've been into amateur astronomy for 20 plus years, and optics seems to be the common theme in my interests. Over the years, I've accumulated some optics hardware. I now have 15mw he ne laser, a 10mw Coherent Compass 215m (that I hope to upgrade to a least a 50mw version, if not a 315m), a 2' x 4' Melles Griot optical breadboard on innertubes- small, I know, a Newport 900 spatial filter with a 15 micron pinhole and 10x objective (not sure these are the best for 532nm), a variable beamsplitter, various optics mounts for lenses, mirrors, diffusers and beamsplitters. I also have made an electronic shutter and light meter (my Newport 1815 laser power meter is not calibrated). I would still like to get a parabolic mirror for the reference beam, but that should just about cover it.

I plan on starting out simple - single beam Denisyuk, but want to move on to more advanced multi beam setups. So, questions...

Well, first- thank you. For setting up this site, and sharing your hard earned knowledge. Holography continues to be part hard facts, and part intuition. The members of this forum possess an amazing body of knowledge.

I guess my first question would be- can I do much with a 2 x 4 optical table? I know that more room is better (I wish I had room for a 4 x 8 table), but this is what I have to work with for now. I will be building a scaffold structure on the table, to get the reference mirror up at a better angle. Also, my spatial filter setup is producing a nice, clean gaussian beam, but I can't get rid of the diffraction rings (the Airy disc) I suspect that I need to change the pinhole size and/or the objective power for my 532nm Compass 215m, as they were probably chosen for a different wavelength and beam diameter. What have you guys been using? And lastly ( for now), what film types do you recommend? I've read about several mentioned on this forum, and Geola seems to make good stuff.

Thanks for reading, and thanks again for making this forum great!

Ron Neale

Hello and welcome Ron. I have a preference for small tables, mine is only about 18 X 50 inches. With split-beam techniques, it is best to keep the beams as short as possible. More beam travel means more possibility for drift due to component movement. But my setups are restricted to produce certain holograms, there are some cases where more table space is needed, of course. As for objective/pinhole problems, these can get irritating, and I've had my share. I think trial and error is the best approach, purchasing a variety of objectives and pinholes would be a good idea. eBay is a good source for objectives. If you want to use silver halide materials, Ed Wesly recently did some extensive tests and comparisons of different materials. This was linked to somewhere on this forum, I think it was in the "AgX" section. His website was down last time I checked (the link didn't work) but hopefully it will be up again soon.

This forum can be an excellent resource, it has helped me immensely.
Dinesh Thu Dec 19, 2013 10:04 am #3
> Ron Neale: I guess my first question would be- can I do much with a 2 x 4 optical table?
Yes. It depends on the size of your film, but I'm guessing you're going to be starting with a 4x5in plate. A 10X will cover a beam diameter of about 10in over a distance of about 20in and this shoudl be enough to get the centre of the beam over a 4x5 in portrait mode. The diagonal on a 2x4ft table is about four and a half feet.

> Ron Neale: I will be building a scaffold structure on the table, to get the reference mirror up at a better angle.
I had a table with a scaffold structure in my lab at Appplied Holographics back in 82. However, I've found these to be more of a nuisance because it robs table space and you have to work around it. Having said that, there are holographers who do use such structures, so, to each his own.

> Ron Neale: 10x objective (not sure these are the best for 532nm)

> Ron Neale: I can't get rid of the diffraction rings (the Airy disc)
The laser wavelength is not as important as the beam diameter at the entrance of the objective. In other words, it doesn't matter what the wavelength is because you simply calculate the pinhole size for your wavelength and then get the best commercial fit (although having said that, it may not be necessary to get the "best commercial fit" as several people here seem to make their own. Someone just recently posted quite an ingenious method of making pinholes by arcing a capacitor with Al between the plates). The problem arises when you have an unusually large beam divergence so the beam gets very large very soon. The calculation of the pinhole size depends on the NA (usually printed on the side of the objective). For most lasers, the spot size is given by

s = 1.22(lambda)/NA

In terms of practical experience, for a 10X, I'd use a 25u pinhole. As you're using a 15 micron, I suspect the "rings" you see are diffraction off the edge of the pinhole rather an overspill of the Airy rings.

> Ron Neale: what film types do you recommend? I've read about several mentioned on this forum, and Geola seems to make good stuff.
I think your best bet is to get in touch with Alec Jeong at Integraf ([http://www.integraf.com/](http://www.integraf.com/)). He distributes holographic film to a lot of people: companies, universities and hobbyists. He also has beginner kits with all the necessary chemicals to start. Personally, when I use AgX, I use VRP-M for blue/green (488, 535, 514) and PFG-01 for red (633).
