To determine the beam ratio for a single beam Denisyuk. Make a small ish hole a white card.. Place the card in the plateholder and hit it with the reference beam. The central area of the beam goes through the hole ands scatters off the object onto the back of the card. By visually examining the front and back of the card, you can determine how much the object beam is and where it's going.

Perhaps I was unclear. It tells you the beam ratio at the plate. There have been many posts where people have complained of dim holograms. One reason may have been that the majority of the light in a single beam Denisyuk is missing the reference or that the beam is crooked. The white card method shows you how clear and uniform the reference is, how much overlap there is on either side of the plate, and how reflective your object is. True if you're doing a specific object the actual ratio is pretty much fixed, but I remember when I started I pretty much picked an object at random with no idea of it's reflectivity. If you simply want to make a hologram, without too much regard for the actual object, then this method wil allow you to choose your object so that it reflects back onto the refrence and tells you strong that reflectivity is.

Diverting some of the beam around the plate may or may not be a single beam Denisyuk, depending on what is meant by "single beam". It's "single" in the sense that there's no beamsplitter in the setup, but it's not "single" in the sense that the object has an independant light on it. However, if the additional light is stolen from the reference, it may be possible to say it's still "single" beam, since the object light is not truly independant.
