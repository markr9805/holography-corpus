Tom B. Sat Mar 19, 2005 6:00 am #3
> JohnFP: I got some optical grade gelatin and would like to try mold coating. I found a suggestion by Danny to use Rain-X which I may try.Any other suggestions to try?

Try Rain-X first. It works very well. Just chill the plates in the fridge for 30-60 minutes before separating, and the coated plate peels off very cleanly once you get a corner or edge started.

I was able to make some very clean films of uniform thickness on the first attempt. Well, OK most of them were messed up, had bubbles etc. but a couple were perfect, and I could see how, with practice and some refinement of the technique, consistently good results could be obtained.

I was coating 2.5 inch plates. My technique was to smear and polish a drop or two of Rain-X onto a large glass sheet, apply long double strips of strong transparent tape as a spacer for each edge of the plates to be cast, apply a glob of warm gelatin onto the (warmed) sheet with an eyedropper, then carefully lower the 2.5" plate on top so the gelatin spread out to the edges (with the tape spacer limiting the thickness). Then onto the next plate. After cooling, the tape was strong enough so that by grasping the tape on both sides and lifting (careful to avoid tearing the tape by stressing sharp corners), the top (coated) plate would pop off without too much trouble and then could be cleaned of excess gelatin and put aside for drying.

JohnFP Mon Mar 21, 2005 10:41 am #7
Well, I tried some mold coating with Knox without AmDi just to get a feel for it. I like it very much. I tried Rain-x, transparencies, and Saran wrap. The plates were 5" x 7".

I had a large piece of glass that I layed down on a surface that was leveled in both directions. I treated part of the glass top with Rain-x, taped a piece of tranparancy to part and covered a part with Saran wrap.

I made the emulsion minus the AmDi and poured a puddle on the area to be covered right against the glass plate edge while I was holding the glass plate vertically. I then layed the glass plate down (which had tape on both sides as spacers) slowly, watching the puddle move along the glass plate until the plate was flat. I then used two 1/2" diameter steel rods for weights which I placed directly on the plate just inside the tape spacer edge on both sides. Then let the plates set for 2 hours.

Rain-x - The plate removed easily with just a little pry under one edge as stated here on the forum. The emulsion looked to be very smooth and uniform.

Tansparency - I was able to remove this from that glass top and then peel the transparancy off very easily. The emulsion looked very smooth and uniform.

Saran wrap - This also was easy to remove from glass top and then peel the wrap off. Although I tried to stretch and tape the Saran Wrap with no wrinkles, I got wrinkles in the emulsion. I don't like this method.

The tape I used was an off brand cheap tape which yielded a 25 micron emulsion thickness using a C type Micrometer as best I could measure. I could not tell if the thickness of the emulsion was different in the center as the micrometers only reach about 3 or 4 cm in from the edge.

I liked the results very much and the entire 5 x 7 plate (except for 1/4" down two sides) seems to be evenly covered. I guess the real test will be to use AmDi and make some holograms.

I even made two batches at the same time last night. One with the Knox as a control as used in the test and one of the Bone Gelatin. Both acted the same way.

Technically your "control" wasn't (but I suspect you might have realized that now). A control would have been raw gelatin made at the same time you made your DCG.

Do you do anything different when you add the AmDi? Cook it more, change the temperature, etc? If so, do the same thing to the raw gelatin without actually adding the AmDi. Believe me, I know how easy/tempting it is to change more than one variable at a time but you'll come out ahead in the end if you don't. Some of the more chemically oriented can probably tell you what AmDi will do to gelatin but even without that information, if you control the variables, you can find out what will happen even if you don't know/understand why. The why is much easier (for me anyway) to track down if you already have a cause and effect relationship established.
