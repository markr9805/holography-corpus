Hi folks -

(This post regards volumetric 3-D and lenticular 3-D displays. If this needs to be moved to "off topic," that's fine with me.)

I thought it might be of general interest to say that two papers I presented this week are now available online. One regards the use of a volumetric 3-D display for cancer treatment planning and modification, and the other regards progress toward a "highly multi-view" 3-D display -- a lenticular system that would be capable of 24 view imagery at XGA-resolution per view. An interesting aspect of this is its underlying 2-D display engine, which provides a 20 megapixel image from a single 0.8 megapixel projector. Another part of the paper discusses how software techniques can be used to precompensate for the effects of defocused optics.

The conference, [Stereoscopic Displays & Applications](http://www.stereoscopic.org), is a part of the SPIE-IS&T Electronic Imaging 2008 symposium. 2009 will be our 20th anniversary, and you might want to consider coming if electronic 3-D displays is up your alley. I thought this year was intellectually stimulating and, frankly, a lot of fun. It is clear that stereoscopic cinema is generating a lot of cash and, consequently, a lot of attention. The keynote and plenary sessions included [Larry Hornbeck](http://www.stereoscopic.org/2008/program.html#key) (co-inventor of the TI DMD) and David Bolinsky of XVIVO. If you haven't watched David's landmark "Inner Life of the Cell," I highly recommend it. Molecular biology has changed a lot since most of us were in high school! [Here is a small version of the movie](http://multimedia.mcb.harvard.edu/) - evidently, this uses the most accurate molecular data available (they note, of course, that if they showed everything the scenes would be occluded by water molecules, etc. The systems, such as the "walking" ball, do act something like that, but they're being buffeted about by molecules not shown.).

Anyhow, the preprints are available through my personal website:

Woods, Nicolas S. Holliman, and John O. Merritt, Proceedings of SPIE-IS&T Electronic Imaging, SPIE Vol. 6803 (article CID number pending) (2008). IN PRESS

Favalora, ["Radiation therapy planning using a volumetric 3-D display: PerspectaRAD,"](http://www.greggandjenny.com/gregg/PerspectaRAD_SPIE_SDnA_2008.pdf) in Stereoscopic Displays and Applications XIX, edited by Andrew J.
