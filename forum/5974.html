I've often heard that a drop off in sensitivity in silver halide emulsions (eg BB-640) is due to the drying out and loss of water in the emulsion after manufacturing. Specifically, there is a presensitization step available for BB.

Would it not be possible to increase sensitivity merely by restoring the moisture by placing the emulsion in an environment of 100% humidity for 24 hours before use?

Tom B. Fri Jan 12, 2007 2:18 am #3
> Tony: Would it not be possible to increase sensitivity merely by restoring the moisture by placing the emulsion in an environment of 100% humidity for 24 hours before use?

This would add water to the emulsion, but the emulsion would be swelled so drying would still be needed afterward unless you wanted the swelliing effect.

Unlike water soaking, what it would NOT do is dissolve out any chemical inhibitors that may have been added to the emulsion to extend useful shelf life. It's hard to say without experimenting how much of the sensitivity increase is due to this. So try it!
Tony Sun Jan 14, 2007 6:50 pm #4
I conducted an experiment to see if the emulsion sensitivity of BB-640 can be affected by humidity. I used two 2.5x2.5” plates, one as a control, and a second as an experimental plate. Both plates were from an old box of plates that were at least a year old, and had been stored in the original (but unsealed) packaging at room temperature and humidity. The experimental plate was subjected to 100% humidity for a period of 24 hours before exposure. Additionally, half of the experimental plate was rinsed in water for 20 seconds, then squeegeed dry before exposure. The two plates were taped together and mounted side-by-side in the plateholder. The plates were exposed for 30 seconds, for a total exposure of around 450uJ/cm^2. The plates were developed for 20 seconds in JD-4, rinsed for 10 seconds in plain water, and fixed for 1 minute, then rinsed for 5 minutes and dried. After drying, the densities were measured using an HP-1 meter, with a 650nm laser pointer as a source.

The measured densities were as follows:

Control plate: OD = 0.983

Experimental plate, are rinsed in water: OD= 1.218

Experimental plate, humidified only: OD= 0.983

From this, I conclude that rinsing BB-640 plates has a moderate effect (25% increase) on sensitivity, but that exposure to high humidity has no significant effect.

A transmission hologram was formed on both plates. There was no noticeable difference in the brightness of the hologram between the two plates.
