Different Frequencies:

The beams from two different lasers would not be coherent, meaning they would have different frequency and phase, and that difference could change over time. So the two beams could be different by a relatively small amount in frequency, but this is still a large frequency in absolute terms, like many megahertz. With a frequency difference of 1 megahertz, the fringes would precess over one light-dark-light cycle every microsecond. So your fringe locker would have to have the capability of correcting one of the beams at this rate - not very likely to be easy to build such a device. Also, I'm not sure if fringe lockers are capable of dealing with continuous drift over more than one wave - I suspect they would suffer from problems as the phases 'wrap around'.

Split beam from one laser

Looking at light as electromagnetic radiation, a light beam is a travelling pattern of electric field vectors which alternate every half cycle. Interference occurs when the net electric field from two beams adds by superposition. So therefore by this model, the interference occurs in space - it has nothing to do with the interaction with matter. The recording of the pattern is the result of the net summed electromagnetic field of the two beams interacting with the matter.

Two monochromatic sources from two different sources may be coherent within about a wavelength or so, but will quickly go out of coherence. Even within the coherence length, the wave properties are not that constant or consistent., except under certain special conditions. Imagine a floating object, say a rubber ball, bobbing up and down so as to generate water waves. If you bobbed the ball at an absolutely constant rate and the water were absolutley still and at excatly the same temperature and depth, the waves would be very coherent. Any movement of the water prior to a wave disturbance or any change in the physical conditions of the water would slightly destroy the coherence. The greater the change in the water, the greater the decoherence. Now, if you had two rubber balls and both bobbed at exactly the same rate, you'd get interference lines. If both did not bob at the same rate, you may get local interference, but it wont last long. In other words, you'd see areas of the water where interference occurs but quickly gets destroyed, only to re-appear in some other part of the water. The rate of this appearance and disappearance will depend on how different the rate of bobbing was. Multiply this by millions of rubber balls and coherence would only occur over a significant area and last a significant time if every rubber ball were going at exactly the same rate. Any change in the bob rate of any ball in these millions of balls would cause decoherence. No source is that exact unless the balls were somehow tied together and forced to bob in harmony, which is sort of what happens in any one laser tube. With two lasers, even of the same frequency, the "balls" have no idea what their cousins in the other tube are doing and so you may get very local interference for very short times, but in general they wont interfere. However, what if the balls of one tube were to tell the balls of the other tube what they're doing? This is possible and you can get two lasers to be coherent (up to a point) if one laser were fed into the amp of the other, ie if the lasers were coherently coupled. In a real laser, the atoms inside the cavity are the "rubber balls" and each ball is not only just vibrating but also moving relative to it's neighbouring ball, due to thermal energies. This motion of the atoms relative to each other causes other "bob frequencies" which causes decoherence and is known as Doppler Broadening. There's also atoms colliding against the sides of the tube, atoms colliding with each other, atoms rotating etc etc. As an aside, all thermal sources also cause momentary interference at a level of about 10^(-8 ) secs. In other words, right now in your room there are interference fringes appearing and disappearing on you walls at a rate of about one fringe set every 10 millionts of a second. When they show SF movies of characters living in speeded up time, they never show these interference fringes!

Light is the oscillation of an electric field. This electric field is simply a physical effect that can create certain physical changes. Nothing actually goes up and down except pure energy. I think I've tried to use the voltage across a parralel plate capacitor as a model before now. A field of strength "10" means that the electric field goes from zero volts/cm to 10 volts/cm over a quarter wave, then down to zero volts/cm and then to -10 volts/cm. and, after a full wavelength, back to zero volts again. While it's doing this, it's also moving forward. The voltmeter idea I posted a while back should give you an idea. No idea where it is so I can't post a link.

Interference is caused by the electric vectors of two different light sources adding up algebraically. The net electric field of two sources is therefore the algebraicc sum of each source at that point (whatever "point" means here!). Whether there's a material there or not, this net electric field still exists. If you have a source with a wavelength of 1cm (microwaves) with an amplitude ("height") of 15 volts/m, and another source of 1 cm wavelength but this time with an amplitude of 5 volts/cm and starting at zero, then a quarter of a cm after the source point, one wave has a E field of 15 V/cm and the other has one of 5, thus the E-field at 1/4 cm from the source is 15+5=20 V/cm. This field of 20 v/cm will be at this point regardless of whether yesterday's lunch happends to be in the same location in space.. Sometimes one wave has a E-field of 10 V/cm and the other one has -10 V/cm. In tbis case, the net voltage at this point is zero volts/cm. When a wave hits a material, the atoms of that material react to the square of the e field, whatever it may be. Thus in our microwave example, the atoms of, say yesterdays lunch, will jiggle up and down in reaction with an oscillating electric field of 225 volts/cm (15^2). In some parts of where there is no electric field because the algebraic sum was zero, the lunch molecules won't react at all, since there's no energy there.

In photography (or holography) you choose a material with molecules such that when certain energy derived from the electrical oscillations from light hit them, they undergo chemical or physical changes. Silver Bromide, for example, will chemically reduce to Silver when hit by the e-field. If the net voltage is zero due to the algebraic sum of two waves being zero, then there's no voltage there to resct to and the silver bromide remains as silver bromide. Not all materials react to this particular form of energy. When such a reaction occurs, it's called "actinic"

Not only that, but the wavetrains are ejected at random times. The v=f(lambda) equation is too simplistic for a real set of waves. This equation assumes an infinite wavetrain of only one frequency. In practice, a single frequency is impossible because it'd require a measurement over a period of zero time - a contradiction (not to say quantum mechanically impossible!) Real waves occur in bunches or wavetrains whose components are given by a Fourier Transform. These components have an independant velocity inside the wavetrain - the phase velocity (which can be faster than c) - while the wavetrain itself has a group velocity. The length of a wavetrain is the coherence length, but each wavetrain is independant of each other. In different lasers, the wavetrains would not be correlated unless the laser were coherently coupled.
