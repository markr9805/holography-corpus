JohnFP Mon Apr 21, 2008 3:25 pm #5
Hey Tony, take one of you already processed holograms and soak it in a solution of AmDi and water. About 5% or so. Then wipe off the excess moisture (no drops or liquid on emulsion, and dry off and clean glass side real well)and expose immediately to white light. Try a quartz halogen light at about 16 inches for about 3 minutes. The more light the more hardening. Then, fix, rinse and process as normal. You should get a red shift. If you do the same but let the plate dry for a good hour or more after dipping in AmDi solution, you should get a blue shift. The thought is to harden the fringe structure in swollen stated (no time after AmDi soak) or in the shrunken stated (an hours time to allow emulsion to dry after the AmDi soak).

I have not fully tested this but it did seem to have a positive effect when I was playing with it. If found to work, this would be a great way of "tuning" your hologram to the color wanted.

[http://www.holograms3d.com/Test_Research/ReSenExp.htm](http://www.holograms3d.com/Test_Research/ReSenExp.htm)

Tony, just to add my voice to the confusion:

Why do you want to make pseudocolor holograms? Pseudocolor holography is for people who only have one laser: you have two, both of them high-power and ideal for holography, one at 458nm and the other at 532nm. Why not just combine them using a cheap dichroic mirror, and make holograms of colored objects. You will get all kinds of colors in the final hologram. There's no need to bother with any kind of pseudocolor process; just combine your lasers using the dichroic mirror, and start shooting! Your whole process can remain basically the same.

JohnFP Mon Apr 28, 2008 11:28 am #33
Or if you are in the pioneering mood, I have an idea that I have not spoke of yet because I wanted to try it first. But feel free to experiement with it if you want.

Take your processed hologram and soak it in a warm solution of AmDi and gelatin. Carefully squeegy or blot off excess liquid and imediately expose the plate to white light (quartz halogen or sunlight). The reprocess with fixer, water and alcohol. You may be able to go all the way to red from a blue hologram.

The idea is from the following understanding. A reflection hologram has the fringes laid as plates parallel to the surface of the glass. The already exposed fringes have full crosslinking and thus will not absorb too much of the AmDi and Gelatin solution and thus will not expand at their apex. The unexposed frineges will not be fully crosslink and should be able to expand at their troughs absorbing the AmDi and Gelatin. The exposure to light adds more support in the unexposed fringes in their swollen state thus making the fringes wider upon drying because of the addition support latice.
