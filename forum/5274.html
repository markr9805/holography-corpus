While flying home from Brazil, I had time to do some thought experiments. I just came in my house and I cannot wait to ask some more questions. And collect enough knowledge for making my own plates. Here goes:

1] When immersing the plate in the LiBr+dye bath (after having loaded with AgNo3), what determines the fial size of the created AgBr+dye crystals? Is it the time during which the plate is soaked in the LiBr? Does the dye have something to do with it?

2] If the time factor is the most important, why is it then that when you make panchromatic plates and do the second soak in LiBr+second dye that the new deposits of AgBr don't attach to the already existing crystals? Or do they attach to them and make them bigger, thus degrading the resolution of the film? Or does the previous dye prevent the newly dyed deposits from attaching to it's own crystals?

3] Would it in that case be possible to make higher concentrations of small crystals by using different dyes for the same wavelength and doing several soaks with these different dyes?

4] Would it be advantagious to have crystals of different sizes in one emulsion or is it better to have them all of the same size? I can imagine if they would all be of the same size it would be more difficult to record gradients of intensities, i.e. the exposed+developed film would become more newspaper like black/white dithering.

5] If it is an advantage to have crystals of different sizes, might it be a good idea to repeat the AgNO3 -> LiBr+dye steps a few times with shorter baths in the LiBr and thus with each step increasing the size of the already existing crystals a little, but also introducing fresh super small crystals? I imagine that having more crystals would help in sensitivity and resolution, and having the different sizes would help with gradients and thus increase the bandwidth of the film.

6] If it is just the amount of similar sized crystals that's important, would it be possible to do several soaks with some kind of magical growth blocker saok in-between the baths that encapsulates the already existing crystals in a similar way that pyrogallol can encapsulate already exposed crystals?

I'm sorry to take up your time with my silly questions. I promise I will order the Bjelkhagen book tomorrow. Perhaps I can answer some questions of other people after I have read it.

But if dye B is already combined with AgBr from a previous step then it takes more energy for A to displace B than to go for a virgin AgBr. So with the limited time the liquid phase is available dye B can survive on the grain. I do not know the ratio of dye molecules to AgBrgrainsI would like to know the rough answer to this.
