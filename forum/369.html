John Sonley Fri Oct 28, 2011 10:43 am #9
Hi John - I go for film every time, the cost is much less and a film hologram is easier to transport, handle and mount for display.

What else is better - get rid of that awful indexing mess, whether it be glycerine, xylene or even simple white spirit. Go for a dry glass 'sandwich' - glass/film/glass , have an overhead reference beam at the Brewster Angle, have a half wave plate in the reference beam and turn it for minimum reflected reference beam light from the glass filmholder and you don't get woodgrain effects.

There's much more to this story than I can type - but I attach a few images that may help

rzeheb Fri Oct 28, 2011 1:02 pm #13
I have had excellent results using the technique that John Sonley describes. My film is sandwiched between two glass plates without any index matching and I get zero woodgrain or other noticable reflection artifacts.

A year or so ago I, too, tried making and using a film holder that uses no glass backing. The design was slightly different from yours but essentially it functioned the same way. What I learned was that, as mentioned by earlier posters, the device acts like a giant eardrum. Basically, if you can hear a sound, so can the film holder; and it can almost certainly also "hear" fainter sounds than you can. I had variable results. On one occassion I made a reasonably decent hologram. On another a car passed my home as I was exposing the film; I could hear it, so could the film. The developed hologram had a great big "hole" right in the middle of the film (where, presumably, the greatest vibration would have occurred). Needless to say I abandoned this technique pretty quickly. I suppose there is no harm in your trying and experimenting a bit but unless you make holograms in a sound proof room I think the odds of success are pretty bad.
John Sonley Fri Oct 28, 2011 2:31 pm #14
John - I wouldn't go for the spring method - in my mind it's doomed. Any movement of more than a quarter wavelength of the light you're using and a very dim hologram - half wavelength and no hologram at all.

I did a few sums a while ago about the thermal expansion of metals and recall that a temperature change of only 0.02 deg C over the exposure time will cause a movement of a quarter wavelength of red light of a 0.5 metre length of SS rod - the equivalent figure for green light is 0.018 deg C ............... and that's solid rod, what chance do you have with springs!

John - go for a 3 point ball bearing mount.

As usual, observations will answer your questions. Look at an object through both those thicknesses of glass and see if there is any difference. John Sonley Fri Nov 04, 2011 8:53 am #31
Hi John - I just use ordinary 2mm window glass for my dry glass/film/glass sandwich, clipped together with the type of plastic stripping you get in a stationary shop to hold together a file of papers.

What is important is that the pinch points of your clipping arrangement should squeeze film and not the gap between the edge of the film and the edge of the glass, otherwise the glass will bow and you'll probably get movement effects.

I usually prepare my glass/film'glass sandwich in the evening and leave it to stabilise overnight in my light tight glove - I think that is probably excessive time - but film costs money!

Well, as I stated the hologram is orange, like the back of the train throughout the hologram. The digital camera, for some reason, makes the front image planed slightly brighter part of the hologram yellow.

But to answer you question. I processed completely with the JD2 developing kit from Integraf.

I am not sure of the density as I dont have a "density chart' to judge it off of but I had a 2:1 ref:object ratio, shot at about 80uJ/cm^2. Processed 2 mins in dev. Then 15 second water rinse. Then bleach till clear and then 15 seconds (about 30 seconds total). Then water rinse for 2 mins and finally a 30 second rinse in water with photoflow. Hung to dry and they (my impatients as always) blew dry every so slightly with hair dryer on cool and from arms distance away. Very, very genlty and just until I saw a nice even color. Let let set for an hour or so before mounting in frame.

If you want to do dry glass sandwich, you should really try to set a few pieced in the sandwich a day before. I do and put them in a light tight box. Then as you can see, the one piece of glass is larger then the other. It is this large piece of glass that is mounted (or touching) the plateholder only. That way the "sandwich" does not get disturbed. I have not done film in years and years and the first 6 holograms I made in film with this set up had no movement fringes and no wood grain. Works extreemely well.
