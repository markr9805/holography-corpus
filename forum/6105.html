The way I think of a hologram there is no redundency at all! Each view point is simply able to remember the object from it's own view, no others. With a slit you only see the image from through the slit. So there is a large data loss however you still have vertical paralax for the slit width, since this is somewhare close to you pupil diameter it is irrelevent to display holography.

Take a normal transmision hologram and view it through a slit. That is the only information retained.

> DrOutlier: For the sake of argument, let's assume you are right for now. Is there any significant conversion of the vertical parallax into noise assuming the setup and isolation table are ideal? For now this is purely a thought experiment.

Actually it's not a pure thought experiment at all.

Masking off the master to reduce the vertical parallax when making a white-light copy does increase the brightness of the final copy. You have to take care not to mask the master too much but how much is enough or too much depends entirely on your table geometry.

I'm short on time so this will be brief but each point on the copy receives (in general) light from every point coming from the master. By masking down the master you reduce cross-talk/destructive interference and increase overall brightness.
