Originally I and others were just delighted to be able to make green images in DCG with a red laser. I am now wanting to move on and make a panchromatic system. There is of course a big problem reconciling the low pH high dichromate needed for good green response without dye with a low chromate high pH needed for a result with dye. So that is something I will be sorting out . I hoped to have done it already but there were problems and other projects had to take priority. What I can say is the fact that if you cut down the TMG total down to about only about 30% of what you are using then you really can get a good scarlet red. --Yes there is a damned trade-off ------a much longer exposure time. However we now have really great powerful laser diode red sources compared to the hugely more expensive HeNe lasers.

1) I don't know if plain chromate is less sensitive to blue light than dichromate. If I had to guess, I'd say maybe. I haven't seen any reports of anyone using it for DCG so it's likely not optimal. I guess it would be just CG for chromated gelatin holograms .

2) Since DCG holos are so fragile, I expect it would be best to incorporate post-swelling into the processing as described here:

[http://www.nli-ltd.com/publications/color_control.htm](http://www.nli-ltd.com/publications/color_control.htm)

I don't think TEA is a good medium for POST swelling of anything (definitely bad for siver halide films) - it's just too chemically active. Often various sugars are suggested, but none are immune to bacterial attack. I was disappointed to see that my little spray bottle of 5% Xylitol had become skungey after a few months in the basement. It could be that higher concentrations are needed, or that comparisons of liquid solutions vs. dry films are meaningless...
Colin Kaminski Sat May 27, 2006 2:39 pm #4
> Tom B.: I was disappointed to see that my little spray bottle of 5% Xylitol had become skungey after a few months in the basement. It could be that higher concentrations are needed, or that comparisons of liquid solutions vs. dry films are meaningless...

5% is way too low to have the sugar protect its self. It would need to be around soda pop concentrations of 25% or so. That would not work well for holograms. Sodium Benzoate could be use but the pH would have to be dropped to below 3.6. Since a DCG hologram will have a glass plate glued to it, there would be no chance for anything to land there. What that means is if you don't have any cells to start with you wont grow any colonies.

Another use for a good HEPA bench.
