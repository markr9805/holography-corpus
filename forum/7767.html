a_k Tue Mar 10, 2009 1:00 pm #1
Hello everyone

Ten months ago i started trying MBDCG. I still can not get good results and they are not consistent in any way. Most of the time the sensitivity and the DE is too low. Very few plates did result in holograms at all with 50mJ/cm2 and those had poor DE. For a decent DE at least 150mJ/cm2 was required.

More than 50 series and a total of about 700 plates have been coated and most of them processed. At least 14 different gelatins, bovine and pig with bloom 80 - 300 have been tried.

This variants of MBDCG have been tried:

Jeff Blyth: Applied optics / HI7 / "Ye olde red sensitive DCG" / MBDCG Patent

Zhu: "Panchromatic DCG"

Zeng Hongjun: "Improving the lifetime of methylen blue sesitized dichromated gelatine"

Hans: Wiki article

Some difficulties encountered and attempts made to resolve them:

- At the very beginning some plates had tiny blue dots: Probably this was caused by too low pH. This was adressed by using a pH meter and adjusting the pH to about 9-9.5. As i had read that the sensitivity of this system has the highest increase between pH 9 and pH 10 i also tried values up to pH 11. At such high pH levels the next problem was visible.

- Violet color of unexposed plates: According to Jeff at pH 11 or so MB is converted to its freebase form which has a violet colour. These plates had no sensitivity to red. In addition the gelatin is supposed to get damaged at such high pH levels. This problem was resolved by strictly adjusting the pH of the mixtures to 9-10 or more often to 9-9.5.

The pH is an evergreen with MBDCG. Too low - > MB precipitates, too high -> plates turn violet, below 9 or above 10 -> loss of sensitivity. So the pH needs to be kept around 9.5 with only +-pH0.5 allowed. One problem is the pH buffering property of gelatin. This i tried to address by adding water to the gelatin 12hrs before using it and sometimes did readjust the pH of the MBDCG mixtures after a day or so, but there was not much adjustment needed. The bigger problem is that the TMG and the acetic acid used for adjustment of the pH are volatile and the pH will change due to this. I'm trying to avoid this by only warming the mixture very gently during the coating and working as fast as possible. I also tried adding a slight excess of TMG as it seems to be more volatile than Acetic acid. I also tried using boric acid like Hans did and other mild organic acids but the results were not too good. Especially citric acid is contraindicated because it results in a separation of the coatings.

- No light sensitivity at all or very low sensitivity. According to Jeffs Applied Optics paper MBDCG performs poorly at low RH, and best at RH of 60-65%. RH 55% should result in a small decrease of sensitivity and at a RH of 45% the sensitivity would be half of that at 66%. In summer the RH was a bit low sometimes and i tried adding glycerine and sorbitol to increase the moisture level in the coatings. There was no apparent benefit of this. At present i'm using a humidifier to keep the RH around 65%. I know that most hygrometers are not very accurate, especially many digital ones. So i'm using two mechanical hygrometers calibrated according to the manufacturers instructions. Even so it is unsure that the 65% displayed are correct but i don't think they are too far off. Nevertheless i tried the range between 55% and 75%. Yesterday i got a series of blank plates after processing, despite having an RH of 65%. I did breath at one plate and this one did result in a hologram. So i increased the RH in the room to 70% and let the plates adapt to it during 24h. This plates still had no sensitivity.

One more reason for reduced or missing sensitivity was using too fresh plates. This was addressed by waiting 24h or longer for the plates to dry. The strange thing about this is that frequently i get completely blind plates if they are that old. The best sensitivities i got were when the plates had been put in a cold airstream during 10-20 minutes. If they were left longer, this resulted in a more or less linear decrease of sensitivity until they were not sensitive at all.

- The thickness of the coatings seems to determine to some degree how much moisture remains in it. So i tried coating thicknesses between 2 and 20um and veil/bar/blade coating and mould casting. Veil and blade coating are the quickest way i found for coating large numbers of test plates. With a coating bar and mould casting i get more uniform plates and the thickness of the coatings can be controlled better. But the results obtained so far with MBDCG did not justify spending so much more time for coating the plates each time. Besides that, in an atmosphere with 65% RH the coatings should not contain much less moisture i guess. And even if the moisture level should be a couple of percent off, this should not result in complete loss of sensitivity.

- The purity and the properties of the ingredients used are an important factor. I'm using TMG and MB from Aldrich and potassium chromate from Martin. I don't know exactly the source for the potassium chromate but did get good results with it with conventional DCG and violet and green lasers. At some point i even questioned the quality of the deionized water used and tried different brands and even distilled water myself The acetic acid used was from more several sources and sometimes freshly distilled too. The biggest variable was of course the gelatin.

- Gelatin: It got difficult getting bovine gelatin here. Because of the "mad cow disease" it is not sold for food purposes anymore. Nevertheless i've tested 14 different sorts and many of them were bovine and had a pH of 240-290. With one exception, all photographic gelatins did perform very poorly. The one sort that did give the best yield was a local pig food gelatine. I still have not found the reason why a pig gelatin did perform better than the bovine ones and why the photographic gelatins gave such bad results. The pig gelatin is supposed to have a high isoelectric point and therefore be useless for MBDCG. One of the photographic gelatins was a low ion variety and that one gave especially bad results. In the beginning i was thinking that the gelatins used were a very important factor for the failures. But after the number of different gelatins used without luck i'm not that sure anymore. Nevertheless, i'm getting and testing more gelatins. Hope dies last.

- Light source: I'm using diode lasers. Most of the tests were made with a Sony SLD1239JL-54. This LD proved to have a usable coherence length and to be stable enough to allow exposure times of 30 minutes and more. Before each exposure i'm performing a glass plate etalon test to make sure that the beam is sufficiently stable. Lately i tested a couple of bluray LDs for DCG and they did produce very strong fringes but were completely useless for holography. I then looked at the reflections from a diffraction grating and saw that 2 equally strong first order reflections were present. This would mean, that those LDs are not monomode but do have two strong modes. The few bluray LDs which did have a coherence length of about 14cm on the other hand did not have such strong fringes with the etalon test but were good for recording "deep" scenes. Lately Martin reported of a red LD which has strong fringes but was not usable neither. So i don't trust the glass etalon test anymore. This simple test is a very useful tool for some LDs but for others it is completely misleading.

For improving the quality of the LDs i reactivated my interest for TECs. There is one nice layout for the MAX1968 and the author might be able to provide ready made PCBs or the layout files (bottom of the page): [http://www.laserfreak.net/forum/viewtop ... &start=100](http://www.laserfreak.net/forum/viewtopic.php?t=43981&postdays=0&postorder=asc&start=100) I'm working at a layout for MAX1978. If anyone is interested, my Eagle files are here: [http://a2.no-ip.org/holotix/TEC/MAX1978/](http://a2.no-ip.org/holotix/TEC/MAX1978/) The layout is not finished yet. The copper area for cooling the chip is too small and some tracks are too thin.

Processing:

- I've tried simple processing with a water bath and two IPA baths

- letting the plates sit for 5 minutes and immediately processing them

- fixer, fixer/hardener, natriumsulfit / alum as first bath

- post hardening with diffuse laser light, heat

- multiple IPA baths with increasing concentrations

- various temperatures for the baths

So, after almost a year of intense work i have not yet managed to get satisfying results with MBDCG. I've tried so many things and the poor results are very frustrating. If anybody sees what i'm doing wrong or what could be done better, please help.

Sorry for the length of my posting

Ahmet

Hello Ahmet,

I don't have much experience with MBDCG, but I will give you my two cents.

Most important: forget about diodes for a while, and get a moderate or high-power HeNe off eBay. They are incredibly cheap these days -- nobody wants them anymore except holographers, and there are hardly any holographers left.

Second: be aboslutely certain your setup (table, supports, plateholder, and especially the object) is absolutely stable. Of course we know this already, but when long exposures are involved -- like with MBDCG -- it becomes extremely important.

Third: no one, to my knowledge, has produced MBDCG holograms that are as good as Cristiano Perrucci. I would carefully review the 101 posts he made on this forum, especially the older ones like:

Hi,

MBDCG is a tricky job. In my experience many variables should be tuned in order to get good repeatable results. For example, gelatine strength has a great impact on the warm water bath temperature. Coating drying time, as well as room RH, influences sensitivity. MB concentration, coating thikness, gelatine/TMG ratio, IPA baths temperature and so on make the situation more complex. Speaking about MB concentration, more MB dissolved in the emulsion makes the emulsion more sensitive but, as MB adsorbs red laser light a Denisyuk hologram will result dim. There are infinite scenarios.

Here is my definitive MBDCG formulation

Pig gelatine 280 Bloom 10 g

Potassium chromate 1 ml (5% water sln)

TMGA 6 ml (25% water sln)

TMG 1.5 ml

Methylene blue 2.5 ml (0.4% water sln)

Water 80 ml

Adjust pH to 9.3 with 25% TMG sln

Emulsion preparation

- swell 10 g of gelatine in 80ml if cold water

- heat to 40-45C to dissolve gelatine -never exceed 45C otherwise gelatine strength migth be altered-

Stirring continuosly @40C:

- slowly add 6ml of 25% TMGA (~ 1ml/sec)

- add 1.5ml 15% TMG very slowly (~0.1ml/sec) constantly checking the pH that never should rise above 9.5. Correct the pH with 10% acetic acid.

- slowly add 1ml of 5% Potassium chromate solution (~ 1ml/sec)

- slowly add 2.5ml of 0.4% Methylene blue solution (~ 1ml/sec)

- stirr for 3-5 minutes

the whole mixing process should require about 10 minutes.

Coating

- Mold coating technique using 80um spacers (3.1mils)

- 6 hours @ 28C gelling time

- before detaching the glass sandwich chill it for 1 hour @5C (this step helps to get a defects free coating)

- dry in steady environment (air stream free) for 12hrs @28-30C 60-70% RH. Drying temperature and RH seems to have great influence on coating characteristics.

Exposure

- Once the holographic setup is ready, wait for about 30 minute to allow all components to stabilize

- Expose for 20minutes with a power density of 20uW/cm^2 for a 200x200mm plate. NOTE: I'm using a 20mW He-Ne laser (JDS 1145-P) warmed for 3 hours.

Development and finishing

- After exposure wait for 15 minutes

- Wash in cold water (10C) for 5 minutes

- Gently immerge the plate in water @ 19 to 25C (this step must be accurately tuned in according to your gelatine characteristics) for 30 seconds

- Dry in 95% IPA @ 20-25 degrees for 1 minute

- Dry in 97% IPA @ 20-25 degrees for 1 minute

- Dry in 99-100% IPA @ 20-25 degrees for 5 minutes

- Force quick IPA evaporation with hairdrier

- Finish the hologram in pre-heated oven @125C for 10minutes

I hope this helps you.

Cristiano

Joe Farina Wed Mar 25, 2009 4:44 pm #18
TMGA means TMG Acetate. In other words, TMG plus glacial acetic acid.

I would suggest finding a copy of the Applied Opticsarticle, since this journal is commonly available in universities (Applied Optics, 1 May 1991, Vol. 30, No. 13, pages 1598-1602). On page 1601 is given the "Appendix: Procedure for Producing DCG Holograms with a He-Ne Laser." This is a very to-the-point description of the formula.

The second "stock solution" he mentions is TMG acetate (TMGA), which consists of 80cc of a 25% TMG solution (25cc TMG and 75cc water) plus about 8cc glacial acetic acid "added slowly while stirring well until the pH is between 7 and 8."
