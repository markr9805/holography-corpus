MichaelH Tue Jan 23, 2007 5:59 pm #4
A pebble in the mountain can start an avalanche.

Is this yet another pebble? I don't really know but I do know that as more stories like this come up, the liklihood that pointers will be banned and more regulation will be applied to higher powered lasers becomes greater.

As a hobbiest do you really want to have to go through certification and regular inspections to keep your high-powered, privately used laser? I don't.

Incidents of lasers being pointed at airplanes have been making the news more and more. Stories of people having their vision impaired (some permanently) because of laser pointers are making the rounds.

The press don't know squat about lasers. They don't know the difference between a hand-held medical laser putting out watts of power that has to be rolled from room to room on a cart vs a hand-held laser pointer putting out milliwatts or even dozens of milliwatts that can be carried in a pocket.

If we don't correct factual mistakes like this, they will continue to spread and eventually some ignorant but (hopefully) well-meaning politian will get it into his head that lasers need much tighter regulation.

We can either fight it now by correcting these misconceptions and errors as they arise or we can wait until it actually becomes a real problem and much harder to fight.

As far regulation of devices that use lasers such as CD/DVD and the upcoming TV's, I think you're right. Keeping those out of the hands of hard-core enthusiasts will be difficult or impossible. What will be possible is legislation that makes repurposing them into laser pointers and selling them on the open market illegal.
