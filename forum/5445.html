BobH Thu Mar 23, 2006 2:37 pm #2
Lighting holograms is every bit as important and exacting as recording them. You're "playing" them, like a record (if you know what that is ). The light source, mounting hardware, and immediate environment constitutes a record player.

I'd hold off on the electrician for a bit. I recommend your talking with a few holographers about it. Look at the Laser Reflections web site. A phone call to Ron Olson or myself will answer all your questions (I'm a slow typist). A few basic steps follow:

1) Site the hologram. Find a place where reflections off the surface will not interfere with the image. Don't let sunlight hit the hologram.

2) Hang the hologram. Make it point at the average viewer's position and height. It's better if it's a bit low on the wall because it's easier for the tallest to bend over.

3) Use the smallest source possible.

4) Do not overlight the hologram. Too bright a source will make the whole room look bad.

5) Use a filter on the source to match the color of the image. Or consider the use of an LED.

JohnFP Thu Mar 23, 2006 7:49 pm #4
I bought a track light in which you can add up to 6 quartz light. They are on a 8 inch flexible removable mount that twists into the track. This track light allows only one AC connection but 6 individual lights that can be spread (6 feet end to end) and aimed as I like so that each light does not overlap the others on the wall. Each light (I only have 4 on it now) illuminates 4 hologram arranged in a cross and every other light is poined a little higher the the neighboring light.

For me this was aesthetically pleasing, cost effective and easy to mount and use. So when I make a new hologram, I simply frame and hang it up within one of the lighted areas.

BobH Fri Mar 24, 2006 2:27 pm #7
All "kits" I'm aware of are made for educational use. They are designed to make very small holograms, like 2.5x2.5". For an object that large, you'll need to learn to be very creative to capture an image with a small laser and one of those kits.

I'd recommend buying a large, comprehensive book (Saxby), a laser >20mW, and go from there. You can certainly make a small hologram of a large deep oblect. Place the object far away from the film.
