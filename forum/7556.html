One word. Usability.

You could do what you suggest but you still need a good surface to project the image onto while you read. Plus you need a holder, otherwise you'll get tired of holding the hologram in one hand and the laser in the other. Then there's the fact that every time you put it down you lose your place.

Tackle usability and you'd have something that might catch on.

MichaelH Wed Oct 08, 2008 12:05 pm #4
That's not usability. That's utility.

Such a thing would be useful but the idea doesn't describe how it's used or how easy it is to use. The latter is the problem with most holographic ideas. The final implementation has some fatal drawback (do you carry a halogen light with you all the time?) that prevents it from being adopted by the masses. A holographic reference work, while technically interesting, doesn't beat a small book that fits in your pocket, will hold a bookmark, can be written upon and doesn't take any power beyond that needed to move your fingers in order to turn the pages.

Once you can invert the difference between the two, then you'll have a holographic reference that everyone wants.
