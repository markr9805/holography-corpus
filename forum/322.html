Dinesh Sat Sep 17, 2011 8:15 am #6
I'll be there too. I'm going to ask if I can have my Leia hologram in the display section. Also, Joy's been bugging me to write up something and so I have 4 or 5 ideas. Any comments or preferences?

"An overview of holographic aberrations"

Basically an overview of what aberrations in holography are, how they show up in an image and why they occur; can they be fixed? If so, how?

"A new colour map for diffractive processes"

Colour maps are basically based on the 1931 CIE chart (based on experiments done in 1929). However, the 1931 CIE was based on print colour and used fairly broadband primaries. The subjects (only about 20 of them, by the way, in the first run experiments - hardly a representative group!) were asked to match colours from three lamps which emitted broad band colour. Today we're used to seeing fairly narrow band sources from lasers and leds and so the reproduction of colour should be based on a colour map that reflects this narrow band illumination. In particular, I think holography may benefit, since I'm going to propose a scheme whereby the exposures/ratio of exposures of a colour hologram may be read off from the coordinates of the colour map. I gave a back-of-an-envelope presentation of this to Hiroshi (actually a back-of-a-napkin presentation at the OSA meeting in Miami ) and he asked about 4 colour primaries, so I extended my scheme to four, or any number, of colours. I think I've found that there is a reason why 4 or more colours are better than three.

"Overcoming cross talk in colour, laser transmission holograms"

Pretty self explanatory, I hope

"Dcg as a resist material 2"

A follow up on the paper at Holopak in 2008. At that time, I wanted to go through the entire process of making a dcg resist hologram, electro-forming it to make a shim and then copying the shim. I didn't have time for all of that, so maybe I may be able to do that this time.

Finally (and a little bit out on a limb): "Wavelet transforms as a possible method of real-time holography from digital imagery" You connect a medium to a computer, show up a 3d graphic and have it show up as a hologram in real time. This is, as you may imagine, a little outre, but I've been playing with this idea for the better part of a year and have had several people ask me write it up, so this may be a venue.

Slightly on topic :

With some of the print on demand services you can have restricted access to books, so there really is no excuse not to offer paper proceedings for those attending who want them and don't mind paying a little extra for the privilege.

As for the open access discussion ... from a science point of view it's of course optimal, but with conferences where most of the contributors are from industry rather than academia authors seem fine with the papers never being easily accessible and don't make any effort to make preprints available (whereas with something like Siggraph you can almost make a complete proceedings before the conference takes place). In some areas people seem only interested in acknowledgement within the small peer group able to attend their conferences, and holography seems definitely one of those areas.

Open access might be what I as an outsider want, but I don't think most of the people actually doing the work want it ... big thanks to Kaveh for spreading at least some of the information outside the inner circle regardless BTW.
