Ed Wesly Tue Nov 22, 2005 1:30 am #3
I don't know if this will help or hinder your project, but here's what I know about pulsed ruby laser holo systems.

In the halcyon years of the '80's, I worked on or saw 3 JK Ruby systems in the Chicago area: the big 25+ Joule at Fermilab, (now lying in disrepair at Anderson Lasers, I believe) and a pair of 1 J models, one at Northwestern Univesity, the other at Holicon. The Holicon one is in possesion of Rick Bruck, the NWU who knows.

Anait Stephens bought her own 1 J, now it's sitting idle at LFC to whom she had donated it. I had heard about but not seen her 10 Joule which she had intended to set up in her retirement chalet in Switzerland, but that never happened, and the last I heard Ralph Cullen was trying to sell it.

I visited a place in Montreal that was set up to do pulsed work, a 10 J JK, whereabouts unknown to me now. Also in Canada Fringe Research had the one that JK built for Nick Phillips when the Who were using him as a tax write off.

Ana Maria Nicholson has a JK, not sure if it's 1, 3 or 10, still functioning in Long Island NY.

There were several Applied Holographics Holo-Copiers made, but I know Fred Unterseher has (had) one of these as a take out, and a dude in this locale named Pat Hurley, whereabouts unknown.

I helped install one at the Center for Night Vision and Electro-Optics at Fort Belvoir, VA. This was a holocamera set up, with beamsplitters, path-matching prisms, and plate holder in one module.

I know that Edwina Orr, Richmond holographics had a 10 J, and the Royal College of Art similarly. There is one in use at Rob Munday's Spatial Imaging group, whether it is one of these units or something else, I don't know. Perhaps one of our British chums knows of their disposition and others in the UK and the continent.

I would estimate maybe about two dozen of the JK (Lumonics, now who knows, but the Rugby, UK plant still is up to something.) Holographic Laser Systems were ever made. But these things were huge, refrigerator sized power supplies, and just a simple oscillator started at $40k, 1985 bucks, $55-60k for 1 J, $70k for 10 J, $135k for the 25 J with the 1" by 8" rod. For a better estimate of all the JK's in North America, I would recommend getting in contact with Dan Morrison, who was the American Sales Engineer, (whereabouts unknown.)

The only other holo pulsed lasers are being shipped by Geola, and I know of only two in the states, Rod Taylor's in Indiana (Forth Dimension) which I know of for sure, and I had heard of another one in the St. Loius area.

I don't know if Ron Olsen's old company is still involved with making large enough lasers for holographic purposes.

So the market is pretty small, and if you threw in all the Korad and Apollo '60's and '70's Ruby, Alexandrite, Yag, whatever, I would guess that there were maybe only about a hundred pulsed lasers made for holography over the last 40 years!

So it's hard to say what kind of a market there is out there. 10's of mJ are required for 4 by 5 Single Beam Reflections, a couple hundred to light up a head and chest to do a transmission master. If a company were to make lasers for this market, they might need to set a price marker at about $5k to make this kind of output. Those JK's, the Rolls-Royce of lasers, were the price of a small house at the time! Geola seems to be the only competition right now.

So I don't know if this will help or hurt your cause. I am always available for consulting. (My avatar is from a self-portrait made at FermiLab with the 25 J.)
