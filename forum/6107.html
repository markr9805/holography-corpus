I don't know what holo setup you're refering to, buy I had 3M send me some samples of that material (assuming it's the same stuff) about 10 years ago. The plastic sheets were reasonably rigid (similar to cheap polarizing sheets), and I was able to make holos with it..my reasoning being that I'd increase the brightness by reducing the horizontal paralax in a reflection holo.

It worked, but not spectacularly so, and when the viewing angle approached the cut-off point of the louvres, the image got pretty dirty (for lack of a better word) and I didn't bother with it any further.

If you write (or phone) them and tell them you have an idea for integrating their product into holographic applications, they'll likely be happy to send you a few small samples. You won't be the first to ask them, but they'll probably still play ball.

Kaveh Thu Mar 08, 2007 8:43 am #6
It works on the principle of two equal and opposite dispersions.

You need to make a diffraction grating with the same reference angle as the holgoram, say 45°. You put the grating and the hologram together, separated by the light control film. Then you shine the white light straight onto the grating, at 0°. The diffracted (first order) light goes through the louvers, but the undiffracted (0 order) is blocked. The diffracted light goes on to reconstruct the image of the hologram. What you see is a monochromatic, sharp image, as each color of light has undergone an equal and opposite diffraction. The whole assembly is in line with the direction of the white light.
