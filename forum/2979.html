Hi, I'd to try my hand at making reflection standout holograms. You know the ones that seems to come out the image towards you.

Anyone any suggestions or advice about the best route to take when making this type of hologram?
Colin Kaminski Fri Dec 24, 2004 8:24 pm #2
The basic process is to make a transmision master then flip in the filmholder to make a real image projected from the plate. Then a reflection hologram is made from this hologram. You have to make the master with a colimated reference beam (usually) and make sure the object is far enought from the plate to make the copy geometry work out. A great book to see the setups is Holography Handbook. It is very graphic. Practical holography explains it in depth as well.
andy75180 Sat Dec 25, 2004 12:21 am #3
Yes I'll have to keep an eye out for those books. What's will happen if my reference beam isn't colimated? Which it isn't! And also is it possible to make projection holograms by making copies from relection holograms or does transmission copies make better ones?
Colin Kaminski Sat Dec 25, 2004 4:11 am #4
You can do this with an un-collimated reference beam and a reflection master. The problem becomes one of processing and beam ratio. You must process the hologram so it replays a very narrow wavelength exactly centered on the wavelength of the laser.

This is something I have always wanted to experiment with but I have not had time. You should even be able to make a separate reference beam for the master and the copy (H1 and H2 are the proper terms). This would allow you to control the beam ratio.

Has anyone tried this? I think most holographers probably just use the transmission master and a collimator because it is quite a bit easier.

If you use a un-collimated transmission master the hologram will have severe aberrations. This could be really cool if you are making something very abstract.

Yes reflection masters can work - the film holo I sent you (dino emerging from Xmas ball) was done this way. I find the metol/ascorbic acid developer with rehalogenating bleach (JD-4?) does not shift the wavelength much. Non-collimated beams will cause image distortion, but for some subjects this might be OK. I used a single-beam bypass setup, dimming the reference beam to the H2 with a neutral density filter big enough to cover the film holder. It's lots of fun to try, and you can get very bright images, though field of view suffers unless you use H1 masters much bigger than the final H2 image.
