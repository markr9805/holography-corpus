The best solution to work is to rotate the laser. Put a piece of glass in the film holder and look at the reflection on a white surface, now rotate the laser until you see the reflection diminish (it usually wont completely dark, only get dim), at the dimmest orientation the light is going thru the glass plate and not bouncing off (wasted light).

I have a small cylinder lens (from Edmund Optics) in front of my diode at an adjustable distance to square up the beam. I think it's about 1" focal length. I have the flat side facing the diode which is good from an optical point of view (minimum incidence angle) but bad for back reflections which could destabilize the diode. Haven't noticed any such problems though. I painted the ground lens edges black to reduce light leakage and also put black tape strips over the lens front top and bottom to reduce other unwanted light leakage.

Does anyone remember HoloDisk? He had the great idea of taking a ring shaped like a men's wedding band and layering seran wrap on it till the thickness was just right to make a multi-order waveplate.

Another idea he had was to buy a good grade of mica from an electronics supplier and make a few thin flakes until the right thickness was achieved. Before I got to try these ideas I aquired a cheap 632nm 1/2 wave plate from e-bay. It worked at 658nm pretty well.

When I was puttering around with polarization for an experiment, I got some cheap plastic half-wave sheets from efston science in Toronto (edmund scientific supplies them I believe). Also, if you go to any camera store, ask them to show you cheapest "circular polarizer" they carry. It's a lens attachment of course, and the strange/unpopular sizes may be really cheap. These are essentially quarter-wave plates and two of them together (oriented correctly with respect to eachother) will give you a half-wave plate. If I remember correctly (I'm pretty foggy on this one), camera stores may even have "polarization rotator" lens attachments which are one-piece half-wave plates. If you shop around, you should be able to get this stuff cheaply.

How well it would work with your particular wavelength, I don't know, but I assume they're designed with the entire visible spectrum in mind. If I recall correctly, the lens attachments worked fine with my HeNe.
