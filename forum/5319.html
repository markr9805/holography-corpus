Tom B. Tue Jan 10, 2006 5:05 am #3
> javamiggs: Hi. If I coat a fluorescent material on an object. Can I make a hologram out of it?Is the light produced due to fluoresence still coherent, just like the laser used to illuminate it?Im hoping to make this study as my college thesis. I hope somebody can help me.

In general, light produced by flourescence is not coherent with (and also occurs at a different wavelength from) the external stimulating source. This would make holography of the glowing object difficult if you were hoping to record interference fringes between the illuminating laser and the flourescing object.

There is a technique called X-ray flourescence holography which uses light emitted by flourescing atoms as both reference and object illumination beam to produce holograms of local atomic structure, but this is useful only for things like crystallography and not for display holography.

Nice little article here: [http://www.physicstoday.org/pt/vol-54/iss-4/p21.html](http://www.physicstoday.org/pt/vol-54/iss-4/p21.html)

While Tom is correct I was thinking along the lines some time ago about making a hologram in the UV. Then have the replay wavefront not only reconstruct in UV but have it fluorese in the visible for a "glow in the dark" hologram. But this too, once thought out, proved difficult if not impossible.

The difficulty arrises in having a UV illumination source hit the hologram and reflect (or transmit) the UV image which it then fluoreses into the visible all the while not allowing the original UV source to fluorese the entire plate and still maintain the original wavefront properties.
