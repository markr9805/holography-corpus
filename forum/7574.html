I have noticed from some recipes for SHSG that Dichromate bleaches are used with quite a lot of KBr present. Usually in the order: develop -> bleach -> fix -> IPA.

Why is the KBr necessary in the bleach if the re-halogenated grains will be washed out anyway during the subsequent step with the fixer? Would it work as well without the KBr?

I am not sure what you mean by a lot of KBr. In the latest paper I've seen about SHSG, the authors (Kim, Bjelkhagen, Phillips) used the usual quantity of KBr in their PBU-bleach.

You don't necessarily need KBr. I made SHSG holograms both on 8E75 and HRT emulsions by using two different reversal bleaches (permanganate and dichromate). The images were pretty bright. On Agfa noise was very high though.
Hans Fri Oct 24, 2008 11:37 am #3
I have an article here for example (by Belendez, Neipp and Pascual) that promotes the use of the following bleach:

PART A:

Ammonium Dichromate 20g

Sulfuric Acid 14ml

DI Water 1l

PARTB:

KBr 92g

DI Water 1l

Just before use mix one part A with 30 parts B.

They claim a diffraction efficiency of 90% on PFG-01. Now, because the solution spoils a certain time after mixing A+B I was wondering why they found it necessary to use the KBr if it can be avoided.

The fixer they used is a non-tanning fixer F-24. Could it be that they hoped that the fixer would locally soften the emulsion where it removed the KBr? I guess it would make sense if that happens because of greater index modulation. But I never heard of fixer having a local effect.
