LARRY LIEBERMAN (USA)

Even though he cannot be considered exclusively as an artist, it is interesting to highlight Larry Lieberman's work since it opens a new access road to the creation of holographic images either for the established holographers as for the artists that work in other media.

Lieberman learned holography during his interdisciplinary studies on Physics, electrical engineering and Fine Arts at Ohio State University. In 1977 he follows T.H. Jeong's teachings at Lake Forest College in Lake Forest (Illinois) and together with Lloyd G. Cross in Chicago puts up a definitive optical printer for the holographic stereogram technique. A hybrid technique of cinema and photography, holographic stereography consists of turning each one of the film's photograms into successive vertical holograms projecting only one image of stereoscopic dimensionality, whose temporary rhythm is similar to that of the movies, but which has to be activated by the spectator's movement or by rotating the image.

In 1978 he founded his first company, Holographic Research Lab, in Columbus, (Ohio) to research, develop and produce holograms of international scale, and in 1982 the second one, Holographic images, inc., specialized on the production of full colour reflection holographic art on a line similar to those made with lithography's traditional methods.

Since 1978 and together with his partner Frank Millman at their new Miami Beach (Florida) head-quarters, Lieberman has collaborated with a number of artists, among them Bruce Hanners, Mark Johnson and Mark Callaghan, on the creation of standard poster size multicolor holograms, which are hallways from the only copy or the limited edition and the massive production of dichromated images and embossing. Belting on the aesthetic quality of holographic images, Lieberman proposes a kind of multiple production which can be considered as collectible art.

"Holography is a true art force. It will carry us into new dimensions of vision expansion and reality. It bridges the gap between the two sides of man: technical and psychic. When these two sides of man's nature are in harmony aesthetic expression flourishes. Holography demands vision, knowledge and skill, which are trait only found in the holistic mind... Holography... brings man into harmony with its true nature, which is both material and spiritual."

Larry Lieberman, "Universal Men and Women" in Jeff Berner, The Holography Book (New York: Avon Brooks, 1980), p. 81.
