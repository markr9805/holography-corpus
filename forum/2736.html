BobH Thu Sep 23, 2004 8:30 pm #4
I used potassium iodide (as a post-processing bath) with pfg-03c in an attempt to prevent printout when reconstructed at 364nm. The result was a very bright grating (in white light), a yellowish-clear plate, and absolutely no image! Apparently, converting the grating to silver iodide prevents printout by absorbing UV. The recording wavelength was also 364nm. I can't say if there was a wavelenght shift, however, but I'd presume it did shift a bit toward the red.

Tom B. Wed Oct 20, 2004 3:15 am #7
> John Klayer: I'm thinking of going green and buy one of those dpss that Mr. Gizmo sells. If I used PFG-03C with the green laser, could I color shift the final image to yellow or orange with TEA or a certain developer?

Yes, post-swelling should work, but the softness of PFG-03 emulsion could cause problems with squeegeeing. TEA is generally not recommended for post swelling because it promotes printout (light - activated darkening of the holo). Sugar or corn syrup will work, but these are attractive to molds and bacteria. Sugar substitutes such as Sorbitol are often recommended. Xylitol is reputedly even less attractive to bugs, so it might be worth a try too. I couldn't find pure Sorbitol locally, but was able to order some Xylitol from a local health food / vitamin store. Have not tried it yet for holograms, but I like the flavor.

I've been playing with PFG-03C all week and I've found:

It's pretty insensitive. A Hans Bjelkhagen paper mentions 3mJ. I found it to be more like 4 or 5.

It seems to be fairly sensitive to color shifting by exposure. I had a green and red object. The red went green and the green went blue. Of course it had to be film shrinkage, right? However as i played with exposure ratios, I found that reducing the red exposure and maintaining the green gave me a good red image while the green image remained blue!

The emulsion is really very soft. It seems to curl up at the edges while in the final water bath. For my usual silver halide, VRP-M, I usually give a quick slosh in water after the bleach (less than 10 seconds) and a quick dip in photoflo and then dry with a hair dryer, to see initial results. Then I put it back in the water and give it a long final water wash. When I tried this with 03C, the emulsion came off completely. However, this last week I found I can use a hair dryer if I go back about a foot and a half and keep the hair dryer moving. In one case, the final reflection hologram plays back as an achromat in reflection. Can't quite explain this yet, but I'm assuming that the emulsion is soft enough to give extremely large bandwidths This has possibilities, if I can control this phenomena.

I ordered Formaldehyde (as per Hans' paper) to pre-harden. However, unfortunately the power supply to the HeNe caught fire last night and almost burnt the lab down! No more playing with color for a while.
