Hi all

You may recall me posting a short time ago regarding the problems that I was having squgeeing plates. No matter what I did there always seemed to be marks left behind which weren't visible until the hologram was developed, dried and the lights turned on. I tried again and again taking on board various suggestions made but the problems still persisted (it must be me!)

Anyway, last weekend I got around to making some more holograms and decided to give the squeegeeing a miss altogether. Instead, I used a compressed air gun with a flattened end (to spread the air into a fan). By gently releasing the flow of air and moving slowly down the plate I was quite successful in dispersing all the water. There were a few little 'water stain' marks visible under the safelight but I chose to ignore these and proceed.

The end result is quite a resounding success - No squegee marks !! 8) Even the little 'water stain' marks weren't visible once the hologram was finished and dried. Flushed with success I am now going to 'retire' my squegee to the refuse bin !!

All I need to do now is take the next (big) step from single beam Denisyuks to some kind of multiple/split beam setup

Cheers

Jem

Jem, I must have missed your post about problems with squeegeeing - I would write this earlier.

Who needs squeegeeing??? Every mechanical contact with emulsion may cause scraps and water marks, especially if you use not hardened emulsion.

I've read the discussion on your post and am really surprised that nobody uses drying in ethanol.

After final rinse in water, put the plate in a bath of 100% (96% to be correct) ethanol. Then for a few minutes lift up one side of the the plate to let the spirit flow down from it. Ethanol will take all the water from emulsion's surface. Then you take the plate out of ethanol, let it flow down and let the plate stand on on a cloth or paper on one corner to dry. You will get ideal dry emulsion with no water marks.

To save ethanol when you make a lot of holograms, usually three baths are used one after another. They are 50%, 80% and 100% ethanol.

In fact you can dry plates in ethanol, acetone, isopropanol and some other solvents but ethanol is less harmfull and stinking
