The last edge lit hologram I recall seeing was at the International Symposium on Display Holography in 2000 in Austria. Ryder Nesbitt did it as his thesis project at MIT, which published plenty of papers on the technique in the '80's through 90's.

I don't know if that would be doable. But I recall edge lits to have some special features: edge lit transmission holograms show less dispersion than “normal” transmission holograms do. In contrast edge lit reflection holograms have much more dispersion than “normal” reflection holograms.

So in order to get little image blur in an edge lit reflection hologram, you need rather thick layers (30um or so). That limits the choice of recording materials. Silver halides may be out in that case. And DCG might also turn out to be unfit for edge lit. Photopolymers might be a better choice.

Moreover, there's the serious problem of matching the index of refraction of the glass/plastic block with that of the recording material...
