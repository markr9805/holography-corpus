ron olson Thu Feb 01, 2007 5:54 pm #1
I will attempt to direct at least a portion of this topic towards holographic imaging and away from dumpster-diving for lasers. I just took possession of a new GEOLA G2J (Nd:YLF/Nd:Phosophate Glass) laser last week and I must say that I'm quite impressed. Although the optical mounts are not quite what I would consider top-of-the-line - the laser seems to behave beautifully. After but a few days operation, I would opine that this laser can be considered for operation by the vast majority of serious holographers - with the disclaimer that any 2J Q-switched laser must be given a great deal of respect. The laser is stable; displays excellent shot-to-shot reproducability; and has a very good beam spatial profile. I can adjust the ouptut power from a few hundred millijoules to >2J at 526nm with ease, and although I have yet to measure the coherence length, the early images I've produced confirm that it's >3meters. The laser (with its power supply) is compact, uses 120V power and employs a water/water heat exchanger (on-demand to minimize water consumption). As an added bonus - the laser is extremely quiet: a marked difference from what I am accustomed to.

Rob Taylor of Forth Dimension (GEOLA's US rep and the owner of an earlier GEOLA design) has relayed to me that in more than 6 years ownership, his 2J system has had the cover off only to clean optics. I am in the process of testing the G2J with all the green sensitive films and will report back as time permits on issues that most concern me: image composition, subject/background lighting, producing reductions and enlargements, color tuning, and film-plate processing/archiving.

As I continue to advocate: if you're really serious about holography why not get out of the sandbox and into imaging. I hear the cackles regarding cost but in honesty there must be a thousand times as many people with sailboats and racecars as there are active holographers and somehow they all manage to pony-up the requisite $100K. Why do you think it is that holography doesn't seem to attract people with more disposable income? You think it just might have something to do with images of circuit boards and bathtub toys?

ron olson Fri Feb 02, 2007 6:14 pm #8
First regarding the laser:

the G2J is a frquency doubled Nd:YLF/Nd:Phosphate Glass laser with a fundamental wavelength of 1053nm. When I rebuilt the studio in Las Vegas I knew that I would need to recruit more operators and that my old Quantel (Nd:YAG/Nd:Silicate Glass) wasn't nearly user-friendly enough to allow operation by non laser-jocks. The G2J has a solid-state Q-switch (no dye/solvents required) and a temperature tuned frequency doubler.

As to the "what's wrong with doing things on-the-cheap" line: if this was a forum about videography what do you feel would be the percentage of contributions regarding putting together a movie camera fashioned out of cast-off hardware? Things cost what they do. In watching the "progress" of the holography produced by the build-it-myselfers, I see nothing to justify even tepid enthusiasm.

As to my line about sailboats, etc.: I am promoting holography as an avocation (i.e. a pursuit for early retirement) which is both exciting and artistically gratifying. I personally know lots of (OK several) people who can afford $100k for something they can literally pour their energy into - much like they can into nitro-fueled dragsters, fine woodworking or home audio equipment. For 15 years I've been advocating that holographers pool their resources where necessary - better to have part-time access to a decently equipped studio than full-time access to marginal technology. At the end of the day, we're talking portfolios.

Hans Fri Nov 23, 2007 9:53 am #25
> Ron, how does it achieve 526nm? ND:Glass frequency doubled?

Because it uses nd:YLF as a master oscillator instead of nd:YAG.

nd:YLF lases at a slightly longer wavelength than nd:YAG. So, when doubled you get 526nm instead of 532nm.
