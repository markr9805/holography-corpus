Dinesh Mon Jul 02, 2012 2:05 pm #6
Setting up mirrors for a shot, setting the exposure meter, leaving the lab (shutter control on the outside), waiting 5 minutes, hitting the shutter, going into lab to retrive plate for developing, realizinig I forgot to load the plate into the plateholder.

Opening (almost new) box of plates to load plateholder, sequence as above except I did load plateholder, leave lab with plate in hand, develop plate, walk back to lab and realise I've left the lab door open to flood light into lab but had forgot to close the box full of plates. I actually rapidly closed the box only to realise the futility of rapidly closing a box already exposed for 20 minutes. (Stupidity definition #1: expecting the speed of an action to negate the impossibility of the result of the action)

Set up a shot needing 300 second exposure, hit shutter control (remember, shutter in the lab and control outside), wait 5 minutes, go into lab to retrieve plate, develop, get zippo efficiency, try again and again and again (stupidity definition #2: trying the same thing several times and expecting a different result, knowing that's stupid, but doing it anyway), finally realising I'd set timer control for 300 milliseconds, not seconds.

Holomark Wed Jul 25, 2012 5:37 pm #10
As I am new to holography I have made many... two stupid mistakes come to mind.

When I was trying to make an image plane I kept moving the H2 plate closer to the H1 plate to try to make the image stick out more. I wasted several plates in this obviously futile attempt.

Next I ruined a final hologram just minutes before I handed it to a new owner. I had someone who was very excited to buy one of my holograms (first sale!!). He was in a rush and headed out of the country. He wanted the hologram to give as a gift on his travels. I rushed home to grab the two holograms he was interest in. I decided that I needed to "sign my work" so I grabbed a gold paint pen with which I signed and dated the hologram. The two holograms were painted black on the back - one with high gloss black paint and one with BBQ high heat paing (matte finish). When I arrived at they buyers I set out the holograms for display and was shocked to find that one was ruined as the gold paint had seeped through the black paint and now showed part of my signature when viewing the hologram. The one with high gloss paint was fine, but the one with matte finish was ruined. While I stood in shock my friend proceeded to tell me how he loved the "ruined" hologram. I was so disgusted I felt like smashing the ruined hologram - instead I just gave it to him and sold him the second hologram.

Uh, I love that part.

Many years ago (no holograms at this time) I made small mirrors. The same story as yours. Everything is fine until sunlight - then fingers begin to darken up to brown/black color.

Next time I decide to use latex gloves. Happy, observing that this time I am saved - gong into town. But, again got brown fingers. This time something went wrong with gloves, probably punctured.

Another mistake is etching gold from PCBs with aqua regia. At first looks like acids are not strong enough, so I decide to put it on hot radiator. Suddenly reaction begins to produce nasty brown fumes, and filling whole room. Quickly, without breathing toxic air, dispose everything outside and leaving room opened. Not only I risk my life, but every metallic part in the room begins to corrode, except maybe few metals.

Since I am new in holography, so far not much 'big' mistakes. By 'big', I mean life threatening. Turning plate upside-down on occasion (red light, hard to see which surface is with emulsion which is plain glass). No shutter yet, just black paper. No sophisticated things, just removing paper, leaving room... and after hour or two back (found that I need long exposure by mistake: my laser has mode hopping, DCG without additive as is G307 or RhB or so). Now working on cleaning laser beam and maybe solving problem with mode hopping.

It will be good to stay that way - no big mistakes any more.
