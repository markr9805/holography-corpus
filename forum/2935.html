Ok, I think that term refers to telescopes, but you get the idea...

I made my first two holograms last night and they both worked.

I purchased a kit from Integraf and followed the instructions and it all worked, but now I have questions.

I am using Integrafs student holokit with JD-4 developer and PFG-03M plates. Both exposures were with the plate-on-pile-of-coins example the mention in the instructions.

First try:

Plate on coins emulsion down. 10 second exposure. Reference beam comming in at about a 30 to 45 degree angle. I developed for about 10 seconds (plate went black almost instantly), rinsed in 200cc distilled water for 3 minutes, bleached "until clear then 10 seconds more", and rinsed in another 200cc distilled water for 3 minutes, photoflow for 20 seconds and let dry.

Probelm 1: Sucessfully identified the emulsion side for exposure, then forgot to flip it before developing. Rinse tray scratched emulsion.

Pseudoproblem 2: Nothing but a faint image only visible on emulsion side, until it started to dry out. As the emulsion dried I got a brighter and brigher image until it was reasonably bright when viewed from the glass side. I would suggest that there is a more explicit statement in the instructions that it has to dry before you will see anything.

While I was discovering this fact, I figured I would give it a try again. I though perhaps I didn't develop long enough and that was why the image was dim.

Second try:

Same as the first try, but this time I developed for 30 seconds despite the plate going dark almost instantly. Rinse and bleach cycles were the same as the first try.

Problem 1: This plate had some plastic things on the sides. I couldn't see what they were until the exposure, so I left them there. I assume they were spacers to keep the plates from scratching each other. I assume they should have been removed before exposure and certainly before developing. I pulled them after while the emulsion was wet and remove the emulsion that was under them (no big deal, it was a test after all)

This exposure had a much stronger image even while wet and since I remembered to flip the plate, I didn't scratch the emulsion while developing.

Now for the questions:

My first plate truned out yellow-green to brass colored and reasonably bright. My depth of field is less than I would have expected, but some of that seems to depend on the lighting. Stronger light and the coins in the background are sharper. I assume that this is typical. There are some artifacts that show up at one angle and are bright bands parallel with the "top" edge of the plate (the side the reference beam enters from). I assume that this is due to light entering the side of the plate and bouncing around inside. I should shaddow that next time.

My second plate came out a redder almost bronze color. It is a little sharper and perhaps a touch brighter, but it is hard to say. Why is that? This plate doesn't have the rainbow stripes, but it had the plastic things on that edge of the plate.

Now, for the reference beam... should I bring it closer to perpendicular to the plate in the future? I suspect that would give a sharper brighter image as more of the reflected light will hit the plate. What depth of field can I expect to get here? Should the background for my objects be light or dark? How critical to development time is everything? This seems much less critical than regular BW photography...

I probably have a million more questions, but this is all I can think of right now.

Thanks!

I had decent results taking the spring out of the laser and screwing the lens in almost all the way. It uncollimates, but produces something closer to nice parallel light when the laser is about 4 feet away.

For Denisyuk holograms (which I think you are making), you probably will get an inch or two depth with your set up? For transmission, you can get much more (like a few feet).

> How critical to development time is everything? This seems much less critical than regular BW photography...

Ah, yes, you are right. It's much less critical. It's hard to overexpose or overdevelop. I was a little confused by the instructions that came with my kit which said something like 'the plate will go dark in about 10 seconds', now I assumed that meant that's how long I should develop, but I think my plates weren't developing consistently across the plate. So, I've been developing for a full minute or more.

There was a great discussion in another thread about this. In essence, we're developing the fringes. There isn't a gradient or grayscale we are dealing with. That makes things much more forgiving when it comes to exposure times and development.

> jski: I still wonder why my first plate truned out green-yellow instead of red.

The color is a result of the emulsion shrinking. I believe JD-4 will always shrink the emulsion on proper exposure but I don't remember off hand.

> jski: The second plate isn't any brighter, but it is a little sharper, particularly on the more distant objects, so perhaps the longer developing time is needed.

Depth of field is related to the holograms replay bandwidth. The narrower the replay bandwidth the more depth of field you will have. The wider the bandwidth the brighter the hologram will appear. This releates to processing issues mostly.

> jski: If I am not mistaken, ideally you want the reference beam and the reflected light to be about the same intensity. You will never get that with a Denisyuk hologram,

Very true.

> jski: unless you are shooting a mirror or mirror like objects.

Flat white objects are best. Mirror like objects don't reflect the light onto the whole plate making the hologram replay with "flashes" of light on the shinyspots from different angles.

> jski: I also wonder if a more reflective background (mine was matte black) would at least help making the hologram appear brighter overall, even if the object itself wasn't any brighter.

This will make the hologram brighter. I like a flat white background often. Since you don't really need the shadows of the object to be in sharp focus, you can add a great sense of depth by using a flat white background. Start with it about 4 inches from the plate and see how you like it.

> jski: I also took a chance and painted the emulsion side of my plates with black paint. They are much easier to see now.

Go ahead and paint. When you are trying to make holograms that last forever then there are some other concerns. But for the test shots you are working on it's cool.
Dinesh Sun Dec 05, 2004 4:16 pm #7
You may want to try exposure tests and/or processing tests. Make a movable mask (say a rectangular slit 1 in wide and as long as the plate), cover the plate so that only a small portion of the plate and the object behind this portion is visible, and shoot with a given exposure, say 10 seconds. Then move the mask and shoot for 15, then 20 etc as long as the number of increments the mask can move and still remain on the plate. Develop entire plate for, say 10 seconds. Repeat procedure and develop for 20 seconds, then 30 etc. In about 2 to 3 plates you'll get a good "feel" for the parameters. There are usually so many variables, the temp of the baths, the acidity of the water wash, the age of the plate etc, that mostly you need to fine tune for your circumstances. There's no Golden Rule. However, once you get this "feel" for the parameters, you can get consistent results. All the results on colour of object, brightness etc depend on these parameters.

Depth of field also depends on the concept of coherence length of the laser. The laser is coherent only to a limited depth. This varies from 100's of feet to barely 1/2 in. Your laser needs to have a coherence length enough to go all the way to the furthest object and back to the plate. So two factors affect "depth" of field - by this I mean the ability to see, as opposed to the ability to capture, objects further back. These are beam ratio - the further away and less (overall) reflective an object is, the dimmer it'll appear - and coherence length of your laser. Unfortunately I've never used the Integraf laser so I don't know what the coherence length is, but several people on the forum have.

The plastic thingummies are plate seperators. They're called "bippies" and will be on every other plate. I've sometimes used them to show holograms. I attach 2 bippies to a glass plate so they stick up vertically upwards from the plate, and then insert the hologram between them.
