info.crew Wed Dec 27, 2006 11:12 pm #5
It reminds me the behaviour of the polymers, where fog appear when the critical volume fraction of crystalic phase is reached. For example it is the diference between the PMMA - clear and HDPE - translucent. PMMA has very little amount of crystalic phase - it's almost amorphic. I think, that the amount of crystalizable components reached some limit with respect to amount of gelatine. This limit can be (and I think is) different for each added chemical.

In the catalog I found the soluibility of the NaH2PO4, which is 850,00 g/l at 20 °C. This strong soluibility may cause also the secondary efect, that the crystalisation starts when the emulsion is almost dry and the anti-crystalisation efect of the gelatin is suppressed or totaly canceled.

Maybe the H3PO4 will work better, besause you need just around 2/3 of original NaH2PO4 amount.

But all this are just my ideas, how it may be and they are not standing on some research....
