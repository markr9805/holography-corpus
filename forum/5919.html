Cristiano_Perrucci Thu Dec 14, 2006 9:52 am #1
After several attemps to produce MBDCG with the Hans' improved formulation, I'm still in deep water with gelatine adhesion on glass surface.

Generally speaking, using the Jeff Blyth's original formulation, I never experienced any problem of dye (MB) crystalization or escape when plates are drying, but I liked very much the idea to replace acetic acid with boric acid.

Following I'm describing my working conditions:

- animal gelatine ~280 bloom

- working temperature 45C +/- 3C degrees

- plates cleaned with 80% chlorine bleach @ room temp for one night, plates were then washed with R.O. water for a few minute.

- warmed @~50C just before coating

- emulsion pH from 9.2 to 10 (I made severals attemps). Note that TMG @25% were added to hot gelatine drop by drop.

I tried to clean up plates with Glassex too after the last bath in R.O. water, without any improvement as well as presubbing clean plates with silane mixture.

Gelatine starts to detach from exposed (or unexposed) plates after ~30" when put in the first water bath (~13C degrees), detaching starts from the edges toward the centre of the plate.

Any idea? Should a make a back step to the original formulation to get that amazing coating I always got?

Thanks.

JohnFP Thu Dec 14, 2006 1:18 pm #3
I soak my glass in a 5% solution of Hydrochloric Acid overnight then wash in hot water and hand dry with a paper towel.

1. You can get Hydrochloric acid under the name of Muratic Acid used to clean brick work.

2. If you have ever cleaned window glass you will notice that when you wipe with a paper towel it should first be slightly wet, then the towel mostly dry, then completely dry to get a streak free cleaning.

3. I can use the same Hydrochloric Acid solution for over a year even to strip old emulsion (exposed or unexposed) off the glass.

I have suggested this a couple of times and I don't recall anyone trying it and reporting back. But I have NEVER had my emulsion lift from the glass.

I have the same problem with detaching gelatin with both Jeff's formula and with my changed formula. That is the reason I pre-treat the plates with silane now. The problem with silane treatment however is that the plates become hydrophobic. That makes it more difficult to use the veil coating method and also makes it quite difficult to use my Meyer Bar coating machine.

I have not noticed any problems of detachment with silanated plates. I must admit that I almost exclusively use my "in a real big hurry" method (at the bottom of the Wiki article). Perhaps there is something in the order of mixing that makes the gelatin more sticky?

How long did you wait after you treated the plates with the silane formula. Did you use my (Wiki) formula or Jeff's for silanating? Did you see a milky, sticky residue on the glass after you rubbed the silane solution on the plates?
