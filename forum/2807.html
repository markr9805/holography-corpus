Actually, PFG-03M was never ment to be used with JD-4 and bleaching.

It's emulsion and grain structure are designed for physical development such as GP2 or GP3 process. I tried JD4 a few times: holograms are hard to see. Another thing is high-power laser diodes: they tend to heat up and this affects quality of the emitted beam.

Dinesh Wed Oct 20, 2004 5:09 pm #14
The effect you're seeing is due to the specular reflection of the coin and beam ratios. It's got nothing to do with polarisation.

The light goes through the plate at 45 degrees. The incoming light illuminates the entire plate but reflects off the coin at 45 degrees the other way. The return beam from the coin therefore mostly misses the incoming reference, except at 45 degrees below the plate (and presumably coin) normal. That is it meets the reference beam that exists at 45 degrees below the coin. At normal incidence, there's very little reflectivity from the coin back to the plate and so the ratio is practiaclly non-existant. To overcome this, paint the coin a flat white so there's Lambertain reflection. If the coin were at some angle, the reflectivity of the edges would also hit the plate at a lower angle more intensely and increase the "local" beam ratio.
