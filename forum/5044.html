Here is a new thought/twist on an old idea I had.

Make a series of sqished 2D images onto a high resolution film with a white light source in a modified enlarger. After each frame is recorded the cylindrical lens in the enlarger is tilted to move the focused, squished image to the next slit area on the film. Once the film has been fully exposed process with high contrast developer as you would Black and White film. Then place the film with all the slits onto holographic table. Have an array of cylindrical lenses matching in size to the slits. Then a focusing lens to overlap the images onto the holgoraphic plate. Expose all at one time.

Dinesh Wed Sep 07, 2005 12:50 pm #4
You will probably get distortions. The way the cylindrical lens is rotating will probably create coma, at least, if not astigmatism. Also, the slit width will not be constant, which in turn, will cause effects similar to pincushion or barrel distortion in the hologram.

What's the purpose of the cylindrical lens array? The light from the high res film seems to be going straight through the lens array with no change power.

The "math" is fairly easy. It's effectively a two-lens imaging system. In practice, you have to be careful with multi-lens systems because the focal length of one lens may be within another piece of equipment. In this case, you cannot physically place a second lens.

There are many lens design programs that'll enable you to calculate all this without any need for hand calculations. I think I've got a sample from some convention, which will enable you to at least see if it's feasible. Even though you may not be able to save, you may be able to print out the various lens parameters. If you're interested email me and I'll try and find it.
