Great to see such high quality holograms being made and shown on this forum.

I just came back from the Holography 2005 conference in Bulgaria:

[http://www.optics.bas.bg/holo05.htm](http://www.optics.bas.bg/holo05.htm)

I have put up some photos here:

[http://www.bazargan.org/holo05](http://www.bazargan.org/holo05)

For me the highlights from a display point of view were the following:

Kubota

=====

He produced an astonishing full color reflection hologram, bettering his previous ones. It's a 3 colour H1/H2 transfer reflection, made on a Slavich panchromatic film. He is one of those guys who can get everything right in a hologram. You can see a movie clip here:

[http://www.bazargan.org/kubota.mov](http://www.bazargan.org/kubota.mov)

Geola

====

This is a very significant development. Geola have produced a printer that takes 3D data and creates a full colour reflection hologram. The process is similar to that of Zebra. They write the hologram pixel by pixel directly onto the film, in reflection format. Pixels are 0.8 or 1.6 mm in size.

Difference with Zebra are:

- Geola produce single parallax images

- It is full colour. Zebra is I think single color

- They use pulse, so no movement problems

- Very cometitive pricing - starting around $200 for 8"X10"

Watch these guys.......

Quality is good, but the single parallax does detract from ultimate realism. The primary market is advertising. They are marketing to printers, etc. They go up to around 1X1.5m, and the price is (very roughly) $4000.

Here is a movie of the hologram they had on show:

[http://www.bazargan.org/geola.mov](http://www.bazargan.org/geola.mov)

I found this abstract which might provide some hints:

[http://link.aip.org/link/?OPSUA3/79/786/1](http://link.aip.org/link/?OPSUA3/79/786/1)

<

Optics and Spectroscopy -- November 1995 -- Volume 79, Issue 5, pp. 786-790

Referenceless selectogram and its properties

Yu. N. Denisyuk

Vavilov State Optical Institute, St. Petersburg, 199034 Russia

(Received March 1, 1995)

A method for recording and reconstruction of three-dimensional images of objects using the so-called referenceless selectogram is proposed. In line with this method, the radiation scattered by an object is filtered by a horizontal slit and then split into two components using a diffraction grating that is placed immediately adjacent to a thick photosensitive medium. The interference pattern resulting from the interaction of components formed by the grating is recorded by a photosensitive layer in the form of a three-dimensional deep hologram. It is shown that the selectogram thus recorded may be put in correspondence with a window or a pupil through which must pass radiation used in its reconstruction. Thus, the pupil is represented by one of the virtual images formed by the grating at the stage of recording. The radiation passing through the pupil may have an arbitrary amplitude and phase distribution. In accordance with this property, the selectogram can be reconstructed using radiation scattered by a diffuse screen placed in the pupil of the system. Processes of recording and reconstruction of a selectogram are analyzed for the cases where recording is performed in coherent and incoherent light. It is shown that, in the case where a selectogram is recorded using coherent radiation, the wave field of radiation reconstructed by it reproduces the wave field of radiation within a certain random phase shift. In the case where recording is carried out using incoherent radiation, phases of points of the reconstructed image coincide with phases of points of the diffuser used for selectogram reconstruction and the intensity distribution is distorted to some extent. It is shown that the structure of a three-dimensional hologram recorded by the axial point of an object is independent of the wavelength because of the achromatic effect of a diffraction grating. The advantages of the referenceless selectogram under consideration are minimum requirements on the coherence of radiation used for recording and reconstruction and the insensitivity to vibrations of the experimental setup. ©1995 MAIK/Interperiodika

>

Dinesh Wed Jun 08, 2005 12:17 pm #12
It's a sort of one-step-reflection rainbow technique. The object references itself by having it's wavefield split by a grating after illumination. The "pupil" is the slit used to represent the wavefield of the original construction. The comments about the effects of coherent and incoherent illumination are obvious as is the comment about the diffuser. The comment about the "axial point" is a little vague, since a "point" cannot be "axial" (no dimension) however, I think the point (sorry!) is that there is no distortion perpendicular to the slit because the original grating split the wavefield parallel to the slit and the slit itself, as a pupil, inhibits this direction.

I understand the selectogram actually is a transmission hologram technique:

> ...the necessity to use a point-like reference source when recording a hologram is one of the most essential obstacles to its application in 3-D-imaging of the objects and scenes illuminated by natural light. Trying to solve this problem and the problem of the creation of holograms that would permit, like a conventional photograph, the reconstruction of 3-D images illuminated by an extended luminous source of light, we have proposed a method of so-called selectograms. According to this method, a 3-D image of an object is formed from the light of an extended luminous background thanks to the angular selectivity property of the grating of a deep 3-D hologram. The experiments on the selectogram recording were carried out with the help of the technique of pseudodeep holograms. This technique allows one to imitate the characteristics of a deep recording when using conventional thin-layered light-sensitive materials.Further study of this method led us to the conclusion that it is possible to form an extended reference source directly from the object wave by splitting this wave into two parts with the help of a diffraction grating, which fits snugly to the surface of alight-sensitive layer. The method has been referred to as “reference-free selectogram”. In the experiments, the selectograms were recorded both in a real thick-layered light-sensitive material and by using the pseudodeep hologram technique.(...)It should be noted here that unlike the case of the Lippmann color photography in which the twin wave W propagates in the direction opposite to the object wave W, in the case of the reference-free selectogram the wave l propagates in the same with the object wave l direction. (...) while in the case of the Lippmann color photograph the antinode surfaces of a standing wave are parallel to the surface of the photographic layer, in the case of the reference-free selectogram they are close to being perpendicular to this surface.

Yuri N. Denisyuk, From Lippmann Photography to Selectograms via White Light Holography, Journal of Imaging Science and Technology, Vol. 41, no 3, May/June 1997, p. 207-208

However, it does seem that it's possible to do this in a reflection geometry.

Some excerpts on selectograms:

“Unlike a hologram, the selectogram is recorded by using an extended reference source of light. In particular, such a reference source can be formed from the light scattered by the very object that is registered on the selectogram. Since in this case the reference light and the light scattered by the object are mutually coherent, the volume images of a remote object can be registered on the selectogram when using the light characterized by the low degree of coherence.”

Denisyuk, Holographic 3-D imaging of remote and close objects, and HOEs for it, SPIE 2152, p.197

> “Among other useful properties of the reference-free selectogram its insensitivity to the vibration at the stage of the recording should be noted. Indeed, unlike the case of a conventional hologram, in this case the selectogram should be recorded without using an interferometer and the object wave is split into two components just before it reaches the light-sensitive material. Under such conditions, the basic requirement to the setup use for the selectogram recording is the absence of horizontal shifts of the grating in respect to the light-sensitive material, which is not difficult to attain.”(Denisyuk, Window effect of a 3-D deep hologram as a base of 3-D imaging by means of a selectogram (Reference-free selectogram), SPIE 2406, p.72

Denisyuk stresses on the similarity with Lippmann photography:

> “When comparing the scheme of the recording of a Lippmann color photograph and that of a reference-free selectogram, it becomes obvious that there is a striking similarity between these methods. In fact, in both cases optical elements that double the recorded wave field are used. The only difference between these two cases are different directions of the propagation of the wave generated by the optical elements. Indee, in the case of the Lippmann color photograph the wave generated by the optical element propagates in the opposite direction to the object wave. As a result, the recorded structure is characterized by a high value of spectral selectivity.In the case of a reference-free selectogram, the wave generated by the optical element propagates at a relatively small angle to the object wave. As a result, the recorded structure having a high value of the angular selectivity is more likely capable to reproduce the spatial distribution of the intensity of the object wave. There is no doubt that some other intermediate variants of the recording when spectral and spatial distributions of the intensity of the object wave are reproduced simultaneously should also be possible”Denisuyk, Ganzherli, Savostyanenko, 3-D imaging by means of a reference-free selectogram recorded in a thick-layered light-sensitive material, SPIE 2026, p. 190

I understand a selectogram essentially is based on a thick recording layer (ideally rather mm than um), not necessarily ultra-high resolution though. By the way, I guess that's where those thick glycerol-gelatine gel (DCG) layers, which don't require any processing, found an interesting application. Other materials suggested implied photorefractive crystals, Reoxan and other photopolymer material...

"Diffraction Analysis of Dielectric Surface-Relief Gratings"

Moharam and Gaylord, Optical Society of America, vol. 72, no. 10/October 1982, pp 1385 - 1391

"Limits of Scalar Diffraction Theory for Conductingf Gratings"

Douglas A. Gremaux and Neal C. Gallagher, App Opt. vol 32, no. 11 1993, pp 1948-1953

"Diffraction charcteristics of Surface Relief Gratings"

Mool C. Gupta and S. T. Peng, App Opt, vol 32, no 16 1993, pp 2911 - 2917

These papers show that, depending on the surface profile and depth vs frequency Raman-Nath diffraction takes on the characterisation of Bragg diffraction. There's even polarisation-dependant efficiencies, something that doesn't exist in standard Bragg diffraction a la Kogelnik (despite so many people being convinced otherwise!)
