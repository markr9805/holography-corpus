Kaveh Fri Apr 08, 2005 2:51 pm #11
I thought I would plot some lines on the CIE diagram to understand what is going on. I am uploading it as it might be interesting for others. What you have done is painted the object in essentially three ways - no paint, paint reflecting green, and paint reflecting blue.

If you record your hologram with no thickness and refractive index change, then your final hologram will show a mixture of 457, 514, and colors in between. By colors in between, I mean any color formed by mixing these colors in different strengths. A rough idea of what these colors will look like can be seen by plotting a straight line on the CIE diagram. See the line marked 0%. I mean zero swelling.

![Image](http://www.bazargan.org/Sites/cie-for-john.jpg)

Now if the hologram is swelled, then both wavelengths will shift up, and we get a new line. I have calculated these new wavelengths by simple increasing them by a percentage, and drawn the lines connecting the new wavelengths. You can see that you can never get white with this combination of wavelengths. The lines are not separated enough spectrally. But if you look around 12-16% swelling, you can see that green, yellow and orange are possible, and that's what we are seeing.

(CIE diagram courtesy of Gernot Hoffman - [http://www.fho-emden.de/~hoffmann/](http://www.fho-emden.de/~hoffmann/))

I think I have an idea how to incorporate the blue color into this technique. Of course it involves doing more testing of paints, developing a swelling/shrinking technique, pulling hair, etc. But here it goes.

Already I have a paint that reflects only 457nm that I swell 16% to 530nm to get green.

I have a paint that reflects only 514nm that I swell 16 % to 596nm to get orange.

I also have a white that reflects all colors pretty nicely.

I have to find a paint that reflects only 488nm but of course after processing and swelled 16 % it will replay at 566nm (near yellow)

Now here is the technique.

Paint the parts on the object to be a final color of orange with paint that reflects only 514.

Paint the parts on the object to be a final color of green with a paint that reflects only 457.

Paint the parts on the object to be a final color of blue with paint that reflects only 488.

Now here is where it gets tricky.

Swell (technique to be determined) the emulsion such that it produces a 100nm shrinkage after processing. Expose the plate to 488nm.

Now take away the swelling.

Expose to 457nm and 514nm sequentially.

Process the plate so that the replay is shifted 16% toward the red as in my original technique.

The 457nm shifts 16% to green (530nm), the 514nm shifts 16% to orange (596nm) and the shrunken by 100nm 488nm (388) shift 16% to blue (450nm).

I hope this is not to confusing.
