Good idea for low powered lasers, and only if the beampath is terminated. Basic laser safety proceedures include waiting for the laser to come on to verify that the beam that spits out of the laser is properly contained. That's why there are key switches, head shutters and time delays. 8)

In the mid-eighties, I had a studio in the master bedroom suite of my apartment. There were two 4x8' tables in the bedroom, a cleanroom hood (bought from Bob Schlesinger) in the walk-in closet, and a fume hood built into the corner shower stall. Ed Wesley saw the set up (with Vince DiBiasi, and unfortunately on the very evening the love of my life left me ).

The lasers, an S-P 107 and an S-P 124B, were on a shelf near the bed in the second bedroom, and their beams went through holes in the wall to their respective tables. I could turn them on as I got out of bed in the morning, and they would be ready to go when I was done with the morning routine. Perhaps, in hindsight, I was focused on the wrong turn-ons!

> BobH: The lasers, an S-P 107 and an S-P 124B....

Since we have been talking about safety on another thread, I would like to share an observation about elderly HeNe's. One day I turned on my SP-124, and there was a sustained HV arc inside the head. Luckily I wasn't touching it. It seems that the insulation of one of the HV wires had become brittle over time, and developed a crack. It just so happened this cracked area was pressed over a metal area of the frame. So for those of us who have older HeNe's with HV wiring inside the head (SP-124, 125, 127, etc.), it might be a good idea to check your wiring occasionally and try to keep it away from metal areas. Has anyone else had an experience like that?
