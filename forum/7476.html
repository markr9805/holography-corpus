Pianomaxx Thu Aug 07, 2008 10:16 pm #1
Hello Holographers,

after some time of absence I recorded some transmissions holos, for later copying, in our "Holo-Lab" at the University. The lab is quite well equipped with a variable beamsplitter, a simple lightmeter, good pinholes, and -the best- a whole bunch of old AGFA 8E75 (some HD) and 10E75 Plates, which expired in 2001. However, they still seem to work, and the quality looks comparable to the PFG-01 from my experience, but yet not perfect:

-Exposure 60sec (because only ~5mW HeNe, and filtered w pinholes)

-Beam Ratio R:O approx. 2:1

-Development 15sec (BW-Film-Developer Dokumol, with Hydrochinon+NaOH, diluted 1:7), bleaching in FerricSodiumEDTA+KBR+Acetic Acid (recipe by Jeff Blyth?) until clear (~1-2min). It turned out that image quality depends strongly on a short development time, for development times longer that 30sec, only the brightest spots would remain visible.

There is still one problem: If we reconstruct the image, the reference beam looks so bright in the center of the plate (looks like scattering on a milky glass), that the object is barely visible there. Towards the borders, the image gets much clearer. So we are currently discussing whether to increase or decrease the reference/object intensity ratio. Intuitively I guess that R-beam and O-beam were not centered on each other, so that in the center of the R-beam the O-beam was too weak, while in the borders, the O-beam dominated. But increasing the object beam intensity stands in contrast to my good experiences with R:O = 2:1 or more, so only exposure time would remain as the critical parameter. What do you think, could also over-exposure cause this strong "reference-beam-scattering"?

Thanks and best regards,

Max

BobH Fri Aug 08, 2008 1:07 am #5
> It turned out that image quality depends strongly on a short development time, for development times longer that 30sec, only the brightest spots would remain visible.

Was this with the same energy at the film plate? If so, I'd spread the reference beam out waaay more like the others have said. That will lower the power at the film plate location, but you can just reset the beam ratio to whatever you like. Then increase the development time to compensate for the lower power, or increase the exposure time (if stability allows).
