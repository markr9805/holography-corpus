Say I was going to build a new house. How do you think I should I design a holography lab into it? Even if money is no problem. Money is a problem but holography more important.
Colin Kaminski Sat Oct 20, 2007 2:01 pm #2
I would need two rooms. One 6 feet larger than the table in both directions. So for a 4 x 10' table the room would be 10 x 16'. This room would be placed under positive pressure with HEPA filtration. It would be painted flat black or better yet Thelo Violet. There would be the sets of lighting, one bright safe light for loading film. One dim safe light aimed at the floor for walking in and out of the room and white light for setup. At one end of the room would be 1' deep shelving with glass doors from floor to ceiling to store optics.

The other room would be 8x8' and have a commercial stainless 3 basin sink from a bar. (Triple Dip is the industry name.) Next to the sink and on the adjacent wall would be kitchen counters. On the wall behind the sink would be a HEPA work station. Next to the HEPA work station would be a counter with an exhaust hood above from a commercial stove. A small electric oven built into one of the counters would be helpful as would as many cabinets as would fit.

Well, this would be a start...
Tom B. Sun Oct 21, 2007 4:45 am #3
Put the lab in an unheated sub-basement, ideally completely encased in concrete with insulated trapdoor entry and filtered air intake as recommended by Colin. Pretend its a fallout shelter Build in a geologically stable area, far from major traffic arteries. Stock up on several month's supply of canned food and lentils so you won't have to leave the lab to fight off mutant zombies until your holograms are perfected.
