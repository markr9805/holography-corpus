Hans Sat Apr 01, 2006 8:05 am #1
Yesterday Kris and Jean and I had a mini PCG meeting at my house. The goal of the project was to make and test Blyth emulsions and test homemde MBDCG emulsions at my humble garage lab. It was a real pleasure to watch experienced holographers like Kris and Jean at work, and we all three learned a lot during the day. We made four Halide plates and exposed them. And we exposed two MBDCG plates. Both according to processes of Jeff Blyth. Here are the findings:

SILVER HALIDE:

- I had pre-coated and hardened the four plates the day before.

- I had pre-treated the plates by subbing them in 1% gelatin/.1% Chrome Alum. Because I never was able to make plates sticky with silane pre-treatment.

- We further pre-preated two plates with a Na2CO3 solution. This was suggested by Jeff Blyth, to encourage absorbtion of the following chemicals.

- We then proceeded to load all the plates with AgNO3 and dried thouroughly with a hair drier, set to a moderately cool temperature.

- LiBr was loaded.

- Plates were washed.

- Plates were sensitized with TEA (4%).

- No drying was done in-between LiBr/TEA treatment.

- Ascorbate developer was prepared.

- The laser was a D&S laser, brightess of about 100uW/cm2

RESULTS:

- The first test plate (exposed 30 seconds) initially turned half dark instantly when immersed in the ascorbate. Then it proceeded to increase in density over time. We washed the plate after two minutes, but discovered the emulsion had separated from most of the plate.

- For the second plate (exposed 30 seconds) we decided to use the less aggressive JD-2 developer. The emulsion remained attached to the glass without problems. During development hardly any density appeared (light grey). After bleaching and drying, a dim (very green) hologram was visible. Great depth and reasonably sharp.

- The third plate we decided to expose half of the plate 15 seconds light and half of the plate for 45 seconds. The 45 seconds exposure certainly got better density in the JD-2, but nothing was visible on that side. The 15 seconds side had a dim green/blue image.

- We had a feeling the plates were shrinking excessively in the JD-2, so decided to try pyrogallol developer for the last plate. The emulsion quickly separated and nothing was visible on the remains.

-It was difficult to make a conclusion about the benificial effect of the pre-treatment with Na2CO3 because of the two failed tests where the emulsion had separated.

-The Blyth emulsion certainly looks promissing, but there are a lot of variables to control. Also, because of the many steps during the process, it is difficult to keep the initially pretty gelatin layer clean of scratches and spots.

MBDCG:

- Jean had brought some pre-prepared homemade MBDCG plates.

- We exposed the first one with Kris's ATI laser (30mW), only middle of the plate exposed for 5 minutes for maximum brightness in that area. That way we could also see the effect of less exposure in the area where the light intensity tapers of to the sides. The result was simply stunning. The exposed part of the plate was super bright and broadband. The brightest area's re-played in white!! Also very sharp. And because the gelatin did not have to be touched, the gelatin layer remained clean and flat. No spots and no scratches. The one scary part was the blow drying the plate, where these interesting white patterns appear over the whole plate. They disappeared again after more drying.

- The second plate was exposed over it's entire surface for 30 minutes with my D&S laser for 30 minutes at about 100uW/cm2. Unfortunately, the object had moved during exposure and it became a shadowgram. The hologram was not broadband and re-played in a green/blue colour. The parts that supported the plate looked bright and clean in the hologram and the aluminum foil in the background was also clearly visible.

RESULTS:

- The MBDCG looks like a great method to me, for doing in house. There are less variables to control.

- Because the emulsion only has to be touched once during application of the gelatin to the plate, it stays clean and free of artifacts.

- The correctly exposed area on the first plate certainly looked bright!!! The hologram was clearly visible, even in my green safetly light. Even when the light was shining at the hologram from the wrong direction (from below). In fact, it might even be brighter than a Ultimate plate hologram Kris and I made during one of his workshops at his house.

- The broadband effect on the first plate was very pleasing to look at.

- The sharpness was also super.

- Unfortunately, we decided to try what the effect of Methanol on the hologram was, because I have a lot of that in house. So we re-processed to see what would happen with my Methanol instead of the IsoPropanol. The hologram disappeared. I will try tonight to make it come back again with a Isopropanol treatment.

I think I will side step for now and concentrate more on doing MBDCG. I will order the 650nm laser (50mW) from ATI to ensure no mode-hopping during the longer exposure times.

One more pleasant thing about the MBDCG is that during processing the plate, after exposure, only chemicals are used that actually make my garage cleaner (water and isopropylalcohol) instead of more chemically contaminated.

Although I have found an article here about the incredible dangers of H2O : [http://www.dhmo.org/facts.html](http://www.dhmo.org/facts.html)

Many thanks to Kris and Jean.

THG or Tetramethylguanidine note:

If one were to list all the common organic bases in order of their strengths as bases or "alkaline substances"

then ammonia might be in the middle above pyridine and analine and below triethanolamine but right at the top of the list would be .........

TMG.

So adding acetic acid to bring its pH down to about 9 still leaves it active but not inclined to evaporate off out of the gelatin or to cause its ungelling

like caustic soda solution.

I am currently testing out a bath of MB-TMG-chromate for treating dry coatings of pure unhardened gelatin. So you can put plates in coated in daylight years ago, dry them , and shoot them the next day.

I will be putting it in a paper in the 7th International Symposium on Display Holography next July with the formulation.

Jeff
