JohnFP Fri Jan 04, 2008 6:24 pm #2
Hey Jussy baby,

Polarization is one of the cool things in holography, ya ya!!!

You're going to get "waste" no matter what you do unless you control your ratios by taking from one beam and giving to the other. To attenuate either with a filter or 1/2 wave plate will be wasting light as only light polarized in the same direction will create the interference fringes. So if you filter out 10% of the light or change the plolarization by 10% (off the 90) then you will be "wasting light". But if you take that 10% and give it to the other beam then there is no waste.

(added)So to recap, a variable beam splitter would be best.

(added also) I used a variable density silver reflecting attenuater to do what you propose. It was very cheap but wasted light.

Hope this is clear...

Peace

John

Jeffrey Weil Tue Jan 15, 2008 4:28 pm #9
Here's what I did when I had no case for waveplates.

Plates for 632.8 are expensive and not really around on the surplus market, but waveplates for 670 and stuff like that are around.

Get one for 5 or 10 bucks somewhere, glass, coated all that. I used to get mine from meradeth or mwk. Its been a while but I'm sure someplace you can still get them cheap.

now, put the plate in your beam, after that put a polarizer. Now, here's the trick. Tilt the waveplace around testing with the polarizer till you can extinguish the beam. When the waveplate is tilted just right to the beams direction of travel it will operate correctly.

Now you can just rotate the beam like a normal waveplate and it will be just fine. Its just not at normal incidence to the beam anymore.

It works this way. When you tilt the waveplate the beam has to travel though a thicker cross section of the plate, the further the tilt off normal the "thicker" the plate looks to the beam. At a certain point it will be a resonent thickness and all will be well.

Good luck.

Jeffrey Weil
