BobH Wed Oct 01, 2008 1:22 am #2
I would not recommend DCG for making stereograms, unless you are already making excellent DCG holograms every day. I worked briefly with Don Broadbent and Eric Van Hammersvelt to make stereogram DCGs. Eric was in charge of filming and had built the film transport mechanisms before I joined the team. Don headed the effort and was in charge of coating and processing DCG on strips of mylar. I made the stereogram masters on Silver Halide 4x5" plates and exposed the DCG in a step and repeat system as reflection transfers (H2s). I still have some in my collection, from 1987 when we did them.

Exposing stereograms into DCG is very risky. You'd likely get lots of dim exposures, and only one will cause you to want to toss the plate. The time to run the set will be so long the film will probably change during the cycle. Running a slit across the surface of DCG might damage it. You'll have lots of time into each plate as you watch a simple little processing streak wreck the whole thing.

Use silver halide plates for mastering stereograms. You'll be happy not wasting time and the images will look their best.

Take Bobs words to heart! I have done this and nothing is worse than shooting half of the holo and getting a black line or dim image from motion on the table, becuase you don't know until the end. Here are a couple more hints:

1) Plan for more slits from the start. Frank describes 8, which is ok for tests, but I found even 30 was too few for good parallax. (my opinion)

2) I would try to modify a DLP projector instead of using an LCD just because it's easier to "project" the images to a diffuse screen and no polarization alignment issues. This requires some electronics aptitude.

3) If you have will shoot a small (4x5") master and your slit width is say ~3mm then you can only fit ~30 images total accross the 4". High quality sterograms can have many hundreds.

Good luck
