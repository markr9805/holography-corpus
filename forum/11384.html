Din Sun Nov 03, 2024 8:54 am #4
It looks a lot like Newton's rings (below). Usually a Fizeau is meant to test surface flatness, so it's possible that your geometry led to two surfaces in close contact, leading to Newton's rings. If these are Newton's rings, then it may be possible to make a hologram, albeit a thin hologram because of limited coherence length since your fringe contrast is not high.

Try a Michelson since that is the best test of coherence. If you get fringes with equal path lengths, vary one path until the fringes disappear, and the difference is the coherence length. In this case you can make a hologram. If the Michelson fringes keep shifting suddenly then you have mode hopping and therefore multiple modes. In this case you can still make a hologram, but the hologram will have superimposed on it a series of diagonal lines across the image (what Colin Kaminski called "sliced bread")
