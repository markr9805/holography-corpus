Hans Sun Jul 29, 2007 1:22 pm #2
Ideally the mirrors should be front surfaced mirrors to prevent double reflections. In a normal mirror, the side that reflects is on the back of the glass. So, with a normal mirror, the light can reflect off the front of the glass and off the back side that has the silver/aluminum.

Perhaps a really thick normal mirror would work. Then you would be able to block off the unwanted reflection that comes from the front side of the glass.
ErichRose Sun Jul 29, 2007 6:28 pm #3
Front surface mirrors are pretty important to doing anything beyond the simplest single beam set up. Equal beam paths can be quite important with most lasers. The mirrors are not always expensive and can be purchased from surplus suppliers. You only need small ones for directing laser beams 2-3cm square. But for directing expanded beams you need larger ones, maybe 15cm square or more. You can sometimes salvage large ones from old copiers, rear-projection TVs and overhead projectors. Make dust covers for them and learn how to clean them (No you don't just wipe them off with a soft cloth!) and they can last as long as you need.

Trying to mask-off the second reflection from a rear surface mirror is a real pain. Also keep in mind that every bounce, even off a good rear surface mirror, will knock the power of the beam down a few percent. So the goal is still to have the least number of bends in the beam paths.
