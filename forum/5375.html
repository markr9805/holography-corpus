Now it looks as though straight healthy ubiquitous ascorbic acid powder would do the job instead.

Ron Michael Wed Apr 05, 2006 10:06 pm #7
Paints, alodine, turns out I've got chromates laying around and I've been reading about the problem with hexavalent chromium (a carcinogen ) in the ground water supply and also about using in-situ remediation efforts instead of letting this stuff loose in my environment. One method I researched looks pretty good and easy enough for us in holography is to reduce the Cr(VI) to trivalent chromium with hydrogen sulfite in a low ph solution. The dichromate bleach solution generally is around ph 2.4 as given by mix formula. To Remediate the dichromate bleach solution or any chromate/dichromate solution as long as the ph is below 4 and best below 3 is to add sodium metasulfite (metabisulfite Na2S2O5 is available at photography formulary etc) until the solution is bright green and then extra to be sure or you can work out the formula if you happen to know the amount of chromates in solution you have. As Jeff mentioned, Na2S2O5 in water hydrolysis forms Sodium hydrogen sulfite (bisulfite) NaHSO3. The insoluble relative non-toxic chromium III hydroxide settles out as precipitate So you can then pour off the solution and find a chemical dump etc for the precipitate. If you have a ph meter then by all means add some sodium bisulfate or sulfuric acid before hand to adjust the ph to below 3 to ensure reduction. My understanding is that most but sometimes not all Cr(VI) will be reduced to Cr(III) using the acid reduction with sulfites method. Other methods of reducing using Fe(II) or Mn(II) were discussed as a remediation effort. Here is a reference to chromium chemistry including using zinc to reduce Cr(VI) to Cr(III) :

[http://www.chemguide.co.uk/inorganic/tr ... omium.html](http://www.chemguide.co.uk/inorganic/transition/chromium.html)
