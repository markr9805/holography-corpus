Hans Tue Jan 23, 2007 11:32 am #2
> Is it necessary that we should expand the laser beam? Will it work otherways?

The laser beam needs to be expanded so that the light that is reflected off the mirror can reach the plate as well of the light that reaches the playe directly.

> Also in the pic, its shown that light should reflect in the holoplate at 90 degree., Is there any significance in chosing 90 degree angle??

Yes, the angle between the reflected beam and the direct beam determines the amount of lines per mm on your grating. The bigger this angle, the more lines per mm. It is always a good idea to choose the angle's such that they are the same with relation to the normal of the holographic plate. That way the interference planes are perpendicular to the emulsion layer. Theoretically, any shrinkage or expansion of the gelatin layer during processing will have no effect on the final result then. In that case you can be sure that the amount of lines per mm will be the same as the one you calculated.

> Will changing the angle will increase or decrease the diffraction efficiency?

Yes. But this depends on the type of film you use. Some types of film have very high diffraction efficiencies at very closely distanced interference planes. Other types of emulsions have the opposite effect.
JohnFP Tue Jan 23, 2007 11:42 am #3
Funny, I interpreted the questions differently. Everything Hans stated is totally correct and I am only presenting a different way of explaining it.

No the beam does not need to be expanded or expanding. You can use the raw laser beam (or expanded and collimated or converging) in the same set up and get a diffraction grating.

Again Han's is correct for a specific grating. But if you just wanted any grating I would P polarize the light (assuming this is a birds eye view) and have the plate and mirror oriented at 56 degrees (brewster's) to suppress internal reflections.

[It's quite funny: You can reconstruct the holo with either mirror, and second mirror appears holographically! ]

The idea that you can make a hologram of a mirror and then have it behave exactly like a mirror is one of the great magical bits of holography. It's very much what HOEs are all about.

I remember looking at a reflection hologram I had just made. It was of a pair of dark sunglasses and when I reached up to adjust the halogen lamp I saw my fingers silhouetted in the reflected image. It took a moment for me to realize it wasn't on the surface of the glass plate, but was actaully on the holographic surface of the sunglasses. I have put magnifying lenses into still lifes just to get that wonderful effect of being able to see the magnified image as it really appeared in the real world.

It's all too much fun. You guys do the math while I start exposing plates...[/quote]
