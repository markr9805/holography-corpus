Because I was afraid that Fuji would stop producing, I just tried some two year old Filmotec HF-55 film and made a H2, and compared it to fresh Fuji Holographic Film.

The HF-55 film is the low resolution variant of Filmotec. Sensitivity is 250uJ, and I guess that can be bettered with a pre-soak&wash in TEA. They also sell the HF-53 film that has double the resolution.

Both appear at the same brightness, though there was some shrinkage in the Filmotec film towards the blue end of the spectrum.

So, now that we know that Fuji stays in keeps producing we have two inexpensive alternatives for hobby holography.

Fuji for red laser light, green laser light and colour Denisyuk style holograms.

Filmotec for fast prototyping and testing with green laser light. And large volume production.

Here is a comparison of prices.

Fuji: 10 sheets of 30x40cm at 294 Euro (this price includes shipping and tax). This comes down to 3.68 Euro per sheet of 10x15cm.

Filmotec: 5 sheets of 50x60cm at 162 Euro (this price does not include shiping and tax). This comes down to 1.62 Euro per sheet of 10x15cm.

PFG-01/VRP (from Geola): 5 sheets of 30x40cm at 105 Euro (this price does not include shiping and tax). So that's 2.63 Euro per sheet of 10x15cm.

PFG-01/VRP (from Integraf): 10 sheets of 30x40cm at 250 Dollar (this price does not include shiping and tax). This comes down to 2.28 Euro per sheet of 10x15cm.

I think all of these films have similar characteristics. Fuji and Filmotec have the added benefit of being so much easier to laminate than PFG-01.

PFG-01 and VRP have the benefit of being almost twice as sensitive over Fuji and Filmotec, but are rather unsuitable for beginners because of difficult index matching/laminating to glass.

Filmotec also sells a higher resolution film that is very suitable for reflection and Denisyuk work and costs the same as the HF-55.

Both Fuji and Filmotec keep very well, even when unrefrigerated. Kris Meerloo recently tested 1.5 year old Fuji film with great success. And the Filmotec that I tried was 2 years old and also unrefrigerated.

So, my personal conclusion would be:

PFG-01/VRP is the hands down winner of speed. It is in the middle of the price range. PFG-01 keeps very well for long times, but VRP seems to spoil rather soon when not refrigerated.

Filmotec is the hands down winner for beginners wanting to get their feet wet in holography and those who have a good green laser available. It's the most inexpensive film currently on the market.

Fuji is the hands down winner for those of us that often switch laser colours (panchromatic). Or those who want to do colour.

All three films are not sensitive to the light from amber Philips deco leds. These are lamps that have the same fittings as standard 230V halogen bulbs. I bathe my garage in yellow light with them. No apparent fogging.

Hans Sat Aug 25, 2007 8:37 am #6
Below are the two test shots. The smaller one is the Filmotec test. It was slightly over exposed (slightly milky because of over exposure and dirt from singlet lens is more visible due to same reason). I still have trouble photographing holograms. Some reflections of my self are visible in the larger Fuji hologram.

Both holograms appear at about the same brightness when viewed in a normal halogen lamp. Both films reveal about the same detail when inspected from short distance.

The Fuji is 10x15cm. I made the Filmotec on a smaller piece because I only have a few test sheets that Kris Meerloo sent to me.

![Image](http://hologramhans.com/pictures/FujiFilmotec.jpg)

Both H2 holograms were made as follows:

- Laser: Coherent C315M.

Reference Beam Power: 20uW

Beam Ratio: 2/1

Exposure time: 10 seconds

Developer: TJ1 Ascorbic Developer. Freshly made for each hologram.

Wash: Destilled Water from a de-humidifier.

Bleach: EDTA Bleach.

Index Matching: With regular lamp oil on 2mm thick glass. Emulsion side on the glass.

Film Type: Fuji Panchromatic (larger). Filmotec HF-55 (smaller).

Next week I will order the high resolution film from Filmotec and see how the H2's will come out.
