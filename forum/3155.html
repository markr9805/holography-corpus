Dinesh Fri Feb 25, 2005 4:35 pm #2
The angle of view of a stereogram, or any H2, is determined by the size of the H1. The larger that is, the larger is the angle of view. The H2 is seen through a "virtual window" of the H1. Thus the angle of view is determined by lines from both edges of the H1, through the same edge of the H2. So if the H1 is 8" x 10" and the H2 is 4"x 5" and placed 6" away, the side-to side angle of view is 36 degrees (assuming portrait geometry). If you make a large H1 and place it fairly near the H2, you get a wide angle.

[quote:35b45cedea="JohnFP"]Single beam reflections have almost 180 degrees of viewing. Although somewhat or rather grossly distorted at the extreems.[/quote:35b45cedea]

The angle of view of a single beam Denisyuk depends on the cone of light given off by the object beam. The wider the throw of the object reflection, the wider the angle of view. If the object is illuminated so as to throw light at 180 degrees, then this will be the angle through which it can be seen. The distortion depends on the angle of the reference beam. The reason it seems distorted at the extreme ends is because off the extreme azimuthal angles being used to reconstruct. So, if you shot an object as a single beam illuminated so as to spread the object light as wide as possible, then so long as you didn't actually twist the plate, there'll be no distortion. In other words, if you reconstructed with a beam same as the ref, kept the plate still and walked around it, there'll be no distortion. However, if you hold the plate in your hand and twist it from side to side, you're effectively changing the azimuthal angle of the reconstruction and you get distortion.
