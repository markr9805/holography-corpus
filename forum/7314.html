JohnFP Thu May 01, 2008 11:03 am #3
> After the first exposure and removing from the water soak, there was a distictive speration in swelling, the unexposed side was much more swollen

This is quite expected. You now see what happens between the construtive fringes and the destructive fringes. The contructive being the ones that receive light and the destrutive one being the ones that do not receive light.

> During the water bath I noticed a fair amount of AmDi in the tray. I have to ask, is part of the color shift possibly due to the reduction in AmDi?

Let's think about this. If you remove some of the AmDi prior to exposure, then the film has slightly less volume. After exposure there is less removal of material, thus this would tend to help shift toward the red (less shrinking). So if your trying to achieve more red shift this is a good thing. If you were doing comparison tests, I would say you have to mix up some AmDi in that water soak at the same concentration of your emulsion to see if the soak alone is the major contributor or if it is the removal of material.

What gets me is this seems to be backwards. Are you sure you went from blue (no soaking) to green (soaking) or could you have that backwards? If I take two exact emulsions and they dry to 10um. And then take one and soak it in water to swell it and only let it dry down to 13um. And then expose both exactly the same, after processing and drying to a final thickness of 6um for each I would assume the more swollen one, the one that was water soaked prior to exposure, would be bluer the the non soaked one. Am I missing something here? Dave doesn't your soaked hologram come out bluer the the first shot?

If in fact I am correct and the soaking hologram should be bluer, then you do not want to remove material, in fact, I think you may actually want to add material (AmDi) at least to it's original level if not a little more. I will have to wait for Dave to reply to make sure I am thinking this through correctly.

Isn't the first exposure to create red or green? Here I am little confused (what else is new) Isn't the swelling is what is shift it red? But isn't a higher exposure shift it blue

3rd color you will get ( red) come from the 2nd exposer stepping on the first... but the variables will play in what you get in the second exposer and the stepping on the first exposer.

so let’s say your first is a angle of 56 deg and we shift the angle 3+ or 3- will shift it to the red or the blues will enforce the playback made with the 2nd exposer + (the water swell and amount of dichromate)

If you noticed with most dcgs you can tilt it 2 to 3 deg and change the over cast color playback. In your 3rd color you’re looking for let’s say red you want to make the angle change so when the 2 exposers step on each other the it will best support that color.

What really happens here is when your expose the first for blue.. then the second because of the preswell you get green... and the combination of the 2 is 1+1+1=3 1 being one wave length, and the extra 1 you get is bandwidth difference of the first and second exposer because they step on each other.

But let’s say the difference between the first and the second was a 1/2 the bandwidth then the third would be yellow green (blue, blue-green,yellow green)

Colin Kaminski Sat May 03, 2008 11:12 pm #16
Tony,

It is possible to make a holder that holds the plate in an exact location after processing. They are used all of the time for holographic interferometry. The basic idea is any solid object has 6 degrees of freedom and if you place a ball bearing as a contact point to eliminate each degree of freedom you can only place the plate in one position. I am jigging up to try some two color H2s and will eventually have a photo of a plate holder to show but I bet it will be the end of summer before I have the table set up in the new lab.
