I am new to holography, and I have done some research, but I am unable to find an answer to my question. Say I was to create a hologram with a laser pointer. Am I correct in understanding that holograms can only be reproduced by light of the same wavelength? If this is so, could I produce a hologram with one laser, and use another laser of the same wavelength to reproduce it? I geuss that all lasers aren't perfectly built, so there must be a slight variation in the wavelength of beams produced by a laser. My question really is does the laser have to be completely identical to the laser that recorded the hologram, or is there a degree of variation allowed.

Also, could a hologram be recorded with a laser of one power (say 10 mW), and reproduced by a laser of lesser power (say 5 mW) as long as they had the same wavelength?

Thanks,

Kevin
Colin Kaminski Sat Dec 22, 2007 2:27 am #2
A transmission hologram requires a narrow band illumination source. It does not have to be as narrow as a laser. And certainly any laser would work but also some LEDs work as well. If you change the color a lot like from red to green the hologram will change size and get distorted.

A reflection hologram and a special transmission hologram called a rainbow hologram will work with white light. If you look at the right on the top of the page you will see a link to the HoloWiki it can explain the different types of holograms.
Tom B. Tue Jan 01, 2008 7:51 am #3
1) No, the recording and playback laser do not have to be identical. There is lots of leeway, but

as you get further away from the original wavelength the reconstruction angles change and efficiency is reduced. Even white light works, but because different colors reconstruct at different angles the image (for transmission holograms) becomes a rainbow blur.

2) The power levels don't matter at all. When I look at a photograph lit with a 60 Watt bulb, I don't care what the original light level was (sunlight or cave light), as long as the exposure was correct for the film.
