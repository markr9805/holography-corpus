Hans Thu May 17, 2007 12:24 pm #1
Is gelatin necessary for the formation of ultra fine grained silver halide precipitation? If not, how about this idea for making a high concentration silver halide grain solution?

- Use the drop (AgNO3) by drop (LiBr) method to make fine grains in water + dye, but without gelatin.

- Next add a solution of table salt to this solution to precipitate the remaining AgNO3, but in a sufficient concentration to make very large AgCl grains.

- Run the solution through a fine filter to separate the large AgCl grains from the ultra fine AgBr grains.

- Dry the solution out some to increase the concentration of the AgBr grains.

- Add this solution to a solution of gelatin.

- Coat the plate.

- Wash the plate to remove the remaining NaCl+LiNO3+LiBr.

A precipitated grain is a dynamic entity by which I mean that its surface ions are going into solution and coming out of solution continuously very rapidly. As the grain grows then the inner ions of the grain are becoming static and thereby there is a gain in energy called lattice energy so there is a strong inclination for tiny grains with a large surface area with respect to the amount of AgBr they contain to become part of a larger system ie a big grain with low surface area with respect to the amount of AgBr it contains. So the smaller grains will disappear if you have larger grains in the vicinity. Gelatin or other big polymeric molecules are vital to protect the grains and prevent them coming together and growing rapidly. Gelatin also of course acts as colloid dispersing agent allowing the particles to stay in suspension and so on.

As a matter of interest ,for some reason 1 molar solution of chloride ions is a more rapid grain growth promoter of a finished bleached hologram than a 1 molar solution of bromide ions and a 1 molar solution of iodide ions is even much worse than the chloride ions. The latter case is due to the ability of iodide ions to form complexes with other halides which are water soluble. (I think concentrated solutions of potassium iodide were used as the very first "FIX" solution by photography pioneers). However why chloride ions are worse than bromide ions I am not so sure about. So in our work in this institute here we prefer to stick our bleached holographic gratings into compounds with bromide ions rather than chloride ions or they soon get too scattery . The chloride ions in biological fluids are not a problem to us usually because we use plain silver gratings and we only run into problems when oxidants are around which would bleach the silver grains.
