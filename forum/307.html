Jeffrey Weil Thu Sep 08, 2011 8:29 pm #5
Hello,

If your going to start collecting holography equipment start with things that not only can you easily afford now but you'll also be able to use for something no matter how far you progress or how serious you might get in the future.

The classic starting mount for decades is the machinist mag mount with adjustable arm from China. Here's one from Amazon for 8 bucks!

[http://www.amazon.com/Import-TR97050-Ma ... 346&sr=1-6](http://www.amazon.com/Import-TR97050-Magnetic-Indicator-Holder/dp/B002YPFRLG/ref=sr_1_6?s=hi&ie=UTF8&qid=1315534346&sr=1-6)

The kind with the round knobs are better quality. Look for them instead of these flat knob types. When you get it the unit will be packed in oil. Take it apart and clean it up however you like, ending with soap and water. It's then ready for optic use.

Don't put heavy optics that need to be still on the end of the adjustable arm. Use a part of the solid cross pole. The adjustable spring will let things move. You have a few ways it can be set up. You'll see once you get one. Always keep optics as close to the main upright pole as you can for stability.

I usually remove the adjustable part or look for the ones that have a single piece arm. For things that don't need to be held still and are light such as apertures and blocking cards the adjustable part is pretty nice to have.

On the working end of the thing is a small removable post with a large thumb nut. Using that with some ingenuity or replacing that post altogether with a normal screw and nut will give you an unlimited number of ways of mounting your equipment including your laser.

If you don't have steel don't worry about it. Since your keeping the optics near the center pole, the mount won't tip over. It can be used as a gravity base. Not a great one though, steel is best.

Jeff Weil

Hello Arturo,

Those mag mounts are for machinists. That's where you want to look. If you have any machinist supply places near by check there. Many times they won't have these mounts in their catalog or on a shelf. You might have to ask the counter person for them.

Often they will only have the more expensive models visible.

Then, there are many places in the States that will ship to you.

[http://www.use-enco.com/CGI/INPDFF?PMPAGE=405&PMCTLG=00](http://www.use-enco.com/CGI/INPDFF?PMPAGE=405&PMCTLG=00)

That's enco. They have the round knob model for 20 bucks. That is the standard price.

[http://www.grizzly.com/search/search.as ... tic%20base](http://www.grizzly.com/search/search.aspx?q=magnetic%20base)

That's Grizzly, another machinist supply that might ship to you.

You'll see models that cost much much more. In general they will not perform better for you. Stay with the cheap ones.

Jeff W
