Howdy all

I'm just spitballin' here...

I have high hopes for my new Stabl-Levl isolator/oldskool Newport table combination,

though feel however inclined to attempt to stack the deck in my own favor as much as possible...

And propose this damping idea.

The isolators will replace the feet threaded to the bottom of the legs, and will therefore bear the entire mass of the table (~500lb).

The isolators claim to have a maximum capacity of 300lb apiece, so by my reckoning, I'll be at roughly 45% of their load-bearing capacity...

And maybe, just maybe - those damping orifices will give tiny pressure surges from vibration somewhere to vent?

Is this idea a stinker?

I just seem to feel that in a closed system, compression of one isolator will cause the other three to expand. Not to good I would think.

Thanks guys for giving this some thought and apologies for not getting back into the discussion earlier...

...guess I've been distracted.

While ultimately my plan is to go with the simple option of just inflating the bladders with only enough pressure to lift the table a hair, and testing the results of that,

I'll continue the chat because.... Well, just...because.

I dig and grok what you are saying John about reservoirs for the "damped" air - chambers for the orifices to puff into - but - would the chambers need to be at line pressure in order to not just be a slow leak in the system? Would the N2 tank not act as said chamber?

I decided to "use" those check valves in my idea as the means that would stop pressure from washing back and forth between the legs of the system... But I guess the damping orifices would negate the check valves if they were tied directly back to the pressure source...

Though individual chambers for the damping orifices to relieve into wouldcome up to pressure eventually, while also solving the problem of the bladders venting into one another. I guess that's the critical flaw in my idea, huh? This idea needs individual damping chambers, doesn't it?

Does anybody have any idea on what order the size of pneumatic damping orifices tend to be? Microns? Fractions of millimeters? Fractions of inches?
