MichaelH Tue Feb 22, 2005 3:11 pm #5
Other people have tried the static lamination and found that it doesn't work well on roll film and that the laminating material is very expensive.

It's cheaper and easier to use Kapco or something similar and permanently laminate the film to glass. Keep your environment clean and it's not hard to keep dust out from between the glass and film.

MichaelH Mon Feb 28, 2005 10:25 am #11
> andy75180: With my crude attempt at sticking on film last week. I noticed I had to increase the exposure with this film/plate idea.

Actually I've experienced the same effect with the Kapco film. My working theory is that the film base is hydrophylic (absorbs moisture) and allows development to happen from both sides of the emulsion, thereby decreasing the development time. With the film adhered to glass, development only happens from one side. I've just thought about sealing the non-emulsion side of the film with some sort of spray-on sealer and doing some tests but don't have time for that right now. I'll put it on my "some day" list though. Right now, I just work with the results and either increase my exposure or development time slightly.

> andy75180: Do you use this idea all the time now instead of buying plates?

I do for all my transmission masters. I sometimes use it for reflections but only when playing around. I prefer to mount the reflection holograms after they're completely processed and I know I've got a keeper. I'm not so good that all my holograms are keepers.

> andy75180: Have you ever tried using the removable adhesive sheets? Roll out film as usual, after exposure just peel of the holographic film?

I never have. I was never able to get a sample of the material that Slavich recommends and I'm not willing to shell out a several hundred $ for something that might not work.
