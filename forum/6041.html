Uli Mon Feb 05, 2007 4:47 pm #1
At Colourholographic Ltd we manufacture the BB range of ultra fine grain photographic materials. We now that our material falls into the range of ultra fine grain emulsion because we had some TEM made in the past showing it, although not conclusively, and because we have the indirect holographic result that we can record a 7100 l/mm blue grating with a high diffraction efficiency of about 70% at 440 nm, and that with a 20% of absorption due to the silver halide grains at that wavelength. With those two results we can guess that the average size of our silver halide crystals is in the range between 14 to 20 nm.

The only problem is that we do not have the final evidence. We have tried different methods recently. The first one was using a TEM with a standard technique used by the photographic industry, using a centrifuge for separating the silver grains from the rest of the components. The figure was about 50 nm. No one can make a blue hologram with high DE with that grain size, since that was Agfa's mean grain size.

The other method was using a new device based on Brownian motion of small particles, a method that is able to determine particle sizes in the order of 1 nm, as their inventors claim. With our emulsion they got a value between 300 and 400 nm.

So that it looks like it is not that easy to measure the grain size of a population of silver halide grains in order to get their mean value, density and size distribution. Since I know that at least a few of the forum users are in the academic or research world, can someone suggest how can this be done, and which is the best technique?
