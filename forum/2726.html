Dinesh Sat Sep 18, 2004 2:18 pm #2
Form your figures, I suspect it's a negative lens. I'd thought it might be a positive lens with an extremely short focal length, but the 6 degree beam spread seems to make that possibility unlikely. If it is a negative lens, it won't focus light to a real point, but expands the beam. There's a virtual focus behind the lens. To focus it, you need another , positive lens.

To find the appropriate lens to focus at a particular point, use the lens formula:

1/u + 1/v = 1/f

u = distance of laser from lens

v=distance of focused point from lens

f = focal length of lens

and the fact that the beam spread seems to be about 6 degrees, which will give you the necessary diameter(d) for the required lens as:

d = (2v)tan(3)

So if you want to focus the beam at 80 cm and you choose to put the lens at 20 cm, then u=20, v=60 and, using the lens formula, you need a lens of focal length

f= 1/( 1/20 + 1/60) = 15cm.

The diameter of the lens will be

d = 260tan(3) = 6cm

Maybe the diode is too close. If the point of divergence of the laser is inside the focal length of the lens, ie the "object distance" is less than the focal length of the lens, then the positive lens would act as a diverging lens, since the "image point" is now virtual.
