Kaveh Sat Jul 10, 2004 7:52 pm #2
My guess is your reference beam glanced off something, like a metal object, and you had a secondary but blurred reference beam. Look at the reference (or reconstruction) beam directly from where the film is and see if it is a pure spot or if there is a reflection from somewhere which is close to the direction of the ref beam.
Dinesh Sun Jul 11, 2004 12:09 am #3
This often happens when a second source of light hits the film causing a second reference. If you're using a collimating mirror and a beam expander in front of a small mirror, so that you have small mirror -> beam expander -> collimator, then unless you iris off the light going into the expander, the collimator 'sees' the reflected light from the small mirror as well as the light from the expander. Both these point sources are collimated by the colliamtor and both provide a reference.

The fact that you can see the secondary image means that the secondary source is pretty bright. The fact that the second image is so close to the first means that the second reference is almost parallel to the intended reference and displaced by a small distance. The fact that the image size of the second, 'ghost' image is pretty much the same as the first, real image means that the second reference is travelling along the same path as the intended reference and has the same angle, it's as collimated or diverging as your intended reference. I'd say whatever mirror is the last mirror before the plate in the reference arm has a second reflection from somewhere.
Tom B. Sun Jul 11, 2004 12:52 am #4
One diagnostiic to try is to use a lens to focus the reference beam onto a white card at at the plate location. It should form a single bright point, but if there are reflections you might see extra points. I mention this because I had been troubled by ghost fringes at the edges of my beam which turned out to be caused by internal reflections in the cylinder lens I use with my laser diode - I could see two spots on either side of the central focused spot. A couple of strips of black tape on the lens cured this.

That would give you a reflection hologram. Also, the image would be sharp. The fact that your secondary image is blurred suggests a reflection from a slightly diffusing object, like a steel pole.

Did you put your head where the film is and look directly at the laser source (using a filter if it is too bright)?

Here is another test. Look through the reconstructed hologram, back at the source. If there is still nothing around the reconstruction beam, then probably that is not the problem.
