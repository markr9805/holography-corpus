Joe Farina Sun Aug 30, 2009 2:18 pm #1
After seeing the results of 532 and 633nm combined in a single beam, I was wondering about the issue of luminosity versus the number or wavelengths used (for color holography). I have to admit that objects look dramatically different when two wavelngths are used instead of one. With one wavelength, colored objects looks like ghosts of the real thing, but with two wavelengths, they look alive (although the colors are different than the real thing).

Long ago we had some discussions about the number of wavelengths "needed" for color holography. I guess it all depends on what is required. If you don't care if the colors in the hologram are bright (i.e., luminous) then I suppose there's no limit to the number of laser lines to include, but that would be silly since brightness is extremely important.

So we have to balance color rendering capability with brightness, because as the number of recording wavelengths goes up, the luminosity goes down. So I'm a firm believer in 3 lasers at most, since the color rendering capacity for 3 correctly-chosen wavelengths is quite adequate (unless there's some special scientific application where higher color accuracy is needed, but offhand I don't know what such an application might be).

But since I've seen the results of 532 and 633nm together, I'm wondering about the luminous intensity of holograms recorded at these two wavelengths, compared to holograms recorded with a 3rd (blue) laser. I would expect the luminous intensity to be more for 2-color hologram. I wonder if the difference is dramatic (Kaveh, if you're reading this, do you know?). It might be something in favor of using 2 wavelengths rather than 3. Of course, natural objects would not have the correct colors. But it shouldn't be a big deal for many holographers to prepare their objects with their own colors, keeping in mind that the recording wavelengths will be 532 and 633nm. Green DPSS lasers and HeNe's are quite affordable these days, and it really is not difficult to get the beams together (it just requires three lenses in a zoom configuration for the DPSS). For people who use silver halide, PFG-03C could be used, or for DCG, MBDCG could be used (with the addition of rhodamine 6G or another dye for the green).

Anyway, I wanted to make a plug for 2-color holography.
Kaveh Sun Aug 30, 2009 10:00 pm #2
Hi Joe

I am fully with you with all you say. Regarding brightness, it depends on chemistry and other factors, so let's put that aside. With good chemistry and film, you might have a bright hologram with 3 wavelengths. In principle, you should not get a fall in brightness, but in practice you do.

I agree that using the right two colors is more than 2/3 of the way to a "full color" hologram. You might think of using a red and 488. The advantage here is that the mixture can make white, so the brain is happy from that point of view.

I firmly believe too that the right 3 wavelengths are enough for good rendition. At least they will give as good rendition as color photography or color TV. Now if those are not good enough for your purposes, you can try more wavelengths. But when was the list time you heard someone complain about the lack of accurate color rendition in a TV program or a movie?

So for people wanting to experiment with real color, try 2-color first. You will be surprised at how good it is. Your brain will fill in the details for you.
