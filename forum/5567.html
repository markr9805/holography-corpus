Dear Holographers,

I am researching methods to determine if the SSY-1 laser is outputting a single longitudinal mode.

![Image](http://wdjensen123.com/Holography/Oscilloscope1.jpg)

By theoretically using a fast oscilloscope like the 400MHz TEK2465B (analog) and Liconix fast photodetector with voltage reverse bias, you might theoretically get a plot like the first one shown. There is basically a smooth peak of the fundamental longitudinal mode, with little ripple undulations corresponding to other unwanted longitudinal modes. The fewer the undulations, the more your beam is approaching single longitudinal mode. It is a single longitudinal mode when there are no ripples at all.

In measuring my laser, I only have a 50MHz scope with the Liconix photodetector. What I got was indicated by figure 2, a sharp spike. It is impossible to tell if this is a single longitudinal mode with this slow of an oscilloscope.

What Can be determined is that the standard SSY-1 with passive dye q-switch and standard pulse forming network (PFN1) only produces a single output spike. This was checked over a longer time period on the 50MHz scope to be certain. That means that the optics were well designed for a single spike as the PFN drains its energy to the flashlamp. Ideal for a military grade rangefinder that measures time-of-flight from a single optical pulse.

It would be detrimental if there were multiple pulses coming out of the laser. It would multiple expose the hologram plate. Such a condition might occur with a longer flashlamp pulse than 100 us provided by another PFN design.

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen
