JohnFP Tue Aug 29, 2006 9:58 am #2
That's great John. I have not read that part of the Saxby book yet but from prior research using Fiber has a few drawbacks. Here are a few I remebember. If the beam is not introduced at the correct angle for complete interneal reflections, losses can be heavy. Most fibers do not maintain polarization.

The awsome thing about using fibers is the beam is divergent when it comes out of the fiber. Path lengths are as simple as matching the length into the fibers then it is easy moving the end of the fiber where it is needed only matching path length from end of fiber to plate (provided fiber lengths are the same). Also, for very tight configurations the benefit of the end of the fiber acting like the last mirror and spatial filter allows the beam placement to be squeezed into and around tight areas.

Keep us posted, this is great!!!

It is not the long coherence length really. The problem is trying to keep the polarizations aligned. The polarization gets messed up in a normal fiber optic. Small radius bends are the worst for messing up the polarization. Also shorter is better. Polarization Maintaining Single Mode fibers (PM fibers) are a good solution but I don't know of any for green (or for red really they are more for IR) and getting the beam into the very small aperture is quite difficult. The proper device is a spatial filter with the pinhole replaced with a fiber optic holder.

> The problem is trying to keep the polarizations aligned.

I needed a polarized laser beam comming out of a fiber the other day. I used two collimating lenses and a fiber from an Ocean Optics spectrometer, with the raw He-Ne beam incident on the input end one. The output end had a cube polarizer glued to the lens holder. I maximiaed the desired polarization by rotating a half waveplate before the input end, which had to be done if the stresses on the fiber changed even slightly.

I suppose an interferometer could be placed in the unused output beam, and its fringes locked with a fringe locker (nice correlation with the other thread 8) ) to keep the output beam being used stable. A motorized rotator for the waveplate would be needed.
