Dinesh Sun Nov 28, 2004 5:07 pm #3
Can you post pictures of objects? It sounds like you may be having motion problems. With long exposures you need exceptional stability to prevent motion of 157nm over 1/2 an hour! The stability requirements for silver may not be sufficent for DCG at these exposures. I find that I need to float my table for exposure above 10 minutes. You could try attaching the object directly to the plate, so that both plate and object will move together.

I doubt reprocessing will work. Reprocessing can vary a bandwidth to apparently create increased brightness, but if there's nothing visible, either the fringes have not formed or they've formed in the UV. Assuming you've not got a UV hologram, reprocessing will not increase a very low de because the fringe modulation isn't there to begin with.

[edit 1] I realised that 315 was the laser make, not the wavelength. The hologram mustn't move by 270 odd nm during the 1/2 hour exposure. In my case, reprocessing by coffee is a necessary requirement for mental modulation.

Dinesh Mon Nov 29, 2004 1:40 am #15
I think there may be motion since the fringes are stable after 30 minutes. If they take 30 minutes to stabilise, they may still jitter imperceptibly. Michael's trick with graph paper may be a way of finding out. If you can arrange for a differential amp right on the edge of the dark and light areas and arrange for a beep when the i/ps are constant, you may be able to get an audio feedback of stability. This should be easy if the fringes are truly stable.

Cutting into 2.5x2.5 in. Good idea. I do this sometimes for myself as an exposure test. Reducing spot size is also a good idea, since it reduces motion requirements. These are not linear so any savings is good. Use a diffusely illuminated object. A mirror-like rfeflection may give you a grating and no image. It may also give you intermod noise. This is when different parts of a shiny object reference each other and give a grating. Fixing the object to the plate will help a lot with the stability problem.

Processing: If the plate looks orange when you put it in water, there's still dichromate in the plate and this will "fog" the plate. It's the water that washes out the dichromate so it's best to keep the plate in the dark until after the water stage. Tap water is not a problem. The function of the alcohol is to dessicate the plate and tap water does not affect the dessication properties of the alcohol.

JohnFP Mon Nov 29, 2004 6:41 pm #17
As Dinesh suggested start simple with a Denisyuk. Take the laser and set it up above the plate and object. Direct the beam down at about a 45 degree angle to the table surface with a mirror to start. Take your object and lay it on its back. A white porcelain object would be best. Choose an obect that is a bit smaller then your plate and has some leading points such that you can lay the plate right on the object horizontally and it is stable. This greatly reduces the chance of movement from object to plate. Make sure you put some black tape on the leading edge of the plate to block off light entering from the edge. You will not have to worry about laser movement or beam movement prior to the light hitting the plate.

Exposure parameters and processing is difficult enough in DCG at first without having to worry about movement. Then when you get your processing down you can move back to other more ellaborate set ups.

With the point on the object that is in contact with the plate you should get burn in just around that point. That is, you can see a definitive difference in the emulsion there. If you are getting a dim hologram and not getting burn in at those points then increase exposure time till you get them.

How are you processing?

What is temperature of fixer, water bath and each alcohol bath?

What times are spent in each?

What are the concentrations of alcohol?

How do you agitate in each, if you agitate at all?

How do you do your final dry?

Do you see any hologram in the 100% alcohol at all, if not you can leave in the final alcohol bath for up to two minutes until an image starts to show. It will be dim but you should see something.

Do you allow the plate to set for about 5 minutes after exposure and before fixing?

I fix until the blue precipitate stops and then 15 seconds. But should be between 20 seconods and two minutes. If it takes too long, increase exposure.

There are two methods for removing the AmDi. One is to fix it out pretty completely as I mentioned above. That being the case, you can use room lights after the fixer as we did at the PCGI meeting. If you are fixing for a short period of time and washing most of the AmDi out with water then you should keep the plate out of room lights until the water rinse in complete or near complete. If your circumstances dictate you need to use room light for the wash, as in taking it to a bath room to rinse and your plates are a little old and require less hardening, then use the fixer with half the hardener and double the normal fixing time to remove most AmDi.

Good luck, and I ensure you when you get your first brilliant DCG hologram you will be hooked.

JohnFP Thu Dec 02, 2004 11:38 am #19
My Argon Ion laser is not even on the table. It is on a bench about 4 feet away from the table. There are two types of stability to be concerned with.

One of course is the relative beam movement of the beams after the first beamsplitter. In the case of a Denisyuk this is after the beam hits the emulsion. Then the beam traveling through the emulsion hitting the object and bounce back to the emulsion has to be stable compared to the original beam that just hit the emulsion. If the second beam, the one that went through the emulsion, hit the object and bounced back does not remain stable, then the fringes move and your hologram degrades or does not form at all. In the case of a beam splitter on a table, then the two beams after the beam splitter have to be stable with respect to one another or as stated above the fringes do not form properly. These relative movements are on the oder of a 1/4 wavelength give or take depending on your set up.

The second type of movement to be concerned about it the actual movement of the original beam before the first beam splitter such that spatial filters become out of alignment or expanded beams move to an unwanted position. This will not directly affect fringe formation as both beams after the splitter move relative to one another and keep the standing wave pattern. This movement can be on the order of a half a mm or so before alignment becomes an issue.

When I was making holograms in Photo Resist with a HeCd laser the laser then was also not on the table and exposures were around 40 minutes. The laser was actually in another room and there was a small hole in the wall to allow the laser beam to pass through to the Isolation table room. This elliminated stray light from the laser, air movements and vibrations from the laser itself.

So to elliminate your air currents and vibrations from the laser try to set up a table, and it does not have to be vibration isolated table, a couple of feet away from the holographic table. You can even partition off the table such that there is no stray light. and vent the air away from the holographic table or whatever you want to do. Then stear the beam to the holographic table at the correct height. As long as your table is not settling or moving grossly, all will be fine.

I still do not understand why some insist on having the laser on the table when it presents problems.

Hope this helps!!!
