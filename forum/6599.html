Since this is closely related to what Dave mentioned about what he wanted to do with his color hologram I thought I would post it. I was hoping to have the hologram done before actually telling how it was done but what the heck. It may open up some discussions and I don't think I will be able to get into the lab for a couple of weeks.

I always wanted to do planets, so here is my idea. I already sculpted the planets out of Paper Clay.

The plan is to get three celestial bodies, the sun, earth and moon all of which are different colors. I would like the earth to be pastel blue, the sun to be gold and the moon to be white. I think I already mentioned I would also like the moon to be pseudoscopic and in front of the plate such that the moon follows the observer as he/she moves.

Here is how I plan to do it. Take each of the celestial bodies and glue them to heavy gauge steel wire support such that the supports are at the 56 degree angle so the body hides the mount. Glue each planet to a separate piece of glass but all registered in their correct relative location. This way I can simply change the glass base with one object for another and the positions will be correct.

Make some film and while it is still young (about 4 hours hold) place the Earth in the object space and expose lightly to get a blue, and since the film is young should be pastel blue. The emulsion is facing the object. Then flip the plate around and change out the earth with the moon and shoot it also while being young but a longer exposure to get deeper fringes for white. Then let the plate set for 24 hours and shoot the sun with emulsion facing object to get gold.

I am really concerned that even if I expose a plate young and then let is set for 24 hours I will lose the broadband tendency of the emulsion for the pastel blue and white. So the second thought is to make the pastel blue earth and white moon on one plate and then make the sun gold on another plate and sandwich the two together after processing.

Getting the bodies such that they don't overlap and with enough "sun" light hitting them was a little trying but I think the below will work. I may add some kicker mirrors to help with the "sun" shadowing which will be painted in also.

Lots of research, testing and fun ahead....

Tom B. Thu Dec 20, 2007 2:20 am #2
Sounds like fun! If you need some stars, little tiny (2 mm?) silvered sugar balls sold as cake decorations under the trade name "Silverettes" work great! Or small ball bearings would work, too.

One thing I noticed when trying Denisyuks from objects mounted on glass was that even if the glass was at Brewster's angle, reflections of depolarized light from the object were a problem, both directly from the sides of the object and indirectly from the object to the plate back to the object mounting glass and back to the plate so multiple images of the object were seen. I think that an AR coating on the object mounting glass would help. Or maybe it would be possible to use these unwanted reflections creatively....

OK, I have done this with an transmission H1-H2...

Take and make a bunch of H1s of star fields, then make some masks that have randomly spaced vertical stripes for openings. After the image is exposed then expose a series of the star fields through the masks. You end up with a nice twinkling star background as the viewer moves.
