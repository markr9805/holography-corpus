I was looking over the old forum after reading Technodreamers questions about transforming the old forum or converting it or some such (too much of a luddite to even understand the question!). Anyway, I came across this:

[http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2293](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2293)

I'm also going through Ponn Farr (Every seven years a Vulcan feels an irresistible urge to make a colour hologram.... ). So the following occurred to me:

I make a narrow band reflection hologram and an achromat transmission hologram (or a Gabor in-line) of the same object. I then arrange them facing toward each other and arrange an optical system such that a beam of light reconstructs the achromatm (or Gabor), goes through it (that is, the zero order goes through) and reconstructs the reflection holo. If I further arrange a set of optics like beamsplitters at 45 degrees so that both the reflection image and the achromat image were superimposed, could I then de-saturate the colour of the reflection hologram? One criticism about colour holograms are that they're too spectral, ie highly saturated. I wondered whether this system might not cure that problem.

True, it's awfully complicated, but chirped gratings would only increase the bandwidth. I don't think it's just a question of bandwidth.

The impression that the eye gets with colour is basically a weighting factor of a colour averaged over the entire spectrum. Photometry rather than radiometry. So when a pastel colour, such as lilac or pink is observed in the photometric sense, it's observed by weighing certain colour primaries over the entire eye response (photopic) curve. I was wondering how one might reconstruct such pastel colours in holography.

It seems that one way is to desaturate the colours. This is usually done by adding white to a pure spectral colour. To get pink, eg, you'd add white to red, but adding white then also adds a certain set of green "wavelengths" and a certain set of blue "wavelengths", according to the primaries in any gamut, to the red - increase of bandwidth plus addition of very different , but specific Bragg components. If you chirp the entire grating set, you'll get white. To get, say lilac what you need to do is get the Bragg seperations for the primaries and record certain strengths of those primaries. These "strengths will have to conform to the photopic curve. But you can't have any of the "in-between" Bragg seperations. This also sounds pretty complicated.

We did that with the old (polyester) films from Agfa. We used sort of a "tank" system (filled with alcohol/water mixtures). The colors achieved, largely depended on the alcohol/water ratio. Some ratios simultaneously created two different states of swelling - leading to two different wavelengths on reconstruction, forming e.g. a pink image. If I remember well, that was ok for Denisyuks but with regards to H2 reflection holograms, it led to two images that did not entirely coincide. Hence on reconstruction, the image became somewhat blurred.

I've managed to get a sort of purple-ish colour using swelling and mixing on 8E75 plates around '90. I was actually wondering about a colour mixing in a full colour hologram rather than just one colour. This was also inspired by a talk Hans gave about two years ago. He mentioned that it should be possible to make holograms of art pieces - classical paintings - and send them around to various galleries and museums . This was not well received, partly because the artists in the audience could not believe that the subtle tones and colours of a real painting could be reproduced holographically (I sort of suspect that the artists also disapproved of recreating classical paintings by zapping them with a laser, especially since Hans' talk came soon after some speculation that the classical Rennaissance Artists used optical projection techniques to "cheat"). However, Hans' purple, glass fish is close enough to the real thing to make it look real.

With H1/H2 setups, you need to change the ref a little in order to align them. There is a paper by McGrew, I believe that gives a geometrical method of calculating the change in angles necesary for aligning pseudocolour reflections. I have this paper if you're interested. Edwina Orr and Dave Traynor from Richmond Holographics made some white reflection holograms using pseudocolour. The whites were pretty good.
