I have big round static bases (the weights off a steel weight lifting set). My table used to be concrete which was not flat. I am finding my existing table, using the 1/2 steel plate is also very pitted. On a few of my bases I took a drill bit and drilled a little dimple in places in a triangular pattern on the bottom of the base. I then epoxied a ball bearrings in each of the pits. This gave a three point stand which worked very well on the concrete and the uneven (pitted steel).

On the bases that do not have the ball bearrings I take the base and set it up. If it wobbles, which is does most of the time, I take a drywall screw and push it in, slightly twisting, into the open part of the wobble. With practice and just the right pressure it takes the wobble out. Then I hot glue the base to the table.

Just a question, does the magnetic mounts from Harbor Freight lend themselves to a tri mount set up as described above? Maybe use the two points in the front and in the rear drill a hole between the two point and scew in a 1/4 20 screw with a point grinded on its end, thus achieving the three point stance? I think your method will only work with tables that are relatively flat across the area of the base. I think a ball bearring would be to much because as you raise the base off the table the magnetic holding power would be reduced. I will work something out, as I would really love to have the smaller profile of the the magnetic mount base.
MichaelH Mon Apr 04, 2005 12:53 pm #2
> JohnFP: Just a question, does the magnetic mounts from Harbor Freight lend themselves to a tri mount set up as described above?

I wouldn't think so unless you used very small ball bearings. As you mentioned, the farther from the top, the less holding power the base will have.

One huge drawback to using tiny bearings would be the chance that they would create dimples in a steel table top. Those bases create a lot of force and the smaller the bearing, the more force applied to the top in a small area.
