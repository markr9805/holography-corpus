Tom B. Mon Feb 28, 2005 2:06 am #6
Exposure was about 3 seconds at 60 uW/cm^2, 660 nm. The 15 nm refers to the nominal grain size of the emulsion. Yves has an 8nm version available, but it is really only needed for short wavelength work - 15 nm is fine for red.

The rays from the pearl were an unexpected bonus - the hula angel is partly lit by a focused spot of light reflecting off the back of the pearl - this comes from a short focal length concave mirror behind the scene which is collecting spill-over light and reflecting it back into the scene. The mirror is pretty scratched up, and the scattered light from the scratches somehow created an interesting 3D ray effect.

I agree the oriental fellow came out too bright relative to the angel. Also, the lower left part of the scene is confusing and should be cleaned up (there's a horizontal mirror there reflecting light up to the angel).

Tom B. Thu Mar 03, 2005 6:58 am #13
Re packaging, I wondered too about the cardboard spacers that BB-plates use. I expect that cardboard with low chemical activity is available, but don't know what they use. What's needed is a thin, soft, chemically neutral spacer that protects the plates from rubbing against each other and causing scratches. The gelatin coating is well hardened, so a contact spacer is probably OK. Maybe individually bagging plates in zip-lock polyethylene bags or very thin foam poly envelopes would work? The outer box just has to be light-tight and contain the plates so that it is easy to remove one at a time without disturbing the rest.

My comments on price were not intended as a criticism. The material is well worth the price to me, but the high cost per plate does have an inhibiting effect on my willingness to experiment with untried setups or processing alternatives. It's also too high for many hobbyists to be willing to take a chance on.

I'd like to see, as a complement to the regular full-price glass plates, a lower cost alternative using a thinner, cheaper, lighter substrate. intended for test shots and hobby/student use. I find handling film to be a pain, and so would prefer a more rigid substrate. I wonder if thin (1-2 mm) polycarbonate or acrylic sheets could be made to work, i.e. if you can get good coating adhesion and whether it would really be any cheaper? The reduced weight and fragility would certainly be welcome to reduce shipping costs.

Your suggestion re batch subscription purchases is welcome. I'd be willing to give it a try when you are ready.

Tom B. Fri Mar 04, 2005 7:52 am #17
I have been using ziploc freezer bags bags to store 4x5 masters, and they seem to work OK. Unfortunately ziploc no longer offers the small (5x7") size which was very convenient - the smallest is now the quart size (7.5x7) which is awkward.

For unexposed plates I might worry a bit about unknown plasticizers etc. volatilizing out and interfering with the chemistry, but I think the risk is small. Another decision is polyethylene vesus polypropylene - polyethylene is much more permeable to water vapor which might be a good or bad thing. I think I would prefer polyethylene as it would allow the emulsion to gradually stabilize at the ambient humidity, rather than all at once when removed from the bag.

A black bag would be nice, but once again there would be concerns about possible contamination from the coloring agent. I'll look around and see if there's anything affordable on the market that looks promising. Antistatic bags are another possibility - the metalized film ones are quite inexpensive in quantity. I'll see if I have any examples on hand of the right size and material.

Re the plastic plate idea - Oh well... I guess the only answer is to get your production volume up to the point where economies of scale take effect and you can pass the savings onto the consumer. I'll do my best to consume more plates
