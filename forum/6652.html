Martin Sun Jan 13, 2008 9:12 am #8
> Hans: Shops have stopped selling Rain-X in Holland. This is a pity because it is so useful with making the cover plate hydrophobic when doing Mold Coating.

There are several viable alternatives available out there:

Here (in Switzerland) we get a similar product, CLEARVUE (Turtlewax). Like RAIN-X it's also meant to make car windshields hydrophobic.

Moreover, there are tons of household products to protect windows, bathroom tiles etc. These are so called “nano-sealants”. Most of them are based on some tiny (nano) particles that make the surface water repellent.

Of course you could also use chlormethyl silane.
Martin Sun Jan 13, 2008 9:31 am #9
> Dutchelm05: Martin suggested in an earlier post to try Lexan plastic (not sure what they call it in Europe).

I am not entirely sure about Lexan – particularly not regarding its stiffness. Principally I suggested to use POLYCARBONATE. And in my particular case, it was Bayer's MAKROFOL. Those films worked nicely for me and my coating solutions (not to make DCG but silver halide emulsions).

> I have used it and indeed it is hydrophobic. The only problem I had was he suggested a very thin piece (.177inches).

Are you sure abut that? My computer calculated that 0.177 inches was 4.496mm

That would not be particularly thin in my opinion.

I used MAKROFOL films of 0.5mm (0.01968inches) and 0.75mm (0.02953inches).

> I found this to be a problem in that the plastic was not stiff enough resulting in some unevenness due to slight bowing. I've been wanting to comment on this in hopes Martin will respond. I recently purchased some thicker plastic (1/3 and 5/8 inch) , the results at first were poor and I have not revisited yet. Once bitten twice shy

There's no problem getting thicker POLYCARBONATE sheets. They seem to be available at roughly the same range of sizes like “plexi” (PMMA) sheets.

So you may have to try different thicknesses with your particular coating solution.

You ought to balance film stiffness versus “bending”. Some degree of “bending” might be key for easy film removal.

> Also Martin, can you tell me about post care of the plastic once used. Can it be washed, soaked in bleach, TSP, and/or hot water?

I used warm water and a micro fiber pad to avoid drying marks.

Keep in mind that those plastic sheets are a lot softer than glass. So scratches occur easily.
