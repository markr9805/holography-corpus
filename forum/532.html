I was thinking this morning, has anyone tried to sandwich 3 or 4 holographic films together inside of the two glass plates and make a single exposure thus creating 4 holograms on 4 separate films at the same time? I know each hologram would be displaced by the thickness of the film relative to one another and you would get a slight difference in ref:obj light ratios from the first to the last film. But I do believe it would work and it shure would save time in creating a "lot" of a image hologram.

NO, but that surely would be another thought.

I was thinking making 3 or 4 copies from a master in an H1 to H2 imaged plane set up. 3 or 4 pieces of unexposed film would be sandwiched together and exposed simutaneously from the master and a ref beam.
Ed Wesly Mon Apr 30, 2012 1:23 pm #4
I know if works for two, as I have done it accidentally. The major objection I can see to this concept is the amount of scatter that could be picked up by light going through all those emulsions.

If you can afford to make a stack of 3 or 4 films, just cut some pieces small enough for one of your eyes to look through, and look at the reference beam. (I am assuming everyone here can figure out how to do this safely!) If the pinhole does not look sharp, like a halo around it, the fringes might lose some contrast and make a poor recording!

I've made transfers from three 12x16" transmission masters simultaneously in the past, and it worked OK. I used glass plates and no index matching at the time, but would certainly index match now. No reason to not, and huge improvement in noise would result. At least use baby oil.
