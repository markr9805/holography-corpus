MichaelH Wed Feb 16, 2005 10:41 am #4
It's true that an inner tube is more likely to be "better" since a sponge will tend to compress into a mass that transmits vibration. All in all though, if you have some sponge available and are using film (and can afford to sacrifice a few test strips) give it a shot and report your results. We're always interested in new information.

> rja4096: Hi,if so, should I use one big piece of sponge the same size as the slab, or have the slab on 4 smaller sponges placed at the corners?Thanks, Rick.

I use soft foam rubber (might be called sponge, 5cm ~ 2 inches thick),

of the size of the 10mm aluminum plate on top (approx 3x5 feet).

There is also a layer of loose bricks below. Also, I put as many

heavy things on top of the table as possible. I never had, to my

knowledge, problems with vibrations, and this even with multi-beam

setups and reflection copies. All this is on a concrete floor at

ground level in a single family home in a quiet neighborhood without

heavy traffic nearby. I often pondered to make a better table, but

so far didn't find a strong reason for this effort.

So, you may be get away with a simple setup, if your environment

is sufficiently well behaved.... but you really need to support the

plate all over its area and not just under its corners, that would

tend to have a pronounced resonance frequency.

One thing I was wondering off and on was whether an ordinary bed

matress would to - it has a good size, has springs and lots of air

in it, and feels also damped. Of course, putting enough weight on it, like a

2inch granite slab. I may try this at some point. Alternatively, a

layer of bags of cotton wool looks interesting - did anybody try

this ?

Can anyone tell me if it would be possible to use sponge, with a paving slab on top, instead of a inner tube and paving slab?if so, should I use one big piece of sponge the same size as the slab, or have the slab on 4 smaller sponges placed at the corners?Thanks, Rick.

This is interesting, I had a thin layer of foam in between my concrete slab and my steel plate. This was simply to try to match the surfaces. There was a little roughness in the concrete. I also thought it would provide some dampning. Even though my steel plate is 1/2" thick the loading was not enough to get into the compression zone to get the results I expected. It had the un-expected result of allowing the plate to ring at a frequency very close to the frequency of the steel posts I use to mont my optics.

The end result was increased settle time. I was able to settle it out so I did not bother to change it but since I just had to move the table I played with some various configurations. If I was doing the same thing now I would spread a very thin layer of expanding foam on the concrete and then place the plate on top. If it did not work it would be quite a mess but I think it would load more evenly and since the two pieces would be stuck together it would eliminate the high frequency resonance.
