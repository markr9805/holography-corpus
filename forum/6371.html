Last night I read an interesting comment by Rallison:

"The addition of ammonium nitrate can make the dichromate several times more sensitive, but decreases the useful life and blue shifts the image. Approximate ammonium nitrate concentrations are usually in a ratio of 1 to 5 by weight to ammonium dichromate up to a maximum of 1 to 1. When the additional substance is washed out of the gelatin a net shrinkage occurs which amounts to a blue shift in reflection holograms..."

If Jeff's red-shifting process works out, I wonder if it could be used to counteract the blue shift mentioned above. Since there are so many people who now have powerful 532nm lasers, it might be possible to make a very simple DCG forumla, if a good red-shifting technique becomes available. A simple 3-30-200 mixture could be used, along with the addition of silver nitrate (3g ammonium dichromate, 30g gelatin, 200ml water, plus the nitrate). I think a low dichromate concentration like this would be best, so that additional shrinkage is not involved when it washes out.

--Interesting little patent. Thanks for pointing it out.

The bottom line is that it only increases the photosensitivity by a factor of 2.

But the photosensitivity values of ~ 1 mj /sq. cm for 488nm are way beyond what Denisyuk DCGers normally talk about . Presumably this is to do with the way the diffraction was measured in these 2 beam transmission gratings (60 degree angle).

As for the chemistry, as far as I know there had not been any published discussion back in 1970 about the intermediate formation of CrV species immediately on exposure prior to the final formation of the CrIII crosslinking species.

The transformation of CrV to CrIII was both helped not only by the heating process (and time delay) but also by raising the pH somewhat before plunging the exposed plate into first water bath. The pH increase probably helped to strengthen the formation of the Cr-gelatin complexes but if it went too far it apparently would weaken again (due to formation of Cr-ammonia complexes perhaps) if the ammonia reaction time was too long.

On the ammonium nitrate observation I am not sure other than to suppose the following. The rule seems to be that:-

Mild acidity or somewhat low pH BEFORE exposure increases the photosensitivity (and unfortunately the dark reaction too),

but not AFTER exposure where in effect one gains photosensitivity

by increasing pH to neutral or slightly alkaline conditions enabling borderline light induced Cr-gelatin bonds to become definite Cr-gelatin bonds as in my last posting above.

Ammonium nitrate is an acidic salt and the nitrate bit can possibly be a

synergistic oxidant with the light activated dichromate. I believe that it is the energy gained by oxidizing parts of the gelatin polymer through light activated dichromate that gives DCG its enhanced photosensitivity compared to say using dichromate PVA which is far less photosensive because it is far less oxidizable.

Joe Farina Sun Aug 05, 2007 2:05 pm #9
There is another comment by Rallison regarding ammonium nitrate in "Notes and considerations for the dichromated gelatin (DCG) holographer" (an excellent paper by the way).

"Fast, hypersensitized with ammonium nitrate film may also casue bowing of the glass but may be reduced by reducing the intensity of the radiation so that proper exposure is at about two to three minutes. Thirty five or forty five seconds is an acceptable exposure for normal fresh film without bowing."

This suggests a couple of things, first that ammonium nitrate hypersensitization is fairly routine, at least in Rallison's lab. Secondly, he mentions plate "bowing" as being a special problem in this case, which further suggests a real increase in speed, for the gelatin (supposedly fresh) to be tightening up like that during exposure.
Joe Farina Wed Aug 08, 2007 10:53 am #10
There is also a paper in SPIE Institute Series Vol. IS 8 (1990) called "Theoretical background and practical processing techniques for art and technical work in dichromated gelatin holography" by Alexander B. Coblijn.

This is an unusual but interesting paper based on practical experience. It is kind of an "independent viewpoint" of DCG based on holographic testing, and is not just an empty rehash of things previously published.

Anyway, he used the following DCG formula:

100g water

6g gelatin

0.9g ammonium dichromate

2ml ammonia 35% (added last)

I'm not sure what the concentration of ordinary household ammonia is, but that's what the above sounds like. I didn't notice a specific explanation regarding the inclusion of the ammonia, presumably it lowers the pH and increases sensitivity as Jeff explained.
