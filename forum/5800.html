Colin Kaminski Mon Sep 25, 2006 4:28 am #2
You will need to figure out the temporal and spatial coherence of the pulse. A piece of Zap-it paper will help with the temporal coherence. The temporal coherence can be measured by a making a hologram. 200mj should work for a small hologram.

JohnFP Tue Sep 26, 2006 11:40 am #5
> Skyko: PS so is there a basic kit of "stuff" I could buy to do a simple transmission pulsed 532nm hologram? (Film, developer, etc.)

I didn't want to answer this as I have no hands on experience with pulsed holography but here is my suggestions on the matter.

I doubt there is a kit you would want to buy mainly because most kits are geared toward the red wavelength. Integraph's kit has a red laser diode and the film is red sensitive. So I think your best bet is buying some film, developer and bleach (for reflections) seperately. You can get film much cheaper as opposed to glass plates and because you are doing pulsed holography the extra stability (and much higher cost) of plates may not be your most econimical solution.

Also, you mention wanting to try a tranmisison hologram? Is there a reason? A single beam reflection is much easier then a single beam tranmission and you will actually have a hologram displayable with white light when you are done.

While the following is Integraf's suggestion for reflections I believe you can use JD2 for tranmisison also. You do not have to use the bleach for tranmisisons.

For reflection you may try (It would be great if others experienced with pulsed reflections chime in) all available on Integraf's web site (link at top of page).

Slavich VRP-M 4x5 film (10 sheets) - $29

JD-2 Developer (contains developer and bleach) recommended by Integraph - $17

TOTOAL - $46 + shipping

You could cut the 4x5's into quarters and have lots of exposure to play with for ~$50.

While I am not sure that the JD-2 would be the best for VRP-M it is a kit with both developer and bleach and would keep you from having to buy and the chemicals separately. If the holograms come out too blue you could post swell them with a little sugar water and get them to shift into green or gold.
