Joe Farina Sat Oct 16, 2004 4:37 pm #1
I remember reading a patent stating that strong UV exposure caused DCG holograms to shrink, resulting in a shift to shorter wavelengths.

If it's practical, it would be a very attractive "alternative" color control method, but that brief description in the patent was the only place that I know of where the technique was described, and I have been unable to find it again in my patent searches.
JohnFP Sun Oct 17, 2004 1:40 pm #2
Joe, I would state that UV exposure would have a hardening effect on DCG emulsion. It would affect the hologram in different ways depending on how fresh the emulsion is, its bloom strength, whether you exposed the emulsion with UV prior to hologram recording exposure or after the hologram recording exposure. If you do some research on the effect of hardenig the DCG emulison it may give you some clues.
Colin Kaminski Sun Oct 17, 2004 2:41 pm #3
The DCG holograms I made at Tripple-take were exposed to UV to dry the glue holding on the backing plate. I noticed no color shift what-so-ever. I was using a processing to get very broadband reconstruction so both images were strongly "white". Perhaps with a narrowband processing the efect would be more noticable.
Joe Farina Sun Oct 17, 2004 2:47 pm #4
I am interested in the UV shrinking method because there are times when fine color adjustment is needed after the DCG/SHSG hologram is complete (but not yet sealed). I wouldn't be too quick to assume that shrinkage due to UV exposure is the simple result of hardening. (Or, at least, the same kind of hardening that can be obtained through chemicals or heat.) Gelatin chemistry is very complex, and having this alternate shrinking process may prove useful.
