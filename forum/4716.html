I have just been to the SPIE Opto Ireland conference in. For your interest, here is a brief feedback.

There was a session dedicated to holography. See session 2 here:

[http://spie.org/Conferences/Programs/05 ... tion=5827A](http://spie.org/Conferences/Programs/05/ire/conferences/index.cfm?fuseaction=5827A)

Of course no display stuff alas, but some work that I think will benefit us. There is a lot of theoretical and practical work involving photopolymers, and other recording materials. The work is driven primarily by holographic memory applications, but the understanding of photopolymer will be good for display holography I feel.

There are four main groups working on this, each pair closely located geographically...

Two groups in Ireland are at Dublin Institute of Technology, and at University College Dublin. The first do mostly practical work, and the second mostly theoretical.

In Spain there are the Universities in Alicante and in Elche. There is a lot of joint work between Alicante and University College Dublin.

There are lots of papers coming out of these universities. For me, the most interesting was this one:

Holographic recording of patterns in thin film acrylamide-based photopolymer, I. G. Naydenova, E. M. Mihaylova, Dublin Institute of Technology (Ireland); K. Loudmer, Univ. Bordeaux 1 (France); S. Martin, V. Toal, Dublin Institute of Technology (Ireland)

They used systematic recording of gratings, with different thicknesses and fringe frequencies. Then they looked at the surface profiles of these gratings, and from that deduced some of the mechanism in the medium, including migration of material. I couldn't grasp it all in 15 minutes, but if anyone is interested in the mechanism of grating formation in photopolymer, I think it's worth getting hold of some publications from this group.
