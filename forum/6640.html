JohnFP Mon Jan 07, 2008 12:08 pm #5
Justin, wood moves with changes in temperature and humidy.

I too am currenly resting my laser, optics and plateholder on 3/4 inch plywood box with no vibration isolation. But I am only doing SBR in DCG. I have a 18" by 18' concrete paving block on the wood table that my object and plateholder set on. When I fire up the laser I let it warm for 20 minutes then usually have to dial the spatial filter back in but I do it without touching the spatial filter. Usually you can tweak the x and y gimbal adjustments on the mirror just prior to the spatial filter and get back to your original spatial filter setting in just a few seconds and quite easily.

Also, even when I had the steel vibration isolation table, I had the laser resting off the table and on a kitchen type counter top. Again I would let the laser warm for 20 minutes and almost always lost the spatial filter settings. But again, I had a mirror just in front of the laser (still off the table on on the counter), and could use that mirror to bring the spatial filter back into alignment without touching the spatial filter. And as a confirmation that my table was indeed stable (all optics on the table remained stable relative to one another), I could adjust that first mirror off the table and just after the laser to allign the reference beam spatial filter and the other two object beam spatial filters would come perfectly into focus also.

Now the kicker is, you don't want to originally allign your spatial filter with that mirror or you may get a funny beam profile. Always remove the lens and pinhole from your spatial filter and insure the beam goes though the spatial filter perpendicularly and hits the plate or object squarely and centered where you want it. Then place the lens in and insure the expanded beam also hits the plate or object squarely and centered at the same spot. Then insert the pinhole. If you do this and come back the next day and the beam has moved off your spatial filter, then you should be safe simply using the mirror described above to readjust the spatial filter. Hope this helps!

Jeffrey Weil Tue Jan 15, 2008 4:17 pm #13
I would never work in a sand box. The rules are "keep it clean" but at the same time "work in a big box of dirt" Never sounded right to me.

I've made a bunch of tables, nothing magic about it. Forget the granite, better ways to spend your money. Granite will not really be any better for you in your situtation than poured concreat. But concreat is MUCH cheaper and MUCH easier to carry around. Its in bags, not a complete slab.

Get a whole bunch of cynder blocks. If your in the UK, breeze blocks. Same thing. Stack up 3 or 4 on their side to make legs. Build a shallow box on top of that, maybe 2 or 3 inches deep. Put in some rebar and pour the concreat. Put down some inertubes. Remember to put a piece of 2x4 of whatever inside the donut hole so if you loose air the table does not come down and crush the tubes. Build another shallow box on top of that, rebar and concreat.

Now lay a whole bunch of cynderblocks, ON THEIR SIDE so no holes are exposed, and morter them together on the sides. Like building a wall horizontally. Then build another shallow box on top of that. No floor this time, just the sides and pour about 1/2 to 1 inch of concrete on top of that to make a nice surface. That also bonds all the blocks together along with the motar on the sides.

Now you have a choice. Either use weighted bases, I wouldn't, or glue a nice single sheet of steel down and use cheap chinese mag mounts. If you look around you can get them for less than 10 dollars each!!!

Ok, so now for about 200 to 300 dollars you have a REAL table. For another 100 bucks or so you have REAL mag mounts. Each block weights close to 30 pounds. so with a few dozen on top your table will weigh plenty.

I've made hundreds of reflection holograms on a table like this. Work great and no sand drift.

The only problem with it was when I got a newport table I had to take it apart! Not easy, but doable.
