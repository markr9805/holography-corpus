tpark Mon Dec 19, 2005 9:18 pm #2
The low power diode laser that Integraf sells works well. I have had good results with that device. High power ones such as ML1016R(Mitsubishi) and LNCQ05PS(panasonic) work too, but you can get sliced bread holograms if the diode is not in a stable part of its power/temperature domain.

I have purchased many laser modules from ebay in my search for cheap lasers for holograms, but the lasers I bought turned out to be unsuitable for holography. I have had good results from some laser pointers, but sometimes they too give sliced bread holograms.

Currently I have been using a 10mw HeNe which I purchased from Sam Goldwasser, and it has given excellent results. With the HeNe, path length

matching is very important, plus it is better to have a spatial filter as the beam is not always so perfect. Even so, the results that the HeNe gives are worth the extra effort in my opinion.

Colin Kaminski Tue Dec 20, 2005 5:17 am #4
The lower power diodes are easier to make work. The higher power diedes are problematic. You can make them work but they take more skill and patience.

I have been able to make a hologram from every tem00 5mw laser diode I have ever tried. I am sure I have used 10 different brands. The 35 to 60 mw diodes I have used are more problematic and work best at about 80% power. The are more sensitive to mode hopping and temperature control becomes quite important. Temperature control ideas can be implemented for as little as $10.

All of that being said, my favorite laser is my c315 532nm dpss.
