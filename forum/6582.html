dave battin Tue Dec 11, 2007 1:28 am #5
Good work Tony if you are getting 30% now this is good, don’t loose site as now my % is better than 95% after a year of trying this technique.

“One was bubbles”,

The best way is to invert your applicator to allow any bubbles to rise and now you pour from the bottom. Again its the hinging down, one hand is holding the drop action, while the other hand is trying to snap the two together, it this push-pull situation that allows the plate to drop with out forming bubbles........

“Two was even though the plate was on a pretty flat surface the Rain-x made the gelatin bead up and move around a bit mostly towards the center. The result was it was hard to get the edges (corners). So I added more emulsion and it tended to ooze on the side.”

That’s easy, as you will learn by trial and error to disperse the correct amount

“The result may have been an uneven coating. I say “may of” because although the film itself looks homogenous, the end results had whitening on one side”.

Make sure to set clamps all around perimeter (clamp over shimmed areas only)

“Three I also still have a few coatings tear off after cooling it even with Rain-x on the tape (albeit still Scotch tape)”

Yes this I becoming a issue here as well, I have changed over to the shiny shipping tape to see if this gives a better release

> Dutchelm05: Three I also still have a few coatings tear off after cooling it even with Rain-x on the tape (albeit still Scotch tape).

Rain-X doesn't adhere to plastic materials...

For spacer tapes you'd have to look for highly hydrophobic plastic materials. There's an adhesive tape named “Crystal” or something like that, which is made of polypropylene (PP). PP is excellent in the context of gelatin coating.

Moreover, as I recently mentioned, I'd highly recommend testing mold coating with plastic materials instead of glass...

the quote below is from our own holowiki (coating methods);

"This glass is pretty clean already. If you’re unsure, I would soak it in a 20% Clorox Solution (soak over night), and after a quick water wash, soak in the Cascade (dishwasher soap) and water mix (I use a small handful for 2 gallons of water or so) again soak overnight after a slight scrubbing action using a plastic scrubby pad.

After removal of the glass from the Cascade, I give it a quick dip into clean water and then a final plunge into what they refer to as (Trisodium Phosphate) substitute. Where I live, they won’t allow the use of the real TSP, as it’s bad for the ground water. Allow to dry by leaning on wall, sitting on a paper towel."
