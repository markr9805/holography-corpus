walschuler Fri Jan 05, 2007 8:34 pm #3
If you are shooting transmission holos, in general humidity will not be a factor. If reflection holos then the effect of humidity depends on the RH during the shoot, and unless you keep the result in humidity-controlled enclosures, on the humidity where you view the images. But in most circumstances the effects will be small. If the humidity is high during the shoot, then after drying the image may show a color shift towards the blue due to drying shrinkage of the emulsion, until it imbibes the air and returns to the original humidity. If shot at moderate humidity and viewed in high humidity the emulsion will on the other hand expand and the color will shift towards the red. If red to start with it will become deeper red. Swelling agents, TEA pre-exposure, and Sorbitol post-exposure, can be used to play with the color. Using a damp object, say a shell, can yield a color bullseye if the object is close to or in contact with the emulsion. This is harder to control.
