I had a look at one of Kaveh's excellent videos:

[http://river-valley.tv/color-holographic-display/](http://river-valley.tv/color-holographic-display/)

and noticed an interesting difference in the presentation of this subject, as compared to the earlier paper by the same author in SPIE 5636 (2005, "True-color reflection holograms recorded in a single-layer panchromatic dichromated gelatin material," by Jianhua Zhu et al.)

The sensitivity values are considerably better in the presentation:

442nm.....25mJ (2005 paper, 40mJ)

488nm.....25mJ (2005 paper, not given)

514.5nm...15mJ (2005 paper, 30mJ)

633nm.....35mJ (2005 paper, 60mJ)

(I didn't notice any other differences between the presentation and the earlier paper.) The sensitivity values are quite a bit different. Maybe I missed something in the presentation, I will have to go over it more carefully.

I was particularly interested in the high sensitivity between 442 and 488, only 25mJ appear to be needed for 80% DE. Looks like good news for anyone with a low-power blue laser with enough coherence. Also interesting is the higher power generally needed at 633 (35mJ). Blue and green are no longer the most "precious" colors for this system, the limiting factor will be red power, most likely HeNe, unless someone has a krypton/etalon system, which is not only extremely expensive, but very rare these days. That's one reason why wler's new diode laser system might be so very useful.

Joe Farina Wed Sep 16, 2009 10:10 am #3
Welcome to the forum, Karsten. I'm looking forward to your participation.

I feel the same way about the acrylamide. The fact that they make such a big deal about it is interesting. But for reasons of toxicity alone, I will probably want to stay clear of it. But I'm debating whether or not to try it, to see if the results are really so dramatic.

But to be honest, I'm rather surprised that they make such a big to-do about shrinking a DCG hologram. Shrinking has traditionally been very easy to do in DCG, and there are many approaches to use. For example, the mere inclusion of TMG in dye-sensitized DCG suggests some shrinkage when it's washed out. Or, in traditional DCG, some excess of ammonium dichromate will also result in shrinkage when that too is washed out. There are still other methods.

But the really difficult thing to do is expand (rather than shrink) DCG. Tony (Dutchelm05) did some groundbreaking work in this area, as reported on this forum, with succinic acid. This was based on some earlier work by Jeff Blyth. This invoved a final step with baking to tune the final color. I don't remember the title of the thread, but if you use the search feature on the forum for "succinic acid" you should find all references to it. I think this method, if it can be applied to dye-sensitized DCG (i.e., Jeff Blyth's system or the newer Chinese method with Rhodamine 6G), will be far, far better than any simple use of acrylamide, because acrylamide can only shrink, and it seems to me that control and reproducibility will not be that good. On the other hand, with Tony's method, the method first expands (or swells) the layer into a too-red condition, then shrinks it back to the desired color by heating in a oven (this stage can be carefully controlled to get the correct color).

However, the succinic acid method needs further work, becasue it was only tried with conventional DCG. The acrylamide method might be worth trying though, just to satisfy your curiosity, and the acrylamide is fairly cheap and easy to find.
