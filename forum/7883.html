rzeheb Wed Jun 24, 2009 9:39 pm #2
Hi Jem,

I'm probably one of the most junior holographers on this board, having started this hobby only about 8 months ago, but it sounds to me that you should be able to make single and split beam transmission and reflection holograms with your equipment. The only possible obstacle might be vibrational isolation (which you didn't really talk about). My "optical bench" consists of a modified childrens multi-use game table into which I poured about 250 pounds of sand. I then placed a 30 inch by 34 inch by quarter inch thick piece of steel plate (used as a base to ship an electron microcope) on top. The setup weights about 400 lbs in all. The whole thing floats on 4 tennis balls (of all things) which themselves sit on 3/4 inch plywood resting on 2 inch thick foam. I, too, use magnetic bases and other creative ways to mount my optics, and for now have been exposing 2 X 2 inch plates. This set-up has allowed me to make both single beam and split beam transmission holograms, and I have made image plane reflection holograms. It's been great fun, made even more so by virtue of the fact that its so "seat of the pants." So,...... go for it!

Hi Jem,

I have used a slightly smaller breadboard than yours with good results. My table is 3' x 2' x 1.5" (roughly 83cm x 61cm x 3.1cm) My 8" collimator mirror has a 29.5" focal length and I use a spatial filter which put the focal point at about 27" away from the mirror, but the beam can still be fairly well collimated, and just barely covers a 4x5 plate. With creative layout and extra "folding" mirrors you can do pretty sophisticated stuff...note: do = experiment

Here is a layout that worked for shooting a ~30 frame stereogram H1. (Please ignore all the glue and hand cut foam core... ) I cheated on the mirror position in the setup shown due to the ultra tight constraints caused by the size of the DLP projector. Usually (when I shoot SBRs) the parabolic mirror is at the farthest diagonal corner from the spatial filter and the plate holder goes just to the left of the spatial filter.
