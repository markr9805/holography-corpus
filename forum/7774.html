MfA Fri Mar 13, 2009 8:44 pm #1
I'm surprised to see no mention of the method from the paper from the topic in the "Different kinds of holograms" section on the Wiki (only Denisyuk is mentioned for the Single Beam Reflection holograms). Don't see a lot of discussion on it period, although that might just be because I didn't search correctly.

The paper is [here](http://iopscience.iop.org/0143-0807/28/2/021/?ejredirect=.iopscience). Can't find a free version, but really none is needed. It's a plain reflection hologram, but instead of putting the plate on top of the object you put it on top of a [Mirage](http://www.optigone.com/) (the optical toy) with the object inside. The only caveat is that the coherence length has to be sufficient for the extra distance in the reflection path.

John Klayer Sun Mar 15, 2009 4:21 am #4
I tried this about 30 or more years ago. I used the Mirage with the plate laying on the top and the reference shining from straight above. I cut four holes 90 degrees apart around the rim for the object beams to enter. Halfway up I placed a black disk suspended by threads to block light from the object going straight up to the plate. The resulting hologram was like looking at what you see with the mirage.
