john kaufman Fri Jan 25, 2008 9:51 pm #5
Colin,

The tip angle information that you want is not only in the Benton paper but is also discussed by Steve McGrew ("A Graphical Method for Calculating Pseudocolor Holograms"). A google search is helpful.

In the 1982 Proceedings of the International Symposium on Display Holography at lake Forest there are two additional papers by myself ("Previsualization and Pseudo-Color Image Plane Reflection Holograms") and Lon Moore ("Pseudo-Color Reflection Holography") that are very helpful. I spoke with Tung Jeong at SPIE this past week about making them available but there are copyright issues. I suggested to him that I could summarize them for posting on the forum and will persue this approach with TJ.

In a post last year I stated, "Note that Lon got his colors to play back together by selectively lighting only parts of his H1 (his H1 was large enough so that he could light the top, middle, and bottom of the plate with a wide and narrow reference beam). His two or three exposures played back with three views of the object overlapping in one spot. This is a good technique when registration is not so important."

Lon replied to me, " I used different H1 locations for multi color RAINBOW holograms. For reflections I varied the H2 reference angles to coordinate the playback colors. I frequently would "shape" the H2 reference to fit the image being projected by the H1( apple pepper,pear for instance). Maybe that's what got you mixed up."
