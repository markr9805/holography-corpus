Bruno Mon Dec 05, 2011 2:40 am #2
OT but I'm surprised that once again they missed the main reason why people get tired watching 3D movies. The L/R frames are shot or rendered at the same time but they're interleaved upon replay so one eye gets delayed by a fraction of a frame (one sixth, because each L/R pair is repeated 3 times to alleviate flicker). The result is that sideways motion gets accompanied by an unwanted backwards/forwards component. Either theatres should equip themselves with 2 projectors or cameras should incorporate the same time offset.
Dinesh Mon Dec 05, 2011 10:36 am #3
One of the main reasons for the headaches some people experience is to do with the conflict between vergence (sometimes called stereopsis) and accommodation . The actual 3D object the eye tries to focus on is behind the screen, sometimes quite a lot behind the screen, while the the L/R frames are on the screen. So, vergence is relative to the screen, while accommodation is relative to some point behind it. This effect was particularly enhanced in Avatar.

Good point. It's a slightly less bad than one might think though. The scene depth changes with seating distance. Imagine the converse: if the scene depth were independent of seating distance, parallax should be greater for viewers seated closer to the screen. That's not the case because the left and right images are all the parallax you're going to get. This is pure trigonometry of course. The two lines of sight to a corresponding point on the left and right image will cross at a point closer to the screen when your eyes are closer to the screen. Who knows this might act as an incentive for kids not to sit too close to the TV set.

Something I noticed when going to "my first 3d screening" (Avatar as it happens), and which I saw happen in other 3D movies as well was that the 3D depth is being varied throughout the feature. I think this is being done deliberately because otherwise everyone on the back row will constantly get an exaggerated depth while those in the front get a shallow one. So now everyone gets it right from time to time (and wrong most of the time).

I should note that the first complaints of headaches all came from the cinema. The amount of refocusing at 10m vs 6m is quite small (the infinity setting on my camera is right next to 2m, while 30cm is quite far away from 60cm) so I do not believe that focus mismatch is the core issue. I did hear specific complaints that the depth of field of the camera work annoyed some mightily. For instance, after Home Tree crashed down there were ashes floating down quite fare out off the screen. The trouble wasn't that they were focused on the screen and out of focus in their virtual depth. It was that they were actually out of focus so there was no way to see them sharply. This would suggest that depth of field should not be used as a depth cue in combination with parallax.

But I must reiterate, what yanked my eyeballs out of their sockets was that whenever something moved quickly to the left it shot right to the back of the scene, even "inside" things in the background) while stuff moving to the right popped out of the screen like mad.
