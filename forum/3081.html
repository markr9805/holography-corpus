Based on the new OD-1 I noted that self life, stabillity and safe could be improved with a new technique:

Borax seems a key ingredient here, providing pH buffering during development time so a more sophisticated pH control could be achieved chemically with the pH jumper effect:

OD-1 formula:

Metol 2 g.

Sodium sulfite, anhyd. 25 g.

Hydroquinone 5 g.

Borax cryst. 2 g.

Sodium thiosulphate 6 g.

Water to 1 l

Sodium sulfite may be substituted with a more "soft" preservative and the undilited developer turned somewhat "neutral" untill dissolution:

Borax works as a moderate alkaline buffer here, more effective in pH range of 9 - 10.5, I guess.

Maybe interesting test (and I propose it for a more safe concentrate) with the pH jump effect, concentrated, the developer have near neutral pH but diluted the pH of the solution increases, An example is the tetrahydroxy borate anion (from borax or other borates) complexed with certain cis-1,2-diols such as sorbitol. Upon dilution, the complex dissociates, liberating free tetrahydroxy borate anions which increase the solution pH. Dissociation of polyborate ions also tends to promote this effect. The ratio of borate to polyol is important and in practice compositions containing approximately 5% borax and 20% sorbitol have been found to be effective.

I also think it'll be a mixed grating, with the colour of the grains determining the ratio of amplitude to phase. Certainly the grain size has a bearing. However, I also think a more important criterion is the grain density. The important conditionfor Rayleigh scattering is that the density of matter must be low (that's why the blue of the sky is not just due to Rayleigh), but the density of matter inside an emulsion is high so that interference effects caused by inter-molecular processes will have a greater effect than just individual scatter from molecules. Since this is the classic case of reflection from a dense medium, perhaps Sergio's idea to put a coefficant of reflection in the classic Kogelnik equation for amplitude modulation may work. I think, as the grain size increases, the inter-molecular interactions will decrease and you'll get either phase or amplitude a la classic Kogelnik. I doubt that at 1/50th lambda, the grain shape has much influence.

Another interesting point is that if the grains were charged ions, then they would oscillate at roughly the amplitude of the driving wave. Since this is 50 times the size of the grain, the mean free path of these molecules must be large. However, at high matter densities, there's nowhere for them to go, which presumably means that there's a lot of heat in there to drive some exotic reactions

That's probably a little diffuicult to say in a classical scheme. I believe the "classical" radius of an atom is about 0.5 nm so a grain that's only 10 times larger than an atom would probably exhibit quantum effects. I suspect the grains themselves will be subject to Van der Waals forces. If I had to make a wild guess, I'd say that, yes, it'll be a phase grating. I can't see any optical absorption at these levels, but I can see molecular absorption. In this respect, the shape of the grain and the charge on it, may make a difference because now we're beginning to see that the number of degrees of freedom of the grain may affect the absorption. If it's spherical, for instance, there won't be any rotational energy levels (all rotational energy levels will be degenrate) and so it'll be a weak absorber in the IR. In terms of holographic properties, I suspect grain density becomes even more important. Certainly the whole aspect of physical development becomes a little trickier. I suspect you'd have to abandon the traditional approaches and see this as a photonic crystal matrix. Perhaps use the Einstein drift equation that the semiconductor people use for p-n junctions.
