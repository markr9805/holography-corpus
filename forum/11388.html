Joe Farina Fri Nov 15, 2024 2:53 pm #2
There is a thread on this forum called "Single beam transmission" which is from 2013. If you haven't seen it, use the search function for "Single beam transmission" (with quotes) to find it.

I've never done one myself, but they used to teach this method at the Fine Arts Research and Holographic Center (and Museum of Holography) at 1134 West Washington in Chicago (now defunct). I've attached a link to a page from the notes they provided when I attended. I don't think there is a real need to expand the beam substantially, it's just that the more you expand it, the bigger the scene becomes. The objects will be back-lit using this method. There is also a second SBT method they described as "division of amplitude" which uses a mirror (maybe this is what you had in mind), and the object is front-lit.

I have made two transmission holograms that came out fine. The wide exposure latitude of the GEOLA film really helps.

I am wondering what the advantages of single beam (SB) transmission are over SB reflection. My observations so far;

1) The viewing angle for transmission holograms is amazing.

2) There is lots of speckle due to the need to use a laser to view the hologram.

3) You need a laser to view the hologram.

4) The lighting appears more natural compared to a reflection Denisyuk.

The books I have don't really describe very well what the advantages/disadvantages of various hologram types are.

Overall I'm more impressed by reflection holograms.
