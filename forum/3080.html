I sent an email to each of Vinteq and Ultimate. I got a reply from Vinteq and no reply from Ultimate, so I bought a box of small BB-640 plates from Vinteq.

So far my experience has been almost two years of using PFG-01 and 8E75. I'm here to tell anyone who wants to listen that BB-640 performs on an entirely different level from those emulsions. I've made 4 holograms on BB-640 so far, two H2's from a 8E75 H1 master, and one H2 from a BB-640 H1.

On the plus side, the BB-640 is much lower noise than the other emulsions, and has more dynamic range as well.

On the negative side, it requires much more energy than the others.

I'm probably going to end up using the faster/cheaper emulsions for geometry test shots, and go with BB for H1 masters and H2 final production copies.

Pardon me if I'm stating the obvious - although it seems the obvious is sometimes difficult to filter out from all the noise...

Tom B. Tue Feb 15, 2005 7:39 am #9
Sorry for lack of reply - I've been travelling and have had poor internet access. Just before leaving, I received some Ultimate red sensitive plates (15 nm with -70 nm factory preshift) and tried a couple. First impressions are very good, results were as bright or brighter than BB-640. Not sure yet how contrast and dynamic range compares, but it is definitely acceptable. Required exposure energy is low, as expected - for my test shot a few hundred microjoules/cm^2 (at 660 nm) was plenty.

Minor quibbles - plates were about 3 mm thick versus 2 mm for BB-640. I find the thinner plates easier to handle and will request this thickness next time. Also the cut edges were a bit more uneven on the Ultimate plates, maybe a side effect of the thickness.

Cosmetically, the Ultimate plates I used were not bad, not quite as free of pinholes and other coating defects as the BB-640, but certainly acceptable. Packaging is not as convenient - instead of a box with cardboard separator sheets, the plates are rolled up in a sheet of plastic which provides no support after a plate or two has been removed.

Re sensitizing BB-640, I also found the water/fotoflo soak to be ineffective, but did have some success with a very dilute ammonia (~ 1 minute) presoak as suggested by Dinesh. Not sure what percentage is best, but 0.25 to 0.5 percent ammonia (household cleaning product) worked for me.

Right now, with the diving US dollar, I think BB-640 and Ultimate plates end up costing about the same amount per plate. This could change.

For now, I'll continue to use both and continue to learn more about their relative strengths and weaknesses.
