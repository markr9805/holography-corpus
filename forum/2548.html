Regarding the question of Denisyuks with VRP, one reason may be that VRP is very sensitive to beam ratios. And I do mean 'very'! When I first started using it, I went through two boxes ($500.00 worth of plates!) getting almost zilch. After trying to literally trying to apply Quantum mechanics to the problem, I found that just a simple, small change in beam ratio solved it. I now get pretty bright reflections on VRP using H1/H2 setups. The H1's I make on VRP are exceptionally bright (I believe >70%), but again, very ratio-sensitive.

wler Thu Jun 24, 2004 5:52 pm #18
Well, there was some while ago a discussion about this issue (how

to best process VRP) at laserfreak.de, where some more or less

professional chemists gave their opinions. I had started the

discussion because of too much noise for my VRP H1 masters.

In a nutshell, the outcome was like this.

H1 VRP transmission masters: no bleach at all, but expose/develop

only to optical density 0.6 (light grey). This gives a dim but

noise-reduced master, and noise is a more important factor for

masters than brightness. Some fixing can be beneficial, tough

(potentially still less noise).

H2 reflection copies: expose to OD 2 (fairly dark). Then bleach but

with a rehalogenating bleach and not with dichromate (it seems to

be known by some people that dichcromate works poorly with VRP).

The rehalo bleach also does not shift color, which is important for

green lasers, obviously (and is less poisoneous as well).

I followed these recommendations and got much better reflection

copies this way, using EDTA bleach for reflection only. Dichromate

bleaching a VRP master really is hopelessly noisy, even more so for

green lasers due to increased scattering (I had spent my fair share

on experimenting with this). I have heard, partly also here, others

saying that PBU-Amidol as well is much better than dichromate for

VRP, but I don't have yet a comparison. At any rate, I think it is

pretty safe to say that dichromate (as in JD-2) should be avoided

for VRP. Otherwise, for me the superior emulsion is Ultimate, which

gives very bright and noise free holograms without great effort.
