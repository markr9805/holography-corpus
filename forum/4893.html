If you make a transmission hologram and illuminate it with a single-color LED, it looks great. You can choose the replay color by what color LED you use (more or less...). However, a very bright LED is needed.

If you illuminate it with a white LED and the object is close to the plate, it looks a bit blurry but otherwise excellent.

The brightness and depth of these holograms is much better than what I've been able to achieve with the Denisyuk setup.

Dinesh Fri Jun 10, 2005 7:47 pm #6
[quote:7abd3e79a1="Tom B."]For close up LED illumination with minimum distortion I imagine you would want to make the hologram with a converging reference beam to compensate for the diverging LED beam ?[/quote:7abd3e79a1]

Yes, but it's a matter of degree.

Ideally, you want the exact same reconstruction as the reference. In practie this is almost impossible, but the closer the LED, the greater is the mismatch between a collimated reference and a converging reconstruction since the greater is the curvature of the wavefront as seen by the hologram.. As Eric states, at some reasonable distance with the image fairly close to the image plane, the LEDs work fine. Also, the closer you get (or the further the image from the image plane), there are other effects besides just straight curvature. The curvature along both axes needs to be the same. Thus if the the LED were elliptical, the you need to match the H2 reference along both axes. Again, the closer the LED, the more important this becomes otjherwise you get either barrel or pincushion distortion along one or both mismatched ax(i,e)s. Again, unless you're extremely close it may not matter. In our case, with the holograms shown above, we were extremely close. I had to calculate the wavefront along the two axes and find a way to match them with the reference beam. 'Tweren't easy!

Of course, you can always exploit the aberrations to get some odd effects.

Dinesh Wed Jun 15, 2005 1:27 pm #10
[quote:8b7b07f361="rja4096"]

I'd say at over 6 inches the results are 'ok' but not as clear as with a spread laser beam. I'd say that the quality when illuminated this way is about the same a one of my denisyuk's - i.e. a clear image for objects near the film, and blurred the further into the background.[/quote:8b7b07f361]

The reason is that the blurring is dependant on the distance of the image to the image plane. Any image exactly at the image plane itself can be reconstructed with an ordinary white light source. As the image moves further out, in either direction, the source needs to be "narrower"(ie the spatial coherence needs to get better). This means that the source size, filament usually, needs to be smaller and smaller as seen by the hologram. The last sentence is important because many people hold a light source too close to the hologram. In this case, the hologram sees a relatively large light source and blurs. Better to hold the source a distance away. The actual colour (temporal coherence) does not matter with a transmission hologram. You can see the hologram without blurring with a laser of any colour (barring some distortion in the dimensions). In the case of a LED, the source size is the size of the diode, which is usually far smaller than a filament, so it's a good source for reconstructing a hologram. However, even the diode presents some size to the hologram, so the further out it is, the smaller it appears to the hologram. Also, holograms appear more blurred in front of the plane than behind it at the same distance. I suspect that's because in front of the plane, there is no reference frame or boundary bounding the image and the eye-brain can't make sense of it whereas behind the plane the hologram itself bounds the image and the eye-brain "sees' the image relative to the frame of the hologram. This can be tested by looking at an image in front of the image plane by say an inch or so in a mirror. Ordinarily, an image about an inch in front of the plane looks blurred while an image about an inch behind the plane looks cleaner. If you reconstruct the hologram but place a mirror in front of it (where you'd normally view the hologram) stand behind the hologram and look into the mirror, it looks cleaner and sharper. The edges of the mirror form a boundary against which the eye-brain forms a frame of reference.

[quote:8b7b07f361="rja4096"]The best results were with the orange one, Think I'm going to order a yellow next. - I have found that the bandwidth of LEDs is 50nm - I wonder if this can be reduced and a clearer image obtained with a coloured filter? what do you guys think???

Regards, Rick.[/quote:8b7b07f361]

The orange LED may have been better for a couple of reasons: the eye response in bright light conditions is beter for orange than red (it peaks at about 550nm or a pale grren-orange, in daytime vision), and/or the diode size of the orange LED may have been slightly smaller. As for narrowing down the bandwidth, you may be able to if the bandwidth of the filter is smaller than the bandwidth of the LED (unlikely), however to increase clarity you need to improve the spatial coherence, not the temporal coherence (assuming you're looking at a transmission hologram).
