Wow, those were three great talks.

[Ramesh Raskar](http://web.media.mit.edu/~raskar/photo/) surveyed a variety of ways to convert ideas from signal processing into ideas for (optical) cameras. Things like "fluttered" camera objectives and coded apertures. E.g. he removed blur from moving cars to read their license plates.

[Ed Boyden](http://edboyden.org/) discussed optical brain control - they genetically modify a virus that then genetically modifies a mouse embryo such that its brain becomes photosensitive...

Michael Bove gave an overview of various 3-D displays, including his recent MIT work using surface acoustic wave technology. They are claiming a VGA-resolution "postcard-sized" holovideo display with 30-degree HPO using GPU rendering of "chirps" [(pdf).](http://web.media.mit.edu/~vmb/papers/7233-1.pdf) Didn't show it because they're optimizing the SAW device for brightness and sharpness, but being at MIT they can spin a new device every WEEK. (Finally it's nice to see someone attacking 3-D display by creating new components.)

And if that wasn't enough, on my drive home up Mass Ave, I passed by the MIT Museum's [exhibition of holograms](http://web.mit.edu/museum/exhibitions/luminouswindows/) placed in the museum windows - at night, lit from inside the museum, were some really extraordinary pieces. I parked my car, got out, enjoyed the holograms, and then drove up the street to the cafe where I got my wife's phone number back in 1999... (aw shucks)

MfA Mon Mar 23, 2009 1:38 pm #8
Hmm, on reflection I'm pretty sure this doesn't generalize to a full parallax hologram when using only SAWs ... you can't simply scan a 1D pattern to get a 2D hologram, you need a 2D fringe active at a single moment to generate the 3D object wave.

You could drive the SAW on both axis of the exit plane, but when used in that way it works more like a holodot generator than a hologram generator. It can produce a full 1D grating the way they use it now is because the de-scanning is matched with the direction and speed of the wave on the SAW ... but that doesn't work in 2D, you can de-scan at an angle and match the speed of the waves on a given axis, but that way the diffraction pattern at a given view point will only stay static if the modulation frequencies were kept static. So the best you can do is holodots and lose a lot of the available bandwidth, I don't think that's practical.

I don't think even a OA-SLM would work for them, they might have the necessary video bandwidth but getting the necessary micron accurate horizontal and vertical alignment between scanlines seems to me very hard.

MfA Tue Mar 24, 2009 11:33 pm #10
It's what makes the magic happen ... the reconstruction of the hologram.

They work backward from the desired lightfield and known fringes for points (ie. a sinusoidal zone plate, although in their case it's only a 1D chirp because they only have horizontal parallax) rather than try to simulate an optical holography setup.

PS. it's a bit primitive though, relatively fast calculation of fringes for arbitrary textured polygons has already been done ... I think they could get that to work with HPO with the kind of models they are using.

PPS. oh hey, they do have a suggestion how to create full parallax with their system "One way to make a full-parallax scene is to multiplex the vertical views in the hololines and use a horizontally oriented lenticular array to disperse the views vertically." Of course if you go that way it throws into question the entire use of fringes altogether, you could use a fly's eye array for both horizontal and vertical dispersion and drastically simplify your optical setup ...
vmb Wed Mar 25, 2009 12:01 pm #11
The chips we're making are now a bit more sophisticated than what Dan Smalley describes in his master's thesis but we're still doing HPO (effectively a stack of 440 1-D holograms). The goal of this particular system is to make it ultra-cheap and to be able to drive it with a standard GPU while computing the diffraction patterns at 30 fps from a PS2-quality 3-D model.

We are also working out how to do full-parallax using related technologies but current GPUs don't have anywhere near the video bandwidth or number of operations/second required to drive such a display.

--Michael Bove

MfA Thu Mar 26, 2009 2:32 pm #14
Is that really necessary any more at this point?

If you dice everything up small enough, Matsushima's algorithm together with a directional touch up pass (for specular/occlusion/fog/etc effects) should be perfectly adequate. There is very little the optical simulation could handle which you can't find a way to hack with just operating on fringes, even mirrors and lenses can be done through portals. The only problem I don't think anyone has found a good algorithm for yet is removing the interference effects from multiplane RGB CGHs, intuitively I'd say there has to be a non iterative method to do it if you have the individual fringe patterns for each color ... but no way I'm going to try to find it.

I'm guessing it won't be long till you have to do it though, If you can do vertical and horizontal diffraction in one SAW putting multiple modulators in a series to handle multiple colours should be possible as well ... good luck, have fun.
