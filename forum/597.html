Dinesh Thu Aug 16, 2012 3:31 pm #3
Depends if you're collimating or not. Although, it makes little difference unless divergence of the ref was pretty large.

So, the beam at the top of your plate is hitting the plate at 60 degrees. The relation between the depth of the object, d, and the distance from the edge of the plate of the shadow line, s, is

s = d tan(60) {Perfectly generally, if theta is the ref angle, the shadow line is at s = dtan(theta) }

So, in your example of an object 6" away from the plate, d=6" and s=6tan(60) = 61.732 = 10.39". The shadow line will be off the bottom of an 8x10 plate laid out in portrait and the ref will completely miss the flat object placed 6" behind the plate. Turning the problem around, supposing you had a flat plate p inches long, how deep must the object be below the plate (d) so that the shadow line misses it and it gets fully illuminated? Assuming that the bottom of the flat object is in line with the bottom of the plate,

d = (10 - p)/(tan(60)) for a plate positioned in portrait

d= (8-p)/tan(60) for a plate positioned landscape

Perfectly generally, if the recording plate has dimension h in the direction of the reference, then

d = (h-p)/tan(60)

And, even more generally, for a ref at theta

d= (h-p)/tan(theta)

So,for a 6" flat plate laid out under an 8x10 holo in portrait, p = 6" and d = (10 - 6)/tan(60) = 4/tan(60) = 2.31". If the holoplate is laid out in landscape, then d = (8-6)/tan(60) = 2/tan(60) = 1.15". To give you some idea of the way the numbers are running, we can repeat for a 4" flat plate under an 8x10 in portrait mode. In this case p=4, d = (10 - 4)/tan(60) = 6/tan(60) = 3.5"

If the beam were diverging at an angle of divergence of delta, then perfectly generally, for a holo recording plate of dimension h in the ref beam direction, with a flat plate of dimension p, the "allowable" distance of the plate below the recording plate, and assuming that a line from the point source hits the centre of the recording plate at an angle of phi is

d = (h-p)/(tan(phi-(delta/2)))

As the beam gets more and more collimated, delta becomes smaller and smaller until finally, at collimation, delta = 0 and we're back to the collimation formula.

It occurred to me that the formulae given above refer to a flat plate placed at the foot of the recording plate. If the plate is to be placed at the centre of the recording plate, then you simply replace (h-p) with (h-p)/2.
