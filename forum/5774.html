Sometimes it pays to wait.

I've been wanting a hotplate stirrer for some time now but didn't have enough time to devote to serious emulsion making and didn't want to spend $50-150 on something that wouldn't be used often.

Over the weekend Andres and I went out to a local tech swap meet called [1st Saturday](http://www.1stsaturday.com/). It's got a long history and I'd been meaning to go for some time now.

I got lucky and found a guy selling a few hotplates and one hotplate stirrer. The stirrer was marked "stirrer doesn't work" but the hotplate worked fine and since the seller only wanted $10 I decided to give it a shot.

[](http://www.dragonseye.com/gallery/v/family/MichaelH/holography/HotplateStirrer.jpg.html?g2_imageViewsIndex=1)

(click for larger picture)

As it turns out, the magnets on the stirrer had come loose and were binding against the bottom of the hotplate. Once I'd glued them back in place the stirrer worked fine.

Now I've got a fully functional hotplate-stirrer and it only cost me $10 and a little cleanup and repair time.
JohnFP Tue Sep 05, 2006 10:29 am #2
Michael, be careful to test the hotplate/stirrer's temperature control with water prior to trying to make some emulsion. I have one hotplate/stirrer that I am very happy with and can easily get the temp to remain at 115F or lower. But the second one I have must have a lowest temperature of about 150F. I have to place a piece of corregated aluminum under the beaker to keep the temp at 115F. Luckily, the magnet is strong enough to still stir through the aluminum. Also the stirrer on the second one does not spin as slow as I like. Some hotplates have a higher minimum temp and a higher minimum RPM then others.

Just something to keep in mind for other when shopping for heater/stirrers.
