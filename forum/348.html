Joe Farina Fri Oct 07, 2011 4:57 am #7
It seems that practically any diode can make holograms under the right conditions. The problem lies in the unpredictability. That's why HeNe's are still so attractive. Many diodes have very long coherence lengths when they're not mode-hopping, but there's no telling when the mode-hopping will occur, usually. Wolfgang apparently made some very good systems, but the electronics and selection of diodes are not trivial matters, and I don't know if he plans to make any more.

The short-wavelength red diodes have promise for color holography, that's why I'm interested in them. Right now, if someone wants to do color holograms, they're limited to HeNe which determines the exposure times and/or the size of the hologram. For silver halide holograms, this isn't a very big problem, but for everything else it is. Of course, there's krypton, but few people have access to those lasers.

For HeNe light, a typical amount of power available would be 35mW. With that Mitsubishi diode for example, if it runs at reduced power (maybe to help stabilize it) and after subtracting losses from focusing optics, maybe 70 or 80mW would be available? (The nominal power is 110mW.) The question of course is stability, and someone would have to gamble $350 and spend the time to find out.
