Hans Thu Mar 23, 2006 11:48 pm #2
I believe that, the setup needs to be stable from the point where object beam and reference beam start their own separate ways. In the case of the Denisyuk Single Beam Reflection, that is the point where the reference beam passes through the plate towards the object and thus becomes the object beam as well. So if the plate and object are touching, or are rigidly connected, there will be less problems with vibrations. With multiple beam (split beam) holograms the vibration requirement starts at the point where the beam is split (first beam splitter). And as there is usually some spacing required between the necessary lenses, spatial filters and mirrors, a rigid and stable surface is required.

Hi,

2nd floor apartment , just above a railway tunnel, close to a secondary road.

Working with a ~20mW HeNe and MBDCG, 15 mins exposure time (yes, 900 seconds!!), I've got nice and bright denysiuks.

The setup consists of a 85x45X20 cm sand box with a 4mm thick iron plate laid on.

The sand box is then suspended on 4 small tubes.

I check for vibration through a mitchelson interf. during exposure.

It seems to work fine.

Ed Wesly Mon Mar 27, 2006 5:23 pm #6
I shoot directly on my basement floor, and have even made holograms of the floor, using my trusty old 7 mW Melles Griot He-Ne. Preliminary trials with a 160 mW red diode have proven unsuccessful.

Another mode of shooting I have invented and implemented is a wooden optical rail, a 4" by 6" by 8' beam, which I have christened "The Big Beam". It is tee-shaped or cruciform, depending on your beliefs, and it works quite nicely for two beam transmission and reflection holos. It was featured in Darkroom and Photographic Techniques magazine twenty years ago, I don't know if it's in any of the Saxby book incarnations, but for sure it is in Making Holograms by Don McNair.

Copies of the papers, the photo magazine and two SPIE variations, are, as usual, available upon request through this forum.
