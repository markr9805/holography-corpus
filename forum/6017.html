dave battin Thu Jan 25, 2007 6:23 pm #1
This mold coating process is fantastic! Thanks to everyone who added their info here……

I wanted to post some of my observations to possibly help others;

The glass i use for my mold is 1/4" mirror (back silver), while i wont call this glass optically flat, it is of optical quality.

The rain-x stuff is the key, I put a couple drops on the mold, then buff it in, heat the mold and film plate, and meter out your emulsion. To see the emulsion do a little dance on the rain-x is really amazing!

I have a "two suction cup" handle that I use to hold my plate and slowly drop/ hinge the plate along the taped edge of the mold. I then place six larger paper clamps around the perimeter

My plate size is 5"x 15" and i can get 3 nice 4x5s per mold , If metered carefully (absolutely leveled mold) ,only a few drips will come off the mold ends ………….

As soon as the gel sets (a few mins) I move the still slightly warm

plate and mold, on to a frozen piece of granite, where it rapidly cools down. Buy the way the granite can work both ways, for both cooling, and heating! I purchased these as granite tiles a the local Depot (12"x12"), and cut them up ........

The tape I used is plain scotch tape and is .0025” or @ 63 microns thick.

And to my surprise the film thickness came in @ .0004” or @ 10 Microns

After making a good number of mold coated plates i now have a few observations...............

By using the mirror as a casting plate it will allow you to see the micro bubbles easer

Micro bubbles are easily removed by dipping a fresh lint-free tissue into the puddle you just poured .

Curing time is much longer (im not sure why)? is it the rainX inhibiting evaporation ? When doing veil or Meyer bar coating the cure time is little as 2 hours, with the mold coating it takes at least 4hours, and with the cold temps lately in my lab up to 12 hours to cure without additional heat/air!

MarkCavin and i were comparing similar results based on the cold in both our labs, basically causing milkyness. In the end turns out to be the moisture still remaining in the film (now requires a 12 hour cure time) ………….

good news to hear john!

works well the ice blanket is adaptable to any size, as now i use the marble tiles (3/4 inch thick ) and cut specifically to size to fit nicely to the carrier plate bottom'(while my paper clips are still in place).

To apply emulsion i use the same old catsup bottle i used for veil coating ...when im ready pour my puddle, i invert it with my finger over the whole, and then "tap" it lightly to move any bubbles to the top, now remove finger and flow will start ,contact nozzle to glass, and in my case slide sideways to produce elongated bubble free puddle.

when you hinge it down push down slightly with one or two fingers from one hand and hold the plate with up with the other hand . to make these muscles work apposing each other, causing a much more uniform "hinging down" rate. If this action is not uniform you will pick up new bubbles again.

in the beginning you will have many failures, I would recommend put them on a pile for cleaning later

dave battin Wed Jan 02, 2008 12:10 pm #25
Since this last post it became apparent that the long taped edges were making a nuisance in pulling off the edges of the wet emulsion. it was about the same time i was reading about how Yuri Denisyak was mold coating in the early days, it said something like he "he used shim under each clamp ", anyway I ran a test and it worked perfectly with no edge pull, all I did was remove the tape /shim edges on my mold plate, and now only attach small pieces of tape where the clamp will contact the plate (attach these to film plate),

Later on you can remove the tape tab with your finger nail. The added bonus is you can freely pour back the emulsion, so it won’t require a clean-up on the edges before separation.

Please give it a try …….. The little peel off label/stickers you could use as shims?
