BobH Tue Dec 16, 2008 4:36 pm #11
I wouldn't recommend putting air isolators under the legs of an optical table. I've seen it done before and it's a very unstable arrangement. The center of gravity is way too high, and any shifting of weight on the table makes it want to tip over.

If you have those solid legs, you might consider bolting them to a piece of 3/4" plywood, and putting the air cushions between the wood panel and the table top. That gives you a convenient shelf under the table as well!

Ed!

I am up on my Stabl-Levls and I hope it's not too late for you to consider this option.

I've got ~900 lb of oldskool Newport table/crazy healthy legs up on four SLM-3 pneumatic isolators and have spent the last number of hours watching Michelson fringes.

It's currently early Monday morning and the world is waking up around me - I live about 1/8 mile from a busy thoroughfare and it sounds like the whole world is rumbling to work at this moment. My fringes remain unconcerned.

I had pizza delivered to my door last night. The fringes didn't budge.

My neighbor (connecting apartment) made a notable amount of noise setting off to work this morning (dresser drawers?, front door, car door). The fringes sat in blissful stillness through it all.

Every now and then the light out on the main road brings traffic flow to a halt. A few moments later I can hear a whole pack of vehicles get on the throttle all at once... The fringes don't notice.

I live right by a sharp corner on my not-very-busy street - the centrifugal force involved with rounding that corner in 2000lb of Detroit steel is considerable and happens only about 30 yards away. The fringes don't care.

In fact, the only way I can get my fringes to dance is to tap on the table, jump vigorously up and down right next to the table, or slam my bathroom door.

Hell, I have yet to get my stereo loud enough to see fringe movement. I listen to a lot of Drum n Bass through some big ol' Kenwood speakers, so this is no small thing to note!

The Stabl-Levl isolators I have here: Max load 300lb/isolator... max pressure 60psi. The instructions for these hogs had me bring them up to 45psi during installation. Fringes were sensitive at that pressure and I was worried I had just invested a lot of dough and time and effort in a bad system... At 45psi the isolators were a kinda funny shape (bulging/domed/peaked up in the center), and I went around and let out a little pressure one isolator at a time until they were more like their un-inflated shape. My Michelson (which by the by, remained aligned throughout), suddenly became still. The jitter was gone and suddenly stomping my feet next to my table wasn't enough to jiggle them - I have to jump up and down now with intent.

So far, the only vibration that disturbs my interferometer is that which originates from within my living space. Oh, how am I ever to avoid tapping on my table and jumping up and down and slamming my bathroom door during exposures? It'll be tough, but I'll keep my head down and power through somehow.
