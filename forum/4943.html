Digikey has a Sharp 50 mW 654 nm diode (Sharp GH06550B2B), Digikey p/n 425-1807-ND for $74.03. I haven't tried it, but it's cheap enough so it's not a disaster if it's not suitable for holography.

You may also want to look into getting a laser power meter - the topic has come up here several times. Because of part-to-part variation, it's very difficult to work with bare diodes unless you have a way to measure the output power.

Re constant current versus constant power - For holography I prefer constant current (actually I use constant voltage + resistor). This requires a thermally stabilized laser which can be done either actively via Peltier, or passively by coupling to a large thermal mass which is insulated from ambient temperature changes. I prefer the latter. The archives contain extensive discussions of this topic.
Colin Kaminski Sun Jul 10, 2005 2:43 pm #6
I would strongly advise you to use constant current. Constant power only works when it settles into a constant current state. When a diode stops mode hopping it is constant current, constant power and a constant temperature. You must have all three things or it will mode hop.

I used a 4 amp TEC but it was way overkill. You could use a much smaller one. Also, it is very important to seperate the diode from the otherside of the TEC in a thermal sense. Please read the information at melcor.com if you don't have any experience with TECs.
