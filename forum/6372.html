Hello all!

After endless tweeking, I have achieved reasonably bright, reasonably clear, reasonably detailed off-axis reflection holograms with a CLM-15 DPSS module (15mW @ 532nM). I am making exposures on VRP-M plates and developing with JD-2. I have had success color-shifting with table sugar in my wetting agent.

My only arguement at this point is the rings and stripes from mode hops. My laser is rather unsophisticated and that is fine because it was very affordable. It is air-cooled and apparently no aftermarket frequency stabilization is available...

It was suggested, however, that a combination of low-pass and high-pass edge filters (I had never even heard of edge filters - my laser knowledge is extremely limited) may help me with short-duration mode hops. The next suggestion was a notch filter (again new news).

I looked up edge filters online and was shocked and awed by the prices Notch filters were even more unreasonably-priced.

Might anyone know of an affordable solution for stabilizing the output of a DPSS module? Suggestions greatly appreciated!
Tom B. Sat Aug 04, 2007 6:44 am #2
1) Adding a filter outside the laser cavity will not by itself reduce mode hops. You need to stabilize the laser temperature to do this, since mode hops are due to laser cavity length changes with temperature. Once the laser temperature is stable, the linewidth can be reduced by using a wavelength selective device like a grating or etalon (normal optical filters are far too broadband) either inside the cavity, or, for diodes, with sufficient optical feedback to couple into the internal diode laser cavity and dominate the frequency. This seems to be very tricky to do properly. I think wler reported some discouraging results here for a non-DPSS diode setup. Might be interesting to think about how the DPSS version might work, if it worked at all.

2) It could be that the "rings and stripes" you report are not due to mode hops. Most rings (dark rings, anyway) are due to diffraction from dust on optical surfaces which is nearly impossible to avoid without using a spatial filter (lens + pinhole). Stripes can be caused by object or plate movement, but when they just refuse to go away and look like contour lines on a topographic map (or "sliced bread") it is most likely due to the laser being multimode and not single longitudinal mode (SLM) which is what's needed for holography. I've encountered several red diodes and modules that turned out to be multimode and the only solution was to set them aside and find something better.

3) Congratulations! You have made holograms with a generic green DPSS module. Most of us thought this would be hard or impossible since these are generally not good lasers for holography due to problems with stability and multimode behaviour. Since you have developed some green expertise, you might look onto getting a good DPSS laser like one of the surplus Coherent Compass lasers that comes up for sale now and then. A couple are mentioned in recent posts in the "For Sale" section.
