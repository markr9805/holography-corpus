I've heard this hunter/gatherer throey and I don't think it's completely valid. There's a hilarious send-up of the whole concept in a one-man play entitled "Defending theCaveman" It played in New York in 96 or so and arrived in LA about three years ago. Basically, he pokes fun at the whole male/female thing based on this theory. For example, shopping. Women are gatherers. They need to sift and choose from what's available. They need evaluate the different choices (presumably brought home by the hunters). So, when women shop, say for a shirt, they look at each shirt, compare them to each other, look at them in different lights, against each other etc, etc, etc. Now men, they hunt for a shirt. They seek their prey: a Shirt - damn the colour!. When they find A Shirt, they say, "Me have Shirt. Now, me go watch TV!"

However, since having got married, I have obviously been in close contact with one particular women. As always, when I see endemic or systemic behaviour in a system, I feel compelled to study it and see if there is any way to generalise the data and find any pattern to the data. Once a pattern is found, the next step is to create an hypothesis and thence a theory.

Since Joy is a member of a 150-women-strong Barbershop chorus, I've had occasion to study a large number of women. Also, having met several women at SPIE gatherings and having worked for several women, Edwina at Richmond and Joanna Jannsen at POC I've had a pretty broad set of "experimental data" to sift from. So, here are some conclusions:

Women tend to think inductively while men think deductively. The "essential" difference being that deductive thinking establishes specific behaviour from a generalised set of "laws" , while inductive thinking establishes a generalised set of "laws" from specific behaviour. John may explain the fact that a bouncing ball never reaches it's original height due to The Law of Conservation of Energy and The Second Law of Thermodynamics ("No system ever regains all it's energy - there's always a loss in energy for any dynamic system"). Understand, John does not have to be a physicist, he simply accepts the fact because there are "Laws of Physics" or "Laws of the Unverse" that decide the height of the bouncing ball - even if he doesn't know what they are exactly. Now Mary explains that the ball never reaches the original height because she saw a bouncing ball last week and that ball never reached it's original height, so, she guesses, bouncing balls never reach their orignal height. Of course, this example is incredibly simplistic, but I hope you see the point.

How does this affect the different approaches to technology? I think because a deductive thinker explains phenomena by starting with a general set of laws and the deconstructing them - "breaking them down" until the specific instance is explained. So the deductive thinker sees complex phenomena and breaks it down to more elementary phenomena and then "adds up" those elementary phenomena to "build back" to the complex. This is pretty much how the so-called "hard sciences" work. You "explain" weather by "adding" the phenomena of wind speed, humidity, electrical activity etc. Each of these constituent phenomena is seen as "independant" but "add up". In this sense, deductive thinkers are fascinated by "how it's done" because what they want to know is, "What are the basic phenomena that I need to 'add up' to make what I see. In a magic trick, the rabbit clearly is not produced inside the hat. Therefore: 1. the rabbit is placed there by the magician in a surreptitious way and then removed. 2. The rabbit was always there but we couldn't see it. 3. The rabbit was in another hat and the magician switched hats etc etc. Basically, what I see is a result of action 1, then action 2, then action 3. Each action being independant but producing a final result.

Now inductive thinkers don't break phenomena down like this. They see the entire phenomena as "one" - a sort of "holistic" approach to phenomena. So when an inductive thinker sees a rabbit out of a hat, she says, "So the rabbit came out of a hat. So what. Clearly that's a trick" and ignores the phenomenon. There's no urge to "break it down" to "see how it's done". If it rains, inductive thinkers - women- accept that it's raining without the need to "explain" rain by analysing relative humidity and air presure.

I think that this explains a number of phenomena:

Holoraphy. Deductive thinkers - men - see something that seems to "need explaining". How does that 3D image get on the glass? They seek to find an explanation by trying to come up with a set of sub-phenomena - interference, diffraction, reference-object beam interation, the plate is exposed like this, the plate is developed like that etc. Inductive thinkers - women - see a 3D mage on a piece of glass. They accept it's a 3D picture. There's no need to try to "explain" it. It's there. OK, it's 3D. So what.

Why women are "bad" at science and mathematics. I don't believe it's true. There are far more women than men in psychological departments. The number of women in chemistry and biological sciences probably outnumbers men, or is damn close to it! There is however, cleary a disparity in physics. I think because the "nub" of physics is the ability to break down phenomena into simpler phenomena and then sum the simpler phenomena back up. In the bouncing ball analogy, the ball starts with velocity of zero. It falls through a height h. It hits the ground at SQR RT(2gh), it rebounds at a velocity of e*SQR RT(2gh) etc. This is a subject for a deductive thinker, not an inductive one!

Mathematics is an odd beast. It seems as if women are not particularly good at analysis, but excel at Linear Algebra. However, Linear Algebra does take a "holistc" approach to problems. The entire vector is altered by a single matrix in one fell swoop. A function is summed as "an infinite series". One thing - an infinite series - taken as a whole entity. This seems to bear out the inductive/deductive hypothesis.

I think also that unfortunately physics and mathematics are badly taught. Mathematics is taught in an "algorithmic" way - to "divide", you do this, then you do that, then you do this, and then you get "an answer" - while physics is taught as a set of Laws, almost unbreakable"rules", set in stone, whose understanding is proved by a solving a set of problems where you break the problem down and then solve each sub-problem. However, physics, especially mathematical or theoretical physics, needs to be seen in this "holistic manner". If it can be taught in this manner, I think it'll be far less intimidating. I've sort of had a little success teaching it like this to a few private students I've had over the years. Mostly, I got my students by word of mouth - one student recommends someone else to see me who then recommends someone else. Curiously, almost all my students were female.

Let’s try to rationalize this “oddity” which cannot be due to chance . Neo-Darwinism is the only game in town which offers a reasonable answer .

1)We humans have obviously NOT become the top predators on the planet by superior running ability or muscle force use.

2) We have done it primarily by trap-setting and deceitful use of camouflage . Snare traps made from vine-ropes and strained saplings, pits covered and camouflaged cleverly to deceive other animals to fall into them, and large groups of stone age males working together made their most lucrative catches by deceiving herds of large animals to stampede over cliffs . (The ability to deceive has become so selected for in our species that it has become a major challenge to deal with it in our crook and scammer-ridden modern societies). However getting back to the “noble” craft of beast trapping by guile our ancestors brought home more bacon by working together and discussing the most effective trapping methods than those who wanted to go it alone. So males who cooperate on hunting techniques have left a good proportion of modern males with the evolved gene arrangements that drive them to co-operate and exchange experiences and ideas of the best trapping methods with other males.

And what is the fundamental point behind Colin’s forum? ---To cooperate and learn the best ways to trap those so elusive standing waves of light on our recording plates, ……Light, the fastest moving stuff in the universe, and here we all are contemplating trapping it with same degree of excitement as Masai warriors stalking the worlds fastest moving antelope as it stops for a quick nibble in the bush. That word excitement has cropped up in my posts along this thread again and again. Excitement only occurs when we are satisfying a genetically driven tendency. Thankfully I note that Joy P’s great revealing contribution to this discussion is still with us in this thread.
