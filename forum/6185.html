Joe Farina Tue Apr 17, 2007 6:46 pm #2
Based on Shankoff's graph in Applied Optics, Volume 7, Number 10 (1968), page 2102, the absorption of DCG practically peaks at 405nm. Yes, I saw that cheap 405nm diode system on fleabay. But how are you going to shift the reconstruction wavelength upward? A narrowband hologram reconstructing at 405nm would be extremely dim, and no shrinkage could be tolerated if you want a visible image. But maybe you could broadband the daylights out of it, and get something reasonably visible.

JohnFP Wed Apr 18, 2007 10:20 am #7
I will take a stab in the dark and say at 405 DCG only needs about 5 - 10 mJ/cm^2 (maybe even less).

Red shifting is not difficult to do if you plan it that way from the begining. Broadband would be better for display and it quite easy.

To get broadband;

User a thicker emulsion (about 15 - 25um)

Expose the emulsion while it is young.

Fix

Use warm water for swelling (about 5 mins)

Then use warm alcohol for processing and only keep in each alcohol 10 seconds except the last bath. Keep it in the last bath until you see the hologram fully developed (but dim).

Blow dry hot and heavy.

You will get a very nice bright hologram. Isay go for it.

Its actually more difficult to produce a very narrow band then broad band because quite a few parameters need to be regulated very closely; emulsion thickness, pre exposure gelatin hardness, processing time, processing temperature, etc...
