JohnFP Wed Jun 07, 2006 9:14 am #3
The woodgrain is caused by internal reflections. As the glass/emulsion has a different index of refraction then air you will get reflections at the air to glass/emulsion interface and from the glass/emulsion to air interface (both sides of the plate). This second reflection goes back through the glass/emulsion and again hits the glass/emulsion to air interface and is again reflected backin into the glass/emulsion. This happens many times. I believe it is the interference between the second, fourth etc... reflections in which the light is traveling near to the same direction of the reference beam which causes the wood grain (small angular difference thus large fringes). I used glass/emulsion together in this as their index of refraction is very near the same.

Understand that P polarized means horizontal to the table for a side reference beam and perpendicular to the tabe for an overhead reference beam. P polarization in conjunction with the brewsters angle (appox 56 degrees from the normal) will illiminate the wood grain effect.

Joe Farina Mon Jun 12, 2006 11:45 am #6
John, it sounds like you never had the unpleasant experience of laser diode holography

With diode mode-hopping, the lines do appear on the object. It looks like a zebra. The width of the zebra lines depends on the wavelength differential which results from mode-hopping. Narrow lines mean a large differential (i.e., short coherence length). Broader lines mean a smaller differential (i.e., longer coherence length).

On the other hand, an indication of object movement would be irregulardark lines, patches, etc., on the object. But it wouldn't look like a zebra.
