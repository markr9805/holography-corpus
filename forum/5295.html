I'm surprised nobody replied to this one. So...

Dunno. Probably not, unless you can keep the chloride ions from reacting with the emulsion, something else, or just going away. Otherwise, photo paper that had been exposed to enough light to darken it could just be left in the dark long enough to "reset".

Yes, it's known in photography as a "chloride emulsion", in which the main silver halide content is silver chloride (usually referring to B/W printing paper). I'm not sure how much chloride is in your average film, but the bromides accept dye sensitizaion well enough.

Silver nitrate and table salt to make silver chloride? Yes. Early dry plates were made more or less this way, except the silver nitrate was cooked in with gelatin. Lacking the gelatin, I believe the resulting silver chloride nuggets will become too large. Silver chloride is mostly insoluble in water, so the mix would cloud up and then settle.

Copper sulfate and table salt, I'm not sure about that one. Copper metal and hydrochloric acid maybe, I've grown copper crystals that way.
