Here are holograms of two objects I made by hand specifaclly as test objects.

The first image below is a stereogram of a hologram that was shot as a Single-Beam Denisyuk.

Although it appears as just another skull hologram, it is rather unique in that it reconstructs orthoscopically, with the image floating 1-2cm in front of the glass, as if it were a split-beam image-planed H2, but with a wider view angle like most regular single-beam Denisyuks.

This image is about technique rather than content.

Type: Single-Beam Denisyuk

Medium: 4x5 PFG-01 Glass Plate

Laser: 7mW Unpolarized HeNe

Note: Green is due to over-exposure.

![Image](http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/albums/album20/lmdstereoskull.jpg)



This next single-beam Denisyuk is similar to the one above in that it reconstructs orthoscopically, but with the image floating ~3cm in front of the glass.

Note the interference fringes visible on the object. I believe this is not from motion, but rather is from reflections between the gold-leafed object, and the glass plate that sandwiched the film, a little like a Fabry-Perot interferometer.

Type: Single-Beam Denisyuk

Medium: 4x5 PFG-01 Film

Laser: 7mW Unpolarized HeNe

![Image](http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/albums/album20/Ortho_Cross.jpg)

Good guess Rick...Exactly I have never read Practical Holography, but I did decide to try making the objects this way after accidentally putting the plate in the holder backwards The cross was also made from a clay impression of a wodden cross, then I used a paint on gold-leave (it actually uses copper particles suspended in Xylene) to get a metalic look.

> MichaelH: How do the fringes move on the cross when you rotate the hologram side-to-side?

I'll have to look again, but I'd say there's greater than 100 deg. view angle, and the fringes move about one-half to one full fringe total across the surface. I'll edit this post later if I find something different when I look at it again.

The imaging of the fringes was completely accidental. Family and friends that I've shown it to love the effect. I actually shot three test shots on film, moving the object slightly more away from the plate on each shot, and all three exhibited the fringes. When looking through the plate at the object I tried to get the absolute brightest reflection from the metallic surface shining exactly into the normal of the hologram plate. If I rotate the object slightly off axis, the fringes don't appear.
