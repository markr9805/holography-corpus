Joe Farina Tue Apr 12, 2005 11:23 am #1
Thanks to everyone who has provided feedback.

I want to make split-beam color reflection holograms, and there are a number of problems to address as I set up the lab.

I have two lasers and am looking for the third. The first two are a SP-127 doing 37.5mW at 633, and a 315M doing 100mW at 532. (I was getting more power from the 315M previously, before I bought Sam's autostart adapter, but I don't want to drive it too hard, and using the autostart adapter provides a stable 100mW, which should be enough. For people with a 315M, I recommend the autostart adapter, which is available on Sam's classified page, for $50, I think.)

I'm fairly certain the 315M can be used without forced cooling, if large heat sinks are used for the laser head and controller. The SP-127 of course doesn't have any special cooling requirements.

Providing the blue wavelength is problematic in more ways than the cost. Assuming an argon laser is used, the water-cooling can introduce vibrations which don't seem to be compatible with split-beam holography, if the laser is attached to the table.

What I'm trying to decide is whether or not the lasers should be attached to the table or not. Based on what others have said so far, I'm thinking that all three should be off the table. I haven't used a spatial filter with a laser off the table, but it seems like a number of people do it this way. I figure that if this isn't too much of a problem, then adding the two other beams (to make three co-linear beams) should be possible. And I think Bob said that he does this. I guess a fair amount of care has to be taken so that the diameter of the beams (and divergence) are matched, as Bob and Colin stated.

Anyway, I have to give these things some thought before the laser-layout is planned, and the table is put together.

[quote:62aac6103c="Joe"]Assuming an argon laser is used, the water-cooling can introduce vibrations which don't seem to be compatible with split-beam holography, if the laser is attached to the table.[/quote:62aac6103c]

Vibration and motion are funny things that are very difficult to quantify objectively. In some cases you're convinced something will move and it doesn't and in other cases you make holograms of rocks and they move! Unfortunately many people make holograms of things that are traditinally supposed to "move" and, in their case, it doesn't. This convinces them that generations of well-known holographers are talking hooey!

Anyway, the reason I bring this up is that I have a Newport table with a water-cooled Argon laser on it and I have no problems with 10 minute exposures. However, in another comapny in the same circumstances (water-cooled Argon on the table) we get getting motion on an 18" thick, floated Newport. Impossible, right? Anyway, we solved the problem, walked around it really, by filling a large processing tray with sand and placing the input water hose on it. This seemed to solve the problem, if not the reason for it.
