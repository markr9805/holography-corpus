Tony Wed Jan 24, 2007 1:24 am #2
In order to view a hologram, the reconstruction beam must match (fairly closely) the reference beam used to record the hologram. This all works out OK when the reference beam is plane waves (perfectly collimated) or spherical wavefronts as from an expanding lens. These can be close enough to a diverging wavefront from a point source lamp, or an expanding beam from a laser (in the case of transmission holograms) that the image is reconstructed fairly well.

Using a diffused source for a reference beam could work fine - as long as you use an exactly matching beam for reconstruction. However, that's nearly impossible, as it would be nearly impossible to match the wavefront distortions and phase variations of the original reference beam.

Not to overlook the fact that the polarization would be random after the diffuser.

So, the answer is theoretically yes; practically, no.

Manipulating the reference beam was discussed in the early years as a way of encoding holograms so that only those that had the contrived wavefront could view them. Who knows where that idea went?

What I have seen in the way of diffused reference beams was to simply hit a ground glass with an undiverged beam, and use the beam that got spread that way as the reference for a Single Beam Reflection holo. It worked, however the surface fo the 'gram had that laser speckle texture on it.

So yes, it can be made to work!
