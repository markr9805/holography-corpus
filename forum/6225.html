Bill Alschuler wrote:

> Recently (see the 2006 Daguerreian Annual) a hologram was produced on a daguerreotype plate; it was in silver halide on polished silver on a copper plate with no emulsion. It was very slow and of poor visual quality.

Could you please elaborate on this? Is the final product visible on the Web?

The only experiments that I have heard of using daguerreotype plates for holography were performed by Mike Teitel when he worked for Craig Newswanger, circa 1988? Not sure what incarnation Craig's company was in, ADD, Applied, whatever.

The goal was to make gratings to test the usefulness of the plates for holographic replication. It was thought that a metal plate would be more conducive to electro-forming. However, it pooped out at a spatial frequency of around 200 line pairs per mm.

I don't think I have ever seen a paper detailing this, all I know is from Craig without ever seeing any results.

One would think that these extremely slow processes would have practically infinte resolution, but I guess not!

I'm sorry to dredge up this seemingly ancient topic but I have a question about this process.

Being new to the holography process and relatively acquainted with the daguerreotype process (as a working practitioner), I'm wondering if someone can tell me about the LASER and other equipment used for this process.

It seems that a 5-minute exposure was made using a 330mW Ar-ion laser at 458nm. Something with that many numbers next to it sounds rather expensive. Where does one find such a thing and what sort of price range are they?

With an exposure that long, I assume you need a serious isolation table?
walschuler Wed Jul 25, 2007 8:45 am #6
Argon lasers are generally in the thousands of dollars, and 5 minute exposures do require excellent stable tables, but the latter requirement can be met at reasonable cost and with low tech, with a good location, slab on grade floor, away from major traffic, good vibration isolation, and little structurally coupled vibration (such as from motors for A/C, etc.).

Argon lasers can be found at Meredith, MK Lasers, some light show equipment purveyors and sometimes on EBay. Others reading this may offer other suggestions, and you could post on this forum to see if anyone has one to sell.
