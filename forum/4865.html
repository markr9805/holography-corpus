JohnFP Thu May 26, 2005 9:15 am #2
Brian, set up an interferometer with the Air Cooled Ion laser. It will tell you everything. Start off with path lengths of each leg being the same length. Monitor the fringes.

As far as the pulsed laser, if it is pumped by Xenon flash lamps then it is pulsed. Does the power supply have different pulse rates, ie. 1 PPS. 5 PPS, 50 PPS etc? If so it may be the wattage is a function of the higher pulse rate but, and may be be a caculation based on the raw IR energy without doubling and cleaning. But this is just a guess based on my experience working with pulsed YAG lasers (not for holography). Is the pulsed laser head water cooled? I think it would be. Look at the head for inlet out oulet water connections. We used objectives and simple glass lenses for our high power pulsed laser but the objective has to be the ones that do not have optical adhesives within them (this blackens on high power). For a pinhole, high power pinholes are available. Depending on wavelength used one can purchase, Gold plated, Molybdenum, Tungsten, ceramic and even Diamond pinholes. For puled holography, which is using a single pulse, you would need to determine the energy per pulse first then you can spec out the optics required.

Eyeware can be purchased easily (do a search on the interenet - even eBay) and is decided on by the output wavelength.

> meekan01: It does seem to have a large amount of optics, including what looks like a "Q switch" and a frequency doubler. I'm not quite sure what a Q switch is, but for some reason I'm thinking this can be used to make pulses... or am I confused?

A q-switch is basically a very fast optical switch. It's generally used in pulsed lasers to concentrate the energy of the laser into a very short pulse, rather than a lower-peak-power wider one that would otherwise result.

If you have a q-switched laser with CW pumping of the lasing medium, you'll probably get a train of very fast high-power pulses that average to 130W. Kind of like PWM modulating the output of a chip to get an analog voltage...

To use it as a pulsed laser for holography, you want one single BIG pulse. I did a quick search on alt.lasers and came up with the following related thread:

[http://groups-beta.google.com/group/alt ... 17b267f4d3](http://groups-beta.google.com/group/alt.lasers/browse_thread/thread/cc8f28149757a25f/b7f1f217b267f4d3?q=lamps+xenon&rnum=1&hl=en#b7f1f217b267f4d3)

"1. CW arc lamps have very thin walls - below 1mm, more like 0.5mm

This is in order to keep thermal resistance low.

2. Flashlamps are much more thick-walled. The discharge acoustic wave

is a big stress to envelope material. "

So you probably won't be able to use your existing arc-lamps for pulsed holography. (The laser might very well work for regular CW holography, however.)

But, you might be able to replace them. Please post to alt.lasers asking the professionals for advice before you try it on my say-so, I'm just a hobbyist!

[http://repairfaq.ece.drexel.edu/sam/COR ... 04-04.html](http://repairfaq.ece.drexel.edu/sam/CORD/leot/course04_mod04/mod04-04.html)

Judging by this page, you guys will probably need to heavily modify the power supply in addition to replacing the arc-lamp with a flash-lamp. Assuming you can find a lamp that fits appropriately in the cavity, the power-supply modification should be fairly simple. You already have the monster 1F demonstration array, this just bumps the voltages up a bit and adds some pulse-shaping
