Well, if you insist

Bear in mind, statistics was never my favourite subject. I, sort of, believe:"There are lies, there are damned lies and there are statistics". However, let's proceed in this way. You have a bin of all the entries (let's call it bin_1 and N entries), you take two out and pick one, either by merit or by chance. The losing entry is discarded and the winning entry goes into another bin(bin_2). You conitinue this until there are none in bin_1, ie there were an even number of entries, or there is one, ie there was an odd number. If there is 1 left over, you transfer that into bin_2 unjudged. You repeat the process with a new bin, bin_3. You n=continue this process till there is only one left in bin_n.

The number os ways to pick 2 objects out of N is 2_C_N, where

n_C_r = n!/(r!)((r-n)!)

Thus, having picked two objects, there are now N-2 left in bin_1. Repeat to give 2_C_(N-2), 2_C_N-4...2_N_N-m, where m N-m=0 so that we have the number of ways, W_1, of picking all the entries to place in bin_2 is:

W_1 = 2_C_N + 2_C_N-2 + 2_C_N-4 + ...+2_C_N-m.

If each pair is chsen at random, then there is a probability of 0.5 of picking either of the pair. So, the probability of your hologram being transferred from bin1 to bin_2, P_1 is:

P_1 = 0.5W_1

If the entries are chosen by merit, then we'd have to introduce an "expectation value" of probabilities of characteristics N_1 (which may represent, say, beauty), N_2 (which may represent technical ability) etc. In this case:

P_1 = (0.5)(N_! + N_2 +...N_q)(W_1)

We can then proceed in this way to get the appropriate probabilities of surviving into bin_3, bin_4 etc. Finally, the final probablitiy that yours will win is using the "bin" method:

P_bin = 1/( P_1P_2P_3....)

However, if they're all presented all at once,P_all,, then the only method I can see is an "expectation value" method, but altered to the fact that the "result of experiment" is the number of judges, J_n, in each of n categories:

P_all = N_1J_1 + N_2J_2 + ...N_m*J_m

It seems off hand, that the number of entries and the number of judgement criteria will determine which of these two methods will succesfully get a "win". Since the ratio of P_bin/P_all depends on the number of terms in each case.

This is a (very!) back-of-an-envelope calculation, so it's probably nowhere near as rigorous as it should be. In other words, take it with a large dose of salts!
