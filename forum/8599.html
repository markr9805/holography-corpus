Din Tue Aug 16, 2016 12:27 pm #2
No, it can't be done. Any imaging system must scatter light, or deviate light through an optic, such as a lens. In a hologram, the image is formed by interaction with light onto an already recorded hologram. Once the hologram is recorded with an object source (say, a porcelain cat) the object is "hard-wired" onto the holographic medium. Illuminating the holographic medium will create an image of a (already recorded) porcelain cat, but it'll be static. Also, it can only be observed within the confines of the holographic medium. So, if the holographic medium is a glass sheet 8" x 10", the image of the cat can only be seen inline and within the confines of 8" x 10". It is possible to encode video onto a hologram, but the video will play out as the hologram is rotated or shifted laterally. It is possible to record a video, and have the equipment rotate the hologram (for example: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Nq4ri2t-HI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Nq4ri2t-HI) ), but again, the previous limitation remain. The hologram is pre-recorded and the image is fixed, also it can only be viewed within the confines of the medium.
