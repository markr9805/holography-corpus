A nail stuck at right angles in a piece of cardboard or plexiglass makes a great angle measuring device for spread beams. This fleshed-out normal (or gnomon, in sundial terms, notice the root word) casts a shadow on the substrate.

When the shadow is the same size as the nail, the angle of incidence is 45 degrees. Drawing concentric circles whose radii are the tangent of the angle times the height of the nail gives you other angles of incidence.

For instance, the shadow cast by a 25 mm nail with a 60 degree incident beam will be (tan 60 =) 1.7320508075688772935274463415059 * 25 mm = 43.301270189221932338186158537647 mm

Just being silly, one or two decimal place precison is all that's necessary!

This device was mentioned in the article I mentioned in my other post on Expired Plates in this forum, "Recycling Holographic Plates", Proceedings of the Third International Symposium on Display Holography, Lake Forest College, 1988.
