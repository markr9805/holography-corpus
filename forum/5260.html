I suspect that a simplification is becoming generalized...

The simplification is that in a Denisyuk setup, by locking the object and the plate together, the stability requirements are considerably reduced.

I think this is becoming generalized to read that as long as the object and plate are locked together, any movement can be tolerated.

I don't think the generalization is true. In a typical Denisyuk setup, where the laser is some distance from the plate, there's usually enough stability provided to at least maintain the angle between the beam and the plate to a good (sufficient) degree.

Consider a very simple setup, making a Denisyuk hologram of a flat mirror. Further assume that the beam is perfectly collimated, and therefore has planar wavefronts. At the plate, there would be two plane waves interfering, one from the source and the other from the object (mirror). These would interfere to form fringes whose spacing depends on the wavelength used and the angle between the two beams. If the incident angle between the reference beam and the object beam changes, then the fringe spacing will change. If this happens during exposure, then the hologram brightness will be reduced, possibly to zero.

The simplification serves to eliminate the deleterious effect of the most significant motion, but it does not elimnate the effects of all motions.

Furthermore, I would also suspect that the heat from your hand might cause thermal effects which would result in motion between object and plate - better wear an oven mitt!

Let us know if it works!
