You will need,

Unflavored, uncolored plain gelatin powder

Ammonium Dichromate

Deionized or Distilled water

That's it!
Colin Kaminski Mon Jul 19, 2004 3:23 pm #3
A heated stir plate is handy but not required. A thermometer is required. The exact recipie and the processing scheme you use must be matched and will take some trial and error.

You can do green with out adding a dye. You will need pure alcahol and some processing trays. You will also need a way to harden the galetin after exposure. Fixer with a hardner is often employed.

The basic idea is to add gelatin and water and heat and stir till it is consistant. Then add the dichromate. Filter and coat the plates. Let dry for a day or two and don't use plates after a week or so.

Expose with a lot of energy, then fix and dry the plate.

To dry the plate you will step it through baths of 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% then dry with a hair dryer under high heat.

The only exact recipie I have was shown to me by Tripple Take Holography and I don't want to divuldge it as it is how they make their money.

If you search the old forum you will find some references to the ratios of water gelatin and dichromate. Setting up to do this is a long path and expect 100s of failures untill you get everything dialed in.
Dinesh Tue Jul 20, 2004 1:27 pm #4
You start with gelatin. There are many sources, but the three main ones are (in no particular order):

Vyse ( [http://www.vyse.com/vysehomepage.htm](http://www.vyse.com/vysehomepage.htm) )

Great Lakes ( [http://www.greatlakesgelatin.com/](http://www.greatlakesgelatin.com/) )

Knox ( [http://www.kraftfoods.com/knox/](http://www.kraftfoods.com/knox/) )

Which one you choose is pretty much personal preference, intended results and experience. The important thing is something called "Bloom Strength". This is a measure of hardness of the gelatin and will determine factors such as bandwidth and color. There are also other variables like pig or cow gelatin and particulate or non-particulate. These companies usually have someone who can advise you, since most DCG holographers go to one or other of them. The exact amount again depends on what you intend to do with it. A good, basic starting formula is about 40 gms of gelatin to about 300 ml of water. Then you add the Ammonium (or Potassium) Dichromate. Again, the exact amount depends, but you can start with about 5 gms.

The idea is to slowly dissolve the gelatin in the water, then add the Dichromate, keeping the mixture at below 60 degrees C. It's a good idea to use a stirring plate, but you can always stir with a spatula. The important thing is to mix well.

There are many ways to coat, from spin to wire wound. The most often used way is to pour the gel mixture onto the plate and put the plate on an old 78 rpm record player. Spin until the plate is uniformly coated, usually about 2 - 3 minutes.

the coating thickness is fairly important and is controlled by temperature. if the coating is too thick (greater than 30 microns) you'll have a great coating for HOEs but not much good for display. If too thin (less than 5 microns), it'll be noisy. A good depth is about 10 - 15 microns. Put the plates in a well-ventilated area for drying. The drying time varies, again depending on the results you want. Some keep it two or three days and some use it in hours. The exposure is much higher than with Silver, from 30 to 100 times as long.
