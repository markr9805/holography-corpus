MichaelH Sun Nov 07, 2004 2:12 pm #4
There are almost as many options as there are members on this board.

If you want to back your hologram (as opposed to painting it, which I'm opposed to) I highly recommend Oracal 651 which I happen to buy through Advantage Sign Supply but you should be able to get from any sign supply house. It provides a permanent deep black backing and since you're putting two curved plastics together back to back they tend to cancel out and the film flattens.

For holograms I don't intend to frame I then get use an acid-free glue stick and adhere the hologram to a piece of picture-matte cardboard. That provides a nice stiff backing.

Actually, some of the holograms I've framed have also had that backing. It just depends on how much room there is between the glass and backing board of the frame.

While I haven't been brave enough to permanently adhere my reflection holograms to glass, Andres has been happy with a couple he's laminated using the Kapco OPCLR film I gave him. It's an optically clear film that you roll out onto the glass and then roll your hologram face down. You have to be extremely careful about dust since once the hologram is laminated you can't remove it without ruining it.

Martin Mon Nov 08, 2004 1:07 pm #7
> DannyBruza: why cant you use jello to mount film to glass

Water based materials (e.g. gelatin solution) are likely to provide little success in

such case.

One problem is the TAC film support being hydrophilic might

absorb a relatively high amount of water.

Secondly, in my experience it may prove be quite tricky to apply a water based

solution to the film base without getting small amounts of that solution on

the emulsion side.

The opposite case, index matching the emulsion side on glass by means of gelatin etc. has to be discarded since it takes quite some time to get that layer dry. In addition, the drying process leads to irregular drying marks (color changes), severely damaging the “cosmetics”...

Having said that, I all the same have to point to Bjelkhagen. In his book he mentions that Kaufman used to seal

his holograms with gelatin solutions. It remains unclear to me whether this was meant to be applied to the old Agfa (polyester) films though.
