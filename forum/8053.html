Joe Farina Sun Nov 01, 2009 2:19 pm #1
I tested a warm white 8mm LED which is supposed to be 210,000mcd, 140 degree, 300mA @ 3.4 to 3.6V (eBay item number 390070765926).

Right off the bat, I thougt these LEDs were defective because they wouldn't light up. Then, just for the heck of it, I reversed the polarity, and it worked. So the long pin, normally called the anode post, was connected to the negative side of the battery, while the short post, normally called the cathode post (with the chip) is connected to positve. That's really strange. Maybe somehow I messed up, but it just didn't seem to work the other way around. To get the full 300mA, it has to go well over 4V. And the leads quickly got hot at that current, enough to burn my finger. I don't know what's going on.

Anyway, I took some photos. One thing I forgot was to take a picture of the beam on a white sheet of paper. It's very smooth, which is to be expected with a 140 degree LED, with no hot spots, and is similar in this respect to the wide-angle LED shown on the other thread.

The first photo shows the ambient lighting in the room, without the LED. The next ones show the LED at different currents, 83, 126, and and 238mA. Judging from the photos, the appearence of the hologram does not appear to change much. In reality, it doesn't either. But I can tell the light is considerably brighter as the current goes up. This probably has more to do with the high diffraction efficiency of the hologram itself.

The last photo is the sprectrum of the LED from my $10 spectrometer (basically a diffraction grating in a box). The spectrum is on the left, the one on the right is some kind of reflection. Overall, I'm very pleased with the spectrum of the LED. Undoubtedly it has a much higher CRI as compared to the "plain" white LEDs that I tested earlier. Plus the color is more pleasing in general. The plain white LEDs are kind of like cool fluorescent lights, while the warm whites are more like incandescent bulbs. Overall, very, very attractive light sources for small holograms.

Joe Farina Sun Nov 01, 2009 4:14 pm #3
Thanks Tony. I have to prefer the warm white LEDs due to their color, and I'm fairly certain they will be much better for color holography. I believe that the warmer white light (with lower color temperature) will have a better color rendering capability, compared to the the cool white LEDs (with higher color temperature). They supposedly have "high CRI" LEDs nowadays, but I know little about them.

The only other difference between the two types is that the warm whites are supposed to be less bright than their cool white counterparts. The person who tested those white LEDs on the candlepower forum claims that warm whites simply use a higher amount of doping (more phosphor) on the blue chip which results in a warmer white and less power getting through. I don't know if that's true, and I've read elsewhere that the warm whites use multiple phosphors. But this brightness issue is really immaterial, because it has no practical impact for our purposes. Slightly less power makes no real difference. The important part of choosing a LED is the distance it will be from the hologram. Close to the hologram (say 1 to 3 inches), it should be a wide angle LED (~120 to 140 degrees), or if it's further away, it should have less divergence. Those which have a more tightly focused (i.e., different reflector cup for the chip and different case compared to the "straw hat") have much higher brightness ratings.
