Here's a photo of this week's project - another homebrew spatial filter. This one is made of 1" aluminum angle and 1/4" Lexan. (acryilic would work fine but I had the Lexan left over from a combat robot project).

The basic idea is a cantilevered beam made from angle. One end pivots and slides on the Lexan corner. The other end is positioned by screws in 3 axes. A pinhole is mounted to the beam in front of the objective.

The screws are 4-40's. These will soon be replaced with Thorlabs adjusters.

Note the high-tech springs! (I wonder what holographers would do without rubber bands and magnets?)

That is a Really Cool (tm) spatial filter... The lever gives mechanical advantage to the adjustment screws, which gives a smaller motion of the pin hole for some motion of the screw. Put the pin hole on the other end of the lever (closest to the pivot), and the ratio of pinhole motion to screw motion will be even smaller.

Or, put another set of X and Y screws on the pivot end. The pivot end would be a coarse adjustment, and the opposite end is a fine adjustment. The only problem with that arrangement is that if the lever is allowed to rotate, the pinhole would move too far. The pivot (really 2 pivots, one in the X direction and one in the Y direction) need to be bars which the lever sits on, not points like a screw would be.
Tony Wed Jun 01, 2005 12:28 pm #4
This is actually V2.0 of the cantilevered beam spatial filter. Version 1.0 had more 'leverage', with the adjustment screws being twice as far from the pivot point as the pinhole. With 4-40 screws, that one was easy to adjust.

V2.0 was designed to accomodate a wider variety of microscope objectives, and have a shorter overall length. Because the Thorlabs adjusters are available, not as much emphasis was placed on the adjustment resolution. With 4-40's (and loose ones at that) adjustment on V2.0 is moderately touchy.

With any of these cantilevered beam designs, there is some interaction between adjustment axes, because the axis of the lever is not the same as the axis of the lens. There are various means of correcting this error, which shall remain trade secrets...
