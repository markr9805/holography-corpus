Dear Holographers,

Spatial filtering objective: to clean up the green beam for better and clearer holograms with the pulsed holography system.

I have performed a series of tests on the spatial filtering of the SSY-1 frequency doubled output (green).

The lens is a 15mm focal plano-conves lens from Edmunds Optical for $24. I am using an XYZ positioning optical mount with the apertures taped into a 1 inch circular cardboard mount.

Stainless steel apertures are totally destroyed by the 4nm pulses of about 20mJ using the standard PFN1 and trigger boards.

Molybdenum apertures are also melted by this intense energy. This was unexpected.

I now have a ceramic aperture under test. This material was recommended by the aperture supplier after learning of the fusing of the Molybdenum material.

The problem I am having is that this is a translucent white material and passes a dim diffuse area of green, which makes it hard to align. My best results are using the Joulemeter to align it, but I have been unsuccesful so far. I may use my blown Molybdenum aperture to help me get it close, by placing it over the hole in the ceramic to get me close.

More reports as they come in.

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen

Bill Jensen Fri Jun 09, 2006 11:56 am #4
Dear Holographers,

My focal length of about 15 mm was chosen so I can take holograms at about 12 to 20 inches away from the green laser output.

I can pulse about 5 seconds between shots at lower voltages of 650 VDC for aligning the spatial filter. Even so, it does not help.

I have currently given up trying to use a spatial filter of ceramic material, which seems to be able to withstand the high energy pulses from my SSY-1 system. There is too much diffuse scattering through the translucent white ceramic for me to be able to see the focus through the tiny hole. I will just use cleaner plano-concave optics I bought from Edmunds Optical to diverge the beam. -15mm focal length. There wasa such a suggestion on this board.

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen
