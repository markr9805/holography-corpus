Grey Sat Apr 02, 2005 10:27 pm #1
Actually, I should say 'we'. My brother, my girlfriend and myself. We did a total of 5 single-beam exposures, using Slavich 01 film cut into 2 inch pieces, and a D&S laser. We used a cinderblock painted black sitting on 8 pieces of bubblewrap, 2 pieces of glass held together with spring clips and magnets to form a base, a clothespin in a cup of sand sitting on bricks to hold the laser, and a green diode flashlight for the safelight. Our subject was 3 dice arranged in a pyramid. The developer was the JD-2 kit.

The first attempt was a complete disaster... We turned off the lights and sat, waiting for everything to settle down. About a minute later, we realized that we could see each other... quite well in fact. Light was bleeding into the room in all directions. Rather than throw in the towel, we went ahead and exposed for 15 seconds. Then, as I was walking to the table to develop the film, I tripped and fell into the wall. And in an attempt to steady myself, guess where my hand landed? You got it. The light switch. I shut it off as quickly as possible (of course, my partners where doubled over in tears, laughing), and developed the film.

We weren't expecting much, and that's exactly what we got. So, we packed up the setup and put it in the bedroom's walk in closet. My brother and I holed up in there with all the equipment and chemicals (God, it was so hot), and went quickly to work, weathering all the head on collisions with the closet shelving, and each other. This time we exposed for 5 seconds, and developed. Nothing. We decided to take a break, and as I was walking out of the room, I saw something out of the corner of my eye. IT WAS DICE!!! I grabbed the film and ran outside. Sure enough, it was dim, but the image was there! Fueled with excitement, we returned to the closet. This time we exposed for 3 seconds... and we ended up with a much brighter image. Next, we tried 1.5, and this turned out to be perfect. A beautiful set of dice with a wide field of view. Our last experiment was for .5 seconds, with a result similar to the second.

Man, I had no idea this could be so much fun (even screwing up!). This has been a dream of mine since I was 14, wayyyy back in 1978. I can't believe its finally happened!!!

I just wanted to thank you for all the advice you guys gave me. It worked out beautifully. Hopefully, I can get ahold of a decent camera and post a shot soon.

Thanks again,

Mike
