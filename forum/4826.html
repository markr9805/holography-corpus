JohnFP Wed May 11, 2005 9:23 am #2
I actually made a spatial filter years ago. The z-axis travel should be fairly precise. If this travel does not maintain a perpendicularity to the pinhole then as you adjust the z-axis (tune) the pinhole becomes out of focus and must be brought in with the x and or y adjustments. Not impossibly but it does make tuning the spatial filter a little more tricky. Also, when the x and y adjustments are turned it should move the pinhole only in the direction of the axis being tuned. If it moves it along the z axis then again tuning become more complex and difficult.

I am not dicouraging building ones own spatial filter, just take the time to be as accurate as you can be. You will appreciate it later.

Ted, I don't know what you mean by "better results" with the real thing. With a homemade spatial filter, allignment may be harder but if you do get it alligned it will perform as any "real" spatial filter will.

If the pinhole is dirty, or not perfectly round you will get less then optimal ouput. If the pinhole is too small you will lose light. If the pinhole is too big you will not get rid of all the rings on the outside.

How is your spatial filter performing that makes you think it is not optimal?
