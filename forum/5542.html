I know this one may require a little oops. But Kris and I were brain storming on the phone about the following idea:

Suppose we create a AmDi gelatin layer on glass. Then we expose it a bit in normal diffuse light. This hardens the gelatin right?

Now we dip the plate in a small, but cold, bath of AgNO3 and let the hardened gelatin absorb the AgNO3.

Next we dip the plate in a little less cold bath of LiBr+dye.

Next we sensitize the plate in a warm bath of Ascorbate solution at about 28C.

So in the last (sensitizing) bath we have swelled the hardened gelatin a bit right?

Now we expose the hologram in red light.

During development, hopefully something interesting should occur. The developer develops the exposed AgBr crystals, but at the same time, the still present AmDi will instantly bleach the developed Ag metal again. It was our thought that perhaps the area's of the gelatin that now loose their AmDi will become less hard, or perhaps a little uncrosslinked and collapse. Instant SHSG!

Could such a system work?

(Kris and I are not chemists and have very little knowledge about the subject of chemistry, but we thought it was an interesting discussion)

> JohnFP wrote:......I thought AmDi was a reversal bleach when used with silver halide. That is, it attacks ONLY the exposed and developed silver and makes them soluble which is washed away. It does nothing to the unexposed Sliver Halide grains.So in the process of making a Silver Halide and AmDi plate or soaking an already fabricated Silver Halide plate, the Silver Halide and the AmDi get along just fine. .............................................?

This statement is not just really so John. As you know, the wonderful virtually unique way silver halide can be broken down by light relies on the formation of latent image atoms of silver being formed by light normally with sensitizing dyes involved. These freed silver atoms can then go on to infect the whole grain of thousands of AgBr ion-pairs when the plate is put in developer. The photosensitivity process is dependent on there being an easily available source of electrons present which can readily supply the silver ion Ag+ with its missing electron to make it “Ag0” ---the formula for that black metal.

In otherwords it needs a reducing environment to enable it to occur , the developer of course being a strong reducing agent . Dichromate (which becomes chromate in alkaline soln.but the same oxygen to chromium ratio is maintained), is as you know a powerful oxidant because each two chromium atoms only need 3 oxygens between them to form their strong very stable “chromium lll” arrangements and that leaves another 3 dichromate oxygens with their missing electrons to go off and seize them back to form more energetically favourable bonds with organic substances, above all with those strongly reducing photographic developers, but they can also get electrons off silver metal, Ag0 to form Ag+ again.

The most energetic form of silver metal is the latent image spec which is made up of just a few atoms. The slightest whiff of dichromate and those latent image specs are gone! ( to reform Ag+ again or perhaps more correctly never allow the formation of Ag0 metal in the first place).

Quite by coincidence our photoscience archive plunderer Martin send me the following at the end of an email today.

Martin

“P.S. Digging in those old French journals, Bulletin de la société française de photographie (put online at Gallica), I run into some articles that raised my curiosity. There was a description of a method of forming a silver halide emulsion under daylight. The magic ingredient said to allow that, was potassium chromate, which had been added early on to the emulsion. “

So the coexistence of dichromate/chromate and latent silver image is just not a goer and neither is the necessary developer for the silver possible in the presence of dichromate. You can introduce the dichromate after the silver development step to get the SHDCG effect of course and you and many of us have discussed this at length on this forum with Joe’s post of ? and earlier.

I understand what you are saying Jeff, thank you.

But how about if we omit the development process.

Our idea was to have a pre-swelled light hardened plate with Chromate, and then nullifying the hardening locally again with the exposed AgBr fringes. (Reaction of hardened AmDi+gel with metallic Ag). We did not want to develop the AgBr to enhance the silver effect. Rather, we want the Ag and AgBr out of the plate when finished. And most of all, we wanted to get the AmDi effect on the hardened plate to be lessened (gelatin shrinkage) on the exposed parts. So basically the exact opposite of a normal DCG.

So the idea was not to enhance any of the two processes. It was to de-enhance the effect of a diffusely illuminated+process AmDi plate with blue/green light locally with AgBr+dye in red light. Sort of like an instant positive slide that is processed in the reverse direction.
