Your way would work too expect for a few plusses in owning a cutter.

The two great things about having a dedicated matte cutter are:

1) Each matte ends up costing just a few pennies instead of dollars. I absorb the labor time to cut the matte into the settling time for making the hologram.

2) The window and frame can be sized perfectly. No more "well, that's not exactly what I want but it's the best I can buy".

ok, three things:

3) Custom matte elements like the text window, v-grooves, special shapes all become much cheaper to obtain.

ok, four:

4) A matte cutter is kinda fun to use.
HoloM Sat Nov 26, 2005 2:20 pm #4
I also bought a special matte cutter for the same reasons as stated by Michael. But I had very poor results with the first cutter. I wasn't able to make straight cuts. they were always slightly curved. this was a problem especially for small mattes. I found the reason was the way the cutter was built. It was some sort of plastic. this plastic bend while increasing the pressure for cutting. because of this the cutter moved in his relative position to the ruler. so i made no useable mat with this cutter. I gave it back and took another, made fully out of metal. this worked better, but also with this i have the problem with slightly curved cuts. The curves are not so strong as with the first one. but i can't avoid them fully. Another problem was to keep the ruler in the same place while cutting. the mat often moved under the ruler, and also the cutting lines weren't straight. I solved this problem with mounting the ruler on a piece of wood with two screws . and i wedge the matte between the ruler and the wood. this works well now.
