A work in progress. Should have spread the scene more horizontally, fixed her necklace, used a bit more TEA etc. but I'm nearly out of plates. The stars suck too. My usual single beam Denisyuk technique with BB-640, lighting for photo was amber LED. The frosty glass chess piece was internally lit by a focused spot from a concave mirror catching spillover light.

The stars here were metallic beads sitting on black velvet, which can work well, but here they were too far from the plate causing blurring both in the holo and due to the camera depth of field.

It might be interesting to try cheap small diameter plastic fiber optics (Xmas tree type) for stars. The bundle could be lit at one end and split off into individual fibers, ends facing the plate, to make lots of bright little stars.
Colin Kaminski Sat Jul 02, 2005 1:31 am #4
> Tom B.: Colin Kaminski wrote:Nice work!If you can split off a beam, stars look very nice with pinpricks in a black card illuminated from behind.The stars here were metallic beads sitting on black velvet, which can work well, but here they were too far from the plate causing blurring both in the holo and due to the camera depth of field.It might be interesting to try cheap small diameter plastic fiber optics (Xmas tree type) for stars. The bundle could be lit at one end and split off into individual fibers, ends facing the plate, to make lots of bright little stars.

Yes, I have done this but because the polarization gets randomized they are dim unless you use a long fiber and make a sweeping bend. The first time I tried this I used a very sharp bend and it was bad. I have found that you can use a piece of polarizing film and check the brightness of the fiber to find out if it will work. Also, if you imaging the end of the fiber you will find that the smaller NA fibers show up better as stars. It is a small difference but it helps.
