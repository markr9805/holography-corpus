What kind of machine? A machine to shoot holograms? A machine to process holograms? A machine to calculate the Fourier components to shoot holograms?

There have been many, many attempts to make a machine to shoot holograms, like a "polaroid camera" for holograms. So far, none of them have been succesful. The first I'm aware of was a long black box, approx 2' (60 cm) long and about 4 in ( 6cm) wide. It had two compartments, one for the object and one for the laser, with a slit to slide the plate/film. The idea was that you placed the object in the "object" compartment, closed the box and slid a film into the slot. The you pressed a button to expose the hologram. I can'tt remember who made this, but I haven't seen this since the mid-80's. The latest I'm aware of is Applied's "holocopier". This was made to simulate a copy machine. It had a pulse laser coming in from the underside with a compartment for the object. The film was a roll of film with two rollers. The film was advanced between these rollers and the pulse laser pulsed for each sheet of film. It was apparently capable of making a few hundred holograms a day. One problem was the inability to pinhole the puulse (I believe they used a Ruby back then - ca 1983).

The only machine I know of to process holograms is the Hope processor. This too is not used any more, as far as I know. Zebra make a machine to calculate the Fourier transform of a hologram and to image the Fourier components ( [http://www.zebraimaging.com/index.html](http://www.zebraimaging.com/index.html) )
