I have been pondering over the statements in the first link. I have also found other sources to support their claims. Why ponder you ask? And please you chemists out there feel free to comment!

Well, if water and alcohol truly do not mix, and that is, they actually chemically bond then how does this effect affect the gelatin drying process and more importantly how is this effect changed when the temperature is raised above room temperature.

So, we take an emulsion that has crosslinked (light fringes) and uncrosslinked areas (dark fringes). We place it in a solution (humm, maybe we can't call alcohol and water a solution anymore) at room temperature. Our first alcohol bath is 35% alcohol and 65% water. Having much more water then alcohol I assume from the article that most alcohol rings (maybe) and chains are already bonded with water into the more stable open ring structures. So how does this alcohol absorb any water from the emulsion at all other then osmosis or simply dilution if you will. I assume no dehydration at all is happening; simply some of the large water pockets in the emulsion are being replaced by these water/alcohol open ring structures. A kind of buffering. But is it also my guess that some water pockets, in the crosslinked areas, are small enough for a water molecule but not large enough for these stable open chains. Thus the water has yet to be dehydrated from the exposed areas. But assuming the uncrosslinked areas have larger open pockets due to the lack of crosslinking some groups of water are replace by the stable open chains.

Now this and the rest is based on using the percentages and it may be that the volume of alcohol and the volume of water that combine to make these stable open ring structures are not 1:1 and thus it may be that the point of saturation for the alcohol to bond with the water is not 1:1 but some other ration. But for arguments sake, I will assume it is 1:1 until that ratio is correct by a chemist. Then we can find the exact percentage of alcohol to water that the alcohol is saturated bonded with the water and a higher alcohol concentration from this point means there is free alcohol ready to chemically bond to the water in the emulsion.

Now we put the plate in a 70% alcohol 30%. Since there is more alcohol then water we should have some free alcohol ready to bond to the more stable alcohol/water open ring structures (see the above paragraph assumption which is open for correction). At this point the alcohol is being connected to the free alcohol until the free alcohol is saturated. I venture to say this critical % is the tuning bath. Just below and hardly and alcohol is bonded and thus virtually no dehydration takes place just as in the 35/70 alcohol mix above. Just above and some water is bonded and comes out of the emulsion.

Now we place the emulsion in the 100% alcohol (I will skip the 90% for simplicity but the explanatory purposes are the same). A lot of alcohol is unbound to water and wants to bond with the water molecules. So it "sucks" the water molecules out of the exposed regions and the bonded water to alcohol in these stable chains, coupled with the fact the chains are too big to take the place of the water molecule cause dehydration of the emulsion. It actually contracts or shrinks to fill the voids in the exposed regions. But in the unexposed regions, where the voids are big enough to hold the alcohol/water stable open rings, there is only those stable open rings with some very small shrinkage. There may be a percentage of areas that are small enough to not allow the stable open chains to get into. Thus the hologram is not visible in the 100% alcohol because we have a different index of refraction.

Now I surmise, when we dry this with forced hot air or in an oven, we are actually trying to remove the stable open alcohol/water chains from the unexposed areas and not from the exposed areas. There is a chance that upon heating for the final dry, we excite the stable chain so much as to allow it to release the water molecule I assume with the energy of steam. This could possibly be driven back into the exposed area and may with softer emulsions. But with a nicely hardened light struck fringe in the emulsion (lots of crosslinking) this happens very negligibly.

Now, what happens if we place a soft emulsion in a 100% alcohol in order to try to get a broad band hologram? Well in the exposed regions, where there is lots of crosslinking I believe the same thing happens as above. The water get absorbed into the alcohol and the stable open chains of alcohol/water are too large (for the most part) to get back into those areas where the water molecules were. So take something out, creates a vacuum, and thus the gelatin has contracted. But in the unexposed areas, there is a rapid water bonding to alcohol on the surface with a very small shrinkage due to a small percentage of areas too small to allow the stable open chains to get in to replace that water. Because the water has bonded to the alcohol on the surface there is no free alcohol to bond with the water in deeper layers so the water comes out of the deeper layers slower. It has to wait for the stable open chains to move away so the free alcohol can be in contact to allow the bonding. Eventually all water has been bonded to the alcohol throughout the emulsion with minimal shrinkage in the unexposed regions.

I don’t know how feasible it would be but I would like to have the Bragg planes perfectly parallel to the surface of the glass and study how that hologram processes. Then I would like to make as close to possible as on axis a transmission hologram with the same energy and plate and processing to study the difference between the two.

Well, there you have it.

Joe Farina Tue Nov 13, 2007 5:41 pm #27
Since Martin mentioned the Russian SHSG theory as described by Usanov et al., I will quote the relevant passage in "Principles of fabricating micropore silver-halide-gelatin holograms, by Usanov and Shevtsov, in Optics & Spectroscopy(USSR) Volume 69 (1), July 1990.

This description is for SHSG, but I think it applies equally for DCG. It sounds like they are pretty confident about the handle they have on the theory.

"The isophase surfaces of the SHG holograms have a discrete structure. In order to achieve the same variation in the reftractive index per unit volume as in the DCG holograms it is necessary to produce micropores of rather significant size without permitting such micropores to form in the unexposed regions of the interference grating. This can be achieved by using isopropyl alcohol heated over 60 degrees C to dehydrate the gelatin in the photosensitive layer of the hologram. We know that the boiling point of an aqueous alcohol solution at alcohol concentrations exceeding 50% is below that of pure isopropanol. When submerged into the alcohol the surface of the gelatin layer that has swollen in the aqueous alcohol solution is dewatered and exchange between the layer and the alcohol ceases. An elevated alcohol-water vapor pressure is produced in the layer, which serves to increase the size of the swelled micropores. When the treated photographic material is removed from the hot alcohol, the latter evaporates and the gelatin rapidly dehydrates, conserving to the maximum possible degree the inhomogeneous structure of the swelled layer of the SHG hologram."

For what it's worth.

Yes and no John,

There are two ways to do SHSG. One is to expose->develop->bleach (with Dichromate)->fix. In this case you are right, because the light struck fringes is where the gelatin hardens.

The other ways to do SHSG is: expose->develop->re-halogenate bleach->re-sensitize->expose again->bleach (with Dichromate)->fix. In this case the fringes where no light was are the place where the gelatin is cross-linked.

The processes occurring in gelatin, I see as follows. Gelatin is the complex and long macromolecule containing hydrophobic and hydrophilic fragments. In a water solution all hydrophilic fragments are hydrated, i.e. are surrounded by molecules of water and are as though in "jacket" named a solvate environment. Hydrophobic fragments in this case are covered hydrophilic and lateral fragments and their contact to water are reduced to a minimum. During formation of gel linear molecules begin are united in a three-core spiral, i.e. twisting in a cord (secondary structure). And is twist so that hydrophobic fragments of three molecules have appeared inside of a cord and did not cooperate with water. At drying a layer of a cord are bent and deformed, forming new, tertiary structure, but three-core the spiral thus is not broken.

Dichromate takes root into sphere of influence the hydrophilic fragments participating, both in secondary, and in tertiary structures.

At swelling the dried up layer in water, that position with which they occupied at formation of gel aspires to occupy molecules, i.e. there is a straightening cords (fusion of tertiary structure). But this process is interfered by new bonds which were formed already in a dry layer, for example, under influence of impurity, dyes and, naturally, chrome III. For break of these bonds the certain energy (temperature of a solution and time of processing) is necessary. Always there is such temperature and such time of processing at which any layer can be dissolved gelatin completely in water and, naturally, to destroy a three-core spiral (fusion of secondary structure). If in a layer there are local areas in which energy of bond between elements of tertiary structure exceeds energy of bond of secondary structure (light fringes) can be reached local destruction of secondary structure in other (dark fringes) areas.

In IPA dry gelatin is not swell also it is not dissolved. Even in boiling. Some its gelatin simply does not see. The situation sharply varies, when alcohol gets in the swelling layer gelatin. Breaking a hydrate environment around hydrophilic fragments, it compels molecules gelatin to be turned out literally on a wrong side. The molecule urgently starts to hide hydrophilic fragments and to show IPA hydrophobic fragments which before were deeply hided along an axis three-core spirals. The compact structure of a spiral is broken and formed a friable statistical ball. Thus all earlier the established bonds with neighbors are broken. As a result the macromolecule turns to a micro particle effectively disseminating light. It is the nature of milkiness.
