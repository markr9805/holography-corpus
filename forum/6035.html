JohnFP Thu Feb 01, 2007 9:20 pm #2
For one thing coins are not the best objects to use unless you paint them with a flat (not glossy) paint for a beginner. As far as the yellowish, I think you will have to provide what type of film you used, what chemicals you used and what your developing proceedure were. Most of the time when I see yellow in Silver Halide it is from a KDi bleach. Again, more detail will be better.

Lastly, I would like to point out to you and all beginners that the best first hologram would be of a flat white painted piece of steel close to or larger then the plate itself.. Lay the plate on the steel and do some test strips, varying the exposure time. Process. Then when you find the best exposure time move up to object. If you are unfamilliar with making test strips, just ask and I or someone else would be glad to outline is step by step.

Pianomaxx Sun Feb 04, 2007 1:37 am #5
Hi Kiffdino,

10 seconds exposure looks quite overexposed to me! With my 5mW HeNe, I only expose 2sec on a 6,3cm x 6,3cm PFG-01 plate. And I read, that JD-2 is not the easiest process for a beginner, and does not yield best results.

Therefore I did never use it, and up to now, I had quite nice results with a simple black-white-photo-developer from Tetenal called "Dokumol". (contains Hydrochinon and NaOH).

Dilute it 1:6 with water, then put in stop bath, rinsing, and bleaching with JD-2 bleach). It stays usable for a long time, and you can easily obtain it in Europe, I dont know, where are you from? I guess, any fine grain photo developer should work as well. The development time with Dokumol is critical, should be at maximum 1 minute. If developed longer, you will get a very dim image.

Best regards and I wish you bright holograms!

Max

Hi Ed,

oh, thats interesting, so the BB640 were originally produced in Germany? Unfortunately, no plates produced in Germany anymore, so I have to order PFG/Slavich from Lithuania, Ultimate from France or BB from England (plus shipping and VAT!).

Concerning Dokumol: We used it for single-beam reflection on PFG01 and also for transmission-holos on old AGFA plates (then being fixated) with good success. It comes as a liquid concentrate in a black bottle, and on the package it states 0,5%-2% hydrochinon and 1%-5% NaOH. The recommended dilution is about 1:4-1:9 with water, I usually take 1:5. Development times more than a minute resulted in very dim holos (PFG-01), I guess because unexposed silver got developed.

It is only a very ordinary film developer, not specially designed for fine grains or strong contrast, this could be a reason why you needed longer development time with finer grained BB plates.

best regards,

Max
