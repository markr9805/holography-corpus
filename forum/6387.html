I have been pondering this for a while and thought I would present it here for discussion.

If my laser is polarized 100:1 and I shoot it through a piece of glass at brewsters oriented correctly for the 100 in the 100:1 then most of the 100 will pass through the glass but a higher percentage of the 1 will be reflected. If I pass this laser again through a series of pieces of glass all at the correct brewster to reinforce the majority of polarization, the 100, will I not increase the polarization ratio?

If so could this be helpful for lasers that are holography ready but have a low polarization ratio or randomly polarized? I may cut down on fog or noise created by these lasers due to the disorented polarized light hitting the plate.

Thoughts?
BobH Thu Aug 09, 2007 9:10 pm #2
You've just described a "stacked plate" polarizer, used for high powered or pulsed lasers.
Hans Fri Aug 10, 2007 9:20 am #3
> If so could this be helpful for lasers that are holography ready but have a low polarization ratio or randomly polarized?

Yes, it could be, but the brightness that comes out of the other end of such a filter (when using a laser that has no Brewster windows) would vary over time. That would make it difficult to determine the correct exposure values.

A better option would be to use a quarter wave plate, followed by a polariser, or a stack of Brewster windows as you describe.
