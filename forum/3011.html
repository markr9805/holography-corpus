Ok, this may be a stretch but I thought I would shoot it out there anyway. If you read the "Hand Drawn Holograms" web page you will see that it is easy to create a 3-dimension "hologram" with etched lines. Now I ask, can these line be lines of different index of refration instead of etched? I believe so. With that being the case here is what I propose. Create a 3-dimensional drawing on a computer and an algorithim that moves an x-y table in an arch corresponding to each point on the computer model. In the reading I found that arc radius determins depth of that point, thus a 3-dimensional object can be created not just a 2 dimensional object behind the film plane. So the depth of the point alters the radius of the arch. This x-y table has a DCG plate on it which is under a laser beam that is focused to a fine point at the film plane. There is no interference being created just a bunch of finely lasered lines which will be developed into different indexes of refration through the processing proceedure. Then process the hologram and vuoala, a computer generated hologram. What I wouldn't do for an x y table and a couple of stepper motors right now?

Dinesh Fri Jan 07, 2005 4:13 pm #2
I'm not sure it would work on DCG. I think the effect works because of the difference in Lambertian scatter off the scratch in the discontinuitiies on a smooth Fresnel-reflecting surface. The light glances off the smooth surface, but scatters off the grooves in the scratch. OK, it's not really Lambertian reflection, but it's "preferential" reflection off the inside of the groove. If the smooth surface is held at a large angle to a source, so the reflection is glancing, then the scatter off the inside of the groove scatters into your eye while the Fresnel reflection does not, at least not much. The circle presumably causes a phase difference to reflect into each eye, thus the radius of curvature would cause a difference in the phase difference and so would cause the apparent depth of the spot. If this is so, it's not the difference in the refractive index that causes the effect, but the depth and roughness of the scratch, and maybe the inside radius of curvature of the sctratch (It would be interesting to try and recreate this effect by scratching glass).

If you use a difference-in- reflectivity model, the difference in index between crossed and uncrossed DCG is about 1/1000 to 1/100, so I'm not sure if the difference in reflectivity is enough.

Dinesh Fri Jan 07, 2005 7:04 pm #5
[quote:1aba05c55f="Colin Kaminski"]I find if you scratch hard enough to make powder from the plexiglass it will not work. You have to make a gentle scratch. Think of a polishing swirl on a automobile[/quote:1aba05c55f]

This might confirm my mechanism. If the groove depth is too great, then the light gets scattered inside the groove and little gets out, whereas if the groove depth is shallow then the light scattering off the inside surface of the groove will scatter outward. Also, I think the thinness of the groove is important. It seems to me that the effect is due to the fact that the (extremely) thin groove acts as a line source of spatially coherent light. This being so, the phase relationship of the scattered light from different parts of the arc is maintained. Thus the phase difference between the arcs of the circle and your two eyes gives the impression of a spot hovering in space. So it sems that the thinner and more shallow the scratch, the better is the effect up to a point (pun extremely intended!) . If the scratch is too thin, there's no scatter, if it's too deep the scatter is mostly interior to the groove and if it's too wide then the source size is too wide to maintain spatial coherence.

Have you tried it in different coloured sources? If I'm right, one test may be that different coloured sources, with different phase properties, may give different depths with the same scratches.

JohnFP Tue Nov 08, 2005 5:41 pm #8
Ok, let me try to explain.

At this site [http://www.amasci.com/amateur/holo1.html](http://www.amasci.com/amateur/holo1.html) they detail how to etch lines with a compass to make a 3-D image. I have done this and it works pretty well. Well I was thinking if the lines were etched with a laser, and computer driven could we get the same effect. By etching I actally mean exposing in some high resolution film. It probably does not even have to be holographic film, as seen in the link the lines do not need that high a resolution.

If I had an x y table hooked up to a computer, I would configure the table to move to make the arches wich corrospond to the points on an object I want to make. I would then us a laser focused to a relatively small but not too small a point. I would burn this arch in the emulsion, in my case DCG. This is just a single beam and no fringes are being recorded just a localized hardening of the gelatin via the AmDi where the intense light from the laser hits. Then after processing just like a DCG hologram the areas (arches) that received the laser light would have a higher index of refraction then the areas that did not get the light. Thus I believe the same effect would be had. It will probably work with Silver halide if it were bleached.
