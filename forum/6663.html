After much:

1. experimentation.

2. mount tweaking.

3. video taping.

4. repeating of 1-3 many times.

I found a 90% solution. I don't remember where I first heard about this but I believe someone at one of the PCG meetings mentioned leaning metal rods against the plate holder to dampen ringing in the holder. That turned out to take care of most of the problem I had in the holder and one of my mirror mounts. I could tap my plate holder and it would ring for 3-4 seconds. Now the effects from tapping the holder (not quite a ring) is down to about a second. I've got some video that I'll post once I've captured it to disk.

I've also found that putting a heavy magnet at the side-top of the mirror mount does much the same thing as leaning a heavy metal rod against it.

After much more testing (although not as much as I'd like, silly work-thing kept getting in the way) I've determined that life in my lab is much better without the inner tubes.

The same day last week that I found evidence of this I later listened to the interview between Frank and Ken Dunkley where Ken mentions that in his testing ages ago he found the same thing. Inner tubes make table stability worse.

So I now know I must be doing something wrong when I try to get the numbers from the datasheet. The specs for the 1/8-5056-.001 material are in the datasheet as follows:

- Nominal Density: 4.5pcf

- Compressive Modulus: K = 185000psi = 2.664 10^7 lbs/feet^2

- Plate Shear Modulus (L direction): Gl = 70000psi = 1.00810^7 lbs/feet^2

- Plate Shear Modulus (W direction):Gw = 28000psi = 0.403210^7 lbs/feet^2

Young's Modulus is not specified in the datasheet. But there is a way to calculate it.

============================================================

To get Young's modulus from these numbers (using the Shear Modulus in the L direction):

Poisson's Ratio = v = (3 K - 2 Gl) / (2 Gl + 6 K) = 0.332

Young's Modulus = E = 3 K (1 - 2 v) = 2.685*10^7 lbs/feet^2

============================================================

Here's where I think I do something wrong because the value that you use and the one that is used in the LEOT tutorial for Young's Modulus is 200 times bigger than the one I just derived with the help of Poisson's ratio.

where: P = Force exerted by the point load (in lbs)

L = Length of the panel (in ft)

b = Width of the panel (in ft)

H = Thickness of the pane (in ft)

T = Thickness of the skins (in ft)

E = Young’s modulus for the skin material (in lb/ft2)

G = Shear modulus for the core (in lb/ft2)

With these units, the deflection will come out in ft.

So the only Young's modulus you need is the skin material itself not the core.
