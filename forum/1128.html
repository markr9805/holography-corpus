Danny suggested a baby bottle warmer for heating the emulsion but I found the emulsion needs way more stirring then I could give attention to so I do not use that method for fabricating my DCG, I use a heater/stirrer.

As for ISO Alcohol heating, yes, I came up with the idea which works great for me and provides a perfect temperature for each of my baths and it is as follows.

I take a sealable sandwich container which is large enough to accommodate my glass plate that I wish to process. It also stores my ISO alcohol when not in use. I then place this sandwich container in a regular processing tray that has a 3 setting heating pad laid in it. The heating pad is the type you would use for an achy back. I did some extensive research on quite a few models and found that each model I researched with three heat setting had approximately the same heat range for each of the settings. SO to recap, I have an 8" by 10" processing tray with a heating pad put in it. I will bend up the sides. I then have the sandwich container placed in the center of this. The cloth sack and anti fungus foam that comes with the heating pad is used around the sides in between the sandwich container and the processing tray for insulation. I found that a low setting on the first, second and third tray is perfect and the medium setting on the fourth tray is good. The first tray is 25% ISO, the second tray is 60% ISO, the third tray is 90% ISO and the last tray is 99.9% ISO. I process in fixer at room temperature (about 70 degrees f.) until all the blue has gone away, you will see it come off as a precipitate then 15 seconds longer and most of the yellow AmDi is gone. It should be no longer then 1 1/2 to 2 minutes. Most the time it is usually about 20 - 40 seconds or so. Then I place in the running water which is the exhaust water from my laser, this is about 75 degrees F. Just slightly warmer then the fixer but not too warm. I keep it in this running water for 4 to 5 minutes. I then place the hologram in the first alcohol bath for about 20 seconds, then the second for 20 seconds then the third for 15 seconds and the forth for about 20 - 40 seconds until I see a entire faint image of the hologram. I hold this container in my hands for the last bath and constantly agitate slowly while rotating the tray slowly looking for the image. Once it appears I insure the entire image is pretty homogeneous. On trays 1 and 3 I do not agitate at all and on tray 2 I agitate very vigorously for the first 5 to 10 seconds. After the last bath I take the hologram out and lean it against a dark wall, and blow dry vigorously. I do not think you can over heat or blow dry and I try to do it as close to the hologram as possible constantly moving. When you see the cracks form continue near the cracks and try to iron them out with the hot air. This step is very rewarding. Another note that I have personally found to yield a better image is when I transport the hologram from one ISO bath to another I do it very, very quickly, basically you do not want any moisture from the air changing the very top surface of the emulsion. DCG dries from the top down and having that slight crust hardened at the surface help lock in some of the water thus you get different shrinkage percentages. This makes for a broader band and more efficient hologram.

Good luck and let me know if there is anything else I can help you with. If you need a photo of the ISO set up let me know and I will post it.

John
