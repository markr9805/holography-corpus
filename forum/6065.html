Hi. This is just a note of something to keep in mind for anyone wanting to use the G307 formula on the Holowiki to get improved DCG sensitivity to 532nm. (It is also a note for John to please add a note to his holowiki write-up).

The key feature about G307 is the use of a little glycerol, (glycerin), together with heat.

I have found (spectrophotometrically) that if you leave the dichromate and glycerol solution in direct sunshine for a few minutes then the glycerol gets oxidized. Even if you store the solution in the dark in a fridge then a dark reaction takes place slowly anyway and my guess is that most of the glycerine has gone in a week or two.

Now this just means that continuing to use that dichromate solution, it will be as if it did not contain any glycerol,--- but it will still work but without that extra enhancement. So adding another dose of fresh glycerol before using it again should be OK to revive it.

How long one could keep doing this for is a matter of chemical calculation .

The G307 formulation has a 10:1 ratio of potassium dichromate to glycerol.

But the molecular weights are 294: 92 so IF they react together in a 1:1 ratio then after doing more than a couple of revivals the KDi level is going to perhaps be significantly lower and a further drop in photosensivity is likely.

As for what the oxidation products are ---I cannot be very sure about.

Glyceric acid formation is pretty likely, that is no big deal but what may be of significance is the reduced form of dichromate , Cr(III) and this will give an overall increased bias hardening--- also no big deal, it will just need higher water temperature in the processing step------ it is all part of the general ever changing nature of the DCG process that is so difficult to master.---(sigh!…..)

Jeff

Colin Kaminski Mon Sep 17, 2007 3:40 am #14
OK so in answer to your question on the is the standard method of processing is goes something like this:

I am not an expert in this by any means but I have done it. It is really finicky! A few seconds here or there make all the difference. Please correct me if I am wrong which is likely.

Drop in to Kodak Rapid Fix.

Rinse in DI water.

Soak in 25% Alcohol solution. (This can be a number of different types depending on what you can get and are used to.)

Soak in 50% Alcohol.

Soak in 75% Alcohol.

Soak in 90+% Alcohol.

Soak in warm (about 105F) 100% (or as close as you can get) Alcohol.

Dry with a hair dryier on high till the plate is warm.

Now you can also skip from the 25% bath to the 90% bath in one step and I believe it will replay more broadband. (Less depth and brighter.)

For timing in these baths you should use about 15 seconds to start. You will see a hologram in the last bath before you dry it.

If you don't like the results then start at the Di rinse and process again changing the sequence and timing. After q million tries it is easy.

One further question... You mention that the alternate drying method is an alternate to forced air drying typically used by classic DCG processes. But the alternate method suggested is using a hair dryer which is also forced air. What's the difference?
JohnFP Mon Sep 17, 2007 11:19 am #22
In the classical method, one uses a blow dryer to completely extract all the remaining water and alcohol out of the film. This can take easily minutes. With the alternate method, you just hit the front and back of the plate for a few seconds to get the surface alcohol off so you're not evaporating all unnecessary alcohol into your oven. The real film drying occures in the oven.

Hope that's clear.
