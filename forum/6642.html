I recently purchased a second hand 20mW HeNe laser (JDS Uniphase 1145AP). It's linearly polarized.

So, I started doing MBDCG with it and found an effect that I have not noticed with my other lasers (C135M and a 658 TEC stabilized diode laser).

MBDCG denisyuks seem to like low reference angles. I don't know why, but if I use a reference angle of more than 45 degrees, I need a whole lot more of object light to make a decent hologram.

But that is not the interesting thing. What I don't understand is that there is a whole lot more of wood-grain when I don't shoot at Brewsters with this laser than when I use one of the two other lasers. Does glass somehow reflect more light at 632nm than at other wavelengths?

The problem I have now is that I get all this ugly wood-grain through my MBDCG holograms.

Is it normal for MBDCG and DCG that it is more difficult to make a hologram at big angles (for example Brewsters angle), or should I change something with my processing scheme?
