Hi all

So, i've now made a couple of successful Denisyuk holograms with my new c315m. The first one was a bit of a tester in order to find out exposure/development/bleaching times etc., so that one wasn't too good. The second one is much better but rather dim when illuminated with a white light point source. However, if I illuminate it with the laser it looks nice and bright with a good deep image. There is a black band across the middle of the plate, I suspect this is caused by movement during the exposure. Still, i'm quite happy with this for my first attempts with this laser.

One of my biggest problems is, and will always be stability. I'm working in a 10' x 8' wooden shed at the bottom of my garden, the floor of the shed is also wood. There isn't much room for an optical table so i'm using a small slab of granite 'floating' on some scooter inner tubes.

I'm interested in trying to make a fiber optic setup as per Saxby's book. I've managed to get my hands on a fiber launcher and some single mode cable and necessary connectors. I'm also just waiting for a fiber optic beam splitter to arrive.

I had an idea (which is always dangerous ), i'll try and explain it as best as I can...

If I put a cube beamsplitter in front of the laser output and allow one beam to pass straight through to a spatial filter, this clean expanded beam could then illuminate my plate as in a standard Denisyuk setup. If I then took the other beam from the beamsplitter cube and passed it through my fiber launcher into the fiber beam splitter, I would then have two fibers each containing a proportion of the laser light.

If I now take those fibers and use them to provide extra illumination to my object, this should (in theory) give a brighter image. This is a sort of hybrid Denisyuk setup as i'm still using the first beam as a traditional Denisyuk setup, but providing extra object illumination from the fibers.

If I can maintain the polarisation of the beam when it emerges from the fibers (possibly may have to use polarisation maintaining fiber), AND I can keep the fibers VERY still during the exposure I can't see a particular reason why this shouldn't work 8)

O.K. Guys, so now's your chance to completely destroy my dreams and tell me why this won't work

Cheers

Jem

Colin Kaminski Tue Jul 31, 2007 2:31 pm #5
Your coherence length on the c315 makes it pretty hard to screw this one up. Figure as long as the paths are within 10 feet or so you still have lots of coherence left.

But if you switched to a short coherence length laser then you would have to calculate the time of flight. The time of flight in a fiber is longer than you would think because the speed of light in glass is slower and the light bounces in the fiber. All moot if you have even an inch of coherence.

The total distance the light travels in each path, from where they are split, to when they hit the plate, must be the same, or at least smaller than the coherence length of the laser. So imagine you are the photons travelling. Start from the output of the laser, and see how far you have to go to reach the film or plate. Do this for each path. If you have an extended object, you can take the average position of the object.

> Jem: Thinking about polarisation, would it be necessary to maintain the polarisation of the light coming out of the fibers as once the beam hits the object the polarisation is destroyed anyway isn't it?Jem

If you have a diffuse object, then yes, you need not take care of polarization, but the specular highlights might change.

Tom, this brings up a thought problem.

If I have a laser that is 1000:1 polarized and use this light as a reference beam, then randomly polarize the light coming off the object because the object is painted flat white and not metallic (Without using calc)……then

I guess that means we would have 1/90 (1.11%) of the light coming off the object at the correct polarization, 1/90 of the light polarized exactly a half wave off (90 degrees, no interference) and each of the other "slices" of 1/90 of the light to be correspondingly 1.11% off of correct polarization and moving to incorrect polarization. Taking the average of all 90 slices, that is the 1st 1/90 with 100% interference and the 90th 1/90 with no interference, the 2nd 1/90 with 98.9% interference and the 89th with 1.15 interference, etc. we get approximately 50% of the light hitting the object being used to create interference fringes with the reference beam.

NOTE: this means that when you measure your light intensities of reference to object and it says 1:1 that really the ratio of the intensity of the beams is 2:1, unless of course the object is a reflective object like a front surface mirror.

Now, let's change this scenario. Let's say that we have the same reference beam but we first randomly polarize the object light with non polarizing maintaining fiber optic or diffuse screen. Then the light hitting the object is already randomly polarized as described above. Now it hits the object and each "photon" is randomly polarized again. I would assume that the probability is that we get a very similar output of randomly polarized light, again as described above. So, I have to agree that randomly polarizing the light prior to hitting the object has absolutely no effect on the outcome of the fringes.

Furthermore, since I think Jem already stated that a polarizing maintaining fiber optic will be used, I think it will not matter of the actual orientation or rotation of the fiber optic that is used to light the object so long as the object is a diffusing object. Each percentage of the beam will be randomly polarized such that only 50% of the light will be able to interfere with the main (the 1000 in the 1000:1) part of the reference beam.

The one thing I will point out is that the exit ends of the fiber optics, in addition to all other objects outside the fiber optic hardware and past the first beam splitter, will indeed have to be stabilized like in a regular holographic split beam set up.

Jem, I am really curious why you have not chosen the very easy method of using some front surface mirrors placed strategically to redirect spill light for additional lighting of the object in a Single Beam Reflection?

The fiber method will surely give you more control but from what I understand the divergence of the light coming out of the fiber optic is very great, matching the launching lens. Thus you need to be pretty close to the object or need an additional optic on the end of the fiber to keep the divergence down.

Just some thoughts and blurbs...
