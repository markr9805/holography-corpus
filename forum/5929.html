which then goes on to reduce chromate ions.

This is discussed in some detail in my original article in

Applied Optics 30. 1598-1602 (1991) .

Well Formic acid is stronger than acetic and also can be oxidised so it is not suitable.

Phosphoric H3PO4 is much too strong with its first H and even its 2nd H but once they are gone by replacing it with something to make say (TMG)2.HPO4 , it could work well. But you would be having to add and waste extra TMG as a neutralizing aid, it would be better to get the cheaply available non-toxic

Na2HPO4 (Disodium hydrogen phosphate) which could be just right.

However, Martin on this thread mentions replacing the whole TMG/weak acid thing with quanidine carbonate , that is a worth doing if cost and availability of TMG is an issue. It is likely do be better than using TEA which can have complications.

Were one to use a quantity of dichromate salt then carbonate might

produce some carbon dioxide bubbles but in this system we are using so little dichromate that that is unlikely to be an issue. (I am assuming that folks already have dichromate salt available (which is acid) rather than the chromate salt used in the original which is slightly alkaline.
