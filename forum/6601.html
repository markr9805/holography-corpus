Kaveh Thu Dec 20, 2007 10:46 pm #2
Hi Raul

Angle B and X will only be equal when A = 0.

In general, in display holography, you will not see the image you are referring to (the -1 order diffraction) because the Bragg angles will not be satisfied. Only if the angles are very small, will you see this image.

This pseudoscopic image is of little interest in display holography anyway. You might be more interested in the pseudoscopic image formed by reversing the reference beam. In this case you will get it exactly where the object was, and bright, as the Bragg condition is fully satisfied.

Normal practice is to use the one with reverse reference beam. My advice is to forget about the -1 order beam for ever. You will never use it!

Simplest way is to use the object on-axis, so B = 0, and use A around 45°. And collimate your ref beam so rays are parallel. After making your hologram, simply rotate your hologram 180° and voila, you will see the real peudoscopic image in front of the hologram. One of the greatest experiences in display holography is to move a diffusing screen through this real image and "cut" through the image at different points.

> RaulBSP: HelloOn the Picture showing H1-H2 copy, when you say “H1 flipped”, that means: rotate H1 180 degrees around axis perpendicular to film plane , keeping emulsion facing reconstruction beam?Raúl

Howdy Raul.

Nope dude. By "flipped" what is meant is that the master hologram (say for example with an overhead beam) flips over forward(or backward) in that beam. This will result in the emulsion facing the opposite way and all of the image(s) inverted. These real images will mirror their original position except simply on the opposite side of the plate and upside down.

Say, for example, you master an object 5 inches from your plate... When you go to copy that master, jig it up "flipped" in the plate holder. The real image being diffrated will coagulate mid-air 5 inches from the plate on this opposite side. This is the ideal image for copying. You can insert your H2 film in front of, behind, or inside that projected image.

All in all, Raul, the idea as I understand it is to arrange for a reconstruction beam that illuminates the hologram at the opposite angle it was exposed at. For obvious reasons, this is much easier to achieve by re-orienting the hologram than by re-orienting your whole table...

For example - a transmission holo exposed with ref beam from above at 56.7 degrees onto your emulsion side will replay your best copying image when replayed with a beam at 56.7 deg from below on your substrate side. "Flipping" the hologram in the plate holder within the original beam will achieve this.
BobH Tue Jan 01, 2008 4:27 pm #16
If a top illuminated hologram is "pitched" 180 degrees (that is flipped in the vertical plane), you'll see the conjugate image. If it is "rolled" or "clocked" by 180 degrees (in its own plane), you will not see the conjugate image. If the hologram is "yawed" (that is flipped in the horizontal plane), you will also not see a conjugate image. Pitch, roll and yaw are the correct terms for motions in three dimensional space. Disregard the confusing terms invented by holography authors for such actions, and everything will make sense.
