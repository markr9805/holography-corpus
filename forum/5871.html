Hans Sun Nov 05, 2006 8:20 pm #3
You don't really need the di-thionate to remove the chemicals. If your gelatin layer is thin enough so that you can wash out the chemicals quickly enough with plain water. The problem here is that if you have a slightly thicker gelatin, the gelatin may detach from the glass plate before all the chemicals are washed out. Don't be tempted to put a plate that is still slightly blue in your alcohol baths as they will contaminated quickly with residual MB and the TMG and become useless.

Luckily, you can overcome the problem also without the di-thionate. Either make your emulsion thinner, or look at my article in the HoloWiki: [http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/MBDCG](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/MBDCG).

You will find a procedure under "Things that can go wrong" where a procedure is shown about how to pre-treat the glass to prevent this detachment. I have found that in un-treated glass, MBDCG gelatin will detach in about 2 minutes in cold water. Another thing you could try is to wash for two minutes, take the plate out, blow dry, and wash again for 2 minutes. That way you can clean an untreated plate with thick gelatin without risk of detachment.

I have made many MBDCG holograms and never used the di-thionate.

Tom B. Mon Nov 06, 2006 5:16 am #5
The common name for this chemical is sodium metabisulfate and it is readily available from any place that sells supplies for home-brewing, wine making etc. Sometimes packaged as "Campden tablets". It is used for sterilizing equipment and raw grapes and is effective because yeasts used in brewing have been bred for resistance to it while unwanted wild yeasts and bacteria have much lower resistance.

Colin Kaminski Mon Nov 06, 2006 5:24 pm #6
"Meta-bi" is used in wine making. You can ask for it at a homebrewing shop or a home wine making shop. Potassium Metibisulfate is also called "Meta-bi" and appears to be used interchangably for home wine making in some locals.

It works well for removing chloramine from water as well. Heating is the prefered method of removing chlorine from water.
