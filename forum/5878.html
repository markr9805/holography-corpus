I was contemplating how to process without seeing the developing in the last bath and then I read your post. How about this!!!???

Set up a transmission hologram as usual. Then take a small 2cm square piece of tin and paint it white and place it on the back side of the plate such that it makes a reflection hologram down in the corner of the plate (not in the subject area). Expose and process such that the white square is the same color as the wavelength you are using. Then place the plate back in the plateholder and see how it realligned with the original object. If it is perfect then you are good. If the hologram has shifted to the blue (the image is higher and further away from the original object) then reprocess the hologram such the reflection square is redder. If it has shifted to the red (image is closer and lower then the original object) then reprocess such that the square is greener. This square could act as an indicator or reference.

Joe Farina Thu Nov 09, 2006 1:06 pm #10
Richard Rallison probably has more experience with this kind of stuff than anyone on planet earth, so I think it may be worthwhile to hear what he has to say. I will quote some comments from his notes from Tutorial IV: DCG and Other Non-Silver Holographic Material, which were provided to students at Lake Forest.

From page 3:

3.4 Processing procedures- DCG is hardened briefly in Kodak Fixer with hardener then rinsed and plunged into several hot or cool alcohol baths. Cool baths produce bettter uniformity and lower noise, hot baths can yield tremendous index modulations with large chirps in Bragg spacing but often with increased noise. Depending on the mixtures, temperatures and times spent in each bath, a wide range of effects can be had. Processing and reprocessing affects bandwidth and center frequency over 100 nm or more and index modulation can be varied from near nothing to .25. If the first pass in the baths produces a shift to red, a second pass can shift it to blue and a 100 nm bandwidth can be reprocessed to yield a 30 nm bandwidth.
Joe Farina Thu Nov 09, 2006 1:14 pm #11
To continue Rallison's comments on page 3:

Typically "master" holograms are processed to construct near the recording wavelength but processing and dichromate content allow the control of color to range from 650 to 450 nm for a straight on exposure at 488 nm. This much versatility in color control is certainly useful and will be covered in more detail. Conformal mirrors on flat substrates can be color shifted easily with angle but more complex shapes must be tuned with processing and film formula juggling. As an example of a reprocessing procedure, a red shifted broadband mirror may be narrowed and blue shifted by agitation for 30 seconds in a room temp 50:50 mixture of water and alcohol then plunged into 99% hot alcohol with agitation for 30 seconds and pulled slowly from the hot bath.
Joe Farina Thu Nov 09, 2006 1:19 pm #12
Continuing Rallison's comments:

The direction of the shift can be controlled by the ratio of alcohol and water in the first bath and the amount of shift can be controlled by the time in the same bath. A near ideal tuning bath has a specific gravity of .86 when it is warmed to about 55 degrees C. This process can be repeated many times if necessary, especially if the last hot bath is not hot enough to cause excessive scattering center buildup. Multiple buffer baths between the first color control bath and the last dehydration bath help to keep the last bath clean.

Hans, for narrow band, all procesing wants to be gradual. If your water temp is 25C, then the 35% should be around 25C and the first alcohol can be around 25C and the third and final can be around 45C. The last bath does not have to be that hot (65C).

I don't think the last bath has to be hot for anything other then allowing a more even drying with the forced air. The hot alcohol evaporates more quickly and evenly when the temp is raised. So you could try all baths and alcohols at room tepm (25C) and only heat the last bath if you get streaks from the air drying.

35% might be a little low. Remember if you go from a low concentration to a high concentration fastly (35% to 100%) then it wants to replay broadband. Try about 50 - 55% for your first bath.

To get rid of milkyness try one or more of the following;

1. More post exposure hardening

2. Leave the plate longer in the first and subsiquent alcohol baths (about 30 - 60 seconds). I am not sure how you are doing it yet.

3. Increase the concentration of the first bath.

By The Way, how does the milky broadband hologram look from the other side? A lot of my broadband white holograms are milky on the emulsion side but not at all on the glass side. So if I want to make a white replay hologram then I shoot the hologram with the emulsion facing the reference beam not the object and process, then I can display the hologram with the original glass side facing the viewer.
Ed Wesly Thu Nov 09, 2006 8:09 pm #14
I have seen transmission holograms made by Fred Untersheher and have made some myself. His were rainbow explosions of color in space, his Mandela series.

Mine were Holographic Optical Elements that were to be used in a dedicated spectrometer with 442 nm (He-Cd, luckily!) and were made with a 60X objective fairly close on the input side to collect as much light as possible, 45 degrees from the normal, and a converging output beam similarly placed that focused about 10 cm on the other side of the plate where the detector in the apparatus would be placed.

I had worked out my coating recipe for this device using a SBR object, then applied the narrow band processing technique to this application. It's funny, I have never gotten any coatings to work by loading the gel with dichromate liek everybody else, but what always succeeded (well, almost always, we know how it goes) was to coat the gel with Mayer bar the night before, then sensitize in a dichromate bath the next morning and shoot after lunch.

From reading all the posts here there are so many different ways of working the rule of thumb should be whatever you stumble on that works keep it up, or at least use it as a fall back position. Fred U. would always talk about making the gel w/dichro and storing it for days or weeks in the refrigerator!

These HOE's were pretty darn bright, and when they were replaced in the set up I could generate real time fringes when both beams were incident on the plate and the HOE was used a beam combiner. With only one beam incident, there was a black hole or shadow in the zero order!

I think that (without delving into Kogelnik's Coupled Wave Theory) that a narrow band Transmission holo would be very fussy about its replay angle while the broadband processing would be very accepting of most any angle. TJ at LFC had an early (before Omnidex) duPont photopolymer, the kind you stick between two glass plates, so it was real thick, and it was almost impossible to light up if you weren't at not only just the right angle but the proper distance! This may have been a coating about 100 microns thick, so this may have be the source of the problem. But what was interesting was that although it was a fairly deep virtual scene, the image was not very blurry due to disperson but fairly monochromatic!

I am really glad to see how gentlemanly and professional these discussions have been shaping up, as compared to how it used to be. If anyone does this as an experiment, it would be interesting to hear how it replays! Welcome aboard PAB, we need more like you!

I was driving home and I was thinking how I was going to make this and I came up with this;

Use a reference beam

Then use an object beam that is controlled via a LCD tranmission screen (I have a very low res one that I have been dying to use).

I place the screen pretty close (how close depends on the converging/diverging rate of the reference and how I want the bows to come out).

I blacken the entire LCD except for a square up in a corner which lets the light pass though the little square and hit a diffusing screen - plate side.

Then I mask off the hologram except for a little 1cm or so square such that the reference beam and the one light from the object square is only hitting a very small area of the plate.

I then move the square 1cm mask on the plate, close the orignal square on the LCD and open up another square on the LCD screen causing the object beam to be coming from a differnt direction.

So in a sense make quite a few hologram arranged as a matrix on one plate each diffracting that portion of light to a different spot.
