Methylene Blue is in the family of dyes known as Thiazine dyes (includes Rose Bengal).

As Martin says when hit with light they can become oxidants and this is opposite to what one tries trying to achieve by getting light to break down silver bromide grains so that silver can recover its missing electron and return to being silver metal. (It had lost its outer electron becoming bound to bromine to make Br- ion.in the first place)

So in other words MB is a desensitizer of AgBr grains.

Interestingly when methylene blue dyed film is hit by red light in the right damp environment it does loose its colour and becomes bleached (known as leuco MB) So this leuco form is then a reducing agent and it will seek to give back its newly aquired electrons either to ariel oxygen slowly or almost instantly if there is a good oxidant around such as dichromate. So with dichromate present it won’t appear to bleach and still retains its strong blue colour all the time .[Appl. Optics ,Vol 30. no.13 May 1991]

So this then changes the dichromate to “Chromium 3” and this stuff is in effect like chrome alum and hardens gelatin. So that in essence is how it helps to make red sensitive DCG’s.

Over 10 years ago I actually managed to make holograms using just bleached methylene blue in a very thick layer of PVA (0.5-1 mm) The holograms were able to demonstrate real time interference they were VERY feeble and only lasted a minute or two. They were made with the simplest possible transmission set up --virtually a single beam with an anodized aluminium block with some black writing on it next to the MeBlue recording plate all illuminated together by spread beam from a HeNe . laser . After leaving the stablized set-up under .laser illumination for about 2 mins

and then removing the block you could still clearly see the image of the block still there existing only as a bleached Me Blue image. ( It was best to illuminate it for viewing under the laser by cutting down the laser intensity with a bit of filter so that the image would last for longer.) I gave a piece of this special plate to Graham Saxby and he was able to make fleeting interference fringes on a solid coffee cup when he touched it and wrote it up as an observation in our RPS newsletter some years ago. The used plate was then put in a dark place and it oxidized itself back to its full blue colour in a day or two –ready to be used again.. –No wet processing or other treatments required. These plates could still be used years later but they were spoilt often by moulds growing on them in patches, (It was essential to have moisturizers in the material for them to work,) . Gelatin did not work at all well though which is why I chose PVA. . No-I’m afraid I never managed to make a visible reflection hologram with it.

I will do a note on my experience on using PVA for the AgBr diffusion system later.

We then sensitized the emulsion in a MB/TEA solution and re-exposed it to strong incoherent light. I believe the film was subsequently put in a 0,5% am. dichr. solution and kept under dark and humid condition for several hours. Ultimately, the emulsion was developed/dried a la DCG. The results were disapointing. I suspect - once more - the emulsion's "prehistory" (that's to say, its hardening) is paramount for this kind of experiments.

Yes that Azure A looks like it should be best Gary.

The fastest fading dye was the the simplest one in the series--

thionine, - It was however too good and didnt recover in the dark.

I notice that the formula for Azure A is half MB and half thionine. Maybe just the job.

The important trick I developed to get the photosensitivity up and give a demo to a class-room of kids was to add an amine. So I put a sheet of heavily MB dyed film in front of a 100W bulb with a photographic positive transparency over it and after 10 secs the thing could be taken away and viewed as a blue and white transparency.

An amine combination worked best such as triethanolamine approximately neutralized with EDTA (that is the free acid not the sodium salt of EDTA) in the PVA film.

I think I will get some Azure A in and give it a go and write it up for

Kaveh's Holographer.org. It would be great if some of you porcelaincateers picked it up and ran with it--improved it as a fun demo

and for possibly even for serious interferometry
