ron olson Mon Oct 08, 2007 6:32 pm #7
Guys,

I hate to be the bearer of bad news but this is supposed to be a forum about pulsed holography and pulsed holography requires a very good (typically very expensive) laser. While I can't really comment on the (questionable) temporal coherence, the spatial profile from Adam's laser is totally unsuitable for holography and no amount of minor tinkering will change that. Building a short cavity TEMoo resonator with anything in the way of spatial and temporal coherence is extremely difficult - believe me I know from experience. Forum contributors really have to start treating this technique more like serious photography than like an 8th grade science project. Getting “attaboys” from fellow tinkerers does absolutely nothing to improve the technique and as I continue to preach: holography is being judged by a viewing public that cares not one iota about “how you did it” or worse yet, “how you made do” - only about “what you did”.

ron olson Mon Oct 08, 2007 10:15 pm #10
Adam,

50mJ of green light in a single spatial mode is a lot of energy for a short cavity single mode laser. To maintain a truly TEMoo beam will require an intra-cavity aperture (pinhole) <1mm for a 25cm cavity. Without an extremely sophisticated resonator design (a la Gaussian mirrors) you'll be lucky to get 2mJ of high-quality Q-switched green light. Portraits aside, I struggle lighting still lifes with less than 250mJ. Check around (eBay, Laser Resale, et al) for obsoleted Q-switched Nd:YAG lasers. I bought my original hardware kit used (gov't surplus) and reconfigured it to produce 1J at 532nm for ~$5k. I have mentioned this before but it's worth repeating that if you remove (entirely) the cost of my laser, I still am looking at more than $100k in studio hardware every time I walk through the door and, in addition I spend >$10k a year on film. These little (SSY?) mini-YAGs are not the way to go except as proof of principle and I would hope we're a little past that. If you like fast cars or fast boats - you're going to pay a price. If you like fast exposures what makes you think it will be any different?

ron olson Wed Oct 10, 2007 3:14 pm #16
Adam,

If I understand correctly that your intent is indeed to produce ~1cm square transmission H1’s – your mini-YAG will probably suffice. As to the use of a passive Q-switch as an etalon: typically Q-switches a) aren’t cut to be extremely plane-parallel and b) aren’t positioned on high-precision adjustable mounts. If you’re using a dye cuvette, I believe they can be specified with parallel surfaces – but the price of the cuvette will be more than your laser. Passive Q-switches (dyes or crystals) tend to discriminate in favor of strong modes, suppressing weaker ones so you may get the occasional robust pulse exhibiting longer coherence. The rod itself may be acting as an etalon as may the output coupler especially if it’s uncoated. For a passive Q-switch to work properly, you should attempt to stay at or very near lasing threshold. Brewsters windows aligned correctly influence primarily the beams polarization and (to a lesser degree) cavity losses.
