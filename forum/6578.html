Hi Olga, I managed to make a narrow band green collimated beam from a white light that stayed collimated over about 30 metres. I used a 5” x 4” plate from Colour Holographics Ltd.

I made a Denisyuk reflection hologram of a very long focal length concave mirror. Now front surface mirrors like this are pretty expensive but if you want just narrow band green light and not a pretty picture and could therefore tolerate mild

interference stripes then you can do a pretty good job by buying a good ~$100 shaving mirror from a good supplier of bathroom fittings. To judge if a mirror is good you need to perform a sort of up and down dance ritual in the shop---- never mind the onlookers, just see if the upside down image of your face is distortion free at least around the main central area of the mirror.

I then arranged for my laser beam to be diverged through a x 8 microscope lens

so that it hit the concave mirror at the right distance to give a fairly collimated beam. I presume you do not want a perfectly collimated beam so that you can compensate for the divergence of your white light replay source when you have finished. .

To make the simple single beam hologram of a concave mirror I placed the mirror

horizontally on the table and rested the plate over it so that it was firm and wobbleless. ---You may need to use a larger flat glass plate to rest over a large concave mirror and then rest the 5 x 4 plate in the centre of that with a bit of liquid paraffin (or lamp oil ) to eliminate the air interface between the 2 glass sheets. You then can send your diverged laser beam straight down perpendicularly from a flat mirror at 45 degrees fixed overhead.

Now if your laser beam is green then you need to use processing chemistry which

does not cause shrinkage or a shift to the blue. (also remember to first wipe off that lamp oil thoroughly before processing if you have used it.—a bit of alcohol on a tissue serves well.)

The bleach needed to prevent shrinkage is a rehalogenating type as described on the Holowiki here:

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... ing_Bleach](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Rehalogenating_Bleach)

this bleach works best after a straightforward chemical developer such as TJ1 here:

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... _Developer](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/TJ1_Developer)

If you only have a red laser to do this job then you will need to do a “pre-swell “

on the red sensitive plate before making the hologram and that technique is also

well discussed on the Forum but I dont want to make this posting any longer now, you don’t say what laser you have available.
