JohnFP Thu Sep 02, 2004 10:07 am #2
I assume you are talking about a rainbow embossed hologram.

To make a roll of foil holograms, you would need an embossing machine. If you did it by hand, it would take you forever to individually stamp each hologram in the Mylar film and pressure and heat fluctuations would cause irregularities from one hologram to the next.

The steps in making hologram in a "roll" format are as follows.

1. You need to create a master hologram (H1) of the object or design and process it such that it will replay in the wavelength of the laser used for creation of the second hologram (H2) in photo resist.

2. You need to expose a photo resist hologram from the H1.

3. You need to silverize (silver coat) the photo resist plate such that it becomes electrically conductive throughout.

4. You need to take the coated resist and put it in a nickel electroforming bath monitoring and adjusting the PH, heat, nickel content and voltage with constant agitation and allow it to form a nickel shim.

5. You need to remove the nickel shim and put it back into the electroforming tank and grow a child shim from it. The first shim will be backwards. The variables mentioned above need to be modified.

6. You take the nickel shim and place it in an embossing machine which stamps the relief fringes into a plastic substrate with controlled pressure and heat. This is a holographic printer and this is where mass quantities are produced is short times. The more you want of one image the cheaper each piece will be.

If you contract out the work the price of modeling the object all the way to the embossing can be quite high but if you want a roll of lets say 500,000 pieces the embossing process can bring the price down to pennies per piece.

There are so many variables in each step that a beginner would spend months learning to control the variables in order to produce a uniform roll of holograms.

Embossing machines alone are very expensive to purchase or build. There are established companies already set up to make the "roll" type of holograms you want, and again, would be relatively inexpensive in the larger quantities.

If by "design" you simply mean a diffraction pattern then talk to some of the established embossing houses and they may be able to create your "designs" by eliminating some steps or by computer.

I definitely do not recommend trying to produce "roll" holograms yourself if you have no equipment and no experience yet. It will take you 100's of thousands of dollars in equipment investment and quite a lot of hours to learn.

Peace!
Dinesh Thu Sep 02, 2004 1:35 pm #3
As a second opinion, I agree with John, it's simply not worth it in terms of price, time spent and equipment. You need to have 10's of thousands. There are companies that have equipment for small runs, for instance James River ( [http://www.jrpusa.com/en/home.htm](http://www.jrpusa.com/en/home.htm) - look at their "Products" section for an overview of the entire proces for embossed holograms) but small means about 100,000. The cost of origination (shooting H1's and then H2), electroforming and creating the first shim is already half the cost and you haven't even got a hologram yet. I noticed that John didn't mention that resist is only sensitive at very deep blue, so you need a laser that produces 457 (Argon) or below (HeCd, Krypton) and neither of these is cheap.

I suggest if you needed embossed holograms in these quantities to go to someone like James River. IHMA ( International Hologram Manufacturing Association [http://www.ihma.org/](http://www.ihma.org/) ) may help you find one. Or (unabashed plug approaching ) you could come to us!
