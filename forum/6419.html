Hans Sun Aug 26, 2007 4:37 pm #1
In previous experiments with SHSG on PFG-01 I got a dim SHG image. I used the following procedure:

Expose -> Develop in TJ1 -> Bleach in Dichromate -> Fix -> Wash -> Dry in Alcohol -> Heat with Hair dryer.

I am sure they were SHSG images because when I wetted a part, the image collapsed into the blue. Just like a normal DCG does. Whereas a normal Silver Halide hologram would disappear into the red when wetted. Wetted area's did not come back when dried. Only after the film was re-washed in DI water and then put in IPA and heated, did the whole image re-appear.

So, for today's experiment I tried the following (based on the idea that a EDTA bleach will dissolve exposed Silver Crystals and re-deposit them onto neighbouring unexposed AgBr crystals.)

1] Expose

2] Develop in AAC developer (this is ascorbic acid + NaCO3, without the tanning component Metol).

3] Wash in DI water.

4] Bleach in EDTA bleach.

Up to step 4, would be almost like normal processing, with the exception that I used a developer without any tanning agent. I believed I would now have much more AgBr in the dark fringes than before. A slightly dim hologram was visible after step 4.

5] Re-sensitize in 5% TEA in DI water. This step actually worked. I next exposed the film to a lot of UV light from a facial tanner. The idea was here to turn the now thicker dark AgBr fringes into exposed area's, whereas the previously exposed fringes were empty because of step 4.

6] Re-Develop in AAC Developer. The film turned black again quickly. Proving that the re-sensitization worked.

7] Wash in DI water.

8] Bleach in warm Dichromate bleach. I hoped that the dichromate would tan the gelatin surrounding Silver crystals of the now inverted hologram fringes, but with a lot more silver in the new dark fringes than after step 4. I hoped that the higher silver content in those new fringes would help the Dichromate enable to tan the surrounding gelatin more.

9] Wash in DI water.

10] Dry in IPA.

11] Heat the film.

Something very unexpected happened. A very dim hologram was visible. But it was not Dichromated Gelatin at all. When I wetted a part of the film, the image collapsed towards the red quickly and came back from red into green as it dried again.

So, here is what I don't understand:

A] If after step 4 the EDTA had done it's work, no AgBr should be left in the dark fringes. Much of the original exposed silver should have redeposited it-self into the neighbouring unexposed crystals right?

B] After re-exposure of the remaining AgBr crystals (the ones that were left unexposed before step 4 + the ones that had deposited them onto those unexposed crystals after step 4) were washed out during the Dichromate Bleach right?

C] So, if there was no tanning effect of the Dichromate bleach, no hologram should have been visible at all after step 11 because all AgBr has washed out, right?

D] If there had been tanning from the Dichromate bleach, the hologram would have the typical DCG characteristics, right?

However a hologram was visible after step 11. But rather than behaving like a proper DCG hologram when exposed to moisture, it behaved like a normal silver halide hologram. This is in contradiction with C and D.

Hans, as Martin says, there must be a lot of AgBr or AgCl hanging around, --the reversal bleach system is usually not perfect and you have to rigorously exclude all soluble halides to optimize it or the soluble silver dichromate will reform silver halide all over the place. Perhaps you have got there 2 counteracting systems of DCG fringes and AgHal fringes . I suggest you give the failed SHDCG tests a thorough wash in concentrated sodium thiosulfate fix solution to remove all AgHalide traces and then DI wash and reprocessing alcohol baths etc.

I just did a new test:

1] Expose

2] Develop in TJ1 (Ascorbic+Metol)

3] Was in DI water

4] Bleach in EDTA bleach

5] Re-Sensitize in 4% TEA

6] Re-expose with UV light

7] Re-develop in fresh TJ1

11] Wash in warm DI water

14] Dehydrated in hot 99% IPA

For DCG, soft gelatin is needed in order to create refractive index gradients.

For SHSG, the presence of solid silver or/and silver halide may give us the chance to follow

a slightly different approach, which may be more effective, at least theoretically.

A general scheme:

1. Develop (NON TANNING) and Bleach

2. Harden the gelatin entirely, e.g with chromium or/and formaldehyde.

3. Fix.

I think that the critical step for this approach is the hardening of the gelatin around the silver grains.

Gelatin must get so hard, that the air voids will not collapse during the drying step. The refractive index difference is n=1.5

Such an hologram should also withstand air humidity much better than DCG.

About 3 months ago, I made some rapid tests with ORWO HF-53, high resolution film which is very hard.

Results: The test holograms were glass transparent, but not as bright as the traditionally treated ones (develop, bleach).

Unfortunately, they were not resistant to humidity. I suspect the emulsion have not been hardened enough.

I haven't had any time for further tests.
