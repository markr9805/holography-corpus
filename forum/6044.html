Yes Michael, but with now more than 25 years of hindsight there is something that should be said.

After it was revealed in the press in 1984 that I had made a contact copy of a so-called unforgeable hologram (on a piece of holographic film). I was visited in my house by a group of gentlemen from the security printing industry for me to give a demo, which I duly did---- in my living room on a coffee table with the simplest Denisyuk set up, holding a little HeNe laser on my shoulder just beaming down to make the copy!

They were certainly worried (I think probably very worried). The first embossed holos were about to come out on Mastercard. I then within a few days found myself sitting right in the front of the nose cone (ie. 1st class) of a 747 bound for Boston to give a demonstration there to members of the banking fraternity, and of course they were very concerned. But I think the consensus was that it was “Now too late to stop the big ship going full steam ahead.”

One person who expressed his disapproval of my action at the time when I met him

was Steve Benton. (But we always remained on the best of friendly terms with each other right up to the sad end.)

So with the benefit of 25 years of hindsight I think Steve Benton was right to have reservations about my action.( I had my own agenda at the time of wanting to promote my multiple color reflection H ‘s to prevent copying on which I had the US and UK patents). ---

If I had given that forgery demo just a few weeks earlier that big ship would in all probability have been brought to a halt . Just think of the consequences...........

Over 20 years many millions of dollars of fraudulent counterfeiting would not have been prevented. and loads and loads of folk in the hologram industry would not have had their jobs!

So as security measures go, for quite a long time that Emporer was covered well enough not to be seen as naked and do you know what?

........out of the now many forgeries that turned up in later years apparently not one instance has turned up of a simple fraudulent Denisyuk copy in holographic film was among them!

Do you have data that supports the statement that millions of dollars in counterfeiting was prevented? I don't, which is why I ask. On the computer software side of holographic security (which is the only area I can claim any familiarity) it's clear to me that holographic security is nothing more than smoke and mirrors. Most purchasers don't look for the security seal and wouldn't know a counterfeit even if they did look. "Is it sparkly? Check. Does it have the right company and product name? Check. Must be authentic." The only good it does is during prosecution after the criminals have been caught using traditional police methods. It does nothing to prevent the fraud.

The big ship you mention might indeed have been stopped at that time but the companies would in all likelihood have put more effort into coming up with a method that wasn't so easily forged. As it was, it sounds like they just said "let's hope nobody notices" and went on with life.

Do you or anyone else know of a case where holographic security was actually used to prevent piracy or to verify authenticity ahead of the fraud and not merely used during prosecution to show that fraud had been committed?

Well I think the security holo ball really started rolling in the early ‘80s when the then CEO of Mastercard (Mastercharge) got offered a fake Mastercard when he was down the back streets of NY. Losses from this source of fraud were climbing exponentially. Once Mastercard and shortly after Visa both had holograms on their cards, then losses from this type of counterfeit fraud virtually stopped for more than a decade. So on a world wide basis it is not an exaggeration to say that it prevented millions of dollars of theft.-- Yes it did nothing to stop other types of credit card fraud. Then in the software field Microsoft have acknowledged that holograms have certainly put the brakes on a good deal of overt piracy.

I just dont have figures at my finger tips but the detail is available if you really want to find it.

They wouldn't do this if they didn't work! Of course, how they work, and whether it is the public or trained examiners who examine the holograms.
