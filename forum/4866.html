Hi,

I've been playing around with PFG-01 film and I have some questions.

1) has anyone here used TEA to preswell this emulsion?

2) is there any advantage to using dichromate bleaches over copper sulphate based ones?

The emulsion on this material is much harder, and it seems that the consistency of coating is much better for this product than PFG-03M. I also like the shorter exposure as well, even if it is at the expense of greater grain size.

--Ted.
aghisays Thu May 26, 2005 4:12 pm #2
I use PFG-01 film all the time. Both in a glass sandwich or laminated to glass. TEA works really well and cuts the exposure down between 1/3 to 1/2 the time.

Recently, MichaelH and I started experimenting with Fe-EDTA bleach and it works really well for both transmission and reflection holograms. On top of this, you get better color control than with Potassium Dichromate bleach for reflection holograms because I believe you get less emulsion shrinkage. But you can get good results with both.

Developer wise, I use Kodak D-19 or JD-2 with equal success.

I Hope that helps.

tpark Mon May 30, 2005 8:07 pm #4
With my wusso 10mw HeNe, I use exposures of around 1 second, which with my setup works out to around 150-200 microjoules/cm2. I'm sure some of my photons are being eaten by the lens/reflections from the spatial filter, loss from my first surface mirror, evil spirits, etc. The last exposure we did was an accidental one - I was moving the card after setting up the scene (usually I let things rest for a while before doing a shot) and I knocked the card over! I think** that exposure was about a second or however long it took to get the card set up - amazingly enough, we got a good hologram out of that one, although I didn't really have high hopes for that one. It was around 2 seconds exposure using the 7mw hene.

Maybe its just me, but I found that great caution is needed when using PFG-03M - some batches also seem to be better than others. I could see uneven coating under safelight conditions just after pulling the plates out of the box.

--Ted.
