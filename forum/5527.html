Vidar Thu May 04, 2006 8:30 am #1
I am currently making a master of a duck and I have now exposed 6 masters with the a small spot in the exact same location on each master. At first I thought this was due a spec between the glass and the index matched film, then I thought there was a problem with the glass plate or the black backing so I changed the glass plate with a new one. But the problem was still there. I carefully checked the reference beam, assuming a problem on the main reference mirror, but this was OK. Finally I found the problem:

All the sheets in my very last box of 20x25 8E75 are having a minor mechanical damage on the centre on each sheet, probably due to a speck or something on a roller when the film was produced. The emulsion looks OK, the problem is more like a small bump in the plastic itself. When I index match a sheet, the fluid creeps slowly into this bump and the result is movement a black spot in the middle of the master. I even tried to let the sandwich rest over night before exposing, but even then the black spot is still there just slightly smaller than before. The black spot is about 3 millimetres in diameter and circular. I start to wonder if the spot will be visible when I make the reflection master.

Since the split beam H1 setup is taking a long time to set up again, I hope that someone on the forum have some experience with spots on their masters. The reflection H2 on BB640 will be 20 cm from the transmission master. The master is very bright. In my experience H1 scratches is not impacting the quality of H2, but since this is a 3 millimetre black spot and not a minor scratch I am worried that there will be a black spot hovering 20 centimeters in front of the H2. I would not care if the object was small, but in this master the Duck is filling almost the whole master so the spot will be in the way regardless of viwing position.

Has anyone any experience with visibility of such a spot on the final H2?
Tony Thu May 04, 2006 8:38 am #2
I doubt it will be much of a problem. Remember, when viewing the H2, it's as if you are viewing through an aperture defined by the H1, and the H1 is at the same distance in front of the H2 as during the H2 exposure. This acts to blur any details of the H1. For this format, your H1-H2 distance is probably 15-20 cm, so I think the spot will be pretty blurred out.

You could try making a smaller H2 on film as a test. All you have to do is find the location of the spot when viewing it.
