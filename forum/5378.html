Ed Wesly Mon Feb 13, 2006 3:02 pm #2
Kinegram is a registered trademark of Landis & Gyr of Zug, Switzerland. They belong to a family of "secure" elements that are classified as Optically Variable Devices (OVD's)

They publish nothing about the machine that makes them, but more than likely it is an electron beam fringe writer. A photo resist plate is put into an electron microscope and instead of using the electron beam to scan and illuminate an object line by line to image it, a diffraction grating pattern is written line by line, with a variety of pitches to generate different colors and at different angles to animate the patterns. (I saw a machine that uses this technique at the University of Wisconsin at Madison, and they were using it to write very small masks for MEMS and circuit fabrication. There was interest in renting this machine by the company I formerly worked for to make these kinds of OVD's.)

Typically these kinegrams are tiny, with very tiny details, like text that can only be seen with a magnifying glass or 10 -20 X microscopes. There really isn't any 3-D effect, just diffractive colors that change with viewing angle.

A good resource for this and other OVD's is:

[http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/089006 ... e&n=283155](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/0890069824/102-9138158-8700113?v=glance&n=283155)

This process was sold by Photographer's Formulary as the JD-1 kit, after I told TJ how well it worked for both transmission and reflection holograms.
