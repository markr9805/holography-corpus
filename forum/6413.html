Hi all

My fiber optic holography setup is coming along quite nicely. Last night I 'squirted' 99.5Mw of 532Nm light from my C315m into a 50x (0.85NA) objective and focussed it into a 6um single mode fiber. The best output I could acheive from the fiber was 81.3Mw.

I have to admit that I suspect that I haven't got perfect alignment of the laser into the objective as yet. This is due to me lacking micro adjustable height on my fiber launcher, at the moment it's sat on bits of paper to get the approximate height.

The output from the fiber is beautiful and clean, I have yet to check how much the polarisation has been affected, but as i'm only using 1.5 meter fibers i'm hopeful that it shouldn't be too bad.

Does anyone here know what output efficiency I should be able to expect from my fibers? I would like to acheive 90%+ but i'm not sure that's possible.

Cheers

Jem

Thanks for the reply. I haven't had a problem with melting the fibers (as yet ). I'm using single mode fiber patch cables that are usually used for telecommunications. The longest i've had my laser shining into one of these fibers is 4 hours, this was for an experiment to test whether or not the fiber could stand it. After four hours continuous running I figured that the cable was robust enough to stand pretty much anything

I found this interesting article whilst I was searching for information on Durell, who seems to have been something of a pioneer in Fiber Optics and Holography. Unfortunately i'm not sure whether or not he's still in business, or even if he's still alive

Here's the article...

Fiberoptic focusing system

The DBL3 is an integral focusing system that enables users to capture and split a laser beam into three separate beams for holography and other applications. Each beam can be launched into a separate optical fiber. The power in each beam can be varied as a percentage of the total power available. Alignment and focus are accomplished via 700:1 mechanical advantage pitch, yaw, and x and yaxis correction knobs. Once aligned, the object and film can be illuminated as soon as fiber clamps are aimed and tightened. This focusing system uses highquality singlemode fibers that preserve the temporal and spatial coherence of the input beam. Lasertofilm efficiencies exceed 70% for most holography applications.

Durell Laboratories North Barrington, IL

Mon Aug 01 00:00:00 CDT 1994
