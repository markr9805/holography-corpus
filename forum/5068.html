Take two solutions of gelatin and water. One at 100ml water and 10g of gelatin and the other 100ml of water and 12g of gelatin at 120F and pour them on plates and spin them at the same speed for x minutes. I assume the viscosity of the two are nearly identical and would yield a final wet thickness nearly the same. Then let the plates dry.

Do the properties of the gelatin only allow it to bond and compact at a certain density such that the higher concentration solution dries thicker and retains more water but the density of the gelatin is the same per cubic area. Or will the higher density gelatin solution give up the same amount of water as the lower concentration solution and thus have a higher concentration of gelatin per cubic area after dry.

Then, do the two dry at different rates, even thought the wet thicknesses are the same? That is, does it take the same time to get 60% of the water to evaportate out of each?
