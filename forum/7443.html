Joe Farina Fri Jul 25, 2008 5:32 pm #3
> JohnFP: Joe, is foreshortening the exagerated perspective?

John, yes, foreshortening is pretty much the same thing as perspective. Maybe some day I will be lucky enough to go to Palermo, but judging from what I've read, those reliefs by G. Serpotta are shallower than they appear. In this case, the use of perspective is so skillful that it's difficult to tell from the photo.

Recently I've been brushing up on perspective. I've found that if you take a "floor" or platform that is 135 millimeters deep, and make a "duplicate" in forced perspective that involves "tilting" the floor (towards the back) upwards at 10 degrees, the actual depth of the floor is only 55 millimeters, even though it looks like 135 (from a fixed point in the front, that is). For DCG holography, the improvement in overall quality for an image only 55mm deep (compared to 135mm) would be huge.

I hope you will try your walkway/trees idea, it sounds like a good application of forced perspective.

Ed Wesly Fri Jul 25, 2008 8:11 pm #5
This forced perspective modeling reminds me of some of the embossed holograms of the '80's, the ones I have in mind are a series of Snap, Crackle, and Pop on Rice Krispies, the models not being all that deep, so there is some semblance of depth under the fluorescently lit grocery store display.

But the idea of forced perspective in holograms dates back to Dennis Gabor! Not necessarily from his first papers on "A New Microscopic Technique" but when laser holography became popular and he moved to the States and worked for CBS. His idea was to make a model with forced perspective to record with a foot or so of coherence, but have a lens be part of the set up so that when viewing the holo it would look like a window streching off into the distance!
