I guess everyone was doing multiplex holograms last weekend... My efforts also just came together and can be found at [http://www.bobdbob.com/~tjohnson/multiplex/](http://www.bobdbob.com/~tjohnson/multiplex/).

It was shot on VRP-M processed with JD-2, exposed with a 315M laser. 34 second exposure, 40 second settling time between strips, about 5 to 1 beam ratio. I used an LCD panel intended for an overhead to show the images. The source images were gathered with a cheap digital camera on a rail, of a plant in my living room. I used 50 strips, 1/10 of an inch wide.

The actual hologram looks a whole lot better than the picture at the bottom of the web page... (cheap digital camera... q-: )

My understanding is that it is better to move the slit. It has been some time since I read the MIT papers but that was my take on them. Also, unless you are doing predistortions you need to use round film for rotation subjects and flat film for cameras on straight rails.

Pre distortions can fix all of these problems but I don't have any idea what computations are involved. I guess this is a case where one would really need to understand the literature and derive their own set of equations.

The Liti multiplex holograms have some interesting clipping in the first few and last few frames. So here are now a few pics of my setup, and my first multiplex i made today. I used a Hene and PFG-01.

The hologram has still some weird artefacts. I calculated 30 pictures and took 30 exposures of 85 sec. With a settling time of about 50 sec.

two of the slits are a little bit dim, seems the film has moved.

The pictures are not right calculated i think, because there are some parts of the object missing when you move by. Watch the video to see it.

The biggest problem is the display i think. the outer slits have a very dim object because the display gives of the light only in a very small angle. then to bigger angels (at the outer slits) the ratio becomes worse, resulting in a dim object. you can see this on the video, too

Great start HoloM. You will be able to learn so much with each little modification you do.

There is a big advantage to the way that Tommy did his system. Since he leaves the slit in a fixed position and moves the plate, he can focus the light from the object beam or reference beam or both with a cylidrical lens to form a line right at the slit, thus concentrating all available light and significantly reducing the exposure times and making for more even exposures accross the plate.
