> Hans: ...........................................1] 3-amino-propyltriethoxysilane: It's necessary to scrub the plates with this stuff first to make them more willing to adhere gelatin. (http://publish.uwo.ca/~jkiernan/adhesivs.htm) I guess it is the H2N part that helps the gelatin stick.

Yes

> Would this mean that acryl glass, that contains lots of CH on the side could be used in stead of treated glass?

> 2] AgBr loading: AgNO3 in water is ionized in Ag+ and NO3-. I guess when the plate is dried after the AGNO3 soak, this christallizes back to specks of AGNO3?

No it is very important that this does not happen.

The AgNO3 remains in the solid gel as a SOLUTION! If it is thoroughly dried with hot air for a time it does start to crystallize out and then the system does not work.

> 3] Why does the plate need to dry before the dye+bromide aplication? Is this because the dye+bromide is an alcohol solution that otherwise does something with the water from the previous step?

No, I have found that putting plate into the bromide bath while it is still swollen makes the silver nitrate migrate OUT faster than the bromide ions can migrate IN.

YES

> Is this why udeveloped pfg-01 is blue?

YES.

> Does this molecule bind to the AgBr molecules,

YES

> or does it spread evenly throughout the emulsion?

No, it chemically binds to the AgBr grains and cannot be removed by prolonged alcohol washing. But note the following point : Once the exposure and development step is complete the dye is still held in inside the AgBr grains that have not developed. Were you to then remove these grains with fix (sodium thiosulfite solution) then the dye is free and will go rapidly into a wet alcohol wash. It is best for higher diffraction with reflection but not transmission holos, to go straight to using rehalogenating bleach ferric-EDTA without first using fix .The reasons are somewhat subtle (have you got access to AgBr book by Hans Bjelkhagen?).

> 5] Is the remaining potassium/lithium+ascorbic acid washed out of the plate during the rinsing step after that?

Yes, before the exposure step.

> 6] How do Vitamin C and TEA make a plate more sensitive? And how can a plate remain more sensitive, even after you wash out the TEA?

It creates a reducing environment ie electron donating environment for sure. Thus it makes the Ag in the AgBr more ready to be converted from Ag(+) to Ag(o) , the metal.

> 7] I learned that during development of the emulsion, something like this takes place: Developer + Ag(+) + Br(-) => Developer(-H2) + Ag + 2H(+) + Br(-).Why is the NaCO3 necessary for this to take place. Is it there to absorb the released H(+) ions?


Yes

> What happens to the remaining Br(-) ions in the emulsion?

If you evaporate off all the water from USED developer which never contained Br- ions originally you would get sodium bromide salt and other products. Those bromide ions were originally those in the developed up AgBr

> 8] Final question. Why does the developer also attack the other AgBr molecules in a christal that has only a few AgBr molecules of which an electron is knocked out by light? Does the developer somehow steal electrons from the neighbouring atoms in the process and therefore ionize them? Is then the emulsion a good enough isolator to prevent this from happening with the neighbouring christals?

Yes apparently so .

> Or am I thinking in the wrong direction here?

No I think you are doing good thinking .

> Where does the knocked-out electron stay between illumination and development?

There are inner atomic orbitals in the silver ion which are heavily involved in the intermediate steps.

> Does it stick to the gelatin molecules and make it statically charged?

In photographic science the light damaged AgBr "molecules" spread their damage throughout the grain by an infectious process . (There is about 100 miles of library shelving discussing all this. but you've got too much else to do as well as planes to fly I understand ! )
