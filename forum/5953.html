Colin Kaminski Mon Jan 01, 2007 2:14 am #2
The problem will be in how will you print the image. You can generate an image with depth but then you will need to print it to a transparency. The limit of your printers abilities will be the limiting factor. If you look at the equations for off axis transmision holography you will find that 600lpi is not very good. Look for Ed Wesly's great article in the wiki for some of the simple math.

In order to make the program you require you will need to be very familiar with an FFT.

Martin123,

Yes, there are many examples of computer-generated holograms with impressive depth. A recent example is that quite a few were made at the MIT Media Laboratory's Spatial Imaging Group. Many of their publications were made available on their website.

Otherwise, this is an area in which you'll need access to a good library (or a school with access to online journal databases) for the "real articles." Do you have access to such things? If so I'd be happy to point you at a few. However, they all require a fairly good knowledge of math and optics.
