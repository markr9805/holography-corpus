Ed Wesly Thu Jun 22, 2006 3:17 pm #3
The big problem in using any Holographic Optical Elements is the dispersion; all the different colors of the spectrum will form their own separate images of different sizes and locations. So looking at the real through any "holographic lenses" will be more like viewing fireworks!

Many years ago 3-M attempted to bring to the opthalmic marketplace "bifocal" contact lenses. One focal length was formed by the refraction by the plastic lens in the conventional manner, and the other was formed by a holographic zone plate. The brightness of the images were controlled so they were about equal. The images themselves were superimposed on top of each other, and it was thought that accomodation by the eye would switch from one to the other.

I remember that they were seeking FDA approval, but I have never seen a sample of one of these in real life. If anyone knows more about the fate of this holographic product, please chime in. After having a pair of contacts dry out on my eyeballs and abrade both corneas simultaneously (the worst pain I had ever suffered until I passed a kidney stone!) I have not kept up with that type of vision solution technology.

If anyone would like some pdf's of an extremely thorough article written by Suzanne St. Cyr for holosphere about the project I will send them if you get in contact with me via this forum.
