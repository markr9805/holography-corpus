Jim McPherson Tue Mar 01, 2005 2:01 am #6
Be careful removing the case on diodes. Some diodes have special windows for reflection back to the photodiode. I addition some high power diodes have a gas mixture inside to preserve the operation of the diode. Taking the can off can ruin that.

-Jim
Colin Kaminski Tue Mar 01, 2005 7:46 am #7
I use a pin hole specified for the wide diameter of the beam and have had good but not perfect results with a spatial filter. If you shrink the pinhole you can get a perfectly clean beam but you use up some light. I now use cylindrical lenses to circularize the beam but if I was buying new equipment I would use anamorphic prisms.
tpark Tue Mar 01, 2005 2:30 pm #8
The diodes I've got have all had clean output, so I've been lucky. When not in use, I keep stuff covered so it doesn't get dusty. I may purchase an electrostatic filter for the house. I've made holograms with less than perfect beams (hene, no spatial filter) and you get results, but they aren't as good as when you've got a perfectly clean beam.

As far as cleaning goes, I managed to get the window of a laser pointer diode clean with some soapy water, then isopropyl alcohol. I used "dawn" dish soap, then a distilled water rinse, then the alcohol. It wasn't absolutely perfect, but I use the thing for viewing holograms only, so I didn't care that much.

--Ted.
