Jeffrey Weil Sat Sep 06, 2008 12:55 pm #5
Hello Everyone,

I never like the method of using an interferometer to check the recording possibilities of a laser. Too many other things going on.

Here is my favorite way. I lay a yardstick down on the table. The high numbers of the stick are towards the laser. Put a plate at the start of the stick, tilt it forward at whatever you like for a ref angle. Take a spatial filter and light up the stick and the plate. The beam will be skating across the table where the yardstick is but also filling the plate to be the ref. A single beam transmission.

Now just play back the hologram and where ever the stick disappears.....thats your real max recording distance. It helps to put some nuts or something every few inches to help read the results.

Jeffrey Weil

BobH Sat Sep 06, 2008 11:28 pm #9
Coherence length is longer in shorter tubes because fewer modes are available for lasing. As the tube gets longer, many more modes can oscillate, and you eventually need an etalon to isolate one of them.

I decided to come back and confuse everyone more! How about multi coherence volume lighting!

Use more than one beam to light up your object front to back and adjust each beam path so things are sill within the coherence distance of the laser when the light gets to the recording plate. This way, split beam of course, you could light up an object of any depth only limited by your laser power and table size, not your coherence length.

BobH Sun Sep 07, 2008 3:01 pm #17
> How about multi coherence volume lighting!

I've never had success with that technique. For example, if you use a laser with coherence length of 100mm and a collimated reference beam at 58degrees, you'll have an 85mm variation in your path length from top to bottom. A top lighting object beam, when it hits the recording plate, will be a minimum distance when it travels from the top of the object to the top of the plate. That same object beam will be very long after going from the source, to the bottom of the object and then again to the top of the plate.

So the reference beam is short at the top and 85mm longer at the bottom. If the object beam is balanced to equal the reference beam at the top (for example), a viewer looking at the image through the top of the recording will see the image bright at the top and a bit dim at the bottom. However, a viewer looking through the bottom of the recording will see the image go very dim at the top, because the reference beam there was longer than the object light.

If multiple object beams are used, each with a different balancing point along the plate, a viewer will see them all vary independently as they move their view from top to bottom of the hologram. Not much of a problem for someone shooting masters for Rainbow holograms (as for embossing), but a big problem for those making full parallax holograms. So I'd only recommend that technique for making masters for horizontal parallax only holograms (aka halfograms ).
BobH Sun Sep 07, 2008 3:05 pm #18
> i have done a back lit screen (lit thru the rear side)and was able to place/move this screen at any distance behind my film and still be in the correct coherence volume

You're not changing the path length of the beams as you move a back lit screen away from the plate, and you'll still see a variation top to bottom if the range of path lengths in the reference beam is in the same neighborhood as the height of the plate.

Konrad, take note, that's why I loved Jeffrey Weil's suggestion and Colin's response.

If you look at this link on the Wiki, it explains how your coherence length can go in and out due to two or more modes oscillating at the same time. You may have a continuous coherence length of 20cm and then it may fuzz out, then come back in. So your way of testing coherence length maximum is the common way but flawed unless you step the one leg of the interferometer every few mm or so.

Review the section "Longitudinal Modes and Coherence Length" at this link

[http://www.holowiki.com/index.php/Laser](http://www.holowiki.com/index.php/Laser)

The beauty with Jeffrey's suggestion is that it is definitive and a one step process (you do need to burn a plate though).
