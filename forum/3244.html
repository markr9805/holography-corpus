Many developers we use oxidize when in contact with the air. To make your solutions last longer, remove as much air from your storage bottles as you can.

Some methods for this include:

o Squeezing plastic bottles until the solution reaches the top and then putting the lid on. This generally works until the bottle is 1/4 full.

o Using concertina bottles that are collapsible. This also works until the bottle is about 1/4 full and then the bottle generally won't collapse further.

o Use glass bottles in conjunction with [Wine savers](http://www.google.com/search?q=wine+saver&start=0&start=0&ie=utf-8&oe=utf-8&client=firefox-a&rls=org.mozilla:en-US:official) to suck the air out of the bottle. I've found this works well all the way to the end of the solution.

o Use the [nitrogen filled bottles](http://www.google.com/search?q=wine+nitrogen+bottle&start=0&start=0&ie=utf-8&oe=utf-8&client=firefox-a&rls=org.mozilla:en-US:official) (used with wine) to force the the air out and replace it with nitrogen.
MichaelH Sat Apr 02, 2005 7:09 pm #2
btw, Colin was the person who recommended the nitrogen to me. I haven't found it necessary with the chemicals I use (wine savers have been sufficient) but if you're using highly reactive solutions you might want to go that route.
