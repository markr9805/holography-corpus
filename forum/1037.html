lobaz Mon Apr 06, 2015 10:39 am #13
Hello, Alan,

I have a nice Lasos Lasnova 50 mW 532 nm bought two years ago from Phil Bergeron (142laser user at this forum) for about 1000 USD. Ask him directly if he has more of them.

"Long exposure time" definitely depends on your table stability and setup complexity. If you have a super-stable table in the basement, heavy components and a simple setup, then 20 mW should be enough.

Let's calculate: if you spread your beam to the circle of diameter around 80 cm, then its center 30x40 cm^2 area should be uniform enough to serve as a reference. Such a circle has an area about pi*40^2 = 5000 cm^2. If the illumination of the circle was uniform, you have 20 mW / 5000 = 4 uW/cm^2. However, you need some light for the object beam; suppose you send 75% of light to the object and 25% to the reference. In the end, you should expect about 1 um/cm^2 on the plate or less.

Sensitivity of e.g. VRP-M (by Slavich) is about 50 uJ/cm^2, so you should need exposure about 50/1 = 50 seconds. I don't say it is impossible, but your light loss in the setup should be very small (this calculation was very optimistic) and the setup should be very, very stable.

Best regards,

Petr

Hi Petr,

As you say, it's difficult to judge these kinds of things without any idea of the object reflectivity, the ratio, or the number of object lights. As I mentioned, there's the number of mirrors in the setup, each one losing up to 30% or 40% (depending on angle of incidence). If the object is also about 15x20, it'll probably need two object lights, maybe even three. Such large-ish objects tend to have a lot of curves and edges and so I don't think it will be completely lit with just one object light. Of course, the more the number of object lights, the less is the lighting for the ref. Also, to cover the 30x40 plate, you need a 40X, or even a 60X, depending on how far away the objective is from the plate.

We have not made any display pieces for quite a long time. Looking back over my notes, we made a 30x40 H1 for Jason Sappan in 2006. In that shot, 30x40 H1, we used a ~200mW laser at 514nm, with about 4 object lightings. The power at the plate was about 20 uW and the exposure was about 8 seconds. Of course, the exposure also depends on the ratio, but for the ratios I use, I expose based on sensitivity of about 150 uJ. The film was Slavich VRP. Dev with CWC2 and PBQ. So, for a 20mW laser, the exposure would be about 80 seconds, assuming linearity, so I'd say your estimate is about correct. But, the part about "assuming linearity" is a big assumption!
