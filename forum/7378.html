Joe Farina Mon Jun 16, 2008 2:34 pm #2
John took a finished, blue narrowband DCG hologram, re-sensitized the DCG layer with dichromate, exposed it to light in a swollen condition, then reprocessed. The result was a green hologram. (This is according to my memory.) John's theory behind this was that the gelatin was slightly hardened while in the swollen state, and couldn't spring back to its original "blue" size, thus the green result.

I really liked John's theory and experiment. I think there was some discussion on Dave's skypecast regarding these results, and it was suggested that the green shift was due to extra water retention, not because of the dichromate treatment. I think Jeff tried this method, and couldn't get it to work (Jeff, please correct me if I'm wrong). I think John stuck to his original argument/experiment. So there seems to be some difference of opinion regarding John's method.

Actually I adopted John's basic idea to explain what was going on with the succinic acid/DCG (though I may be wrong), but instead of "hardening" the swollen layer, I prefer to use the term "rigidize." I don't think the gelatin is becoming hardened with citric or succinic acid, becasue it seems that with washing out, the layer goes back to normal.

The citric acid seems to be very suitable for shifting silver halide holograms after exposure (see the results by Paulos and Ron in the "pseudocolor with green laser" thread).

Tony did some work with citric acid on DCG, and although the shift was greater (as opposed to succinic) the results seemed a bit erratic, plus the holograms appear to be prone to vanishing with citric for some reason.

With the succinic acid, Tony gives the details in his recent thread (regarding concentrations, time in microwave, time and temperature in conventional oven, etc.).

ron olson Thu Jun 19, 2008 3:03 pm #11
Ed,

For Agfa (Millimask): ~90 grams of Citric Acid per liter of water. For VRP-M: ~65 grams per liter.

I soak the plates (without light) for 30 minutes, squeegee and air dry. This to shift green to amber. This is a reversabe process and if I don't like the color I resoak in water and alter my Citric Acid concentration.
