Dinesh Mon May 20, 2013 12:40 pm #9
My very first introduction to holography was a shot by David Traynor at Richmond Studios around 1981. I took the one week course Richmond offered which consisted of theory on the first morning, then David making a hologram while we watched. The next 4 days involved making an Denisyuk, making an H1, and, from the H1, making reflection and rainbow H2's. Anyway, that first shot that David did, with us watching, was a single beam transmission of a row of wine glasses, must have been three feet deep, with a 10mW HeNe. He arranged all the glasses in a row, slightly off-centre to the plate , gave us a quick look at the row through the plate to show us how the glasses were arranged, and zapped them from behind the plate, along, but slightly off, the row of glasses. He then developed the plate, replaced it, looked through the plate and frowned. He said something to the effect that the shot failed. So, we looked through the plate and, sure enough, there were the row of wine glasses, exactly the same as they were before he took the shot. Meanwhile, I felt David slip something into my hand. But, being disappointed that the shot didn't work, we were all trying to be sympathetic. Then I looked at what David slipped into my hand and it was a wine glass! The row of glasses we were looking at through the plate was the hologram. Three feet deep! I knew then I was hooked.

By the way, Edwina arranged that class into groups of four at a time. In the class I was in, my fellow students were Paul Newman and Chris Lambert, who went to create Laza Holographics. All of us went on to well-established careers in holography. Edwina always said that this particular class was the best class she had. Also, by the way, Rob Mundy (who some of you might know) was the lab tech at Richmond at the time.
