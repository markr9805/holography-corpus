I wanted to try Martins method for holograms based on ferric ammonium oxalate (FAO) and ferric ammonium citrate (FAC). Because i did not have these compounds, i searched for simple ways of making them.

An easy synthesis starts with iron sheets, i had some salvaged from transformators. They look like this:

![dscn2484.jpg](https://holographyforum.org/forum/download/file.php?id=6276)

The only other ingredients needed are citric acid, ammonia and water.

In a glass beaker 15g of citric acid are dissolved in about 60-80ml deionized water. To this 6ml of 25% ammonia is added. Two iron plates are connected to the (+) and (-) of a power supply. The voltage needed is 2.5 - 4.5V. I used a constant current regulator set to 600mA. The iron plates are put into the beaker and the power is turned on. After 12-14h the anode will be dissolved and the reaction is complete.

The now dark green - brown coloured liquid is poured into a shallow dish. After evaporation of the water a compact mass of dark green - brown solid mass is obtained. After crushing a dark green powder is obtained. It is a good idea to put a plastic wrap in the dish before pouring the liquid because otherwise it will be difficult to remove the product later.

![dscn2480.jpg](https://holographyforum.org/forum/download/file.php?id=6275)

The product can be purified by washing with absolute alcohol to remove traces of citric acid.

There is a green and a brown form of FAC. The green sort is composed of 14.5-16% iron, 7.5% NH3, 75% citric acid. The brown one 16-18.5% iron, 9% NH3, 65% citric acid. The green FAC is supposed to be more sensitive to light.

Replacing the citric acid with oxalic acid would result in FAO i think, but i have not tried this yet.

Pure citric acid (food quality) can be bought here for < $4 for 375g, so i would guess that the costs for making 100g FAC will be $1 roughly.

The metal plates in transformators have the added benefit that they do only contain iron and silicon as far as i know - no other metals. Silicon does not react with either citric acid or ammonia, so these plates should be a quite pure and cheap source for iron.

Sorry for persisting in this OT digression but I found this intriguing bit on the discovery of phosphorus:

1669 [Hennig Brand or Brandt, also known as Dr. Teutonicus]

Brand was an Hamburg alchemist who discovered phosphorus during his attempts to turn base metals into gold. He allowed a vat of urine to stand until it putrified. He boiled the resulting liquid down to a paste, which he heated to a high temperature, so that the vapors could be drawn into water and condensed into... gold. Brand didn't get gold, but he did obtain a waxy white substance that glowed in the dark. This was phosphorus, one of the first elements to be isolated other than those which exist free in nature. Evaporating urine produced ammonium sodium hydrogenphosphate (microcosmic salt), which yielded sodium phosphite upon heating. When heated with carbon (charcoal) this decomposed into white phosphorus and sodium pyrophosphate:

(NH4)NaHPO4 —› NaPO3 + NH3 + H2O

8NaPO3 + 10C —› 2Na4P2O7 + 10CO + P4

Although Brand tried to keep his process a secret, he sold his discovery to a German chemist, Krafft, who exhibited phosphorus throughout Europe. Word leaked out that the substance was made from urine, which was all Kunckel and Boyle needed to work out their own means of purifying phosphorus.

source: [http://chemistry.about.com/od/everydayc ... atches.htm](http://chemistry.about.com/od/everydaychemistry/a/matches.htm)
