Hans Sun Jul 12, 2009 9:54 am #1
I have read Wlers interesting web site over and over again and downloaded several pdf's about ECDL setups. But there is one thing I just can't figure out:

With the ECDL setup the grating reflects some of the light back into the diode with the grating being at an angle with respect to the wavefront. That would mean that light reflected from one side of the grating will have traveled a shorter distance than light reflected from the other side. So we would have a extended cavity that has a variable length along the width of the beam.

How can the resultant beam still be single transverse mode in that case? Does somehow the wavefront adapt to the shape of the extended cavity in this case? But if that is so, then why does this not happen in a badly aligned HeNe cavity?

This question would also apply to a ring laser I guess, because with the ring laser the outside of the beam travels a longer distance than the inside of the beam.
salvee Sun Jul 12, 2009 7:20 pm #2
With the ECDL set-up, what goes back into the laser is not the reflected but the diffracted portion of the beam. The wavefront of this diffracted beam is the same as the the wavefront of the incident beam.

In the case of a ring laser, the number of reflections add up to 180 degrees or 360 degrees, meaning that both the beam and the wavefront are co-linear to the originals on the second/third etc... pass thru the lasing medium
MfA Sun Jul 12, 2009 7:22 pm #3
Single mode laser diodes are limited by internal geometry to a single spatial mode just like a single mode fiber.

Multimode laser diodes [seemingly](http://rose.bris.ac.uk/dspace/bitstream/1983/914/1/gkl_vcsel.pdf) get a quadratic increase in modes.

Hah, I think I just answered my own question about the ring laser: I forgot to take into account that upon each reflection off a mirror the inside edge and outside edge of the beam swap places (mirror image). In a ring laser with three mirrors this would mean that after one circle the beam is it's own mirror image after the first pass, but that will be corrected during the second and fourth pass, etc...

In a four mirror system, the beam enters the lasing medium correctly each time.

Does this mean that in a three mirror (triangular) ring laser , there will be two wavefronts superimposed upon eachother? How would that affect the usability for holography?
Hans Tue Jul 14, 2009 4:24 pm #6
Oh, hah, ooh.

I answered my last question...

I took a long strip of paper which I could imagine as a horizontal slice of laser beam. The sort edges representing the wavefronts. I then folded this strip into a triangle, each fold representing one of the mirrors. And of course at the third fold the two ends could be folded onto eachother so that the ends lined up perfectly onto the formed triangle with the sort edges still perpendicular to the beam path. So that answers my last question: each part of the beam always travels the same distance, the beam only needs to travel around the triangle ring once for the waves to line up. And this is true regardless of the amount of mirrors in the ring.
