wler Tue Jan 11, 2005 12:25 pm #7
> edelbrp: But, no vibration sensor?

May not be a stupid idea... eg to monitor an auxiliary interferometer

to see whether things are stable (for detecting mode jumps, it

suffices to check for sudden variations in laser power). Not that

this would directly help making better holograms, but it might be

useful for determining a posteriori what the reason was why a

hologram has failed. Fortunately, vibration is for me not an issue

(at least compared to other factors).

One may add light sensors to verify that both object and ref beams

were unobstructed (for me one of the main reasons for spoiling

holograms: forgetting to lift a beam blocker after having made an

illumination measurement). Moreover, as Colin has mentioned, the

light intensity may have changed (eg for me, the spatial filter

tends to de-adjust, esp after some heavy bump at the table while

struggling in the dark. So far I always rush in after the shot and

check the illumination as soon as possible. )

wler Wed Jan 19, 2005 6:10 pm #10
Looks interesting. Though I am a Mac guy, and so can't easily run a Win-app. But I glanced through the source - one comment here.

It seems that your hardware uses the I2C bus - this is relatively common for interfacing to ADC and DAC and other devices like thermometers. So one may very simply connect more circuitry to the bus, for having more than having just two analog channels. I found having more than a few analog channels very handy, it allows to do other things with the hardware than just controlling the holo-setup, like doing certain measurements (eg I was determining control loop responses of TEC circuits).

The good news is that one can get such IC's for free, as samples from various manufacturers' web sites, and to drive them software-wise is not difficult. I have built various circuits with such and if there is interest, I can offer some help in developing a versatile analog-computer interface.

Actually the whole thing can be very cheap, as said the analog-digital circuits are free, and I didn't even use a pre-fabricated module but just a single extra IC that connects the I2C bus to a computer's USB port, this thingy is like $15 and that's more or less the whole expense (apart from the printed circuit board that needs to be done).
