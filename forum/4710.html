Tony Mon Apr 04, 2005 2:08 pm #2
The JD-2 kit uses Potassium Dichromate bleach which is a solvent bleach and shrinks the emulsion due to removing the silver. Therefore it causes an exposure-dependent color shift. If it comes out gold-green, your exposure is about right. Red is underexposed. Green-blue is overexposed.

As an aside, the brightness of a hologram is not strongly related to exposure, but to other factors like coherence, stability, film resolution, etc...

After trying an alternate bleach, I've split this off. This new thread could easily have any number of titles including "how I fell in love with EDTA bleach" or "when I decided to start collecting chemicals again" or "Michael's been getting up too early and should stay in bed longer"

As Colin and Tony wrote, the majority of the shrinkage I have seen as a result of using the combination of D-19 and the JD-2 bleach is from the solvent action of the bleach.

I do see increased shifting when using D-19 vs JD2 developer so there is some additional dissolving action happening during development with the D-19.

I gave EDTA ([as used by Colin here](http://www.holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?p=14341#14341)) a shot and the end result for me is a total absence of burnout in the areas of my latest hologram that received a bit more light than the other areas. Instead of a hologram that is mostly red-orange with a few parts (too many) that are deep green, the entire hologram is a gorgeous, very bright, red. I'm going to try to get a good picture as soon as I throw another piece of film on the table.

I've been avoiding mixing my own chemicals because they're smelly, sometimes dangerous, need a place to be stored and the JD kits and D-19 are just too convenient. Having complete control over the final color and greater lattitude in lighting is sufficient reason for me to get back into measuring out and mixing my own chemicals again.

Kudos to Gary Deal for helping me to see the light.
Tony Wed Apr 06, 2005 10:34 am #7
Just for yucks and convenience, last night I tried developing a bit of 8E75 in the JD-4 developer. I exposed it as usual (for D-19 or JD-2). It reached the desired density much quicker than usual.

I then tried to bleach it with JD-3 bleach, which is a copper sulfate / potassium bromide rehalogenating bleach, but more dilute than JD-4 bleach. I use the JD-3 concentration because I have a bunch made up. It works very well, although slowly, for BB.

It partially bleached but was unable to completely go clear. It left a pinkish tinge, which is characteristic of incompletely bleached 8E75.

Despite not gettting it clear, I did get a nice bright unshifted hologram. Even seems brighter than with JD-2/D-19, though it's hard to compare brightness between red and green playback. It was noisier than usual, I assume because of the incomplete bleaching.
Tom B. Thu Apr 07, 2005 3:58 am #8
My experience with trying a rehalogenating bleach (ferric EDTA) on coarser-grained materials like PFG-01 was that I got an unacceptable increase in scattering noise due (I suppose) to excessive grain growth. The dichromate bleach was much cleaner. Perhaps I should not have given up so easily ... maybe reducing the bromide concentration would have helped.
