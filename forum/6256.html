I made a bulk gelatin solution, with holographic quality, today. Here's the recepy:

STOCK SOLUTIONS

1- 2g gelatin in 80 ml DI water

2- 1g AgNO3 in 10ml water

3- .57g LiBr in 10ml water

4- 1g pinacyanol in 1l methanol

PROCESSING STEPS

1- Warm the gelatin solution to 35C on a magnetic stirrer.

2- Add about 5ml of pinacyanol to the gelatin solution (I lost count of the exact amount when I did the experiment)

3- Take two syringes. Put 3.5ml of solution 2 in one syringe and 3.5ml of solution 3 in the second syringe.

4- Put 1ml from the AgNO3 syringe in the gelatin.

5- Add two drops from the AgNO3 syringe. Add 3 drops from the LiBr syringe.

6- Repeat step 5 until both syringes are empty.

7- Add 1ml of photoflow to the gelatin.

8- Pre-treat a glass plate with silane to make it sticky to gelatin.

9- Stick Scotch tape on the edges of the glass plate. This way you have a 60 micrometer barrier for the gelatin solution to rest in.

10- Warm the glass plate with a hair drier.

11- Put enough of the gelatin on the glass plate until the gelatin just starts to overflow the tape barrier.

12- Wait a few hours.

13- After the plate has dried, sensitize it in a bath of 1% wv ascorbic acid that has it's PH adjusted to 5 with Na2CO3.

14- Expose and use like any other plate.

After the first exposure test, I noticed that the gelatin was a little soft. That made processing a little difficult. Then I noticed in the formula for a hardening fixer F5 that the acidity of that mix is the same as the acidity of the ascorbic sensitizer. So I decided to add 2.5g of Alum to that solution. The gelatin stayed nice and hard, but the hologram replayed in green. I am not sure why this happened.

The first holograms are a little dim, but the experiment shows that it is possible to pre-mix a silver halide gelatin solution that can be coated at a later time. I am sure that there is a problem with further grain growth as the gelatin solution is resting, but I read somewhere that this can be stopped with a solution of citric acid.

Dear Hans

Are you using Chrome alum? When I make my emulsion, I add 2ml of a 1% solution of Chrome alum to about 100ml of solution. Also why use the lithium salt?

This is what I 've been doing (from Darren Green)

solution 1 4g gelatin (knox food gelatin)

90ml distilled water

solution 2 1g silver nitrate

10ml distilled water

solution 3 .79g potassium bromide

2.4ml of a 2% solution of postassium iodide

Soak gelatin for 20 min to soften, then heat to 50c and stir to dissolve

Take 20ml of gelatin solution at 35c,add to solution 3,stir to dissolve

Add solution 2 to the remaining 70ml of gelatin at 35 c and stir to dissolve

in darkroom

use a 20ml syringe with needle (18ga) to add the halide/gel solution to the silver/gel solution over 45-60 sec stirring constantly and for 90 sec after finishing. I usually put the tip of the needle under the surface when I add the halide to the silver, and keep the temp at about 35 c

Then pour the gel into a small 4x4 plastic food container, wrap with tinfoil, and put into the freezer untill set (about 20 min)

Once set take back to the darkroom,unwrap and open container,and slice up the gel into 1/4in blocks(I use a plastic knife) and break them apart from each other. Then add some chilled distilled water, put the lid back on and agitate for 5 min,pouring off the cubes into a plastic strainer,and back in the container. Repeat 8 times(This part I don't like)

Once done,drain off the water,and put the cubes into a beaker and melt in water bath,not exceeding 38c

Once melted I add 2 ml pinacyandol .1g/100ml meoh

1ml quinaldine red .1g/100ml meoh

2 ml 1% chrome alum solution

( I was using the emulsion to do Lippman photography, that's why the quinaldine red)

Once its done you coat the plates . I sub them a gelatine solution.

I have tried Meyer bars, and plain coating bars, but the coating are either too thin or too thick. In fact I have a bad set now that gives me a silver appearence in the less exposed portions of the plate, and badly covers the images. For developing I use gp-2. I willl get some images up to you fellows, if I can find where my son has removed our digital camera to.

Best of luck Hans

Some remarks:

Lithium bromide is said to produce smaller grains.

I've also tried Darran Green's formula.

Using burettes may be an alternative to syringes or pipettes.

> d paul: Once set take back to the darkroom,unwrap and open container,and slice up the gel into 1/4in blocks(I use a plastic knife) and break them apart from each other. Then add some chilled distilled water, put the lid back on and agitate for 5 min,pouring off the cubes into a plastic strainer,and back in the container. Repeat 8 times(This part I don't like)

I also tried this approach which omits cube/”noodle” washing: pour the warm, freshly prepared silver halide gel in a cooled glass or plastic tray put on granite tiles (which had been cooled in a deep freezer for several hours). Thus grain growth won't be given a chance. Put the tray in the freezer for a couple of hours. Then remove it and, let the emulsion thaw smoothly at room temperature. This produces a fair amount of condensation water, which contains most of the salts you want to get rid of. To be on the safe side, you may add a distilled (chilled) water bath.

Not the least of the fascinating puzzles about using AgBr to record holograms is the fact that the amount of silver used is frequently quite out of line with the diffraction efficiency obtained.

Using the Thiry type precipitation method years ago I sometimes got hardly any darkening in the developer. and I remember rinsing a hologram that had refused to darken to anything more than a feint yellow color and I chucked it aside, it was so hopelessly lacking in silver compared to the norm which was of course Agfa 8E75 HD.

Yet catching sight of this discarded but dry hologram later it was so bright that it took my breath away.

Since then using other polymer substrates than gelatin and my diffusion method I have seen this discrepancy and diffraction efficiency many times.

What can be stated now is that when you produce AgBr in a particularly well hardened substrate the grain size tends to be particularly low and also the amount of silver tends to be particularly low but the diffraction efficiency if all the other holographic parameters allow it, can be particularly high.

However the other important factor which was never out of line with what you might expect is that the more silver you had in the more photosensitive

it could be. In that regard the old Agfa material was supreme on the compromise between grain size and amount of silver.

Note that I am not saying here that a low amount of silver is good it usually isn’t good and generally means low photosensitivity and when it gives good holograms it is inspite of poor silver content not because of it.

You ask about how to increase the concentration of grains . In my experience I think your starting concentration of gelatin is far too low Hans although I know it is the way advocated by early Lippmann emulsion makers.

Anyway I get such good results from the diffusion method which is so quick and convenient that I would not advocate going back to that wretched precipitation rigmarole you have got into unless it has some special reasons such as for making panchromatic emulsions.

Even from that point if you use pinacyanol chloride as the dye in the diffusion method you do get a fair degree of 532nm sensitivity apart from the good 640nm sensitivity.

Jeff
