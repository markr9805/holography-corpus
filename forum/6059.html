o I will try again - this time with a better description of my situation - I have obstinately been trying to make a decent hologram with my 15mw 532nm DPSS module (complete with noisy fan that keeps it mounted across the room) - across the room into a ~ 36x19 tupperwarelike sandbox. I recently had the opportunity to (using my beamsplitting piece of glass) mirror some beams straight back and turn my split beam arrangement into a michelsonish interferometer that showed me A. my setup is stable and B. settling time is no joke.

Images I had been getting on VRP-M plates had tended toward the blue and faint (even before adequete vibration measures) and after the introduction of the sandbox became notably less spectral though still dim and blue. Occasional and apparently accidental images in greenish showed up, though rife with issues and not quite holograms. I brought up the idea of a matte black dummy plate to a very helpful online buddy, and described my light at the plate as "an even green sheen" seemingly equal in intensity on both sides of the plate.

He said HMM, nifty idea, but it sounds like you have a 1:1 ratio at your plate, while a 10:1 is actually what you want for reflection holography, with the reference beam doing most of the work.

What the hell? Sounds funky, I thought it was an interference patern that would be clearest with equal light making it, but I'll give it a shot......

So I zoomed my Frankenstein reference beam lens in closer to my plate and stepped up the intensity.

It was at this point that my plates in the developer turned a purty solid black, then bleached to a clear plate with a brown tint and a strange oil smoke haze,

...and dried into holograms!

For the first time I had an idea of what I might be looking at even before the plate was sprayed black on the back, and these were also the first that belyed an image even under inappropriate light! Very exciting

I seem to be about where I want to be with a 15 second exposure, and plan to continue to play with relative beam intensities to discover what effects it has on color - I bring this up beacuse if you watch the video below, you will note a strong element of blueness that I hope to massage out of my setup. It has been brought to my attention that often the blue is a result of emulsion shrinkage, and I have begun to wonder if presoaking might be good for me....

ErichRose Fri Mar 30, 2007 7:27 pm #5
I don't get the logic of a black dummy plate. May I suggest:

Make yourself a white card with cross hairs that fits in the plate holder. Flat white matte board is good or even better "museum" board which has a solid white color and is excellent for building models as well as target cards. When setting up, start with the staright laser beam (no lense yet) centered on the card. Then insert the lense. All should then line up well.

It isn't too hard to eye-ball intensity ratios on white card. To a great extent everything is relative. The higher ratio is important when you have extra shiny objects that might create hot-spots that would throw the ratio too far the other way. By example a pile of sparkly glass marbles might look better recorded with a 10:1 ratio versus all flat white objects set back from the plate which could be exposed closer to 1:1. Please someone jump in here if they have a better way of explaining this. It took me a number of plates before I understood that one.
