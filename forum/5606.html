Vidar Tue Jun 06, 2006 11:00 am #1
Henrik Ibsen is a known Norwegian writer. The Wild Duck novel (1884) is considered by many to be Ibsen's finest work, and when the museum asked Ronny and me to make a Wild Duck hologram we hesitated at first, because we were afraid to fail and disappoint the museum. Anyway, we did it, and here are some pictures we took during the process.

Estimating film plane position of the final hologram during mastering. We placed the eyes of the duck in the film plane.

![Image](http://www.techsoft.no/holography/previs.jpg)

We made the transmission master using a standard Saxby split beam setup. Point source illumination from the left, and a diffuse beam from the right. Overspill from the big overhead reference mirror provides background illumination. The master was shot on 8E75, exposure 45 seconds with a 15mW HeNe. Standard Saxby master developer and bleach.

![Image](http://www.techsoft.no/holography/master1.jpg)

Here you can se the reference mirror providing background illumination:

![Image](http://www.techsoft.no/holography/master2.jpg)

The transfer table. We used BB640, 20x25 cm (8x10 inches) plates with 3% TEA, 40mW HeNe and exposed for 1 minute 15 seconds. Same chemistry as for the master.

![Image](http://www.techsoft.no/holography/transfer.jpg)

The final Wild Duck. Due to a total dept of about 20 cm, this hologram requires a good spotlight. The museum is using a very good one, but this picture is taken with the hologram lying on the floor using the sun as replay source.

![Image](http://www.techsoft.no/holography/duck.jpg)

Ronny and I may make some further copies in the future, but at the time of writing we have had enough of ducks for a while

Vidar Thu Jun 08, 2006 7:07 am #6
Ronny and I had several problems with the film quality for both the master and transfer before we finally managed to make a good Wild Duck. In addition we tweaked the setups and chemistry several times before we were pleased, wasting several films and plates in the process. It is not often that the first exposure turns out to be perfect in split beam setups; there are simply too many parameters that need to be adjusted. So even if the above pictures indicate a straight forward H1 and H2 process, we struggled for a while before we got it right. But we had a lot of fun!
