My guess has been that the large crystals block more of the object beam before it gets to the object lowering the ratio even more than one would expect. I have never tested this and I have made bright single beam reflection holograms on PFG-01 which I would describe as a large grain.

> Colin Kaminski: My guess has been that the large crystals block more of the object beam before it gets to the object lowering the ratio even more than one would expect. I have never tested this and I have made bright single beam reflection holograms on PFG-01 which I would describe as a large grain.

One traditional way of addressing the problem of highly absorbing AgX layers (Kodak's panchromatic 649-F materials used to champion that area) is to apply a very dilute fixing solution prior to the holographic exposure. Of course this has to be done carefully in order to not dissolve too much AgX. With VRP-M or PFG-01 (the high densities achieved through development indicate large amounts of silver halide present in the emulsion) I'd start with say, a 5g/L sodium thiosulfate solution, for maybe 30s. A thorough water rinse has to follow that treatment.

This is not quite the same.

The treatment with a small amount of “hypo” prior to the holographic exposure would allow to get rid of some AgX crystals + spectral sensitizer dye in order to achieve a more transparent layer for a higher beam ratio.

The silver solvent in the developer solution makes a special kind of development (solution-physical development), similar to physical development.

Tom B. Wed Dec 31, 2008 11:50 am #8
For single beam work (at 660 nm) with Fuji film my impression was that the the Fuji dyes absorb a LOT of light making Denisyuks rather dim. You could see a lot of attenuation of the light through the film during exposure. That said, I never did get acceptably bright split-beam results either. I mean they were maybe OK but nothing like BB plates or Ultimate. More light on the object would (as always) help.
