ron olson Thu Jun 15, 2006 2:43 pm #1
Can anyone out there explain to me the issue of reciprocity failure as it applies specifically to the elapsed time between exposure and developing? Many years ago Hans told me (and Bernadette and Bob) that following exposure with a Q-switched ruby, the Agfa 8E75 plate must be immersed in the developer ASAP. I believe Hans would try to get the plate in the bath within 15 seconds fearing a fall-off in brightness. I have never seen this to be the case with my Nd:YAG/Glass laser and Agfa or Slavich materials but now that I am working with different emulsions (ie Fuji and Filmotec) I thought it worth asking for any empirical data or personal observations that would support this alleged need for immediate processing. It seems odd that it would vary as a function of the laser wavelength or the photosensitive dye given that both lasers have roughly the same pulsewidth/peak power and that both silver halide emulsions have roughly the same sensitivity. As I move to larger formats, I can use all the help I can get with sensitivity, but I hate to think I'm making a mad rush for the darkroom when if it's only for a marginal (or neglible) gain.
BobH Thu Jun 15, 2006 5:45 pm #2
I read somewhere subsequently that processing within a few seconds of exposure made a small difference, but starting development any time after a 3 second delay (up to many hours) produces similar results. That was probably for Agfa material.

Yes Ron, Ed Wesly raised this issue recently in the pulsed laser section where I replied that I used to see that High Intensity problem with pulsed Ruby and thought it was a fact of life you had to put up with. So when I started working with a ~300mj pulsed FD YAG about 12 years ago I was expecting to see it all again. But after 12 years using a 6nsec. 532nm pulse it has not been noticeable with silver halide although I have not been making proper comparisons with CW lasers. So perhaps alot of the problem was to do with 694nm and /or the dyes used.

I have been using the "TJ1" developer to get higher sensitivity. It works fine on the Fuji and Slavich film and on BB plates, used without any mad rushing after the pulsed exposure other than trying to keep the time in developer to within 30 secs for reasons explained here:

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... _Developer](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/TJ1_Developer)

However for large formats it is important to wet the plate first in DI water before going into very fast acting dev or uneveness of development is a serious issue. The pre -rinsing helps to homogenize the dev time through fast acting osmotic pressure forces over the whole plate area because it eliminates the extra seconds it takes to imbibe developer in the centre of a large dry plate compared to the edges. But I expect you have been doing this for years anyway.
