> Sharzady: Ok, I made some holographs this weekend, (first ever attempt) but they didn't quite turn out...I tried reflection not transmission method (would someone explain transmission?) used white ceramic chess pieces (matte, but still very white!) and all that recorded was the weight bar (somewhat shiny BLACK bar that was holding the film glass holders and was within 1 inch of film. will re-attempt next weekend, please help!!!

Ok, start with the simple things, Was the chess piece illuminated with the laser light?

Did you warm up the laser for at least 10 minutes?

Is your laser known to produce holograms?

Can you see anything near the object?

If all you can see is the magnet, it is likely that the film moved in the with a hinge point at the magnet. This would erase the image of the object but leave the image of the magnet. Try whenever possible to have things contact in three points only. This is the problem with most film holders and most objects. Make sure your chess piece sits flat. If it does not, hot glue it in place. Make sure to let the glue dry for 10 minutes. I think hot melt glue is hard after a few seconds but why take the risk.

I have had many problems with using magnets as a film holder but I still will do it in a pinch. First, pay very close attention to the polar alignment of the magnets. It is easy to make the magnets slightly repel themselves and still appear to hold the film in place.

If the film can rock it will.

John is right, having the film touch the plate is a great way to make things work out.

Keep trying, It took me 6 shots before I got one that showed the object without issues. Some get it in the first shot some take 20 shots.

If you can, post a picture. We can tell you a lot more even if the picture is not very good.

JohnFP Fri Dec 02, 2005 11:01 am #10
Sharzady, you are making a reflection with side illumination anyway. Take the pieces and lay them flat and place the plate right on them and have the beam come in from the top like this. Use black tape at the top of the plate as shown so no light enters the glass from its edge. You can probably lay the horse down and use its nose against the table to keep it from rolling. The lay the castle next to it. You should be able to find a nice place on top of them such that the plate does not rock. Tap the plate when it is on top of the objects with your finger nail. You want a dulled tink sound instead of a long tinggggg sound. Don't change anything else as you got a hologram.
