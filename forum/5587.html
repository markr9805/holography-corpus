eric343 Tue May 30, 2006 6:28 pm #1
How does the maximum theoretical diffraction efficiency compare across the silver halide, DCG, and photopolymer emulsion types? Is it possible, or desirable, to expect the same kind of performance from an HOE shot on a high-end silver halide emulsion as on DCG or photopolymer?
Ed Wesly Tue May 30, 2006 7:56 pm #2
Old conventional wisdom would say that the DCG and photopolymer would be tied for the best, but having shot enough Ag halide with "black holes" in the zero order I think that all materials, if exposed and processed well, can give results in the same ball park, especially the BB plates and possibly the Gentet Ultimate. I have even seen HOE's on photo resist that virtually deplete the zero order!

I was usually disappointed by the duPont photopolymers, even though they claimed near 100% efficiency in their publications. But in those papers their method of measurement was to interrogate the HOE with an undiverged laser beam, and measure not the diffracted beam but the depleted zero order. But because their material, even though a photopolymer, scattered like crazy, their test was erroneous, although it is a good way to make eyeball in the ballpark observations like the above.
Sergio Wed May 31, 2006 12:27 pm #3
Initial tests with the new Polygrama dry photopolymer (SM-635C) measuring the DE from even a divergent beam included it into near 100% DE, of course depend on the thickness (~20um), the light transmission is very high, the advantage is that is achieved without wet development. Others films are excellent too (for HOE's).

Just a note for the less exprienced, when you illuminate a transmision H1 some of the light goes to the image (1st order) but some still goes straight through the glass, (0 order). Looking at the 0 order is a great way to tell how efficient the H1 is. Low noise is more important than good efficiency in the H1. Noise is the "glow" around the image.
