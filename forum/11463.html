lobaz Sat Dec 06, 2025 2:21 pm #2
Both ways are possible. In case of pre-metallized film, a special composition is often used that comprises a layer of soft polymer that makes embossing easier.

Din Sat Dec 06, 2025 4:38 pm #4
It's a touch more complicated. There are many ways to emboss onto plastic. I was the person responsible for transferring the image onto clear, metalised plastic at ABNH and there are several steps to transfer to plastic. ABNH also invented a different method to make transparent holographic film that I was not involved in.

Another method is used to make transparent holograms onto plastic to be applied onto a windscreen as HUDs on cars, which was used by Luminit .

Still another method is to record directly onto cleal plastic coated DCG. We, Joy and I, made some of these clear DCG coated film at Triple Take.

I have an embossed hologram on metalized film that is slightly translucent when held up to light. Is that typical? I thought most metalized films used were opaque.
lobaz Sun Dec 07, 2025 8:02 am #6
It depends on thickness of the metal (often aluminium) layer. A layer about 20 nm think shows quite good reflectance, but is still substantially translucent. So yes, it is typical that the metallized film is partially translucent.
