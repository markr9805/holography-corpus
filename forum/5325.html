Hey, thanks for the heads up! And thanks Frank!

Some notes from the talk for the impatient until official specs are available: (apologies in advance for any transcription errors)

The new film is more or less panchromatic, but can be used with a safelight because there is a dip in the response around 570? nm. A sodium vapor light or an OptiLeds amber LED works well ( with some additional filtering), or, I imagine, any source with a sufficiently sharp filter.

Sensitivity is about 1/2 stop slower than Agfa. Resolution about 3000 LP/mm, grain size 30-40 nm. Film base is triacetate 8 mils thick, emulsion 8 microns. A nice feature is an anti curl-layer on the non-emulsion side - this should make index matching MUCH easier.

Expected availability - end of January. Formats available: 30x40 cm, 50x60 cm, 1mx10m roll. No 4x5" (10x12.5cm) sheets, customer would have to cut from larger sheets. Minimum order 1 box (10 sheets). Cost has not been determined but was estimated to be equivalent to $3 to $5 per 4x5 inch (10 x 12.5 cm) sheet.

This is a great development - good film has been unavailable for WAY too long.

I have done some tests with the Fuji film. Tests done with 633nm reflection were nice and bright, and

tests done with 532nm were also nice and bright (cw or 6ns pulsed) .

There was some indication of a bit of pre-swelling compared to say BB640 plates. However processing could no doubt be altered if slight shrinkage HAD to be avoided. (The presence of a small amount of humectant [if that is the cause of the slight pre-swelling] helps to increase photosensitivity, and the photosensitivity of this fine grained material is certainly good) .

For those who got a notion that they would like to shoot a 2 color reflection straight off I warn that it is not easy. The problems are no fault of the film just the practicalities of doing it.

I tried getting both green and red cw beams through the same pinhole with the help of a good dichroic mirror which reflected green and transmitted red with low losses. I just about managed that but then you have to do a lot of tests with separate shutters on your green and red lasers. (I was using a “20 mw” 633nm HeNe and a 532nm “20mw” £200 laser pointer the latter running off a stabilized power supply set at ~2.5V instead of the 3V from 2 AAA batteries supplied, (This avoided instabilities and mode-hoping that has been discussed on the forum a number of times.) I needed to give longer exposure from the green laser. (I was pondering whether on the nano scale, exposures done simultaneously would differ from the same amount of energy delivered sequentially. ie would either the red or green photons produce stronger results on the grains in combination or separately?.

Then there is the silliest problem : What do you choose for a suitable object to show off your

“panchromatic” reflection ?

I did not manage to get a good panchromatic result color-balance-wise using a red, green and white doll but then I was only trying it roughly as an afternoon experiment. Artists with more patience than me will no doubt succeed.

By the way, a useful tip about the film is that it appears slightly concave on its EMULSION side, you need to remember this as the old lip- test on film to see which side is tacky will not work as the back of the film has a dead gelatin layer on the back to prevent curling.

Jeff

> jeff-blyth: I did not manage to get a good panchromatic result color-balance-wise using a red, green and white doll but then I was only trying it roughly as an afternoon experiment.

I want to stress a point I found out in playing with different paints. Some paints, although to the eye may seem green or red, reflect single narrow wavelengths very differently. That is, what looks green to the eye may reflect very little 532 and may actually contain enough red to reflect more red then green. If you are going to paint your own objects, feel free to use my research on paints for a suitable paint.

If you are using a prepainted object, do not take the colors for granted. Look at each of the colors in each of the wavelengths you are using. You want high reflectivity for the color you want to achieve and low reflectivity for the other colors.

Great Dave, Although my description is a lot cheaper, if you can design a flip out and then be able to flip it back in, it would save a lot of time in setting up the next session as the fliped out mirror would flip back in to nearly it same location. Keep us posted on your work.

BTW, I have alway found that two mirror are better then one. Two mirrors would also negate having to get your lasers at exactly the the same heights and same parallelisms to the table. In other words, have the laser, a mirror then the flip out mirror. That way you can position the beam on the flip out mirror with height and latteral movement, then use the flip out mirror to fine tune to the spatial filter.

Dave, what I did was feed the beam through the spatial filter. After the spatial filter I used a rectangular iris to cut all light except what would hit the plate plus a little bit. When I had to change wavelengths I would put a blocker after the rectangular iris and before the plate such that I could notice the intensity pattern in the retanglur throw on the blocker after the shutter was opened. Of course I had other blockers in place such that after placing the blocker I could open the shutter and view the throw on the blocker with no light getting to the plate. After I adjusted to my liking I closed the shutter and removed the blocker then exposed with the second wavelength.
