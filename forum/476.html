I tested a new temp./humidity logger which I bought off eBay for $70 (new). It works really well. This is particularly relevant to DCG holographers, but also to all holographers, because temp. and humidity stability are very important to keep tables and components stable for long exposures. The need for temperature control is fairly well known, however the need for humiditity stablization is not commonly recognized, though it's extremely important. Humidity fluctuations cause materials to warp, and this is of course bad for holography.

I set the thermostat in my lab (controlling two electric baseboard heaters to heat the lab) to 18C. My dehumidifier was set for 40% RH, and the humidifier was set for 55%. The thermostat, dehumidifier, and humidifier all had digital controls. (The dehumidifier cost about $150 new, and the humidifier cost about $100 new.)

The logger can be set for a wide range of intervals, it this case I set it to take readings every 30 minutes. I started the test at 15:04 yesterday (3:04 PM) with the logger attached to the computer USB port in my house, then moved the logger out into the lab building. That's why there was some wild variation in the beginning. The equipment worked well to keep the temp. and humidity stable. The top yellow line is the RH, it was held pretty close to 53%. But at 3:04 AM (I was asleep) the humidifier ran out of water, and the RH plunged to about 36% (I need to remember to keep the humidifier filled with water). The temp. held stable around 17C (the red line in the middle). The dew point is the green line at the bottom.
