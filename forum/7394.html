Ed Wesly Thu Jun 26, 2008 1:12 pm #7
As always, it's a matter of getting something to work for yourself. When I did DCG yers ago, I was never successful with mixing the dichromate with the gel; it always crystallized out. Something was not working right, my gel, (Knox), my dichromate, my glass, my recipe, my body odor, whatever.

Then I switched to the approach that 7th Son mentions, pre-coating the glass and sensitizing later. At the end of the first day, I would coat the plates with gel and a Mayer bar, and "dry" them under a HEPA hood with trays of water next to the plates, so they were dried under high humidity conditions. I got this tip from none other than the DCG'er extraordinaire, Rich Rallison, who was rumored to pour water on his lab floor where his plates were "drying".

The next morning I would sensitize them in AmDi, and shoot them after lunch. When they worked, I got almost no zero order in transmission HOE's. Which is not to say I didn't shoot objects with this process, as I did, using my trusty waffle iron as my standard test object SBR style. This process was amazingly narrow band, so much so that when I would replace the hologram on the object I could generate real time interference fringes!

Alas and alack, I have to report that this is the only way I know to make DCG; I was never successful with Jeff Blyth's veil coating technique. As always, you should try different techniques (and probably more than once!) to see what is the best system for you.

Hi JohnFP,

There is so much confusion added to all of my posts that I never know how to reply to them. Am I too vague?

I've read through a lot of posts on this site, a common thread that I see is how individuals seem to underestimate the beginning of their process (film formulation, proper gel, lab environment etc). When I see people post Super complicated processing techniques, my first thought is always that they need to take care of their front end problems, and not worry so much about adding a pinch of alcohol to this or a dash of Alum to that. If you get the front end right, you will be amazed at how little you will need to mess with that other stuff (of course it matters, I’m trying to make a point!).

If you want to make holograms with predictable results you need to grab hold of the variables. If you are purposefully humidifying your lab or not, your gelatin IS going to slurp up ambient RH. This is a MASSIVEvariable in DCG holography! My point being, formulate your film to retard this from happening! Make your film NEED supplemental humidity (pre-swell). The big exception to this advice is if you make your film, and plan on immediately banging off some shots then maybe you don’t want to worry about it? But it’s a huge gamble. Is your lab more humid today than it was the last time you shot? Is it drier? These things will change the desired outcome of your shots.

I hope these comments help.

Danny Bee I make HOE’s, and Display images. On this forum I am referring to display holography unless otherwise noted.

JohnFp, I don’t have any good advice on adding AmDi after the plate is gel coated. I’ve never gotten great results that way.
