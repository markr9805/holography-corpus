Tom B. Sat Oct 09, 2004 7:05 am #10
What are your thoughts on liquid lenses for large sizes? Mirrors get AWFULLY expensive past 10" or so, making the idea of crossed cylinder liquid lenses a la Saxby much more attractive. Doesn't have to be liquid, maybe gel-wax?

I tried making a big prism in the oven with gel wax and was much impressed with the clarity in some spots, except where there were bubbles from outgassing of water vapor from the wood frame (mistake), the incompletely cured silicone (or was it epoxy?) holding the frame together (another mistake), cloudiness from the black spray paint dissolving into the gel wax (mistake #3), and leaks in the frame which forced me to remove it from the oven before all bubbles had drifted to the top.

Tom B. Sun Oct 10, 2004 4:42 am #12
> MichaelH: Might there be enough time to get it good and hot/liquid and carefully pour it into your lens rather than subject the entire assembly to an oven heating?

This might be a better approach. The stuff seems to set pretty quickly once below melting point. It would probably be pretty easy to rig some sort of bubble-free pouring method.

I was using glass plates for the sides of my prism, so even if the thought of just pouring the stuff into the cold assembly had occurred to me, I would have been reluctant to try it for fear of the glass cracking from thermal shock. But if I had pre-heated the assemby in the oven and then poured the melted stuff in (rather than melting it in place) it might have worked better.
Colin Kaminski Sun Oct 10, 2004 7:13 am #13
What temperatures are we talking about?
Tom B. Sun Oct 10, 2004 8:07 am #14
> Colin Kaminski: What temperatures are we talking about?

A bit more than 100C (212F) . It will melt in a double boiler, but very slowly, and the bubbles would take forever to rise. I think it would be safest to melt it in an oven at 250F, rather than in a pot on an unregulated heating element, since if left unattended it could easily reach its flash point of 360F with catastrophic consequences. [http://www.candlemaking.org.uk/gel.html](http://www.candlemaking.org.uk/gel.html)
