Ed Wesly Wed Oct 12, 2005 11:11 am #7
This is a stain remover that has been successful for me. It is from a paper entitled "Reversal Bleaching for Low Flare Light in Holograms", R. L. Lamberts and C. N. Kurtz, Applied Optics Vol. 10, No.l 6 June 1971. It was also published in Kodak Pamphlet P-302 on how to process their Type 120/SO-173 Holographic Plates/Film. It seems to be the first paper on using a revesal bleach in holography, pre-dating van Renesse's Pyrochrome system. The authors were with the Kodak Research Laboratories.

They used pyrocatechol as the developing agent and were worried about the stain it would give. So they used Tray Cleaning chemistry to remove the stain.

The recipe:

KODAK STAIN REMOVER S-13

Solution A

Water 750 ml

Potassium Permanganate 2.5 g

Sulfuric Acid Conc. 8.0 ml

Water to make 1 L

Solution B

Water 750 ml

Sodium Bisulfite 10 g

Water to make 1L

No times of immersion or washing cycles were given, but they do say "The latter (S-13) remove brown stain that appear after development. These solutions also make the final plate much less subject to darkening from printout."

I have used these brews on Pyro'ed Agfa plates and it works fine. There is no rinse between the permanganate and bisulfite baths, but I would give a decent wash after that.

That Sodium Bisulfite is a nasty one, with a lung-scorching odor much worse than the dreaded PBQ!

Good luck!

> Michael: "clearing"?

Right. The permanganate badly stains the emulsion.

> Ed Wesly: You need to get rid of the permanganate. What are the details of the ascorbate bath?

An ascorbate solution (say, 50g ascorbic acid/L) clears the layer almost instantly. As that clearing solution actually re-activates the emulsion, thorough water rinsing is required.
