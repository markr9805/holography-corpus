Let's deal with the photochromic system first. The acrylamide stuff is a different ball game.

Yes TEA rather than TMG is the way.

The important trick I discovered (and reveal here for the first time) is to get the best dye- fade photosensitivity by bringing in the acid amine EDTA

as another dye fading amine. The 2 amines in combination have a much better effect than either used alone.

So for example to 100 ml DI water add 15g triethanolamine and 11.5g

EDTA (this must be the free acid and not the sodium salt). This gives a pH

of around 6 and unlike the situation with MBDCG the pH needs to be in the neutral range to work the dye fade reaction best.

However one problem you probably know about well is that of getting the PVA to stick to the glass. For this I needed to presub the glass with a thin layer of PVA solution mixed with a little bit of of sodium silicate solution (Aldrich cat no. 33844-3). I will post the details later.

Jeff

That Glassex sounds like a great discovery! We here in this department

12years ago finished up having to do a partial chemical transformation

of PVA to get it to stick to glass.

For making a real time photochromic recording system the grade of PVA is not critical but for making dichromated PVA it is critical.

For the photochromic system you just need the highest concentration

of PVA . That means lowest molecular weight but also some residual PVAcetate groups.

"4-88" is the Hoechst (Frankfurt) coding system for MOVIOL and is excellent for the purpose . Actually grade 3-75 seemed to be the best of all.

The lower the first figure the lower the molecular weight. The "88" means it is 88% hydrolysed with 12% PVAcetate groups left in . All PVA has to be made from hydrolysing polyvinyl acetate with alkali because "vinyl alcohol" monomer cannot be made, it all has to start from polymerizing

vinyl acetate.

Strangely leaving 12% of the PVAcetate still in makes the stuff more water soluble than having it 98-100% PVA ( better to use the term"PVOH"

to stop confusion as polymer chemists use PVA to mean polyvinyl acetate). 100% PVAcetate is insoluble in water and is the basis of water emulsion paints.

For making dichromated PVOH it is essential to use 100% hydrolysed stuff

with preferably highest molecular wt. Eg. grade 66-100. (The most expensive).
