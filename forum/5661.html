Dear Holography Experts,

I am working towards designing a novel type of optical element for solar collection. Part of the element is a holographic optical element. The HOE is a stack of reflection volume holograms that work to redirect diffuse ambient light from outside the element towards specific directions (that the designer has specified - this is the point of the product) after reflection. So its a bit like a mirror except that it controls where the reflected light goes according to the incoming light's wavelength. So it's a wavelength disperser. Perhaps it could be thought of as being like a reflective prism, except planar. The incident light can come from any angles and still be redirected by the reflective doe to the correct location depending on wavelength. If a diffusing screen were placed parallel to the element on the light incident side in a certain location then an incoming pencil beam of light from an adjacent region could be reflected by the proposed novel element and strike the diffusive screen, forming a speckly rainbow of colours (or if the design were slightly different, distinct regions of different colours).

What books or resources would be helpful for designing this element?

I already have:

Optical Holography, P. Harahan

Principles of Optics, Born & Wolf

I will be getting:

Volume Holography and Volume Gratings, Solymar & Cooke

Practical Holography, Saxby

Diffractive Optics, Design, Fabrication & Test, O'Shea

Optical Holography, Collier

Introduction to Fourier Optics, Goodman

Handbook on Optical holography, Caulfield

"Formation of Optical Elements by Holography", IBM Tech. Disclosure Bulletin, vol. 10, No. 3, Aug. 1967, pp. 267 & 268., Sincerbox, G. T.

"Coupled wave theory for thick hologram gratings", Bell System Technical Journal, 48, 2909 - 2946, 1969.

Spectrograph Design Fundamentals by John James has not been published yet.

I imagine this element I am trying to create is quite similar to a reflective holographic spectrograph.

The element will be fabricated in glass or PMMA. There is quite a lot of research taking place regarding volume holograms in these media by using pulsed lasers.

What other texts or informational resources should I be reading in order to design this reflective hoe. I need to work out a practical set up for creating the element. Where do I position the lasers? What lasers do I use? Some of the light may need to be introduced by total internal reflection. My understanding is that reflective volume holograms tend to be very wavelength specific. So work as rejection filters. However by optimizing the design and stacking them one after the other an entire wavelength band can be ultimately reflected. So with enough recording (layers) the entire visible and near-infra red spectral regions could be reflected in predetermined directions.

What existing texts (or papers) specifically look at this sort of HOE design problem?

Are any software programs helpful for this?

Thanks

Matts, hello and welcome!

First I would mention that because you are inquiring about a type of hologram and actually asking which type of laser to use, this thread should be in the General Section of the forum. Maybe you can request the moderator to place it there and you may get more responses.

Here are a couple of thoughts that may help you achieve your goal. Although I am not 100% sure I understand the full nature of what you are asking.

1. A DCG (Dichromated Gelatin) hologram can be made to reflect broadband. In fact some DCG holograms actually replay white so this may help in limiting your layers to 1 or a few. The theory behind it is in the processing. What happens is the hologram is made with a single wavelength. This creates multiple layers of fringes all with the same spacing. Then the processing is such that the fringes are swelled and differentially shrunken such that the spacing between the fringes varies across the depth of the emulsion. This allows reflection of blue to red creating just about what you want.

2. Now, I have never tried this but I assume if you want incident light to hit a plate and be directed to a specific direction no matter what angle the incident light comes from, my thought is as follows. The object beam in the creation would be a collimated beam emanating from the final reflection direction required. The reference beam would be a converging beam with a convergent rate equal or greater then the extreme angles that the incident light would have in hitting the plate.

3. If you want incident light to hit a plate and be directed to a specific location (focused to a specific point spot) no matter what angle the incident light comes from, my thought is as follows. The object beam in the creation would be a diverging beam emanating from the final point spot that the reflection direction would require. The reference beam would be a converging beam with a convergent rate equal or greater then the extreme angles that the incident light would have in hitting the plate.

4. If you wanted the white light to hit the plate and have one of the two properties described above but each of the wavelengths would be reflected to a different direction or spot, then you may need to created wavelength selective (narrow band DCG or Silver Halide) holograms and you would need at you mention, 1 for each wavelength. Each hologram would need the object beam direction or angle changed such that it mimics what is finally desired. What gets complicated here is that for each narrow band hologram, you will either need a different wavelength laser to create that hologram (almost impossible) or you will need to pre or post swell each individual hologram made from the same wavelength laser such that the fringes are shrunk or expanded to match the replay wavelength desired.

5. I do not believe you will need a pulsed laser for the creation of this HOE but surely one can be used.

Although I am not an expert in this type of design, I hope it enlightens you with some information you can use.
