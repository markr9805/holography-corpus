jeff-blyth Mon Feb 20, 2006 1:34 pm #1
On the “Fuji” thread Michael Harrison comments on the apparent slowness of Ferric-EDTA bleach to “clear”.

Yes, using Ferric EDTA bleach I do also find that the most exposed regions do apparently sometimes not appear to clear . ie. by “clear” we mean all the dark developed up silver is not transformed into a transparent silver bromide.

Tony, (in a private communication) also found parts that did not clear.

My current explanation for this effect is that those stubborn patches are not silver

but dye residues and these unfortunately happen to look just like the silver.

So I recommend you watch the basic overall silver getting bleached away and then when you get to the highlights apparently not bleaching away after 2 or 3 minutes then ignore it and wash the bleach out. If you leave your film in bleach too long you will finish up with an unnecessary amount of scatter and blue shift

on a reflection H.

This also raises the question of the bleach leaving dyes in which could encourage

“print out” ie slow darkening in your display light . (The term comes from the old photographic paper made with silver chloride which was used to make copies from negatives by just using sunlight and no developer at all. Although “fix” was still necessary. It was known as “P.O.P” for Printing Out Paper.)

I am afraid that the only ready answer to this susceptibility to print out is to

wheel out the ancient old dichromate bath again and give a FINAL rinse in that. The dichromate seems to effectively kill all the dyes.

Why use Ferric EDTA instead of dichromate in the first place? Well the answer is you do definitely get higher diffraction efficiency with ferric EDTA

I have proved this using an absorption spectrometer.

A ferric EDTA formulation is:-

40g Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid iron (111) sodium salt [Aldrich cat. No. 35,961-0]

60g. potassium bromide …………………………

70 ml acetic acid

Dissolved up in 1 litre.water

Actually Martin with regard to your question about using Ferric sulfate

(or indeed ferric nitrate) in place of Ferric EDTA there is in my opinion a very subtle difference. It is also the reason why I choose to use acetic acid rather than sulfuric acid (or bisulfate) in my Ferric EDTA bleach.

Based only on observation of results I believe that the Ferric EDTA and acetic acid itself both enable the subtle "carry over" effect allowing

newly formed AgBr to settle ontop of virgin AgBr in the nodes.

I will lift the explanation straight out of Jean's ascorbic acid thread because it needs airing on this thread too.

OK [Ferric EDTA rehalogenating bleach ] is the best type (DEwise) for Denisyuk reflection H's but not transmission ones.

So what happens with that type used WITHOUT FIXING in thiosulfate, is that the developed up silver gets re-oxidized to AgBr. But instead of returning you to square one and leaving you with a uniform coating and distribution of AgBr again just as it was before you exposed , it is energetically more favorable for the re-made AgBr to move over to the adjacent dark fringe made up of virgin AgBr and grow onto that dark fringe using the virgin AgBr grains as seeding centers. That actually requires the bleach solution to have some AgBr solvation ability to enable this carry over effect to occur. It has been found not to occur if the fringe spacing is too great as happens often with transmission Hs(ref. Hariharan). It also cannot happen if the bleach solution has no AgBr solvation ability such as with iodine-alcohol bleach. So the great thing about this carryover effect is that it theoretically uses all the original Ag in your emulsion to make the fringes whereas if you have to use fix you would have removed about half of your original silver content in the thiosulfate solution. The not good thing about it though is that the original

sensitizing dyes become chemically locked into AgBr grains making the emulsion very vulnerable to print out. (This doesnt happen with dev-fix-bleach because the dyes get freed up and removed either in the fix or with the help of a bit of alcohol in the final rinse. This is convenient for transmission Hs where printout can be a more serious issue.) So from

all this one can say that provided your carry over bleach system has worked cleanly and left you with empty antinodes with all the silver migrated onto the adjacent nodes, then by using ascorbate you are gong to cause total printout of all that AgBr with its residual dyes. You can then re-bleach it.

But it would be best to kill off those dyes so that they dont keep reforming absorption Hs instead of the more efficient phase Hs .

.........probably the best is to kill off the dyes with dichromate soln.

Yes O.K., I have just taken the simplest approach. Once the finished H has been in a bath of strong oxidant such as acid dichromate or iodine or ammonium ceric nitrate then after you have given it a brief or limited water rinse it remains moderately print-out resistant to all wavelengths. The AgBr being in therefore an electron-starved environment compared with the electron rich environment you need to make photosensitive plates.

I did do a summer-sun print -out test once on reflection H's on Agfa 8E75 film treated with oxidants and have to admit that after several weeks of direct sunshine considerable detrimental darkening did occur, the least affected being the sample that had been in a final bath of 2% ammonium ceric nitrate. Ultimately though with silver halide don't you think the only way to archival stability is to have the diffraction coming from just colloidal silver metal (possibly with with sulfide too) but with no Ag halide around? The old fine grained Russian material showed how well that could work. Agfa's stuff was not really up to it.

Another aspect is blue shift.-- Some early holograms around 25 years old made with Argon 514nm have been on display continuously in the British Science Museum in South Kensington. They have probably hardly ever been in direct sunshine, but they have sadly lost their original replay color once I guess around 540nm now replaying around 500 (I guess). I think they were originally done with the Nick Phillips' Pyro dev./ PBQ bleach system .
