I looked at the specifications at:

[http://lasers.mellesgriot.com/Specsheet.asp?CatID=11421](http://lasers.mellesgriot.com/Specsheet.asp?CatID=11421)

Is yours the 85 BLD 605? (The two-beam model with 200mW for each beam) I assume that both beams come from the same cavity, and I'm guessing that they are mutually coherent (one useable for the reference beam, and the other for the object). I see the beams are somewhat elliptical (0.15mm X 0.29mm) and I'm wondering if this would complicate the use of a spatial filter. (They have other, lower-power models that have a circular beam shape).

They way I see it now, the Melles-Griot lasers are the best solution for use in color holography. Substantial power is needed in the appropriate region of the blue spectrum (with enough coherence), and all of these requirements are met. In comparison to an argon laser, there's no need for three-phase power or water cooling (involving the additional expense of a chiller, unless the water goes straight down the drain, which can get expensive for people with municipal water). From what I hear, the electrical consumption of ion lasers is quite high also.
