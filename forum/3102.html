Dinesh Sat Feb 05, 2005 3:22 pm #3
The easiest way to start would be to get the Integraf kit (link at top of page). The kit contains everything you need, except for a stable table. For this, you need something pretty heavy that will isolate the components from ground vibrations. You can use a slab of stone (marble, cement etc). Some people on this forum use doors, metal slabs. Anything solid and heavy.

The best books to buy are Graham Saxby's "Practical Holography", reviewed by two of our forum members, Colin ( [http://www.designerinlight.com/holo/graham.htm](http://www.designerinlight.com/holo/graham.htm) ) and Michael ( [http://www.dragonseye.com/holography/re ... axby3.html](http://www.dragonseye.com/holography/reviews_saxby3.html) ), and Fred Untersehers "Holography Handbook" ( [http://www.rossbooks.com/HH/hh.html](http://www.rossbooks.com/HH/hh.html) ). The handbook contains detailed descriptions on how to make a hologram. I would also suggest buying the beamsplitter. This is because if you continue, you'll need to make an interferometer to test stability and you need a beamsplitter to do this. As you get more experienced and familiar, most members of this forum have access to reasonably priced equipment and can help and/or suggest methods/equipment.

My RO, as long as I go slow enough, works well but if I push the speed it lets some through. Normal chlorine like they used to use here is easily filtered out by carbon filters.
