No, not the leafy tea but rather Triethanolomine. More specifically its use in swelling film.

A tip posted on the old board for mixing TEA was to pour it back and forth between two containers in order to get it fully dissolved in water. This morning I was heating water for my final rinse bath and decided to use the hot distilled water (100 deg F) for mixing some fresh TEA solution. That worked like a charm and dissolved the TEA immediately.

I also ended up soaking the film in warm TEA solution since I was short on time and didn't have a quick way to cool it. As far as I can tell that didn't have any negative affects and may even have helped remove the preservative in the PFG-01 film I use and increased the film speed beyond what I'm used to. My development time this morning was cut by 1/3 compared to the same setup on Saturday.
Tom B. Tue Jun 22, 2004 5:47 am #2
When I first tried TEA, I noticed some ugly hazy irridescent streaky stuff in the final holograms, but it didn't seem to be from bad squeegeeing. Eventually I remembered seeing someone's post (thanks, whoever it was) pointing out that it was very important to remove all traces of TEA from the non-emulsion side of the plate with a wet cloth and wipe clean with a dry one, using reflected light from a safelight to tell when it was clean.

I think the TEA on the non-emulsion side dries to form a film that causes extra internal plate reflections which are messy because the thickness is uneven. For film, about the best technique I could think of was to first dry the film, then lay it emulsion side down on a clean piece of glass and wet wipe and dry the other side.

I recently tried warming TEA solution in hopes that the gelatin would then be softer and would swell more evenly to give more narrowband images, but the results were inconclusive.

What about applying a pure water bath prior to TEA swelling? I guess depending on its concentration, it might actually take longer to swell the layer with TEA/water. I guess a TEA/water solution very much behaves like a mixture of alcohol/water. The type of emulsion and the degree of hardening strongly affect the swelling behavior.

By the way you might dissolve TEA in ethanol or in a water/ethanol solution.

When I TEA soak film, I use a sheet of glass as my work surface (thanks to JonathanH for that tip) and squeegee the non-emulsion side, wipe it down with a paper towel, flip it over and squeegee the emulsion side. After I lift the film up the second time I usually give it a gentle going over with a folded paper towel on the non-emulsion side to help remove any remaining TEA residue.

I've seriously thought about index matching with TEA because the film sticks well to the glass when you don't clean the TEA off and I've noticed that those areas that have stuck are cleaner and brighter than the areas that didn't.
