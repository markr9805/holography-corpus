Mithrandir Sun Aug 20, 2006 10:34 pm #1
Hi everyone! I just recently started to experiment with holograms after studying in my physics class. I have made several successful reflection holograms, and am working on trying to get some transmission holograms to turn out nicely. But one thing I noticed is that my laser (the 5mW diode laser from Integraf) seems to project interference fringes onto a white board held in front of it. I didn't notice these fringes when I first starting using the laser, but know it is quite apparent. Looking at my most recent reflection hologram, the fringes have been quite noticeably recorded onto the plate. I've tried compressed air to clear away any particles, and I've turned on a dehumidifier to try and help with any frequency instability. I also let the laser warm up for usually 10 minutes or so before exposing any plates. Despite my efforts, I can't get rid of the fringes. Is there anything I can do to eliminate them, or do I need to purchase a new diode?
Tony Sun Aug 20, 2006 11:11 pm #2
The fringes are probably dust on the lens or diode. It's very difficult to clean all the dust away. This is why most folks use a spatial filter.
Mithrandir Sun Aug 20, 2006 11:40 pm #3
If I could afford a spatial filter, I would go for it, but alas, I cannot. Is there any other way to get the dust out (if that even is the problem)?
Tom B. Mon Aug 21, 2006 4:03 am #4
Are the fringes faint straight lines 1-2 mm apart that are aligned crosswise with the elliptical beam or are they the normal messy circular patterns produced by dust and grease on the window?

If they are straight lines, they might be from reflections within the diode package. I see these with some diodes, don't know the cure.

If they are normal dust fringes, usually gentle cleaning of the window with a cotton swab and very clean alcohol or distilled water + tiny amount of detergent will work. Several attempts are often needed to get a completely clean beam.
Mithrandir Mon Aug 21, 2006 6:10 pm #5
Yeah, Tom, you hit it on the head. The fringes are vertical lines about 2mm apart which are aligned with the elliptical beam. So, you would say that they are internal reflections? When you say that you notice them with some diodes, do you mean you notice that some types of diodes do it and others don't, or do you mean that it can vary from diode to diode (even if they are the same model)?
Tom B. Tue Aug 22, 2006 2:53 am #6

I have not looked at differences between diodes of the same model, so I don't know if it varies. If it critically depended on alignment and distance of the diode chip to the window I can see how it might. I think it might even have been the Integraf diode I first noticed this on. My first diode (which had no lines) was a cheapo laser pointer with collimator sawed off which revealed a bare diode chip not encased in a transistor can with a window like the Integraf. From this, I thought at the time the lines might be due to the window, but have since found that some windowed diodes do not show visible lines. I don't know why this is.

One thing to try would be to place a pinhole aperture over the window in an attempt to allow only light from the tiny diode facet to exit and to block reflections from inside the can - I don't know if this will help or not. Some diode experimenters go so far as to remove the metal transistor can and window with a specialized tool (called a "can opener" ) available from optical research suppliers such as Thorlabs.
