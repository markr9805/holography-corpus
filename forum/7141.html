You don't need a chiller if you use it in an airconditioned room and you shoot only a few shots every 30 mins to 1 hr. You should use distilled water with a particle filter at 0.22um and a separate bed deionizer (both are available at APS water) in a closed loop system. I like using the Danner mag (pondmaster) pumps at 250 to 500 GPH. Currently I just purchased the 500gph pump to test with as I wanted more turbulent flow since the particle filter severly reduces it. Water circuit that I use is on my custom design: pump to particle filter to laser heads to deionizer to tank back to pump. I'm also upgrading my JK2000 to also use a particle filter and deionizer as it is a closed loop system that uses distilled water in the inner loop and it had a stainless steel coil in the water tank for external city water to take the heat away (outside loop).

Ron Michael Fri Feb 29, 2008 1:43 am #19
No I've not owned an apollo laser. I've just used the apollo tubes in my own designed cavities. In my case, I cracked them still knowing this because the tube collided with a cavity part anyway. If too loose they still hit something like the reflector walls etc and will crack the tube. When Kurt was supplying me, it was no big deal just to order a new tube. I liked helicals because of the even illumination, but to be honest, I hated them for all the problems related to their long arc length and high impedance, which required very high trigger voltages and supply voltages. So cavity arcing failures and problematic triggering was in my opinion their downfall. Today I much rather use linears as they have far less mount related issues and electrical related issues. For example my JK2000 uses multiple linears as well as my own designs. Unfortunately the electrical circuit impedances are different than helical support circuits so conversion to them can problematic but with enough linears in series the impedances can be matched with new designed cavities. However then instead of $400 helicals you end up buying $1000 worth of linears unless you find good surplus supplies. Special consideration has to be made for 4 or more linears in series with regard to cavity isolation during triggering as external arcing can easily still happen. So this forces most to use only 1 or 2 tubes in series which is not enough impedance for your your circuits. For example my JK2000 also never uses more that 2 tubes in series even though the amplifier has 4 linears tubes. My own designs uses single and twin tubes.

Ron Michael Mon Mar 17, 2008 12:39 pm #32
Couple of suggestions...

The pockels cell and brewster plates polarizer should be in the laser for any deviation they present to the beam axis and to make sure the hene shows good alignment thru the apertures of all the components before doing any mirror adjustments. Align the c-axis of the pockels cell with the hene axis. The basic setup was half wave (polarizer toward rear mirror, pockels cell toward laser rod) for the pockels cell which means the laser will not lase unless the 1/2 wave voltage is applied to the cell. Then in the free running mode you can fine tune the output for beam quality and total energy output.

Then you can resume q switch operations. Trying to get a laser aligned in the q switch mode is not easy. As I mentioned in the other post, you can use a hipot tester or power supply that limits the current by using 100 megaohm resistor. If DYI, make sure to use a HVX resistor or a string of 2watt to reach the voltage rating of the power supply. Best not to built hv supplies unless you know how to.
Ron Michael Mon Mar 17, 2008 2:40 pm #33
I understand from your comments before that you have not touched the rest of the oscillator setup. In that case just use the rear hene and double check that the hene passes through all apertures centered co-axially as possible. Then verify the alignment of the pockels cell to the hene. John's suggestion of using a white pin hole over the hene is a good technique. I just verify that the rear and front mirror bounce the beam back toward the hene and then I just align the multiple reflections until they are all aligned co-axially. A card place beyond the front mirror toward the amplifier is a good place to watch the reflections. Keep in mind there will be many off axis reflections present that should not distract you during your alignment. Try using a card and passing thru the beam along the oscillator components to help you identify the various off axis reflections.

It should lase even with the q-switch operational. Then fine tune.

It is best to have the laser centered with the hene during initial alignment. During q switch operation, the pockels cell and the laser rod undergo dimensional changes. The laser rod will become a bi-concave lens during this time. If you aligned the system with the hene passing thru other than the center of the rod, the rod will deviate (wedge) the beam and you will find the need to fine tune the output or in severe cases no output at all.
