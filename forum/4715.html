JohnFP Thu Apr 07, 2005 4:23 pm #2
Well, I will take a shot even though I am not familliar with that particular laser. Is the diode in a case? It probably is if it had a collimating lens. It seems to me that the diode may be diverging after the lens is removed and the outside of the beam is hitting the edge of the case before it comes out. This would cause dark rings around the outside of your beam.

Others more familliar with this particular laser may better tell you how to elliminat this permenantly but if it is as describe above, take a piece of black tape and cut a thin strip. Then put this just inside the case to reduce reflections of the inside of the case near the edge. Careful not to touch the diode. This is only temporary but may tell you if it is indeed hitting the edge of the case before it comes out.

Also, is there any other optic after removing the collimating lens? Again be careful not to touch the diode.
tpark Thu Apr 07, 2005 6:31 pm #3
Make sure the diode window is clean. That's typically the cause of weird patterns. I've seen ring type patterns, and it's always been some kind of contamination in the optics. Of course, if you posted an image it would be easier to diagnose what has gone wrong.

I've purchased lots of laser diodes, and as purchased they always produced perfect beams. The window does get dirty/contaminated sometimes, so you might have to clean it.

I use alcohol on a q-tip to dislodge the big pieces, then lens tissue to mop it up some, then one of those compressed gas blowers (which you can get at camera shops) to dry it off.

Your mileage may vary, but this has worked for me.

What diode are you using?

--Ted.
