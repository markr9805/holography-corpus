Funny, I never thought about it. I asked my wife to look into the object "broken mirror" and she hesitated at first. Then I heard (time delay) what I had said. I laughed. "Honey, come look in this broken mirror." hahahahaha - Only a Holographer!! Hehe.

Anyway, Danny, I am not going to use the glass etch for the mirror, although I should, I think it is the effect I want, but I will soon. Hopefully next session as it IS the idea. I just don't want to deal with the mess again so soon.

So I mixed up the plaster an poured it into a sandwich container. I then waited for it to slightly harden up. Then when it was still plyable (but if I do it again I will wait less time-it got a little to hard) I carved out a "parabola" recessed in the middle. I put the mirror on the casting material. I covered with 4 layers of 3 mil trash bag. I banged a screwdriver down in the middle. The casting material was too hard and too soft (a whole nother learning experience). It broke pefectly but plaster was all over the place. Luckely the mirror parts were all recovered and they are great. They fit together like a puzzel. I will have to make a "form" in which to glue them one by one (about 15 pieces) into the parabolic form.

Then, this is what I was thinking. How about doing 2 - 5 exposures, rotating the plate a few degrees? 5 and 10 degrees off center to the left then 5 and 10 degrees off center to the right in addition to the center hologram. That would give me 2 - 5 different references causing a swirl when you moved past the "grating". I call it a grating because that is what it will be, multiple gratings converging. It may be too much light at the center but I am going to try it anyway.

I feel this will teach me

HOE fabrication in broadband DCG by learing what reflected light off a mirror will do at different object angles combined with different reference angles.

Peace!

Things I got to shoot for:

Large view angle to be able to see most pieces at the same time.

Broadband as possible.

JohnFP Wed Jun 16, 2004 2:04 pm #15
These two photos are the first one which was a single exposure. The second hologram (not photographed yet) was a tripple exposure with about 10 degrees left of center, center and about 10 degrees right of center. I took the exposure time of the single hologram (25 seconds) and devided it by 3 then added a couple of seconds for a total of 10 seconds each exposure. The third hologram was a double exposure of 15 seconds each. I rotated the object and plate a full 180 degrees with the second exposure. The effect was not as I thought and I believe the two exposures were competing for fringe formation. The fourth hologram was a double exposure of 20 seconds each with the second exposure being tilted toward the reference beam by about 10 - 15 degree. It looks very cool. I think the multiple exposures are too busy and they take away from the natural beauty of the single exposure. The single exposure is great looking in person when viewed from the pseudoscopic side. The cracks in the mirror are in front of the plate but the reflections from the mirrors themselved remain behind the plate. Entire object depth was about an inch but the hologram has a total depth of 2 inches. Very beautiful colors that change and move as you change your viewing perspective.

I made 8 plates that all look exceptionally clean but could only expose 4 because...dumb dumb dumb... I ran out of epoxy to seal them. I may try to knock off the last 4 this evening or tomorrow. This will also give me a good indication of how the plates keep in the cellar. The cellar is 72 degrees F. and relative humidity of 70 percent and I do seem to see and increase in speed of the film with the increased humidity.

Peace!
