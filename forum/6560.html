MichaelH Tue Nov 27, 2007 7:36 pm #1
I finally took the plunge. With the experimentation needed to use VRP-M it seemed like the time and effort involved with using DCG wouldn't be all that different.

I was mostly right. Mixing gelatin's easy (unless I'm doing it wrong), the mold coating video Dave put out helped a lot and that process is simple, saves gelatin and gives a very even coating.

Unfortunately I accidentally measured out .8mg of glycerine when making the sensitizing solution but went with it anyway. The rest was per the Wiki 0.3g Aluminum Sulfate, 100 ml distilled water, 5g potassium dichromate.

On the 4x5" plate shown below I coated and let the plate cure in the dessicator for 12 hours, sensitized and let it cure in the fridge for another 8 hours before exposing for 90 seconds using a Coherent 315 at 100mW

I don't have an oven so I use a hot plate-stirrer with a inverted pyrex disk for 3 minutes at 100C.

Unfortunately (again) I forgot to do a pre-rinse and ruined my 35% & 70% alcohol rinses. I ended up skimping while doing the rise and making up another 35% solution and then skipping to 99%.

The result? One cruddy really dim but visible hologram.

JohnFP Wed Nov 28, 2007 1:58 pm #11
I am not going to debate the light levels. But mind you, my range was 5-20uJ.

Let's look at the upper limit.

10"diamter = 25.4cm

25.4cm / 2 = 12.7cm (radius)

12.7cm x 12.7cm x 3.14 = 506cm^2

My upper limit

20uJ/cm^2 / 90 seconds = .22mW/cm^2

.22mW/cm^2 * 506cm^2 = 112mW

Unless my math is off again, you have plenty of light. You are at the upper limit and if you use my 5uJ/cm^2 you only need 28mW of light in that spread beam. If you are only getting 1/4 of your laser light to the 12" diameter, then I stand corrected.

But on the other hand, If you have to dump more light on the plate buy increasing exposure time then something is wrong with the Glycerine/baking prcocess and I would concentrate on that. Otherwise you might as well be using standard DCG. But hey, try to increase exposure until you nail down the other variables, then you can decrease exposure.

Now to answer some of your questions:

When you make up the gelatin, you only use gelatin and water and let this dry at room temp for 4 -6 or so hours. Then you can refridgerate until needed. You put the gelatin in the water while it is spinning but cool. Then bring up to about 120F. I cook for an hour or more at this temp but others seem to just cook until it is perfectly clear and some cook at 160F. Then filter the gelatin through 2 coffee filters. I heat my beaker and ceramic funnel with a blow drying to help this process but it is not necessary to heat the beaker and funnel.

I was speaking that the center looked less hardened then the outside, due to milkiness but that is the only place there is a hologram. It seemed the process did not allow milkiness at outside, thus the outside was hardened more then the inside. But the center got the hologram. You may need to get with dave to see if your gel concentration is enough. It may be the outside it too thin.

Sometime when you blow dry a hologram is starts to turn milky but if you continue, sometimes for minutes, the milkiness goes completely away. Just wondering if you stopped to soon to clear the center. Don't be affraid if the hologram turns milky upon initial blow drying and quit. Continue real close to plate, hot and heavy and see if it clears.

Well, Joe, Dave, John and anyone else following this...

I bit the bullet yesterday and cooked up some "classic" dcg with 3-12-100 and the initial set of plates had crystals all over the emulsion after they'd dried.

![Image](http://www.dragonseye.com/gallery/d/13533-4/DCGplatecrystals.jpg)

After talking with Danny and Dave I cooked up some new gelatin (sans kdi) and added 30ml to my existing emulsion.

This morning I coated 8 small plates using the mold method and left them to dry during the day.

This evening I shot three plates with varying exposure times and got three decently bright dcg holograms. Danny and Dave both suggested using a photopolymer hologram as a test subject since there's little chance of it moving and they reflect laser light almost as good as a mirror, so I chose an Enterprise hologram.

![Image](http://www.dragonseye.com/gallery/d/13525-4/enterpriseDCG4.jpg)

The photo both does it more and less justice than it deserves but at least they were all bright.

Now I need to do lots of things such as cover the top edge of the plate, improve my coating technique, processing technique and so on and so on.

They were exposed between 60 @ 210 mJ, fixed for 2 minutes in diluted fixer, rinsed for a couple of minutes and processed and re-processed a variety of ways. The one in the photo was processed once with 25/70/99% alcohol with the last bath at 124F, air dried and put on a hotplate for a couple of minutes.

There's a bit of milkiness to the last plate but overall it's could be a lot worse. Has been actually.
