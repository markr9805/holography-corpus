Julian Thu Mar 27, 2008 11:29 am #1
Dear All,

When I made holograms back in the late 70's early 80's I read that you could not record a living thing on a continuous wave gas laser because of movement. As an example if you tried to make a hologram of an apple, the evaporation of the water within in it would make it shrink sufficiently during exposure to wipe out your interference fringes. Therefore unless you had a pulse laser, you could only use stable models such as off the shelf sculptures or figurines etc.. Has anyone ever made a hologram of a living thing on a hene or Diode laser? Call me naive but I want to try to make a hologram of an apple by coating it with a resin to stop the evaporation, could this work? I would imagine that even if the coating is really thick, it may record the resin wavefronts but the apple inside would disappear. What do you think?
JohnFP Thu Mar 27, 2008 11:46 am #2
I made a 20 second exposure hologram of an ice cube at ambient room temperature.

There is no magical considerations for living subjects. It's pretty straight forward. If your object moves a half a wavelength during exposure you get no hologram. So how fast is that apple shrinking and how long does your exposure have to be? If your exposure has to be 2 minutes you're less likely to be able to get success compared to if your exposure was 2 seconds.

But I have to ask, why make a hologram of a real apple when there are plenty of "fake" apple models out there? Secondly, an apple painted white will show up better then an apple that is red when exposing to a HeNe, unless of course you take a diffraction grating to the market with you and find that perfect apple that reflects a lot of 632.8nm.

I personally tried the ice cube because when making SBR holograms, I have learned over the years that things really don't have to be as stable as I orginally thought and I wanted to prove to myself it is usually the set up (including exposure time) and not the object that causes the failure.

So lay a plate right on that apple and shoot! Then let us know how it turns out.

I can't let this one pass witout comment.

"Why not use a fake apple?" Because it doesn't look like an apple.

Julian, lots of holographic images are made of living subjects. Much like photography evolved from "now hold still for about 10 seconds" to exposures at >0.01 seconds so holography has evolved from "now remain inert for about 10 seconds" to exposures at 0.01 microseconds.

Julian Thu Mar 27, 2008 4:28 pm #5
Thank you for your thoughts. The reason I asked the question is that in my experience holography was often limited artistically by the choice of subject matter. I think we have all chosen a metallic or spray painted white object because we all knew that it was highly stable and reflective. My exposure times for 2 beam reflection holograms with my 25 mw hene laser was always less than 10 seconds. I realise that I can't make a hologram of a human face with a 25 mw hene laser but I was always told that I could not use living objects such as an apple. Therefore I spent weeks making accurate models of human faces in order to simulate real life. Once I am set up again I will give it a go and see what happens. I am pleased that I was wrong.
