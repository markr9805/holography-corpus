JohnFP Tue Aug 05, 2008 11:16 am #7
meekan01, yes the base pattern or image would be displaced by the angle difference in the LED's.

What's more, one could use full color film and have different pattern colors at different depths. In other words, we could have a blue shape or pattern at 2 inches behind the plate and one 2 inches in front of the plate. Then we could have a green pattern at 1 inch in front and a red pattern one inch behind. Our array of LED's would be of all three colors. This would give the effect that you some patterns are in front of or in back of the other patterns and as you moved side to side, the paralax would surely show. We could also get more creative and use 2 LED's for each color, providing a double image for each pattern. If the separation of the two same colored lights is enough, the two images of the same pattern would be separated enough to look like two patterns. Then if we throw in a white, broad band pattern, we would get a single colored image from each of the LED's displaced by the difference in separation of the LED's.

Most raw LED's (no diffuser or collimator) do very well as a point source illuminator for holography. In fact, because they are so narrow band, they provide very crisp detail.

In my opinion, using multiple LED's, even as far as multiple colored LED's would sure open this medium up for the creativity of an artist. Right now I am picturing abstract shapes and colors at different depths. Some shapes are flat and some are 3D but the different depths would produce some very interesting paralax.

I imagine the pattern would look translucent and not opaque in the hologram where they overlap.

Shortly after I posted my finding on the forum someone suggested a book that had the spectral reflectivity of different pigments in it. I pondered for weeks trying to figure out how to make a glow in the dark hologram. The trick is, we can make a UV hologram but the viewer will not see a UV image. The blacklight effect uses Fluorescent paints. These absorb UV but reflect another color in the visible range. So with a glow in the dark hologram we have to somehow convert the UV image wavefront off the hologram to a visible wavelength and use some Fluorescent technique to get those really pastel reds and greens.

meekan01 Tue Jan 13, 2009 8:51 pm #15
Hi Solarenemy,

I've got a lot of experience with LED lighting; my conclusion after working with them extensively is that they're big advantages really shine when you want colored light -- they're less efficient, not as bright, etc, etc as CFLs otherwise. Basically, with a white light, to get green illumination you remove photons whereas with an LED you start with blue photons and then take them away to make white.

Anyway, you probably know all of that, but I wanted to suggest that you may get much better light output by using colored LEDs (not color-changing, but monochromatic) at the frequencies you're interested. If you're making a red hologram, you can use one of the Philips Lumiled Red Rebels and they will give you 85 lumens at only 2.1W (700mA, ~3V). On the other hand, if you use a white LED, only a small amount of the light emitted is red, so even though they put out as much as 180 lumens at 2.8W (700mA, ~4V), the light that will actually be in the range you want is only a small fraction of the total (probably less than 10% from the graphs I'm looking at).

So, if you wanted to, you could replace the white LED in your flashlight with a colored one and probably make your usable illumination increase by ~4-5 times with no additional power usage.
