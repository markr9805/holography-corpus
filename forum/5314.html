For some time now I've used a small black storage container with added racks as a drying case for film that's been wet treated (usually with TEA).

For some time I've had a problem with higher % TEA solutions where the film took several days to dry completely and even hitting it with a hair dryer didn't help all that much. The film would typically stick to the glass sandwich with some parts naturally coming loose during an exposure. The relative humidity around here varies quite a lot but it's never all that high unless it's raining.

Several months ago I added one of these:

[http://www.theairfilterstore.com/allergy/damprid.htm](http://www.theairfilterstore.com/allergy/damprid.htm)

to my drying case and I've found that I can use at least 6% TEA on my film and shoot several hours later with no problems at all, no need for quick drying with a hair dryer ever (unless I'm really impatient) and most of all no more film sticking to the glass.

Since I use roll film, I can tell when the film is fully dry when it's got some serious curl to it again.

I've also found that TEA treated film last quite a bit longer when it's kept completely dry.
