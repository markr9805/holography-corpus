Lasers typically have a jack on the power supply for connecting a remote switch for a door interlock. They usually need a jumper wire installed in the jack for the laser to work. Plug the jumper into its jack and try again!

Tony Sun Apr 22, 2007 12:14 am #4
CDRH regs require that lasers be marked with an absolute maximum output power rating, in your case 35mW. In fact, most lasers typically run around 1/2 of that number. It's amusing to see all the misinformation abounding on eBay because of this...
frothychimp Sun Sep 16, 2007 3:45 am #5
Don't even get me going on eBay, lol. Ok, one peeve, and I know you've seen it: "This laser module is an OEM product for integration into a user end product and therefore is not required to comply with Title 21 CFR1040.10" If it lases when power is added it must conform to 1040 no ifs, ands, or buts unless it's homebuilt and sold as a working unit. Very few people are willing to sell working homebuilt units because of liability issues (plus there are FDA registration requirements for sales).

I'm a homebuilt laser enthusiast but I also sell commercial laser systems so nothing ruffles my feathers more than seeing that statement written next to a working commercial or lab laser system. Whenever I see that statement it tells me to watch out because the next statement about price and power will be too good to be true.

Ok, I'll get off the soapbox. I'm just a stickler for the law, lol. Regardless, I'm glad you got your HeNe working.

I look forward to trying out my own attempts at holograms as I've been interested in them for a long time but it always seemed like it was too much stuff to get set up and running (that and blasting holes in things never gets boring).
