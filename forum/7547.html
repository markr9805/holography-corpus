MichaelH Sat Oct 04, 2008 7:11 pm #1
If anyone's looking for a good controller for the lab that won't cost both arms and a leg, take a look at the [Arduino](http://www.arduino.cc/). It's programmable and comes in several different sizes and flavors and has a cubic ton of "add-ons".

I had to replace my controller after lighting killed it and am glad I'm going from a $130 non-programmable board to a $34 programmable board that's open-source and flexible as heck. It's a lot smaller too.

![Image](http://arduino.cc/en/uploads/Main/arduino316.jpg)

It has 12 usable (two additional pins can be used for serial communication with other devices) digital I/O pins, 6 supporting PWM. 6 10 bit analog inputs and 16K of RAM.

The programming is almost done and I think I'll be able to wire it up to the table tomorrow. Definitely looking forward to some light bouncing around the lab again.

MichaelH Sat Oct 04, 2008 11:36 pm #4
> Danny Bee: wow thats great Michaelgood to see you back.can you share how this works with your 532nm coherent? is it for computer control?

It's for control of most of the lab. Given the programmability and extensibility of the Arduino I'll probably start using it for even more than I do now.

It controls a relay board which in turn controls power for 3 lasers, 3 shutters and a HEPA fan. It also controls two 1-watt LEDs that I use for status indicators outside the lab (lasers on/off and exposure in-progress). It allows me to automate exposures for all three lasers.

I've been using my [HoloController](http://www.dragonseye.com/holocontroller/screenshots.html) software for quite some time with the old controller and it was running under Windows with an MFC user interface.

The PC-level control will have to stay until I add a basic LCD display and keypad to the Arduino. At that point it'll be completely self-contained.
