A QUASI-SCIENTIFIC ATTEMPT TO UNDERSTAND ALCOHOL-ENHANCED DRYING IN CONJUNCTION WITH TABLE SUGAR POST-SWELL

Night 1: Copied H1 transmission master on VRP-M glass plate onto reflection H2 VRP-M glass plate.

Beam ratios 1:0.88 (object:reference) - under normal conditions this scenario would result in bright green replay highlighted with gold/red because...

...to combat the humidity in my clime I post-swell with 1 teaspoon table sugar per 150ml wetting agent (Integraf Form-A-Flow/distilled water)

But last night...

Finished up processing with two baths isopropyl alcohol:

Bath 1 - 45% alcohol concentration - ~10 seconds agitation, lift/angle plate 5X to "drag" off water

Bath 1 - 91% alcohol concentration - ~10 seconds agitation, lift/angle plate 5X to "drag" off water

Stand plate on edge on paper towel to dry

Results: Clear image vivid blue in center (where reference beam was strongest), bright green around periphria - NO RED BLOBBIES!!!!!!!!!

General conclusion: Drying with alcohol un-does post swell...

...but to what degree?

Will a higher concentration of sugar make a difference?

Stay tuned to find out!

JohnFP Tue Sep 25, 2007 10:12 am #8
I think you missed what glajciorz said. The sugar is disolving out of the emulsion, or actually diluting out, in the alcohol baths. It needs to stay there to "puff" up the fringes.

I would try to see if sugar is disolvable in pure alcohol. If it is then put your same 1 teaspoon to alcohol/water mixtures. If it does not disolve in alcohol, then put 1 teaspoon per the water volume in you alcohol/water mixtures.
