Tony Thu Jan 12, 2006 1:33 pm #9
> What do you mean by a doubling of the phase?

I mean that if you introduce a phase change of X degrees in the fundamental, you will see a phase change of 2X in the doubled output.

This is true for ultrasound phenomena, but I don't know if it's true for optical crystals.

> Wouldn't every creats of the longer wavelength and every other creast of the shorter wavlength yield a standing wave antinode? And every trough of the longer wavelength and crest of the shorter wavelength also yield a standing wave antinode?

I don't think so. Consider a time t at which the fundamental wave is at a positive crest and the 2f (second harmonic) wave is also at a positive crest. At this time they add to form an antinode. Now consider a time one half period of the fundamental later. Now the fundamental is at a negative crest, but the 2f wave is back to a positive crest, so you get a node. The pattern is not stationary in time, so as the recording medium integrates over time, there would be no spatial variation of density (fringes).

> Also, can you explain this animation? It is the second one down.http://www.glenbrook.k12.il.us/gbssci/p ... 10l4b.htmlIt seems to me that the antinodes fluxuate between constructive and destructive interference (albeit very quickly for light), and the nodes do not fluxutate.

The antinodes fluctuate between positive sum and negative sum, but the interference is always constructive (i.e. they add in amplitude). The nodes never fluctuate because their sum is always zero. Their interference is always destructive (i.e. they cancel in amplitude).

> Is a fringe the adjacent node and antinode? I always thought it was two anitnodes with one being constructive and one destructive.

I guess one could define a fringe in a number of ways - it could either be a single bright or dark area, or it could be one pair, including a node and an antinode.
