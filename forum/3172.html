Dinesh Fri Mar 04, 2005 5:11 pm #18
[quote:1c0230894d="BobH"][quote:1c0230894d]You can't measure the de of a display hologram, since a display hologram is photometric, not radiometric. [/quote:1c0230894d]

What if I put a reflection hologram in the collimated beam of a spectrometer, at the reference angle, and measure the depletion of the beam. Wouldn't that give me the DE?[/quote:1c0230894d]

It would give you the radiometric efficency with respect to the measured output of the (coherent) laser source. It would not give you the "brightness" of the hologram wrt the white light source reconstructing the hologram. I have several of Don's old reflection master upstairs. Don made them incredibly narrow band in DCG at 488 so they're practically invisible in white light even though they very bright under the laser. If by "de", you mean the popular concept of how "bright" a hologram is, then Don's holograms are very weak. If by de you mean the ratio of input light to output light at a particular wavelength measured in W/sq m, then you need to specify the wavelength. Due to various phenomena such as noise, scattering etc, I suspect that reconstructing a particularly broad band reflection hologram under different laser frequencies will give you different values for the de. Of course if the hologram is narrow band, you won't be able to measure the de under different laser frequencies and so the question of de vs lambda becomes moot. But the principle remains, the "brightness" of a hologram is determined by photopic considerations as well as the colour temperature of the reconstruction source (which would also contribute to the photopic "brightness"), while the ratio I_h/I_O where I_h is the measured intensity of the holographic image and I_O is the measured intensity of the laser, is esentially radiometric. Of course, you also have the spatial frequency range of a display hologram (I mean here the spatial frequencies of the image - the FT of the holographic image - not the spatial frequency of the fringe pattern) which would also come into the radiometric de but not the photometric one.

> If by de you mean the ratio of input light to output light at a particular wavelength measured in W/sq m, then you need to specify the wavelength.

This is indeed what I mean by "DE". If I illuminate a reflection hologram at its best reconstruction angle with the collimated white light beam of an Ocean Optics spectrometer system (detecting the transmitted beam), I'll get a plot of percent transmission vs wavelength. The system is calibrated with a piece of plain glass to remove the effect of reflections. The baseline of the plot shows me the effect of the scatter and/or absorption. The curve shows the DE as a function of wavelength, at the position on the display hologram I'm probing.

My point is that you can measure the DE of a display hologram. I'd also suggest that probing a display hologram with a laser having the same color as the hologram will also yield its DE. I wouldn't recommend it , however, since the beam has a high probability of "printing out" a dark spot on the image!
