Years ago I did some research (as you may remember, Martin) on ultrasonics with regards to improving the performance of silver halide material. But now that I'm working with DCG, I'm wondering if ultrasonics might be useful in this context, and whether or not this has been explored.

I wouldn't hazard a guess as to why ultrasonics improve the quality of silver halide emulsion, but it evidently does (under certain conditions) because this is documented in the patent literature.

I also have a paper called "Effect of Ultrasonic Energy Applied During the After-Ripening Stage of Photographic Emulsion Preparation" by Herold, et al. (1955) in the Journal of the Acoustical Society of Amercia.

The first few paragraphs are interesting:

"The first direct experiments with the application of ultrasonics in the manufacture of photographic emulsions were reported in 1934 by Claus. After consideration of the dispersive and emulsifying influence of ultrasonics reported by earlier investigations, Claus conducted a series of experiments in which high intensity vibrations, at 500 kc, were applied to various stages in the production of photographic emulsions: during mixing, during ripening, to the prepared emulsion, and to the entire manufacturing process. He claimed to have achieved more homogeneous, more stable, and more highly concentrated emulsions than were possible by the usual methods.

Claus further claimed, without providing quantitative data, that the resolving power of the ultrasonically treated emulsion was much increased because of the improved homogeneity, and that the sensitivity of the emulsion ultrasonically treated during after-ripening was much greater than that of the normally after-ripened emulsion.

....Peronnet and Biquard attempted to duplicate the Claus experiments, utilizing frequencies between 250 and 1000 kc. They reported that they were unable to detect the least influence of ultrasonic vibration on the quality of photographic emulsions obtained...."

The authors of the 1955 paper did in fact confirm Claus' assertion, however, at least reagrding the speed increase from ultrasonic treatment during after-ripening. But after I looked into the idea further (I am beginning to remember now) the energy level for the ultrasonic treatment was pretty high, and it would need a way to be measured, if experiments are done.

Just thought it might be an interesting avenue of research for DCG.
