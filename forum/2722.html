BobH Thu Sep 16, 2004 3:18 pm #8
I've seen lots of systems like that shown. It's probably a monitor or LCD being projected by a large, short focal length fresnel lens and reflecting off a flat mirror toward the viewer. The general public accepts the definition of any three dimensional image as "holographic". A two dimensional image floating in space is a three dimensional image to them.

> Yes, One parabolic mirror and a LCD screen and nothing else.

I think you meant to say one spherical mirror, though I doubt this is what's depicted on the web site. An object at the center of radius of a spherical mirror is imaged back onto itself, but the image can be seperated out be a beamsplitter or by offsetting the object. The use of two parabolic mirrors works if a hole is cut into one of them (or off axis mirrors are used), but it's hard to seperate the view of the original object from the view of the image with this approach.
Dinesh Thu Sep 16, 2004 6:01 pm #9
The advantage of a spherical mirror is that the object and it's image are all in-line so that there is no aberration. Since reflective surfaces don't have chromatic aberrations, you have good color rendition as well. Any other type of mirror would work so long as the object distance were greater than the image distance, otherwise the returning image light would hit the object and you'd have shadows. It's also important to make object and image inline to the axis of the mirror to avoid coma and astigmatism. However, spherical mirrors are easier to set up since as Bob says, if placed at the center the object coincides with the image and they're a lot cheaper than parabolic. Also, I believe (without actually calculating this) that spherical mirror are more forgiving of aberrations from small changes. Of course, spherical mirrors are also a lot cheaper!

You can use lenses with which it's easier to seperate out object from image since they're on opposite sides of the lens. It's also easier to make the object and image in-line and so avoid coma and astigmatism. However, you have chromatic aberration issues.

Luckily these systems lose very little light and so the image is pretty bright. This means that you probably have a lot of leeway in level of detail and so you don't need gi-hugic optics (The level of detail focused by an optical system depends on the size of the optics, sort of).
