Hi Joe,

I don't know whether a 25 Watt aquarium heater would have enough power for your system, I suspect that you may need something much bigger.

It all depends on your system, what's the difference between your target temperature and your ambient temperature, and what are the system losses.

If the system is well insulated, you may be OK with just a 25 Watt heater. The advantage of using a low power heater is that you would be less likely to have a temperature overshoot, but it could take you a long time to get to your target temperature. If your system losses are too high, you may never get to your target temperature.

I'm not sure whether a magnetic stirrer will provide enough mixing for 4 litres of water, you will just have to try it and see. You don't want a temperature difference from the top to the bottom of your liquid.

The two items you listed may do the job. You will be able to confirm this if you have access to a suitable thermometer.

I'm waiting for a similar PID controller from China, but I will be using a platinum temperature sensor which is a resistive device rather than a k-type thermocouple.

[http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_thermometer](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_thermometer)

I don't know if all cheap PID temperature controllers from China can be reconfigured to use a different type of temperature sensor,

the one I have ordered can be. Likewise, the second item you list also looks like it can be configured to take an RTD (resistive temperature device).

The second item on your list looks like it can switch a maximum current of 3 Amps without the use of the SSR (solid state relay).

The advantage of using a SSR, is that it has a longer life than the built in relay and can handle a higher current.

The item you have ordered uses a 10K ohm NTC thermistor as a temperature sensor, which is fine.

Without seeing the instruction manual, I would think that the item you ordered will have a set of relay contacts as an output to power your heater.

The relay contacts could be rated for 10 Amps looking at the advert. The item also has a calibration setting, so I suppose you can calibrate it against a known accurate thermometer.

> Joe Farina: Thanks. Yes, it may be for TE coolers. There do seem to be other PID controllers for higher voltages on eBay, I'm looking at some now.

No, I don't think it is for TEC coolers.

This bit gives it away: "Delay protection function."

A delay function is often used in conjunction with a compressor, as in refrigeration.

It's designed to give time for any back pressure in the refrigeration circuit to equalise, otherwise the compressor may start under a heavy load, which is a bad thing as it could kill the motor.

"What a deal you got on the PID controller"

I didn't bid on that particular PID controller, but one of the same model.

Link to the instruction manual for the one I have ordered: [http://www.happybuyerstore.com/manual/new2006power.pdf](http://www.happybuyerstore.com/manual/new2006power.pdf)

The one you have brought says under technical specifications: Main output: RELAY: capacity 250V/3A AC, 30V/3A DC,

but under Features: SSR control ready to connect external Solid State Relay to handle High current.

You will have to power the unit up first, without any connections to your heater or SSR and then go into the setup menu to see if there's an option to select SSR output.

Your manual should provide all that info.

I suggest that you install all the electronics in a box of some form and if it's a metal box, make sure that it is earthed.

I recommend that you bolt the SSR to some form of heat sink. It's also worthwhile to have a switch placed between the output of the SSR and your heater. That way you can play with the settings of the PID controller without power going to your heater.

As regarding the choice of heating device, see if you can identify something that is already available and would fit in with your requirements, rather than starting from scratch with just an heating element. As you know, I'm using 2kW deep fat fryers. I got three for about $90. Until I have the PID controller set up, I don't know how they will perform regarding hitting the target temperature. A 2kW heater may seem overkill for just heating water for the swelling bath, but see 'Note 9 cycle rate' in the instruction manual linked above. The risk of using an overpowered heater for a small volume of water is temperature overshoot.

I plan to use one of the 2kW 3 litre deep fat fryers for my swelling water bath.

I will made a simple frame up to support a standard photo developing dish above the fryer at an angle and have a small water pump immersed in the water of the fryer.

If I make it right, the water should just trickle down from the edge of the raised developing tray back into the fryer. I will place the temperature sensor in the fryer.

I did ponder with the idea of heating the first IPA bath directly as you have done, but it complicates things.

I wanted the IPA containers to be sealed most of the time, I don't like breathing in IPA fumes, maybe I should take up another hobby:-)

The temperature of a directly heated IPA bath can be controlled better as there is no container between the IPA bath and the surrounding temperature controlled water as I'm doing. There is less chance of water contamination with a directly heated IPA bath. I have to wipe the outside of the IPA container after withdrawing from the heated water bath before removing it's lid. With my intermediate IPA water heated bath, I have to switch off the heater before withdrawing an IPA container, as there is less overflow into the deep fat fryer with a container removed.
