JohnFP Tue Apr 24, 2007 1:31 pm #7
Yes, the first thing I see is a great difference in path lengths as Kaveh mentioned. Depending on the coherence length of your laser this may be a problem.

The second thing I see is that even though you are not using a beam splitter, that mirror has to be very very stable. Any movement with it and you will lose you holgoram.

Try a simple single beam hologram first to make sure you film, sandwiching, processing and all other variables are good. Get a good hologram with the Simple SBT and then introduce another mirror for lighting.

I suggest this set up,

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... ansmission](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Single_Beam_Transmission)

Then, if you want additional illumination to your object, place mirrors where there is spill over light and redirect that lighting to the object. As you get a hologram you will be able to see if the additional mirror moves as the hologram will be visible but without the additional lighting (from side normally shadowed in a SBT).

I have used a similiar setup. In the image it looks like the brightest part of the beam from the laser will be missing both mirror and object. If that is true, i would try to get as much light as possible on the object (rotate laser to the left) and then move your mirror in closer. The goal of this would be to improve the ratio of ref:obj and reduce exposure time a bit.

Also, if the object is dark as shown in your rendering, then try to paint it diffuse white so as to kick back as much light toward the plate.

Once successful, be aware that the diverging beam will make it nearly impossible to do an H2 reflection copy . When you place your film back in the holder in the same orientation to view it, it will look cool, but when you flip it around to project the image for copying, it will be GIANT, and from what I've seen, it continues to scale with distance.

JohnFP Wed Apr 25, 2007 10:17 am #13
> KungKrille: Yes, I read about that setup and I too think it´s wise to try that first . You mentioned using mirrors for wasted light - does it matter if some of it hits the film or should it just be the reference beam and the reflections from the object?

You want to steal light from the reference beam and redirect it to add lighting to just the object. The redirected light should not hit the plate or it will cause noise in the form of a grating being made by the referece beam hitting the plate and the light from the redirecting mirror hitting the plate.

ErichRose Wed Apr 25, 2007 12:47 pm #15
You don't necessarily need laboratory grade equipment. I would start by attaching the mirror to something solid. Many folks have made great use of small weights used for exercise. They can be found and purchased used. You can then either glue the mirror directly or make something adjustable. Others have made great use of small sand-filled containers. The classic is the laser held by a clothes pin stuck in a coffee mug full of sand. All of these methods have one thing in common: weight.
