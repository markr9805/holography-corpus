Tony Mon Jul 23, 2007 5:34 pm #6
Yes, Brewster angle (~57 degrees) and proper polarization is the way to get rid of woodgrain.

For clamping film between plates of glass, the trick is not to just put the film between glass sheets and clamp it outside the film area. if you do this, the glass will bow outward due to the leverage between the clamping point and the edge of the film. The outward bowing increases the spacing to more than the thickness of the film, which allows it room to move during exposure.

Either apply the clamps inside the film area (which works but creates shadows at the clamps); or shim the glass with something the same thickness as the film at the clamping point ( a couple layers of transparent tape will do). This will keep the glass flat and not allow the film room to move.
