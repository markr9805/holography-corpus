I've started a number of open-source projects. Some with great success, some which went no where. I'll try to share a little of what I've learned regarding the success of a project:

> Martin: Principally I agree that this would be an interesting idea. Given the latest disputes here, I doubt a bit if this was workable though.

This is a common problem with many open projects. The solution is to have a single, well defined vision and goal. Lay out all the work that needs to be done (in as much detail as is feasable) and then let those who want to tackle a piece do it.

If the vision and goal are well described and set in stone, it prevents much of the bickering and reduces the amount of destructive argueing. For example, the worst thing for a project is to have a bunch of work done, and then get into a big flame war over what the goal was in the first place. Everyone needs to be on the same page when it comes to vision and roadmap to completion.

Now, that's not to say that there won't be arguing or challenging of practises or ideas, but that's a much healthier sort of thing for a project.

The success of the project will be reasonably assured when:

1) The goal and vision are agreeable

2) The work is well described, and is easily and obviously divisible

3) There is a large enough pool of valued contributors on board to make progress

4) There is some basic assurence that the project 'home' and/or coordinator will stick around

Then, it's just a matter of time for the project to be completed.

-Phil
