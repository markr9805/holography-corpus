Hmm - haven't seen that one. Do you have any way to post a picture of a blotchy plate? Are the blotches sharp-edged? Some unlikely suggestions, most of which you've probably already ruled out -

- Tea not well mixed with water? (it can be slow to dissolve)

- Tea not soaking in uniformly? I always add several drops of foto-flow

- Bad squeegee technique? usually seen as streaks, though.

- Inadequate drying time? If the emulsion was still damp in patches during exposure this could definitely cause problems.

- Developer being used? - TEA can cause fogging with some developers if

not rinsed out first (not much of a problem with JD-4, though, and not likely to cause blotches, I think)

- Bad plates? unlikely if they work OK with no TEA. Guess I'm stumped.
Colin Kaminski Thu Aug 05, 2004 6:41 am #3
I usually find blotches are from uneven drying. Perhaps there is dust, if they are small blotches. Otherwise they could be from having a puddle of water and quick drying the plate. Try to squeegee then air dry for a day.
Dinesh Thu Aug 05, 2004 1:13 pm #4
Mike Midori suggests blotting paper to dry the plates after squegeeing.

As for the uneven-ness two thoughts occured to me:

1. The plates aren't uniformly dried. Are the plates dried horizontally or vertically? Do you have photoflo in the TEA? How fast is the air and how much contact to the plate surface (is the air stream a few inches above the plate or right across it )?

2. Presumably the plates are put in this box emulsion side toward the fan (up, if horizonatlly placed). Is the back completely dry. The small film of water on the back side may be causing reflection in those areas where there is still an undried film of water. Either away from the plate, if you're doing single beam Denisyuk, or toward the film, if you're doing H1-H2, and causing a holographic mirror.

Thank you all for your replies - as a newbie at this, any help is useful.

As it turns out, the problems I was having could be summed up as "many of the above". The blotches were, I think, caused by puddles of TEA left on the surface of the emulsion. Also, 14% was far too strong a solution - the holgrams replayed in a violet color, suggesting that the concentration was too high.

As far as "squeegee" technique goes, I'm still learning how to do this properly. It seems fairly easy in the light of day, but in near total darkness it's kind of tricky to tell what's going on.

I use a paper towel to dry the non-emulsion side, so it's preetty dry. The plates are dried face up. The fan sucks air from the box, and it goes through a filter first, but I think I hadn't let it dry quite long enough, maybe a half hour or so.

By using a less concentrated (about 6%) TEA solution, careful squeegeeing, sufficient drying, I've managed to get a good green reflection hologram. I did add photoflow to the TEA mix, and it does seem to help.

Are other wetting agents better that photoflow? I note that Graham Saxby mentions the Illford product in his book. Also, I have some Photoflow 200 here as well - is that supposed to be better?

I'm using a piece of windshield wiper blade to squeegee. Is it worth getting some sort of official squeegee, or should this work fine?

Thanks again for your help,

--Ted

I arrange things so I can look at the reflection of my safelight on the plate - streaks and missed spots are easier to see this way. Also, re drying the non-emulsion side, I noticed that by reflection I could see a sort of oily smear on the glass from the dried TEA which explained some unpleasant noise in my holograms. The way to get it off is to wipe with a wet paper towel, wipe dry, inspect and repeat until clean. This can be done anytime before exposure - the plate is easier to handle when the emulsion is dry.
