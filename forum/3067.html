I've been making some holograms with bb-640. I'm wondering if anyone else here has some comments/suggestions on dealing with this material.

The latest exposures that I've done have been with my intergraf diode. The output of this diode seems to be about 654 nm, but it's kind of hard to read with my spectroscope. This is a little bit beyond the range suggested by the manufacturer, but it seems to work.

I'm using a power density of 10 microwatts/cm2. This is with the probe in the middle of the plate, the sensor direction parallel to the plate. I did two exposures, one of 30 seconds, one of 60 seconds. The 30 second exposure reflection hologram came out, but it wasn't great, and the 60 second one was better, but still not great. The plates were soaked 2 minutes in 3% TEA, then dried. The plate was developed with JD-4 - 20 seconds in the developer. The plate wasn't all that dark after developing.

From the page [http://www.vinteq.com/gen-bb.htm](http://www.vinteq.com/gen-bb.htm) comes the following statement:

"The sensitivity is approx. 150µJ/cm² exposed at 633 nm and developed in a metol/ascorbate developer to a density of 2.5. Please note that this is a very rough estimate only and may vary widely depending on wavelength and development. In most cases one will get good results by giving around three times the exposure which is necessary for conventional products."

Also, for bb640

"Sensitivity: 633 nm (He-Ne), 647 nm (Kr+), NOT for use at 694 nm .

Sensitivity region: 580 nm to 650 nm

Suitable both for reflection and transmission holograms, excellent Denisyuk performance

"

I figure I gave the plate 300 uJ/cm2 and 600 uJ/cm2 for my exposures. The plate didn't have that foggy overexposed look after I developed it either.

1) How dark is a density of 2.5? I figure it's noticibly dark, but not so dark its hard to see through the plate. Should I just soak it in the develooper until its that dark, or should I try and adjust the exposure so it only has 20 seconds or so in the developer?

2) Is frequency critical on the laser? it seems as though I'm a bit out of range on this material - I noticed that the chart on the Slavich PFG-03m has a sharp drop off in sensitivity in that region, could BB-640 also have this as well?

3) If you're using bb-640, what kind of wavelength/exposure time/power density are you using for holograms?

4) I've noticed that exposures seem to vary from batch to batch - could the plates become less sensitive with time? What's the lifetime of the plates?

Thanks for any comments you can provide - I'm sure I'll figure out more with some experiments, but input from others will surely be helpful.

I'll post images of the holograms once I snap some pictures of them.

--Ted.

Tom B. Wed Jan 26, 2005 6:47 am #5
> Ed Wesly: It has been my experience that this stuff needs more like 800 micro to 1.6 milliJoules exposure, and this with like 5' development in Kodak D-8!

This corresponds to my experience for TEA concentrations of a few percent or less. Above 10 percent, sensitivity improves somewhat, but it still needs a lot of light. I mostly use 650-660 nm - I didn't notice any significant difference in sensitivity at 635 nm. I usually develop for a minute or two in metol/ascorbic until it gets to about density 2.0-2.5.
