I was wondering if any of the developing solutions can be stored and re-used later. Foe example...can the bleach solution be used more than once?
Colin Kaminski Mon May 07, 2007 1:51 am #2
It depends on how the chemistry was designed. Usually they fail from picking up oxygen. If you store the chemicals with no head space they will last longer. I have not had a problem with bleach, but developers will start to turn black. I also have had something grow in photoflo solutions that tends to make blobs on the hologram. Photoflo is so cheap that I make it and only use it for a week.

Perhaps some of the chemistry experts can elucidate on the mechanisms of O2 incorporation into developers?
Tom B. Mon May 07, 2007 2:35 am #3
Once mixed, developers that I'm familiar with are only good for a few hours and must be discarded afterward. But if you use a 2 part developer, you only need to mix the small amount needed for a single developing session (50-100 ml)

Bleaches such as the dichromate used in JD-2 and the ferric or copper rehalogenating bleaches used in JD-4 can be re-used many many times over a period of several months. They don't suddenly go bad, they just take longer and longer to work.
glajciorz Mon May 07, 2007 2:13 pm #4
Good day.

I use 0,5l of the same metol-ascorbate (JD4) developer for 3 months.

It turned brown very slightly, but still works fine.

No "floating dish" developing, just using the same container for developing and bleaching, so JD4 contacts with air maybe for 4 minutes at a time. There is 20-30ml of air above the developer when kept in a bottle (I had some spills ).

The only problem with this developer is that it's ruthless for gelatine because of it's high pH.

1l of Neutol works fine for 1,5 months till now.

Fe:EDTA bleach works fine for 4 months till now.

I hope it helps.

greetings

Adam

Yes, I guess developers containing large amounts of sulfite have an extremely long shelf life. This is particularly true for Neutol.

And incidentally, even the old partly oxidized Neutol may still be used – as a colloidal developer.

As for the EDTA bleach, I still use a solution I had prepared more than 10 years ago. And it still works perfectly.

floh Fri May 18, 2007 4:34 pm #7
Develop solution have to be on high pH like 10 or 11 to work. Refresh with carbonate will work for some receipts. I am using an pyrogallol developer that work for one hour and a CW-C2 that works for days. Just keep air (oxygen) away from any developer to increase its life-time.

Bleach is no problem. I still use the same bottle of dichromate bleach since years. It goes a little dark but still works. EDTA bleach is working for months as well.
