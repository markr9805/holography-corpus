BobH Thu Apr 26, 2007 9:33 pm #9
I've made many reflection H2s (mostly from transmission H1s) for use with laser light for contact copying, in AgX and DCG. With AgX, bandwidth is about 30nm (narrow) so the processing needs to be ultra-smooth so you don't see squeege or other processing marks. With DCG, the bandwidth should (I think) be as narrow as the coating and drying uniformity will allow.

Dave points out that, with laser irradiation, a narrow bandwidth reflection hologram will have more of its modulation available for the image at a specific wavelength. This means it will either have higher diffraction efficiency (though possibly look dimmer) or have higher signal-to-noise ratio. Both of these are attributes of a good reflection "intermaster" or "production tool" hologram.

As an addendum to my last comment, I see a lot of opinion being thrown around.

One way to settle things is to measure the DE. Shoot a laser beam through your holograms and measure the relative DE between your narrow and broad band holograms. Post the results.
