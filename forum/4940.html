i was wondering something . . . my understanding is that switching to green (with, say, a c315) and using DCG requires a much higher exposure energy and therefore longer exposure times (1 minute or longer)

Does the superior quality/brightness of a DCG hologram offset the requirement of a longer exposure time?

it seems to me that the brightness would still be greatly dependent on stability, and so it might be better to stick with 2-10 second exposures using a HeNe and PFG film until one has the budget to get something like a 500mW DPSS or greater.

thoughts?
Dinesh Fri Jul 08, 2005 6:27 pm #2
I don't know how anyone else feels about this, but I think it all depends on why you're making holograms at all. There's always a tradeoff between "good" and "work needed for 'good' " and where on that line you stand determines which process is 'better'. Yes, DCG holograms are much brighter than silver holograms and yes making DCG holograms requires a lot of effort. Not just in terms of longer exposures but also in terms of cleaning your own glass, making your own emulsion and coating techniques which are complicated processes. However, the more complex a process, the more can go wrong with it. So DCG has many "fault modes".

If you want to make a hologram to see the stunning visual effect, my feeling is to go with silver first. This'll give you a feeling of how the holographic parameters work (ratio, lighting etc). They'll all change for DCG, but you'll get an idea of how to alter them. If you're the type who likes the process more than the actual result, go for DCG. There's a lot more knobs to twiddle and buttons to push and, in the end, you'll get something that'll look better than silver, but "in the end" is the effort you're prepared to put into it. It does take a lot of tweaking to make it worthwhile.
