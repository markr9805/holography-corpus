Ed Wesly Thu May 15, 2008 9:05 pm #3
Our dear departed Gabor was trying to do just this! His idea would be to create an interference pattern using electrons or X-rays, then read out the recorded interference pattern with visible light, and the magnification of the object would be proportional to the ratio of the wavelengths of recording to the wavelength of playback. If you can conjure up his seminal papers, and those of Gordon Rogers, they showed the proof of concept by recording holograms with a blue line of a mercury vapor lamp and replaying them with the green line, and magnification of the object was observed. For the in-line geometry and the planar objects they were using, it had acceptable results.

If you can look up in SPIE and/or OSA publications, you will find some activity along those lines in the '80's. A cat at Argonne National Lab outside of Chicago gave a talk on his results, but I forget what things looked like. But he was confined to the in-line geometry, and was using Agfa 8E56HD plates to record.

The big problem for X-ray holographers is not only the temporal coherence (wavelength purity) but the trick of generating a point source for spatial coherence. Since X-rays penetrate most things, it's hard to make focusing optics for them. Some dudes at Northwestern University were making mirrors for X-ray space telescopes, and the things looked like bullet noses with the tips circumcised; there was a hole there, and that's where the image came out of. Like light, X-rays will reflect stronger at grazing incidence, so the X-rays were hitting the inside of the mirror cone almost parallel to the surface, and focusing outside of the parabolic reflector.

(Footnote: to see the power of reflectivity at grazing incidence, compare the intensity of transmitted light to reflected light off a plane piece of glass with your laser. At 0 degrees incidence, about 4% is reflected, as the glass is tilted, more light gets reflected, and when the beam is incident around 80 degrees from the normal, you can see the reflected spot is brigher than the transmitted one!)

So X-ray holography has been done, but I guess the results were nothing to really pursue, given the problem of making a point source, and the basic hazards involved.
