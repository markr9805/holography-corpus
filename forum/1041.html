What is the recommended vertical size of a H1? same size as H2?

Why gets the H2 brighter if I limit the vertical size of the H1?
BobH Sun Apr 05, 2015 12:29 pm #2
I think the H2 should be as large as possible, the limiting factor being the collimating mirror or lens for its reference and reconstructing beams. Some use a diverging reference beam for it and an unmatched conjugate beam for reconstruction, but that makes the image distorted and swim around on the plate. Some restrict the vertical dimension of the H2 to make the transfer brighter, but that can also be done with other methods (like using better film or processing, better lasers, more stable equipment (forget the sand box!), and/or noise reduction techniques).

The H2 gets brighter with a smaller H1 because the H2 must record fewer fringes (less information), so the fringes actually recorded have higher visibility and therefore higher diffraction efficiency.
lobaz Mon Apr 06, 2015 11:38 am #3
Everything Bob said is true. However, I think the main reason is somewhere else. To answer why H2 is brighter, it is necessary to understand function of H1 and H2.

A H1 hologram serves as "a window to a virtual world". That is, a properly illuminated H1 hologram creates a virtual image of the object that was present at the time of the hologram recording.

In H1->H2 transfer, you basically reverse rays, so all rays originating from H1 create a real image of the original object; this real image is somehow recorded on the H2 hologram.

Now compare H2 and H1 reconstructions. Let's suppose both have the same area and the reconstruction beams create virtual images.

H1 gets some light according its size. Some of the light energy does not diffract (it is directly transmitted or reflected), and some creates a bunch of diverging rays that form the virtual image. If you put your eyes somewhere to watch the hologram, they detect quite small amount of light - remember that all the rays forming the virtual object are diverging from the area of H1.

Now illuminate H2. It gets exactly the same amount of light as it has the same size as H1. H2 is basically a hologram that recorded rays originating in the H1; so H2 reconstructed by the conjugate of the original reference will create rays converging to the original "H1 window to a virtual world". If you wish to observe the object, your eyes must be located in this bunch of rays. It means that light carrying information about the original object is focused from H2 to the original H1 frame.

Thus, contrary to H1 reconstruction where the light energy was diffracted in all directions to form a virtual image, in H2 reconstruction the energy is concentrated to a narrow beam forming the H1 window. So as H1 gets smaller, energy from H2 gets higher in the original H1 frame, thus gets brighter. As you usually don't want to limit horizontal size of H1, you rather reduce H1 vertical size.

H1 vertical size also depends on type of H2 you make. It could/should be quite large for reflection or laser illuminated transmission H2. It should be quite small (several mm to about 2 cm) for rainbow holograms to limit chromatic blurring.
