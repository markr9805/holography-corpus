I have the following question about creating reflection copies from

a color transmission master that has been recored with several lines

(say R,G,B). Can a single color be used for the copying process ?

The idea is that replaying the color H1 master with a single color

(green, say) should give back the green image, plus two distorted

copies associated with the R,B channels; for those, it is like

replaying a master with a "wrong" color). Turning it around and

making a H2 reflection copy as usual, by using only green (say)

illumination, would record the correct green plus the distorted red

and blue channels on the H2 reflection copy. Turing that around for

replay, wouldn't the interferfence pattern precisely correspond to

the original R,B,G channels, and so replay the original color image upon white light illumination ?

Other question: does anybody have experience with Kubota's technique

for obtaining very-deep image reflection copies ? See:

[http://homepage2.nifty.com/kubotaholo/emy_holo07.htm](http://homepage2.nifty.com/kubotaholo/emy_holo07.htm)

I suspect it would depend a lot on how and on what the original H1 was made. Assuming the H1 was made with colinear references, there may be cross talk. If the film was thick and/or the references were coming in at different angles, you'd reduce or eliminate the cross talk, but your single laser for reconstruction would be off Bragg for anything but the colour you're using. In the scheme you described, the film would have to be thin so that you could take advantage of the angle independance in Raman Nath diffraction. I'm not sure that making an H3 would cancel out the aberrations, however. It seems to me that the aberations would be faithfully reconstructed since these aberrated images are now the diffracted object wavefront. If you choose three primaries whose wavelengths were close wrt to the film thickness, you may be able to get away with it. You'd pretty much have to manilpulate the Q factor for each wavelength.

Another loophole is, I guess, that while it might be possible to

copy color transmission holograms (with a planar interference

pattern) with a single wavelength, creating reflection ones would

be hard, because the standing wave patterns stored in the bulk of

the emulsion depend critcally on the wavelength and I don't see how

they can be reproduced by using a "wrong" wavelength. (Probably

there is a better and more precise way of saying this.)
