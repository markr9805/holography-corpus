IR is not of itself "heat". This is a popular misconception. Zapping metal with IR would be like zapping metal with any radiation, it'd be absorbed very quickly by free electrons within about lambda/2 of the surface. Considering that atomic diameters are of the order of about an Angstrom or two, most of the radiation would be absorbed in about a depth of about 50 atoms. I'd write down the appropriate formulae, but far be it from me to use mathematics. Accusations would fly!

IR is considered as "heat rays" because most large molecules have absorption peaks in the IR range. Thus when these molecules absorb IR they resonate and so the net effect is conversion of IR to kinetic energy. This is then felt as "heat" through Boltzmann's Law.

Another prooblem is that there's not a lot of IR in sunlight. If there were, the light would take on a reddish hue. Rayleigh scattering is a fourth power law and so blue is far more preferentially scattered (thus a blue sky). Most of the IR in sunlight is absorbed by water molecules in the upper atmosphere anyway.

It depends on what you mean by "infrared". The visible spectrum ends at roughly 700nm. Some people can even see at 800nm. However, the sun radiates at all wavelengths, including radio and gamma, but not much. There is a Maxwell Boltzmann distribution of energies over any partcular frequency range. When this radiation hits the upper atmosphere, the UV is filtered off (mostly) by the ozone layer because 0_3 has an absorption resonance at about 300 odd. However, this is an exponential effect. Not all the "UV" is absorbed, depending on what you mean by "UV". On reaching the upper atmopsphere, the particle density is pretty sparse and so Rayleigh scattering dominates. You can derive the Rayleigh scatering formulae from considerations of dipole oscillation. From this , the intensity of a dipole radiator is:

I=(alpha)((omega)^4)(sin^2(theta)/r^2)

where I've lumped all the factors concerning dipole moment and epsilon into alpha.

Notice the omega^4 dependance. This results in a much larger scattering of high omega, allowing most of the lower omega to pass through - hence red skies at sunset. However at lower altitudes, the density of matter increases and so the assumptions of Rayleigh scattering no longer hold. Since there is still blue scattering (distant mountains don't look red) there must be another mechanism involved. This problem was solved (ndependantly) by both Einstein in 1910 and Smoluchowski in 1908. when they showed that random thermal effects created density fluctuations in the lower atmosphere. The statistical averaging of these fluctuations create Rayleigh conditions in the lower atmosphere. Certainly "about half is visible and half is infrared." is not true, most its blue-uv. This is why you get tanned. If there were no uv at all, everyone would look a pasty pale color!

I'm not sure what you mean about "color" and absorption of IR in a "large mass" and retaining heat by conduction. Conduction is a mechanism for not retaining heat! In a metal, the refractive index becomes complex so that

n = n_r + in_I

where, of course, n_r is the real component,

n_I is the imaginary component

i = sqr root(-1)

Thus the E-field from em radiation ( all em radiation including ir), is given by:

E = E_0(exp(-omegan_Iz/c)cos(omega(t - n_rz/c)

You can therefore see that there is an exponential fall-off of the amplitude giving rise to a rapid absorption of energy. Conventionally, this is given by:

I(z) = I_0exp(-alphaz)

The conditions on alpha basically have to do with the frequency of the free electrons at about lambda/2 of the surface. Sometimes bound electrons do contribute, hence the color of copper, but mostly it's just the free electrons. At the frequency of ir, I suspect that the phase reversal due to an oscillator driven by high enough frequencies might actiually oppose the driving lambda - but this is something I need to calculate out. Under certain condition, such as the exotic electron-electron binding of superfluid theories, there might be some transmission, but in real world conditions the enrgy would quickly disperse.
