Ed Wesly Mon Apr 23, 2007 8:42 pm #4
A transmission hologram would certainly work if recorded at NIR and replayed with red. The big trick is to find a recording material that responds to the IR.

The late lamented Agfa 8E75HD had response out to 750 nm, that's where the 75 came from. Whether or not the currently avaialbe materials see that far out is a matter of conjecture and experimental verification.

I did shoot some holograms with a 780 nm diode laser, Spindler and Hoyer unit with lots of coherence, and it did work. Sensitivity was down by at least 10, maybe more, I don't have the log books in from of me. You can just about see this beam.

Based on the tests with the Spindler and Hoyer, we rented lab time at a place called Iowa laser Facility and shot some foot square HOE's for a device called a HOLODAR, which would look for wind turbulence by projecting a structured light pattern and catching it. We used a Watt of power out of a Ti:Sapphire laser, and the exposure time was up to a minute or two.

Yes, you can give it a shot, but be prepared for some real long exposure times. The image will be distorted, but will be there.
