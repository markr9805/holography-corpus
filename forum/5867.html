Sorry, I have to get in the habbit of mentioning parameters.

It is a 5x7.

Low AmDi for redder hologram

200 bloom Type B Bone

About 5 hours old

Double exposure 514 @ 250mw expanded to 10" for 20 seconds and 457 @ 20mw expaned same but beam coverage is smaller diameter (about 8 or 9 inches) for 25 seconds.

Processed with warm, not hot, not cold baths except last alcohol was hot.

Blew dry... hot and heavy.

O-ring 5 minute epoxy seal.

One thing I would like to mention with this technique that I have not mentioned before and it is the way I switch wavelengths. I set up the hologram with 514. Baffle everything and after the spatial filter I have a rectangular aperture. I expose. Then after the shutter closes I put a large black blocker after the aperture. I then open the shutter and tune to 457 while looking at the light coming through the square aperature and hitting the black blocker until it is even and centered. There is no light hitting the plate at this point. I then close the shutter and remove the blocker and do the second exposure. I have a web cam viewing the plateholder so I can see the light hitting the plate real time. It is connected to the same computer that controls the exposure time (opening and closing of shutter).This has saved me quite a few times when a blocker was left in the path ot the shutter didn't open or whatever.

I actually another one after this one that is a little better but has a 3 or 4 mm pit off to the right of his formost knee. Although it is not in the actual image I don't like it. I made 2 prior to this one that were not as good and exposure and processing had to be modified.

The fruit bowl idea is good. But remember the wavelengths are only 57nm apart so that is the maximum differential between replay wavelengths I can get right now. So the blueberry would have to be a lime or something like that with an apple.

I always wanted to get some UV optics for my laser so I could expose in UV like the 351 line in addition to the 457 and 514. Then when I process for red shift I could get blue (from UV), green (from blue), yellow and orange-red (from Green). But a few unknowns would have to be ironed out. First the UV optics for the laser would have to be able to be change in the middle of the session (not a problem with the technique I am already using). Then the transfer optics adn spatial filter outside the laser would need to be able to be used with UV and Visible (I think very possible but not with the optics I have now). Finally, the biggest hurdle is finding a material (paint) that reflects UV but no visible lines. Maybe some type of black with an added UV reflective material (not florescent).

All in all very possible I think with some funding.

A different approach would be to expose in blue and green then repaint the object for blue and implement a swelling technique for the last exposure. Then process for a shift to red but the preswelled region would come out blue for red green and blue colors.

I still have not tried the vacuum technique for altering final fringe size resulting in a color shift. That may be another solution.

One thing I have not tried and may be able to give me the third color (blue) is;

Paint the object as I do for blue and green to swell to green and orange. Remember that prior to shift I use Cobalt Blue for blue alone, Cadmium Yellow for Green alone and Titanium White for reflections of both. Expose both wavelengths 457 andn 514.
