Colin Kaminski Tue Apr 21, 2009 1:44 pm #3
In food science we lower the pH as a preservative. I don't know if it is possible to lower the pH to 4.2 or better 4.0 for storage then raise it back for use. Sodium Benzoate could also work if it did not interfere later.

Outside of preservatives, all mold needs is a single cell to form. Making sure your gelatin is sterile before storing would go a long way toward preserving it. Heating and covering it before storage may be enough to work.

Pasteurization is another process that might help. I have not had to use it but the concept was explained to me as a certain amount of time and a certain heat is 1 pasteurization unit. For each food product a number is created by experimentation and then any way of achieving that PU is equivalent. I know it is common for beer to be heated and cooled a few times to achieve the required PUs. The temperatures and times are closely held secrets. Just with my experience with the product the temperatures have to be within 140 to 165F. Budweiser heats the entire sealed package and cools it. This could be done in a canning jar.

Purging the head space of the storage with CO2 could also help.
