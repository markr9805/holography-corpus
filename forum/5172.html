Ed Wesly Mon Nov 07, 2005 10:00 pm #2
> Does someone ever tried it and so can we do holograms with it (i think no cause the numbers of dot/mm seems to be inapppropriate, or is it only to take pictures of already made holograms?

The latter is what the Kodak brochure is touting. It really does have more deep red response than their other black and white products, which helps in the usual low illumination of the laser.

I have made low spatial frequency diffraction gratings on this film with about a 10 degrees intrabeam angle. I ran a wide range of exposures, developed in the Phyrochrome process, but the gratings weren't anywhere near as bright as on Holotest film.

The biggest problem in making or photographing holograms on Tech Pan is in finding some! The Great Yellow Father discontinued it last year, and I haven't been able to find any from the usual local suspects! If you've got a source of 150 foot bulk rolls, tell me about it!
