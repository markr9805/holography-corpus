Dinesh Sat Mar 12, 2005 3:56 pm #13
You're right. You did say, "high quality" not "bright". In that sense, I misread it. 03 does have a "higher quality" insofar as clarity is concerned. The problem is that holographic parameters are so notoriously subjective that I'm afraid everytime I hear, "it's bright" or even "...incredibly bright" I wonder what's actually meant. Most pics of holograms have nothing else in the picture, sometimes not even the edges of the film/glass. So what's "bright" being compared to - a white wall in direct sunlight? The actual object under the same illumination as the hologram? I feel that holographers tend to see display (or pictorial as Bob says - I'm beginning to like the word!) holograms as a "technical" thing because of it's roots and associations - Gabor, Leith/Upatniks, Kogelnik etc whereas they're really not. They're objects being illuminated. True the object is being created by a diffractive ("technical") process, but the image of the hologram is no different than the image of an object projected by a curved mirror a la the Disney ghosts. In that sense, words like "effficency", "bright" or even "colour" don't have the same "force" as "clarity", "sharpness", "hue" etc. I suppose I've become oversensitive to The Names misuse of terms and limited understanding of the subject. Because they're Names, their views are always "correct" and someone who's not a Name gets ignored. So "zero order tables" is an accepted name because a Name (I won't say who) doesn't understand that "zero order" referes to a mathematical term in the diffractive series - not a table geometry. Or that you 'have to' shoot at "Brewster's angle" (whatever that is) otherwise you won't get a hologram. Or that a ttransmission hologram is "thin" and a reflection hologram is "thick" and that one is seen with reflected light and the other with transmitted light.
