MichaelH Tue Oct 26, 2004 4:44 pm #4
I've used broken light bulbs, both the white frosted kind and the kind that is etched, bits of milk jug plastic, bits of reflector bulb. Basically anything that will transmit light diffusely or reflect it.

While they all reduce the light level, they also provide a bit of spatial filtering which can be a very good thing when you can quite get all the grit out of a microscope objective.

You can also get some interesting lighting effects by experimenting with various materials that transmit or reflect light.

This hologram used broken light bulb for illumination that resulted in a speckly effect that was very pleasing.

Kaveh Wed Oct 27, 2004 10:57 am #9
> wler: Does theimage come out with less grain, or looks better in some other way?I seek to improve the quality of my H2 masters.

I don't think you will get less grain.

Main advantage is the same as photographers get when using diffusers, e.g. umbrella type one. You get softer shadows, for example.

Also, if you have a strong specular reflection off a shiny object, if you don't use a diffuser it will produce a hot spot on the holographic plate. A diffusor will reduce that.

Make sure it is very stable.

MichaelH Wed Oct 27, 2004 11:49 am #12
> wler: Does the image come out with less grain, or looks better in some other way? I seek to improve the quality of my H2 masters.

The results depend entirely on the diffusor you use. You should experiment with several types and choose the one that looks best to you.

You don't necessarily get less "grain" with something that fully diffuses the light but it will be less noticeable.

The most often given reason for using a diffusor is as Kaveh says, to soften the shadows and hilights on an object but that can also be summed up by saying that the subject "looks better".

As you are no doubt thinking, that's subjective and should be driven by the effect you're trying to achieve.

Look around the house for things that either reflect or transmit light diffusely and put them into a beam. What doesn't look good with one subject may be just what you need for some other.

Dinesh Sun Oct 31, 2004 2:33 pm #15
[quote:13e60f7f42]Is the diffuser decrease coherence length? [/quote:13e60f7f42]

There is a small decease, very small, if you're using a standard diffusion screen.

Basically, the fringe contrast is dependant on the path length difference, which in turn affects the phase difference. If the path length difference is d, then the phase difference relationship should be

2pid/lambda <= pi

where d is the path difference

As it goes through the diffuser, the phase shifts by (2(pi)(n-1)*t)/(lambda) where n is the index of the diffusion glass and t it's thickness. If n or t is large the phase shift may matter, but usually it isn't.

I bring this up because we use multiple layers of different types of diffusers to give certain effects. In our case it may matter when I use a HeNe with limited coherence. In most cases of using a standard diffuser setup, the diffuser has negligible thickness.
