Dinesh Mon Feb 14, 2005 10:40 pm #22
At an emulsion thickness of 80 to 100 microns, it may just be too narrow band to see in white light. This makes sense when you say you see it in laser light but not white light. Try a LED. These sort of emulsion depths are usually used for technical holograms or memories, I've never heard of a display piece this deep.

For those not aware of it, bandwidth, ie the speread of colours that can diffract off the hologram, is dependant on emulsion thickness. Most display emulsions are of the order of 5 - 15 microns giving a 50nm odd bandwidth (since the entire band of visible light is from 400-700, a 300 nm bandwidth, you're diffracting about 1/6th or approx 20% of the white light). If the bandwidth goes much below 20 microns, there's too little diffracting to see anything in white light. If you like, the smaller the amount of the spectrum that's diffracting, the less the amount of light is coming off the hologram. In my HOE days, I used to get approx 20nm, 10 if I tried hard, with a 40 micron emulsion, which was almost impossible to see in white light. At twice the emulsion depth, the bandwidth could easily be les than 10 nm.
