MichaelH Sun Apr 01, 2007 12:52 pm #1
During the skype conference yesterday I posed the following question to the largely DCG-oriented group.

> Why do so many DCG users focus on single-beam holography?

The primary answer was split-beam setups take longer exposures.

> Why not master in silver and copy to DCG?

to which the general group replied in a way that could be distilled down to 'silver is too complex, with DCG you just use alcohol and water'

I've been thinking about it since and come to the conclusion that, like so many things in holography (laser used, chemicals, lab environment, final color output), it's all relative and dependent on the individuals need and comfort level.

I haven't made my own emulsions yet (although I did cook some practice gelatin) because I can buy a reliable emulsion off the shelf and haven't been able to spend more time perfecting my own recipe because that would cost me time actually making holograms.

Chemicals for silver are easy to come by and no more difficult to use than alcohol and water, really. You mix the chemicals once and then can easily get 10-20 holograms out of a batch, depending on how many you make in any given session. You also don't have to worry about temperature during processing (though heat helps speed the process up).

It comes down to need and comfort in the end.

And time.

And money.

Oh, and peeps. We all need peeps this time of year.

What would take to get you out of your comfort zone? What do you need in order to try something new that other people "know" would make a difference in how your holograms turn out?

For me it would really be nice to have another couple of days off in the week where I can do whatever I want, all day long with no place I have to be or anyone I have to interact with. Just another day or two to spend all day long in the lab. I'd set aside at least one of these days for pure experimentation without a need to produce anything. Just spend time making attempts at whatever with only the results (success or failure) to show for it.

Finally- someone talking sense!

I've also wondered why people don't master using silver halides, make H2 copies on silver halides, and then "contact print" onto DCG. I know that noise is often sited as the reason, but surely with availability of low noise emulsions from "colour holographics" and "ultimate", noise can be kept low? if the noise levels are still too high, then there are any number of papers describing "silver halide sensitized gelatine holograms" which yield low noise results from silver halide emulsions.

For holography to make inroads into the average household, it will need to overcome two main problems- color & lighting. Putting aside issues to do with lighting, I think the lack of sensitivity of 'straight' DCG to red wavelengths can be alleviated somewhat by using Silver Halide masters & DCG contact prints.

JohnFP Mon Apr 02, 2007 11:09 am #6
Well, when I first got into DCG I only did SBR because it was the easiest way to elliminate all variables except DCG emulsion fabrication and processing. And who even said DCG is easier to process is mistaking. Yes, you only fix, water and alcohol but I a not sure how that is easier then dev., rinse and bleach for a silver.

Also, even though I have lots of power, I have to agree I liked the fact of stability in a SBR set up elliminating the need for long exposures with a split beam. In fact if you look at some of the illustrations on my web site you will see some very novel ways to do "split beam" holograms using a single beam. I have very rarely lost a hologram due to movement.

Another point I may want to add is the fact that if you do silver and DCG you need twice as many chemicals, trays, processing techniques etc... It's a little easy for the strictly silver guys to express that silver is easy enought to do in conjunction with DCG but having twice the wet processing is MORE.

Finally, the main reason I do mostly SBR DCG is because it is cheap. Silver halide plates are expensive. The film is a little time consuming to work with in that you need to index match it between the glass, not to mention another chemical needed.

I think to sum it all up, it has mostly to do with what you want. If you want a non image planed but super bright hologram, SBR DCG is the way to go. I have found it a little difficult to produce contact copy reflection DCG's from SBR DCG's due to geometry, back reflection from master plate and the need for strick color control of the reflection master coupled with the fact that if the environment changes too much an uncoved reflection DCG master can shift wavelengths etc...

Now, if you want an image planed DCG then silver to DCG is the way to go. I have produced a few silver to DCG and not only can you get a brighter, almost blinding DCG hologram but it is easier then trying to control a DCG reflection master for use in a proximity geometry. I like it.
