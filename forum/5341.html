Tom B. Sun Feb 12, 2006 7:49 am #14
I don't understand the reason, but I have noticed that the apparent depth of reflection holograms increases with their size. I think it's something like the intrinsic angular resolution being unchanged, but when spread over a larger area, the effective blur-free depth increases - or something like that. There was an earlier discussion here which came to a similar inconclusive conclusion but there was general agreement that big reflection holograms seem to have more useful depth.
Colin Kaminski Sun Feb 12, 2006 9:10 pm #15
Also, I should remind the newer holographers that processing of reflection holograms greatly effects the usable depth. If you use a broadband processing scheme you get a brighter, whiter hologram but with less depth. With narrow band you get a more intense color and more depth but less brightness.
