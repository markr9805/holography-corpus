Hi all,

After a forced long time of inactivity, last night I put hands in my chemicals box and I prepared 5 MBDCG plates.

Now, I do have a question concerning red sensitive DCG homebrew plates.

Reading the original paper of prof. Blyth, I found that emulsion sensitivity is dramatically influenced by gel moisture. It seems that the best results are achieved around 66% of Relative Humidity, while below 33% RH plates becomes unusable.

Is this a one way process or dried plates (RH below 33%) may be brought back to working conditions just rising the relative humidity?

Thanks,

Cristiano, IT

Yes of course they can become resensitized by higher humidity Cristiano,

it puzzles me on what basis you could have thought otherwise.

Keeping unexposed plates very dry is a good way to extend their effective lifetimes which are much longer than standard dichromate plates anyway.

Once you have rehumidified them it is a good idea to lock that moisture level up in a glass sandwich and allow a good settling time before shooting.

Jeff

Is this not the case? Does old dried out and remoisturized gelatin lose water more quickly then fresh plates?

The reason I ask is I am thinking of the negatives of pulling off the glass cover after exposure and prior to processing an wet and thus softened gelatin.

Yes that is a very good point John, but the thing about the MBDCG system is that it is more moist and has an appreciable bit of the amine TMG which also acts as a humectant. This makes it less difficult to remove a cover plate. From your stand point it would be better to use a very thin

clear isotropic plastic film, like the covering on a newly opened pot of jam!
