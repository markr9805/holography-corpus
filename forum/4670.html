Ok, don't make fun of my work. I have had some problems with this batch but worked out most and still learned a lot. I found another trick that enabled me to take a blotchy DCG and elliminate with a second processing. Just something I tried and worked. I am sure my emulsion is thinner then it has ever been because I was in a warm room, heated the plates, used a low gelatin concentration and spun. I am beging to believe that if you go thin, variation in thickness of the emulsion is more of a factor to deal with then thick. In other words. If you have a 1 micron difference in emulsion thickness with a 10 micron film thickness it behaves worst then if you had a 1 micron difference in a 40 micron film. I will test this thought though. Also, I ran out of 5 x7's and this is a 4 x 5 plate.

This hologram was painted with the paints that reflect the two wavelengths 514 and 457. As seen in the large fish the top and near the tail was painted with the 457 paint from my paint tests. This paint reflects the highest amount of 457 while absorbing the most 514. These parts came out light blue to green. The orange parts of the body were painted with a paint that reflects most 514 while absorbing most 457. It is actually a yellow paint. Then the parts of the large fish that are colored yellowish/white was left the white color of the dried modeling clay.

I am pleased of the color result and cannot wait to make another batch of plates. I would still like to do more testing with exposure ratios of 514 and 457.
