I was talking to Criss with Skype and I told him how easy it was to make a simple table for DCG by using a single inner tube and a concrete paver to overcome a "bad" location for holography. The simplicity lies in the fact that you can take your object and lay it down and lay the DCG plate right on it. Single Beam Reflection holograms are very bright in DCG and I believe much brighter then Silver Halide Single Beam Reflection holograms. Or at least for me.

But my point is, after reviewing what I had talked about, I realized I had tried a single inner tube with an 18 inch (.5 meter) cinder block and it was hard to keep the table balanced. Especially if you mount your laser off the table.

SOOOO.... I suggest four smaller tractor tire tubes or the same, under the single .5 meter concrete paver.

I am putting together a POV-Ray drawing of the simple .5meter^2 table with four inner tubes and the Single Beam Reflection set up with the object laying on it's back.

I think if beginner understands how bright a Single Beam Reflection hologram is in DCG and how stable a simple 4 inner tube-1 concrete paver set up is...coupled with the fact that the object can lie on it's back and the plate rest on the object that beginners will surely elliminate expensive table contructions, ellaborate set-ups and will enjoy very bright holograms. AND the best part is, this set up allow long exposure for low power lasers or MBDCG.

JohnFP Thu Mar 16, 2006 4:23 pm #3
I found when I tried to ballance a walking stone (paver) on a single tube, no matter how flat I made the tube the paver would move very much if I did anything on the paver, like load a plate. If your laser (and spatial filter or lens) is mounted off the table then this becomes a problem with keeping the center of the beam in the center of the plate.

If your laser is on the paver also, then this is not too much of a problem as everything moves together.
