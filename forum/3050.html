Tony Thu Jan 20, 2005 11:41 pm #2
MichaelH has done it, using lamination to stick film to glass. I'm sure he'll reply...

I've pretty much settled on a dry glass-film-glass sandwich. There are some secrets to this technique. If you don't want the clamps to cast shadows on the film, your tendency is to position them outside the area of the film. However, this makes the glass bend away from the film in the middle. What I've done to solve the problem is put a couple of layers of translucent tape around the film area on one of the pieces of glass. The thickness of the tape should be as close to the thickness of the film as you can get. This way the clamps don't bow the glass.

I've also found the only place where it really matters to use glass plates instead of film is for H1 masters. For H2 copies, film seems nearly as good as glass plates, if you do a good job of sqeegeeing & drying. Glass H1s are definitely cleaner, lower noise than film H1s.
Dinesh Fri Jan 21, 2005 12:36 am #3
One disadvantage of film is woodgrain, unless you orientate it properly. I've used film for H1's, I used to make 6"x8" H1's with film. However, because the plastic is usually birefringent, you get a lot of multiple reflections inside the flm. If the film is not orientated exactly vertically or horizontally the woodgrain becomes horrendous!

-----------------------

Has anyone here used a material such as [https://www.kapco.com/kapcographics/mou ... sives.html](https://www.kapco.com/kapcographics/mounting_adhesives.html) or [http://www.flexcon.com/ap_perf_bond_graphic.asp](http://www.flexcon.com/ap_perf_bond_graphic.asp) ?

Apparently Bernadette at Laser Reflections has been using the Flexmount recently to laminate film to glass for masters (which is where I got this tip).

I've tried a few tests with the Kapco material (it's cheaper) and decided to buy a roll for longer term testing.

So far I like a lot. It goes on easy with a roller and I allow 12 hrs curing between steps (put the laminate on the glass, sit for 12 hours, put the film on the laminate, sit for 12 hrs) to get the best adhesion.

Once it's on, I can only tell it's there near the edges where I don't get perfect film/laminate overlap. I don't have to deal with the multiple reflections from sandwiching the film between two glass plates. This also means that I can use glass for masters (thus ensuring that the master goes in the plateholder the same way every time) but purchase the cheaper film.
BobH Fri Jan 21, 2005 2:57 am #6
Mounting film to glass as the first step would definately be my proceedure of choice, if I were to use film-based materials. I'd do it under a HEPA filter, and with a laminator to prevent bubbles. Holograms love to be flat, from box to frame. Definately the way to go (in my opinion), and worth the time and expense of the proper equipment and its learning curve.

I could not easily get hold of any 'Lamp Oil' so I tried using veg oil (as used in cooking). I put 3 drips on the glass, place the film on, then 3 more drips on top of the film, then place the other piece of glass on top, then clamp.

With only using 3 drips per side, when clamped, the oil only spreads half an inch beyond the film all the way around (film size is only 1 inch by 1 inch).

Seems to have stopped the wood grain problem. - Not sure what others may think of using cooking oil???
