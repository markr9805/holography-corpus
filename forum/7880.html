wler Wed Jun 24, 2009 5:16 am #9
Mfa,Sergio,

one can do 40,50mW single mode even without an ECDL configuration, this is what I meant. With an extra grating one can go up much higher, beyond 100mW, with a cheap $15 DVD diode.

There is of course a difference how one buys. Buying all parts for full list price is always very costly, for example the grating, but with some luck one can get eg a small grating for $25 on ebay, so a ECDL setup can be done for less than $100 (not counting the driver). Of course time is a factor here, as these things don't show up too often. Unfortunately the seller of those gratings doesn't seem to exist any more.

BTW just tonight I finished a first test round with the celebrated Opnext HL6385 140mW/642nm single mode diodes, with disappointing results. See my site. Next I will try them in an ECDL configuration, I hope they will justify their price...

Best,

Wolfgang

I have an Ondax red diode - yes expensive - but while a controller manufacturer was debugging their specs - they told me the problem was the BlueSky diode I had selected - so I bought the most stable I could find. Then when the Ondax claimed linewidth specs were not reached with the controller, it became obvious that the controller temperature wasn't sensitive enough! and when they replaced the TEC unit with the newest spec I ended up with a very stable system! But not circular beam. BlueSky has that, and probably good for holography also (my first choice but I did not have the patience to retry with that diode after I got a rock solid system).

I agree with Colin - building the controller from scratch (which I have also done) is not that easy or reliable (more importantly

But at some point maybe we as a group could put the specs that do work on the table and make holographic DIY diode controller blueprint. Has to have diode with a 'wide' temperature stability range (i.e. no mode hop or drift) to start with, though. And that is not at all well documented - which are trash and which are gold.

Then the choice of TEC etc.

If someone can find a source for holographic blue diodes with some power that aren't too expensive (< $1000) I will document the components that work for DIY using off the shelf controller components. But right now the blue visible diodes that are stable and low noise cost too much - or you have to burn up lots of $$ doing trial and error testing of which are suitable...
