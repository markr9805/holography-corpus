I was curious why those high power DPSS that only have linewidths of .1nm cannot be made to have longer coherence lengths. I looked at Sam page and saw an illustration of a DPSS laser and it seems to have a large enough oscillator to squeeze in an etalon. It the cavity non accessible on DPSS lasers?

Then I did a little digging and found a bunch of links concerning narrowing the linewidth of lasers with an external cavity.

[http://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/e ... 261t.alice](http://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/els/00304018/2003/00000218/00000001/art01175;jsessionid=108rdfsef261t.alice)

But all the papers are for sale. Does anyone know of this process?

I guess it would be very advantageous for holographers if we could find a way to use those very inexpensive DPSS green lasers found on E-bay for very cheap but only have a linewidth of .1nm.

Any thoughts or discussions?

Hans Fri Jul 28, 2006 1:57 pm #3
The problems with some of the cheap DPSS lasers is that they use a hybrid crystal. These are crystals where the nd:YVO4 crystal is glued to the KTP crystal. The facets of the crystals are coated like mirrors. So, if you have a laser where the crystals are separated and the mirrors are not the facets of the crystals, I guess it should be fairly easy.

Ron Michael Fri Jul 28, 2006 8:45 pm #7
Unless you can get etalons cheap... they are generally expensive as they require high precision figure of lambda/10 and 10/5 scratch/dig and require parallelism of a few arc seconds not arc minutes or degrees.

Additionally high reflectivity is needed to give a high finese inorder to effectively mode select. Likewise Fabry perot resonators make good discriminators for observing modes in lasers

No comment on the paper I gave and it's technique so I assume you guys already have read it, but it is a cheap way of making a slm tem00 laser using just a few cheap parts available from vendors like casix and a cheap bk7 plate plus a moderate expensive 1.2W fiber coupled diode pump and collimator. Depending on your technique even that can be done away with a well focused free space propagated diode pump. The point of papers like that is if you take advantage of short resonators and microchip parts and some simple but effective gain modulation techniques as they and you have suggested, it's easy to get to slm mode lasers.
