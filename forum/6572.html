jeff-blyth Fri Dec 07, 2007 1:52 am #7
> Hans: I thought that it is the fix bath that you cannot use on one of the bleach methods. You can fix with a dichromate reversal bleach, but you cannot with a re-halogenating bleach because you want the developed silver to move over to the virgin (undeveloped) AgBr grains.

I am afraid this is not right. If you use FIX after development then that means that you have dissolved away the undeveloped virgin silver bromide. Then

if you carry on and use reversal bleach then that means you use the dichromate to oxidize the developed silver metal and dissolve it up in solution as the salt

silver dichromate which is slightly soluble. You are then left in the emulsion with no silver, no silver halide and no hologram!

Now in the case of rehalogenating bleach if you use fix before that bleach is used you get a poor scattery shorter wavelength hologram for reasons explained in the first part here:

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... ing_Bleach](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Rehalogenating_Bleach)

However that bleach is deliberately designed to have some "carry-over" ability to enable the newly formed AgBr to sit down on the virgin AgBr grains as you said

Hans and of course there would no longer be any virgin AgBr because it has all been removed by the fix.

However it is important to keep in mind that if your bleach doesnt have any "carry over" power it then won't have any inclination to cause newly formed AgBr

to wander away from the exact place where it was formed from the developed silver metal speck and cause scatter, i.e. it will then sit down right on that speck until all that metal speck is converted to AgBr.

This is exactly what happens with the iodine bleach used here under the section headed "A good bleach for transmission holograms"

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... ing_Bleach](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Rehalogenating_Bleach)

If you use this bleach for reflection (Denisyuk) holograms then at least you will not get scatter and you may well get say a nice green image made with a red laser. -----But you will certainly get less diffraction efficiency because you have lost a lot of your AgBr in the fix. However it may look bright enough compared to

say a red hologram because our eyes are more green sensitive.

Jeff

This kind of post-treatment has been introduced to get a better signal-noise ratio on reflection holograms on the not-so-fine-grained silver halide materials like Agfa's 8E75.

Basically, it adapts “Russian” technology (colloidal development) to “Western” materials. By post-exposing/processing the final hologram a thin absorption filter of colloidal silver is being formed within the emulsion. By the way, it only uses a tiny part of the remaining silver halide grains within the layer. It mainly suppresses UV-blue light. That part of the spectrum becomes strongly scattered by larger silver halide grains.

On the one hand the colloidal silver layer reduces noise but on the other hand, since it's absorptive, it decreases the image brightness. This might be particularly pronounced with blue images and least with red images.

To some extent the colloidal layer may slow down the printout effect.

One might wonder how important that post-treatment method remains. Finer grained emulsions have become available in the meantime. Different bleaching method have been introduced. And with the arrival of high power LEDs, much better reconstruction lights can be found.
