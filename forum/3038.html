I'm going to try doing some multiple-exposure holograms. I was wondering if anyone has any recommendations about total exposure. Should I adjust the time of each exposure to target the usual density for the type of hologram (1 for transmission, 2 for reflection)? Or should I let the total density go higher? I'm sure I'll have to do a bunch of experimentation anyway, but it would be nice to have a starting point...
Dinesh Mon Jan 17, 2005 1:05 am #2
The more important parameter is exposure, as opposed to the density of development. Basically, you don't want to over-expose beyond the reciprocity limit. There are two situations: double exposure with no swelling or other treatment between exposures and double exposure with treatment. The first case may be because you have 2 H1's you want on the same H2 and the second because you're doing pseudo-colour.

If I'm just shooting two exposures without pretreatment, I use an exposure just short of my normal exposure for the first and one just a bit shorter still for the second. So, if I'd normally shoot a plate for 20 seconds, I'd shoot the first at about 18 and the second at about 15. Subsequent exposures go down in a sort of exponential drop. This has also got to do with spatial frequencies, which has to do with reference angles. Usually in Silver Halide it's not an issue. However, the sensitivity of most emulsions falls off as the spatial frequency incrases. So a shot with a 40 degree ref would have a different exposure to one with a 70 degree ref. But, the response of most Silver emulsions is pretty flat from about 20 to about 80, so it's not really an issue. It is an issue (a major one!) with resist.

If I shoot an exposure, then TEA it, then shoot a second at the same reference, I generally shoot the first as above, but cut down a little more for my second. The reason is that TEA sensitises the plate and the "normal" exposure would have been shorter and so the 2nd exposure needs to be shorter still. In this case, I'd shoot one at 18 seconds, TEA it (let's say a nominal 2% for about 30 seconds for red - approx yellow green), then shoot the second for abbout 15.

Another factor you need to be careful of is ratio. This is important enough in single shot holograms, but becomes even more important in double exposure. The reason is that you're recording two sets of fringes that are supposed to be independant. However, the fringes cannot really be independant since they're both in the same emulsion. They need to be good neighbours. If the fringes are fat and square-wave, they'll interfere with each at the "cross-over" points and you'll get noise. This is due to the two fringes causing a third, low frequency, fringe and creating a sort of "beat" frequency that forms a halo around your image. If the fringes are clean and sinusoidal then you minimise this. The important thing for the fringe profile is a good ratio.

This is kind of general and needs tuning for your particular film. The above worked well on Agfa, but needed tweaking when I started on Slavich VRP.
