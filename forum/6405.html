I just wanted to share a couple of observations, having done my first four exposures using "regular" DCG.

First, TSP works quite well to clean plates prior to coating, I didn't have gelatin lift off. I just added 1/2 cup TSP to each gallon of hot water, and made a pool in the bottom of the sink (a plastic laundry tub). Then I put the glass underneath, and scrubbed it with a sponge. Then I let it set there for another 15 minutes, and scrubbed again. Then I drained the tub and rinsed the glass. It was nice and squeaky afterwards. Thanks, Dave, for the tip.

Also, optical hardening works very well instead of fixer. (I tried fixer first for comparison.) I took a regular 100-watt frosted bulb in a reflector, and placed it 6 inches away from the plate. In my case, I did this for 15 seconds.

I'm spoiled having so much blue light available -- holograms were easily made each time, but coating and processing errors made them look pretty awful.

Joe Farina Tue Aug 21, 2007 12:23 pm #3
> dave battin: what are your current methods and processes?

Dave, I'm just using basic procedures to get my feet wet. Low dichromate concentration (to avoid blue shifting, since I'm exposing at 458nm, I can't have a lot of shrinkage, or it will vanish into the UV), Mayer bar coating under a homemade laminar flow, single-beam Denisyuk.

I've been testing the alcohol sequence to see what happens with different combinations. Also how the shelf life holds up for coated plates, and emulsion in the refrigerator. I also want to see how much the plates should be aged before exposure, and see how post-exposure dark reaction helps.

As for the non-uniformity of the finished hologram, I want to try a cover-plate under the water and alcohol baths (using a piece of Lexan cut to the same size as my plates, which is 4 X 5). Also, John had mentioned that prolonged stirring when making the emulsion (45 minutes) helped to improve the quality of the emulsion (this was also the reason I wondered if dichromate should be added to a little water first, although I think John was under the impression that the prolonged stirring caused the gelatin to mix better, not the dichromate). I only filtered once through a coffee filter, maybe I should try going through 3 filters. When the plate is coated, I poured from a small beaker, and did a poor job. I'm looking for a better way to apply emulsion to the glass (just before using the bar). I saw a syrup dispenser the other day that looked interesting, I may try that.

The good news is that my homemade laminar flow seems to be doing its job. Also, the optical hardening method is much more convenient than fixer. I hope this can also be used with panchromatic MBDCG.

The two decent holograms I made turned out green, so there was a fair amount of expansion. When I tried to make a broadband hologram, I went too far, and got opaque white patches on the plate. Just need to keep testing and keep good notes.
