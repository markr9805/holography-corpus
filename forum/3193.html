JohnFP Tue Mar 08, 2005 12:40 pm #1
For extra stability and to reduce resonance of optical components for long exposures, one can use beeswax or modeling clay. Press the modeling clay into any joined pieces. As in around the base of the optic where the base meets the table. Or between the base and the pole or pole and optic holder etc... This should not be used alone to secure.
MichaelH Tue Mar 08, 2005 1:47 pm #2
You can also use the material that's sold for attaching posters to walls as your dampening helper. It's often sold under the name Blu-Tak (don't get the yellow stuff, it's greasy and too sticky).

I prefer this over clay as it doesn't leave any residue behind.

You can also attach blobs of it to the tops of mounts to prevent them from ringing.
Tony Tue Mar 08, 2005 2:02 pm #3
I like to use a hot melt glue gun to secure mirrors and optics. It can be removed and cleaned up easily.

BobH Tue Mar 08, 2005 4:27 pm #5
Although all these techniques probably work well, I increase stability of components by attaching a second post (making a tripod) with a dab of 5-minute epoxy. It's cleaner than hot melt (no strings, wires or drooling of the gun), and wax (no residue on components or hands), and it picks off easily. The important thing is to do something, because most components pick up vibrations too easily on their own!

The best place I found for dampening is at the points where the plate meets the plate holder. I can instantly tell from the "tink" or "thunk" that the additional beeswax or whatever is working and adding dampening.
