After looking at this [post in the gallery](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2148) about Densiyuks that reconstruct out towards the viewer, I borrowed a cheesy Pharaoh lamp from a friend here which is an inward embossed image that's lit from underneith. The effect is that it looks like it is coming out at you. I thought it would be a perfect subject to use for the style of Denisyuk that LarryD made.

I found a site which sells a line of them, although they are painted (the one I'm borrowing is white [well, almost white] unpainted casting):

[http://www.coolstuffcheap.com/3dimmaklam.html](http://www.coolstuffcheap.com/3dimmaklam.html)

They have a hole coming from the back where a small lamp fixture comes through. I'm already thinking of a suplimentary lighting idea using a split beam which comes through that hole from the back and hits a white piece of paper or something to diffuse and create a lighting effect from below.

I wonder if anyone else might have run across similar objects worthly of this type of holography?

BobH Thu Apr 07, 2005 10:39 pm #2
One of my first pieces was of a plaster mould of a friend's face. Instead of making it a Denisyuk type and reconstructing the pseudoscopic image, I made it an Image-planed Reflection type and simply reconstructed the orthoscopic image. I lit it only from below to cast the appropriate shadows inside, and from an angle that suggested overhead lighting at about the same angle as that planned for reconstructing the final image.

The result had Nick Phillips (at the '85 Lake Forest Conference) swearing the image was projecting forward! I get that from about half of the people that see the image.

edelbrp Sun Apr 10, 2005 2:09 am #7
First attempt seems to have failed. :'( Likely because I didn't settle long enough. I'm not a night person and it stays light so late these days! I was worried the extra daylight scatter from around the doors would ruin the film during settling so I reduced settling to 5 min.

I also switched to D-76 as the developer (1:1 deluted), but still using the bleach which came with my JD-4 kit. It didn't develop as darkly (about 50% after 13 minutes with often agitation), so I need to increase the exposure (I used 30 seconds, so a full minute or more should be good). The method of splitting the beam and sending the object beam through the hole which normally holds the light fixture (that I removed) seems to work great! It looks great in the laser light.

Lastly, this object seems to be made perfectly for 4x5 plates! It has a nice shelf to set the plate down on and 4x5 just covers the entire area. It's a big heavy casting done in something hard like concrete.

I think I'll try to be a little less ambitious and do a regular Denisyuk shot and then go from there.

-Phil

edelbrp Mon Apr 11, 2005 2:05 am #10
Follow up:

A day later the D-76 hologram dried out, and I can see part of the Pharaoh's ear (dimly, but the depth is an inch or so)! I know I didn't settle long enough, didn't use a remote shutter, didn't use the proper dilution, and underexposed. I was sure it was a loss. But, I can clearly see something now! Also, the emulsion is very, very clear. The emulsion isn't fogged at all, which is nice.

I also shot a new (identical setup) hologram tonight with a longer exposure (40 seconds), remote shutter, proper settling time (> 15 min) and developed with the last of my JD-4. I can see the whole thing, but it is dim (just a little brighter than the D-76 hologram) and the emulsion has strange milky fog patterns on it which I've seen before w/ JD-4. It may be because it isn't dry, so I continue to dry it as I type this but its been a few hours now.

Colin Kaminski Mon Apr 11, 2005 4:10 am #11
If you only have access to photographic supplies you can usually find D19. It works OK as a developer for most holographic films. I have used it for BB640 and PFG-01.

It has been my experience that brightness seems to be a factor of proper density and proper bleaching and post treatment; more than some magic formulation.
