I have a paper called "Application Note No. 21: Complete Fringe Stabilization Techniques" that was provided by a company in Ypsilanti, Michigan, called Data Optics.

I have a voluminous amount of literature on holography, collected over many years, but this 8-page paper is by far the most detailed, inclusive, and useful document I have regarding the problems of fringe instability in holography. They sell their own fringe-locking systems (and environmental enclosures), and as a result, published the above for marketing puroses. However, these people are definitely "in the know" regarding this subject. As a matter of fact, I think this company developed in close proximity to (and perhaps in association with?) the pioneering work in holography done by University of Michigan scientists in the 1960's. I was reading that new book on the history of holography (which is very wide-ranging by the way), and it seems that the Willow Run laboratories, where modern holography developed, is actually in Ypsilanti, rather than Ann Arbor, as I had previously thought. Does anyone know this for sure?

Anyway, I will be happy to email this paper to anyone who is interested.

JohnFP Wed Dec 12, 2007 12:21 pm #8
I just want to point out, that in this paper they use a single beam combiner (beam splitter) in such a place that it combines the object and reference beams. But note that this is an HOE reflection set up and that means that the intensity of each beam is very close to equal. Also this geometry lends itself to a single beam combiner for the interferomter, so you are only adding one optic that can cause negative movement in the original geometry. For most other table geometries, you will find you need to add a mirror to two to capture the light such that the finge locker optics are not in the hologram and the place where the captured light is caught is bright enough to use. Each additional optic external to the holographic geometry is additional points of failures being introduced. I am not trying to dicourage the use of a fring locker, just some food for thought.
