Tom B. Thu Jun 23, 2005 5:11 am #2
> Bill B: Lastly, when the development process was done, I noticed some slimy green stuff on one side of the plates that I wiped off before I actually thought about it. This of course is the coating (I hope). Should there be slimy green stuff after I develop the plate? Should I have left the slimy green stuff instead of wiping it off?

Yes, the green slime is the gelatin emulsion which contains the hologram. For PFG-03, it's very soft when wet, but I don't remember it being all that easy to wipe off, unless the rinse water was too hot? It's probably best to use a cold water rinse for this emulsion. Tap water is fine for initial experiments.

Anyway, leave the slime on the plate and carefully dry it without touching it, (Use a hairdryer if impatient) then look at it carefully tilting it at various angles in sunlight or a bright halogen lamp. You should at least see some rainbowish glints. When you get the right lighting angle (the same angle that the laser hit the plate at) you should see your hologram. If it's very dim, try looking at it in a dark room with your laser as the light source. I don't know what object you were using, but in general shiny white objects (like pearls) close to the plate work well.
Dinesh Fri Jun 24, 2005 5:18 pm #3
20 seconds in the developer seems a little fast. The fact that it bleaches in 60 - 90 seconds seems to indicate it's a little dark. If it goes very dark very fast it's overexposed. I'd reduce the exposure a bit so that it goes about 20% dark (light grey) in about 10 to 20 seconds. If you're using a white processing tray in a dark room, this would be about the point where you can j-u-u-st see the bottom of the tray through the plate. After that, keep developing until it goes about 50% to 60% dark in the next minute or so, at which point you won't see the tray through the plate and a safelight (assuming you're using one) will appear almost extinguished. Then wash about 2 mins, bleach should be about 1 minute and wash for a further 5 minutes. At this pont if you're in a hurry to be amazed (or disappointed) use a hair dryer, but gently. Set it on low heat and hit it froma distance of about 6 inches. If you're not in a hurry, soak the film in a solution of a few drops of photoflo (available from any photo-chemical supplier)/200mls, or about two drops of photofo in 2 fl oz of water, for about 30 seconds and let drip dry. If you're using plates, lean it on a paper towel. If you're using PFG-03 my experience is that it goes very dark very fast and you've pretty much got to take exposure on faith. In this case, cut up, or mask, different parts of the film and expose for different times with the same setup. Tap water or not tap water is one of those personal things. Some swear that you must use distilled and some use tap water anyway. I use tap water and get pretty good results, for what it's worth.

As for Liti, Paul told me that the intent of the film was simply to get a hologram, not necessarily (or even expectedly) a good one. The material needs long exposures and if you're in a noisy area, you may not be able to get anything because it needs to be stable for quite long periods. However, insofar as the material does make holograms, it seems to "work". The hologram may be disapointing (or even non-existant) and it may be nothing like what you expected, assuming you get anything at all. Is this a scam? Welll, it depends. If you were buying a car, expecting a Ferrari and ended up with a clapped-out, rusty piece of junk that barely got up your driveway in first, you might say it was. Or, you might be happy you got wheels at all. It's the difference between market-ese and reality. The liti material won't give you holograms with anywhere near decent quality, but it will give you holograms. Again, for what it's worth, we managed to get a weak grating when a bunch of experienced holographers tried it here in San Diego, but we were unable to get a hologram of an object. The Integraf kit will give you better holograms, but they're more process oriented. It's not just a case of shooting, as you're discovering, but also of correct mixing and developing. One factor that may need to be factored in: if you continue trying to make holograms the silver material (Integraf's) is the common material and will give very good results. The photopolymer material of Liti could be expensive and difficult to get hold of. You might try Martin and Sergio's material also ( [http://polygrama.com/Index.htm](http://polygrama.com/Index.htm) ). I've got pretty good results with it.

Make sure to be very careful during development. The cloudy spots could be trapped water and will dry overnight. Make sure to use only distilled water rinses before and after development. After bleaching it is OK to use tap water. Make sure that the tap water does not flow directly onto a PFG-03 plate or you can risk washing off the gelatin.

I wonder if some packages have been opened in red darkroom conditions rather than under green light - that would cause fogging.

I found that a plate that gives a good hologram will be quite dark after 20 seconds in the developer. The developer doesn't really have that long a lifetime once mixed - even before it goes all dark it may have lost its developing power.

This is for making reflection holograms with PFG-03M and JD-4 (sulphate) developer/bleach.

But note the "flotation method" (I think it's called) where you float a tray above the developer so as to exclude air from it. I remember Jeff mentioned it a while back. This extends the lifetime of mixed developer. I've used this method to keep developer good for 2 days.

I use CWC2/PBU and I find that I get best results when the plate goes about 20% dark in less than 30 seconds. This looks awfully dark in a darkroom, but I check by looking at the safelight (a 20W incandescent bulb) through the plate. If the safelight does not seem appreciably darkened, just a little darker, it's probably about 20% dark.
