Does any one know if there exists a double sided heat seal film? (Such as the film used for heat seal laminating film). I am looking for a type that is not like the normal pouches, but one that has the glue on two sides of the film.

MichaelH Sat Jan 05, 2008 12:11 pm #2
It's not heat sealed per-se but I've used a laminator to apply it and that works fine for permanently mounting PFG-01 onto glass. It's [3M 8141 laminating film](http://products3.3m.com/catalog/us/en001/graphicarts/scotchprint/node_0CLF7DQHLZbe/root_GST1T4S9TCgv/vroot_K9JT0N94GJge/gvel_FRZQNXMJSNgl/theme_us_scotchprint_3_0/command_AbcPageHandler/output_html). Its big advantage is there's no center carrier film. It's an adhesive with outer liners only. It's expensive and I've only done a few tests with sample material but it would work well if you need enough of it.

Another option is [Kapco opclr](https://www.kapco.com/cms/site/cd78d3343dd9b1f2/index.html). It does have a carrier which but I've used it for years on my PFG mounted holograms with no problems. Its advantage is that it's a bit cheaper and easier to work with.

Ive used epoxy to glue film to glass, it worked very well.

Put the epoxy on the glass put the film on it and put somthinh heavy on top so it stays together.

Then I put it in the oven for an hour or so at 70°C.

I did that with filmotec film.

I have 10 meter of the Kapco. But I did not have very good results with it. For me it does not always seem to adhere in a completely transparent way.

I have further tried:

- Warm gelatin. => Does not adhere well to film as it cools down.

- 6 different types of glue => Only glue made by Pritt seems to work very well. But only when temperature is just right for it.

- Index match with lamp oil => Works for exposures up to 20 seconds.

- Index match with lamp oil and sealed with tape on the sides => Works well for long exposures. But when I sensitize with Potassium Dichromate, the tape separates. Messy to re-index match.

A thermal hardening glue would be perfect for this purpose, because a laminator would allow the glue to spread between the film and glass and only then harden the glue. But I have no idea how to make or buy a thermal hardening glue.
