Joe Farina Wed Dec 26, 2007 1:09 pm #1
After Dave provided the tip about using soapy water, I was wondering what specific drying problems others have had, and what measures might be taken to resolve them.

I'm mainly thinking about removing the hologram from the final alcohol bath, but also each time the emulsion goes into/out of any liquid. Sometimes I see a very fine "dribble," and I think this is from being pulled from the last alcohol bath. I haven't tried the soapy water yet, so I wonder if it helps with this problem also.

There is a paper which describes another method (again, which I haven't tried). When the plate goes from one bath to another, some kind of flat plate (i.e., maybe glass or thick plastic) is pressed against the face of the emulsion, while the whole thing is under the liquid. Then this sandwich is lifted out, with the liquid still between the plates, then submerged into the next liquid, then opened and the cover plate removed. This way, there would be no "dripping" across the emulsion. However, at the very end, these authors use a rather odd procedure to dry the plates: after pulling the sandwich out of the 100% alcohol, they apply hot forced air to the sandwich itself, so that vapor escapes through the edges of the sandwich, and thus the emulsion is dried. Of course, at the very beginning, the emulsion is exposed to water or whatever in an irregular way, but this may not be of any significance. After telling Jeff about this, he confirmed that it works (at least a portion of it, as I'm not sure if he tried the "sandwich drying" part, or just used it for the earlier baths) and incorporated it into the G307 paper.

However, all this may be unneccesary if the soapy water method is equally reliable. Dave or Michael, have you seen drying marks?
dave battin Wed Dec 26, 2007 1:43 pm #2
The cover plate procedure doesn’t really make any sense to me, as the plate is still saturated from the previous bath and as soon as the cover is removed the fluid is still migrated out. The water marks you mention can be due the migration of alch into the emulsion. in your last water bath add a small amount of photo flow, enough to see the difference when you withdraw the plate from the water bath, without using any type of surfactant, there will be a beading of water on the films surface, now if you add a small amount of photo flow or soap, you will no longer see this beading on the films surface. Now when you place the plate into the first alch bath, the migration of alch bath will be a lot smoother. also when your last alch bath become water saturated it will show up as small dark lines in the drying film, these will go away with enought heat/blowing .....

MichaelH Thu Dec 27, 2007 5:08 pm #5
I've been using liquid dish soap and am happy with the results so far. I have a small bottle that I put about an inch worth in and then fill the rest with DI water do dilute it a fair amount. You'll have to find the dilution that works for you since I don't really "measure" the amount I'm using.

I fill up my rinse container and then put a squirt of the diluted soap into the water. If you put the soap in and then fill the rinse container you'll likely end up with bubble that will cause their own drying problems.
