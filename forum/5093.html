Ed Wesly Mon Oct 10, 2005 8:46 am #20
What got me involved in this thread was this statement: "An imaged plane H2 with part of the image in front of the plane is not a real image.There is no light actually there, only a divergent waveform." My experiment showed that the part of an image plane image in front of a holographic plate really is real!

Looking at the recording of your hologram #2 of the cat image, when the nose is in front of the plate, the wavefront forming the nose is converging on its way to its real focus, and thereafter divergent by the time it gets to the viewer's eyes. It happens to be converging as it passes the through holographic plate and interferes with the reference. After it converges to its focus, then it diverges.

If a groundglass were put in your set up before the plate holder were loaded at the position of the cat's nose there would be a real image focused there. After removing the groundglass, the plateholder is loaded with a plate, which is then exposed, processed, replaced and illuminated with the analogue of the reference beam, an image of the cat's nose would then be focused on the same piece of groundglass replaced where the image of the cat's nose were focused before. (The problem in seeing this is in the background of the reference beam if things are too close to the plate.) This means that the wavefront issuing from the hologram plate is convergent before divergent, as the holographic wavefront is the optical equivalent of the original one.

In an image planing system where the image straddles the holographic plate, the wavefronts of the image from the H1, mirror, or lens are either converging, or already converged and are now diverging, or are at their focus by the time they meet the holographic plate's plane. Illumination during replay will result in them forming virtual or real images. The stuff behind is virtual. The stuff in front is real. They will all be ortho or pseudo simultaneously, ie an ortho real in front with an ortho virtual behind, which flips into a pseudo real in front and a pseudo virtual behind.

All wavefronts by the time they get to the viewers' eyes will be divergent, but some, the ones apparently in front of the plate, will converge to a real focus that can be projected on to a groundglass before they diverge. This refutes your statement above, which is what needed to be straightened out.
