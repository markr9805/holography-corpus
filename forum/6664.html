When starting out in holography you'll find all sorts of recommendations and rules for how to set your "lab" up. Everything from using a concrete slab, sand, inner tubes, magnetic and gravity mounts and so on.

Take nothing for granted.

Depending on your environment you may be able to do without some of the "standard" table elements.

While working to isolate my [DCG-related stability problems](http://www.holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?f=20&t=5580) I decided to see how the table acted if the inner tubes were removed. I'd always assumed that I'd need them since my lab is on the second floor of a wood-frame house.

I don't.

In fact, they were making my table less stable because they breathed with every pressure change and amplified any movement coming up through the floor. It didn't matter how mushy or tight they were inflated. I could vary the extent to which the table moved but never get rid of the problem altogether. I always thought it was because I was on wood flooring on the second story.

Now that the inner tubes are gone I can actually stand next to the table without seeing any movement, tap on the table with a wrench and the fringes settle immediately and walk around outside the room on the second floor without causing any movement.

There goes any hope of moving the lab into the garage.
