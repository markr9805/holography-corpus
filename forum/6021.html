Today I became a member of the telescope maker club in my town. They have all the equipment to make mirrors of up to 40cm, and they have the vacuum chamber to put aluminum on the mirrors and even dielectric coatings.

They offered to make a 30 or 40cm mirror with me. Now I have to make a some choices:

- What would be the ideal focal distance for a collimating mirror?

- What would be the ideal shape. Off-axis parabolic is out of the question for a mirror that size. So I have to make a choice between spherical and parabolic. Both are possible options.

- Up to what accuracy should the mirror be. They have the possibility to make highly accurate mirrors with their foccoult testing equipment. But it seems to me that a visually correct mirror should suit my purpose fine. Or should this type of mirror at least be 1/2labda accuracy? I would think not because errors are already introduced by the off axis use of the mirror.
BobH Fri Jan 26, 2007 4:15 am #2
The best focal length is that which will fit on your table. Make it a bit longer and fold the beam with flat mirrors if you'd like, but more surfaces means more dirt in your beams (really ugly in the reference beam!). Go for a conventional focal length the astronomers use so you'll have a resale market.

Best shape is parabolic. Some will say spherical is just as good, but there's no resale market.

Astronomers will sometimes compromise the center of their mirrors' figure because it will be shadowed by the diagonal anyway. Don't let that happen to your mirror!

Don't make a bad mirror because it will be used in a strange way. Why add insult to injury? But seriously, 1/4 wave is probably good enough.
