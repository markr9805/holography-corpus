While saving up on money to make my own pulsed laser, I thought it would be a nice experiment to try to make my own KDP crystal. Here is a photo of the first result. This crystal has several small inclusions and is unsuitable for laser use because of that, but the experiment does show that it is quite simple to grow your own KDP and ADP crystals. The crystal is about 2cm in size and it took me about one week to grow in a plastic cup at a cost of a few Euro worth of chemicals. The main reason for the small inclusions is that I was impatient to just wait for the result. Instead I picked up the container many times each day to inspect the progress. Also, a crack is visible in the crystal. This is because when I took the crystal out of the solution, I tried to clean it's facets to prevent microcystals forming on them as the crystal dried. I did this by holding the crystal in a stream of very cold water. This created a thermal shock in the crystal and was the cause of the thin crack.

![Image](http://www.hologramhans.com/pictures/KDP_Crystal.jpg)

I grew the crystal by allowing several seed crystals to form in a warm super saturated solution of KDP in deionized water. Then I took the best small crystal and tied a thin polyester wire around it. I took the remaining solution and poured it into a clean cup. This cup was placed in a pan with water that was kept at a stable temperature of 33C with a aquarium heater. The crystal was hung into the plastic cup from the rope, and the cup was covered with a piece of paper to prevent dust from falling in.
Hans Thu Sep 21, 2006 6:14 pm #2
In a second test I tried to make a little crystal growing machine. I made a flat rectangular container with the bottom painted black on the right side. A small lamp is placed below the black part of the bottom of the container. The container is filled with a saturated solution. A layer of KDP is placed on the bottom of the container above the black patch. The lamp heats up the solution and dissolves some of the KDP above the black patch. The warmer water, that is now saturated with the newly dissolved KDP rises and flows up and to the left. On the left side of the container is no heat source and the water sinks again and becomes super-saturated. That is the place to hang the seed crystal.

![Image](http://www.hologramhans.com/pictures/CrystalMachine.jpg)

I was able to grow single Alum (cheaper than KDP) crystals of 1cm in size in less than 3 hours with this setup. But because there was no control over the growth rate (due to temperature differential), the crystals turned out opaque. The next version will include a TEC for the heated right-hand bottom of the container, and another TEC on the left side of the container to cool. With that setup a precise differential will be possible with control over the growth rate of the crystal. If inclusions appear, it should be possible to reverse the temperature differential (and thus the flow of the water) and to shrink the crystal back to where no inclusions are appearent.
