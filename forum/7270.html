Ed Wesly Thu Apr 10, 2008 6:23 pm #2
From above:

> I've never seen a white light viewable TH so I don't know exactly what they look like.

I should sure hope that you have seen one, if you have ever looked at a credit card holo or magazine cover, etc. The embossed holos are white light transmission images, metalized so that they don't have to be back lit. But their fringe structure is of the white light transmission variety, as opposed to the true reflection style.

> Does this technique work for white light viewable transmission holograms?

Color in the WLT holo is controlled by geometry: where the slit-like H1 is with respect to the H2 copy plate. What would happen if you TEA-treated a WLT holo is that the replay reference angle would be quite different than the recording reference beam angle. If you ask in which direction, I would reply, as they say in the math books, that this is an exercise for the student!
BobH Thu Apr 10, 2008 7:54 pm #3
Another method to manipulate the colors of images in transmission holograms is to make it an "achromatic", or "open aperture" transfer and emboss it on colored plastic. Or use a selective mirror. Or a reflection hologram behind it.
