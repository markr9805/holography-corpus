Dinesh Fri Feb 18, 2005 3:40 pm #2
Which is why DCG is so incredibly complicated. Of course nobody's going to reveal everything in a paper. The days when a scientific publication purported to reveal some sort of "scientific knowledge" are long gone. Non scientists always seem to think that papers are encapsulation of some sort of information that's going to increase our knowledge base in this subject or that. More and more papers have been more about the the obtaining of fame in a particular field than about knowledge per se. The author wants you to know that he/she is knowledgeable and "expert" in this or that field so that he/she will get promotions, prizes, prestige and, in terms of business orientated papers, more orders based on the "expertise" of the authors organisation. Bell, Labs, in humble opinion, was the last of "science for science sake". There's as much politics in science as in any other field of human endeavour . If you think any criticism I may have made of your tests is, let's say a little over the top, you have no idea how vicious the world of scientific publications can get. I was in the room with a Nobel Laureate when he was manipulating a paper so as to get the maximum poltical clout for himself and his university. My brother is a professor of History of Art in London University. He seems to be making a bit of an international reputation in the field. He's asked to give invited papers and he's getting the idea to pick and choose where he'll get maximum effect. This is one reason I never publish. Can't be bothered with "fame" and the apparent posessor of "intelligence" whatever that is, has never been of particular concern to me.

I've never actually deterred you from these tests. However, if you publish them, and relating the tests and results in the forum is certainly publishing, then expect to be called on your methods and results. If you publish banal and obvious facts, expect that someone, if not me, will point out that the facts are banal and obvious. If you misappropriate a word due to ignorance of it, or misuse an equation, then expect that someone will call you on it. Publishing any sort of scientific or semisciientific work opens you to the public gaze and some parts of the public will gaze! That's one reason why papers have a scarcity of information. The author sometimes doesn't want the "gaze" to go in a particular direction so he/she downplays that aspect of the paper.
