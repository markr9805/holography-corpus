Dinesh Mon Apr 18, 2005 7:28 pm #2
It takes longer because it's a photopolymer. All photopolymers are less sensitive than silver halide. Usually about 100 times less sensitive.

I also had a talk with Paul at Liti. He mentioned that his concept was not to provide a top class, new holographic material to compete with Slavich or DCG, but to provide a material that complete beginners could use to make their first holograms without the need for wet processing or any chemicals. He specifically has said that experienced holographers would be disappointed with this material.

In that respect some have posted that they have made holograms, albeit not very good ones, so that Paul's concept seems to be working. Paul also mentioned that he's always available for consultation with people trying the material.

My take is that insofar as the material does seem to produce holograms, it satisfies the criterion that Paul has placed. When we tried it here in San Diego, we got no image and a relatively poor grating after a fairly long exposure, but we were experienced holographers so maybe we expected more. Beginners now have two choices: Liti and Integraf's kit. Liti's holograms are apparently weak with long exposures but simple(-ish) to make while Integraf's needs wet processing (included in the kit) but are brighter. Unfortunately, a beginner has no way of directly comparing his/her holograms with high quality work. There are pictures here and elsewhere on the web, but there's no comparison to seeing a good, high qaulity hologram "in the flesh", and these are not easy to find.

Not having to use wet processing is a good goal. I can see that this material would be good for demonstrating holograms, since it develops "in place", so you can show reconstruction by simply removing the object after sufficient exposure. I suppose that a display with some reflection and transmission holograms could be provided to demonstrate brighter and more dramatic holograms.

fkienast Sat Jul 30, 2005 12:36 pm #9
Early in July (before joining this forum and reading the reviews), I bid on a hologram kit from paulonline3d on EBay. Turns out I had the only bid and won the item at the reserve price.

The shipping was delayed to the seller having a family emergency. Yesterday I finally received my Liti hologram kit.

I will say that the kit was packaged and looked professional. Instructions were clear, and it did not look like the company was trying to "cut corners" with the materials they supplied.

I set up the kit in a bathroom I could darken according to the instructions, using the supplied Matchbox truck as the object. Instructions called for a normal exposure of 5 minutes, although the exposure could be increased to "up to 12 minutes". I did a 10 minute exposure. After that, the plate still had most of the blue coating, although I could see it was reduced where the laser had hit the plate directly (the laser does not cover the entire plate with the setup in the instructions). However, no hologram was visible, even after exposing to light from a bulb for several minutes.

I then turned the truck on its side, so the shiny bottom was facing the plate, and exposed for 20 minutes. I noticed that the laser mount was wobbly and was not hitting the expanding lens directly on, so I supported it using the batteries so it hit directly on. After the 20 minute exposure, I removed the truck. I was able to see a very faint hologram. The hologram brightened slightly after exposing to a light bulb. The bottom of the truck is almost clear enough to read the small lettering on it when viewed with a green laser pointer. It is visible with the supplied red laser as well. I was not able to make out any hologram using the supplied green LED.

I then did a hologram of some coins, again still using a transmission setup. I exposed for 30 minutes. The coins turned out very dim. Although they are visible, they are dim enough that it is impossible to read the year on the coins even when illuminated with a green laser. The denomination of each coin can be distinguised, with difficulty.

I then tried doing a reflection hologram, using the laser and setup I used previously with the Integraf kit for a 10 second exposure. After a 30 minute exposure, I found most of the blue coating on the plate had barely been affected. I was not able to see any hologram (using either transmission from a green laser or reflection from white light).

The kit came with 20 plates (2 boxes of 10). The boxes are stamped with a manufacture date of 07/18/05. If anyone is interested in experimenting with these plates, I will send you the 2nd (unopened) box for free (you will just need to pay me whatever packing/shipping costs).

It sound like my experience is pretty similar to the others I have read here. I do get holograms, but they are very dim and require much longer exposures than the instructions specify.

Frank
