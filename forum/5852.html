Hi, I'm working on making a few small holograms as part of an art course. I'm using a budget hologram kit from integraf [http://www.holokits.com/hologram_kit.htm](http://www.holokits.com/hologram_kit.htm) and I'm wondering why some of my holograms are failing. My last attempted hologram was of a few matchbox cars, sitting in a frying pan, with the film resting directly above it, on two steel rod things (I'll get some photos of it soon, I'm at school at the moment). This is sitting on a wooden block, on top of an inflated tyre tube, to minimise vibration. So far I have pulled of 1 hologram, which was of my mobile phone, sitting in a bowl, with the film placed between 2 pieces of glass sitting directly on the bowl. What I was wondering was, how do you know how long to leave exposures for. can longer exposures destroy the hologram? Does the developer need to be stored in any certain way?

Before you say my failures are due to movement, I can see the metal clips which were used to hold the glass together. If my set up moved, this clips wouldn't have come out, right?
Tony Thu Oct 26, 2006 10:52 am #2
> What I was wondering was, how do you know how long to leave exposures for. can longer exposures destroy the hologram?

You want to expose the film so that after development it has a density around 2, which is like dark sunglasses. In general, exposure is not critical, but if it goes completely totally black right away you could be overexposing it which will result in dim or no hologram.

> Does the developer need to be stored in any certain way?

Best to keep it in air tight containers, and if it's 2-part, only mix what you need and discard after use. The developer will oxidize and go bad if exposed to air.

> Before you say my failures are due to movement, I can see the metal clips which were used to hold the glass together. If my set up moved, this clips wouldn't have come out, right?

Maybe, maybe not. Actually, this almost certainly says your exposure and development was close, but there was movement between the film and the subject. There must be no mevement between the film and the subject (for a Denisyuk, single beam reflection hologram). The clips being firmly attached to the film would not have moved relative to the film, but the subject could still have moved. Try to arrange the film so it rests right on the subject and nothing can rock or move.

Also, make sure the clips are over the area of the film between the pieces of glass. If they are outside the film, the glass can bow, allowing the film to move.
Colin Kaminski Thu Oct 26, 2006 2:31 pm #3
Also, remember supporting film think of three point suspension. Four points will rock. When in doubt, hot melt glue things together you think are already held with gravity.
