Here is something very weird I noticed this past weekend. I was doing the Girl Model and was looking at her in the 457 wavelength. Her skirt is painted Cadmium Yellow such that is reflects almost Zero 457 line but reflects very much of the 514 line (All the greens I tested reflected too much 457). When I look at the skirt in the 457 line it does not appear black but instead look ruby red. Very very deep red. I guess it has something to do with the eye instead of the wavelength(s) beign reflected but it was very weird.
Kaveh Mon Sep 12, 2005 6:47 pm #2
It's fluorescing. It is one of the few instances where a judiciously chosen set of three wavelength will not give you perfect color rendition. But I think the brightness of the fluorescence is so low, I don't think it'll matter.
JohnFP Mon Sep 12, 2005 7:58 pm #3
Very interesting. So the short wavelength of 457 is causing that yellow paint to fluoresce in the red.

Yes, it is very dim but non the less, red. I may try to take a photo.
Tom B. Tue Sep 13, 2005 4:15 am #4
Chloropyll B has a similar blue-UV absorption with deep red flourescence, but the narrow absorption peak looks closer to 430 than 457 nm. Chlorophyll A is closer to 400 nm.

[http://omlc.ogi.edu/spectra/PhotochemCA ... yll-b.html](http://omlc.ogi.edu/spectra/PhotochemCAD/html/chlorophyll-b.html)

If you mash up a fresh leaf in alcohol or acetone, the liquid emits a very pretty deep red glow under a UV LED penlight.
