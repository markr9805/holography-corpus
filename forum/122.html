Dinesh Mon Jan 10, 2011 2:25 pm #3
> baja: it made mewonder if its really ok to order these things for a home lab. I mean,are these things we're not supposed to have outside of zoned labspace?

There are the MSDS documents - Material Safety Data Sheets. These are documents that state all the dangers that any given substance has Here in the US, it's a requirement that any industrial or commercial facility using chemicals have to have the MSDS clearly marked and accessible. Anyway, here's the MSDS for Potassium and Ammonium Dichromate:

[http://www.jtbaker.com/msds/englishhtml/a5760.htm](http://www.jtbaker.com/msds/englishhtml/a5760.htm)

[http://www.jtbaker.com/msds/englishhtml/p5719.htm](http://www.jtbaker.com/msds/englishhtml/p5719.htm)

As you can see, they're both carcinogens and fairly corrosive. So, if there are kids with access to your home lab, you may want to be a bit careful. On the other hand, so long as you don't stir the dichromate solution with your hands and then eat pizza, you're probably OK. I've been dealing with the stuff for nigh on these twenty five years and I'm...well, we'll let that pass...

holorefugee Mon Jan 10, 2011 3:35 pm #5
> baja: it made mewonder if its really ok to order these things for a home lab. I mean,are these things we're not supposed to have outside of zoned labspace?

Dichromates are quite poisonous. Don't breathe the dust and wear gloves when handling the liquids. Also, it is best to evaporate the water from the solutions and dispose of the solid waste. It is not good to put the solutions in the drain.
erose Mon Jan 10, 2011 6:25 pm #6
I found making my own developers and bleaches much less expensive. In NYC I was able to go to several supply houses and buy what I needed. But I did have some discomfort with keeping things like concentrated Sulfuric acid in my NY apartment. As I am getting back into holography I intend to invest in a fire-safe storage cabinet. I also will be setting up a fume hood over the processing station. I had one incident with the S-acid when a paper towel with but a tiny drop on it decided to combust. So there are various hazards beyond the issues of toxicity. Just follow all safety measures and you should be fine.
Jeffrey Weil Mon Jan 10, 2011 11:39 pm #7
I agree, keeping those concentrated acids around is dangerous. You have options though.

For sulfuric try the auto parts store. For battery's they sell 30% acid in distilled water. Perfect for us. We always use less than that percentage. Still nasty but not even close to the pure stuff and it's a lot cheaper. Plus at that concentration it can be stored and sold in plastic containers.

I don't think it'll start a fire either.
