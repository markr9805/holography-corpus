Dinesh Tue Jul 05, 2011 11:53 am #4
> kyodai: Would ANY green LED work as a darkroom light for a red laser

Look up the technical specs for your film and then measure/estimate the bandwidth of your safelight (LED). Alec has specs for the film ( [http://www.integraf.com/Downloads/PFG-01.pdf](http://www.integraf.com/Downloads/PFG-01.pdf) ). You'll notice that PFG-01 (the light grey plot) starts its sensitivity at 550nm, peaks at about 630nm and drops to zero at about 730nm. This mean that the film is most sensitive in the 570 - 700nm region. I don't know what film is in the kit, but Integraf's site will have the technical specs for the film. So, the safelight must not emit anything from 570 ( a greeny-yellowish colour) to 700 (deep red). If you use Colin's trick of looking at the safelight at a very glancing angle off a CD or DVD, you'll see the colours the safelight is emitting. If there's significant yellow in it, it may not be OK (but if there's only a little yellow, you're probably OK). If you see only green, then you're OK. Eye sensitivity peaks at 550, so if you see a lot of green, the safelight is probably emitting at 530 - 570. These numbers assume that the film is PFG-01. If not, look at the specs of the film in the kit and use the same method to determine the safelight. The basic idea is that the film will only see the colours it's sensitive to, all other colours are invisible to the film.

Jeffrey Weil Tue Jul 05, 2011 12:36 pm #5
Do what Dinesh said and get some Christmas lights. If you string them over your whole table on the ceiling you'll have a nice even light everywhere. When you use just a single bulb everything in the room is shadowed and it has to be dim as you might work right underneath the bulb. When your on the other side of the room it becomes very dark.

When you use a long string of leds it also has to be dim but your never far from some of the leds and its coming from all directions so few shadows. It makes things in the room much much easier to see.

If you need green safelights get green leds with a green object over it, like green peppers, or little green balls, whatever. That extra plastic will filter out any side bands coming out of the led.

For example, [http://www.christmaslightsetc.com/c7-c9 ... s--699.htm](http://www.christmaslightsetc.com/c7-c9-LED-Christmas-Lights--699.htm)

do you see how the red and green leds have some little decorative bit of plastic over them, that will help filter the led to be a little more pure.

Next is put them on a dimmer. Put some film in the brightest part of the room with a quarter over it. Leave it under the safelight for much longer than you ever would in real life, then develop it further than you would in real life.

Fix, do not bleach, the film, dry it and put it down on a white piece of paper. The light is now going through the film twice. Once through the film and once on the way out after bouncing off the paper. This will help you see any density differences on the film. If you see any outline of the quarter, lower the safelights with the dimmer and do the test again.

You can do this test using a pretty small piece of film so it's cheap to do. Don't go crazy, if your only going to have the film under the safelights for 1/2 hour you don't need a 12 hour test and an hour of development. Just do double or triple the time the film will be under the lights in a real world shoot. The same with development. If the film is still clear you'll never have problems.

So if you expect your film to be under the lights for an hour total, settling, development, etc...and you normally develop for 5 min. Let the test film sit for 2 hours and develop for 10 min.

A real world test is better than using math....Don't tell Dinesh I said that!

Paulos Fri Jul 08, 2011 4:27 am #10
There is an easy way to first check if your safelight is really safe: In your darkroom, turn on your safelight and look at it through the emulsion.

If it looks glass clear, light is fully safe. If you see it more or less gray, emulsion absorbs light and may get fogged.

I am using red LEDs for green sensitive material and green LEDs for red. Although it is rather bright in my lab during development, I never had fogging problems.
