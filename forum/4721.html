Jeff Blyth has graciously offered to chip in $100 to help cover the cost of distributing the small quantities of dye required to experiment with his DIY holographic plate method. (Whatta Guy!)

If you're familiar with the method it should need no introduction, if not read here:

[http://cabd0.tripod.com/holograms/id3.html](http://cabd0.tripod.com/holograms/id3.html)

Who wants free dye? Keep reading.

Danny has pointed up the idea that us holgraphers need to work together, because there may come a time when there are no holographic emulsions commercially available. Danny is quite right, this is an excellent point. What do holographers do without holographic recording materials? Read papers and start experimenting based on them and what they've read about "other" processes in this forum? That sounds like an abrupt and stomach churning change, and the phrase "hobby killer" has come up more than once...

Yes, I know some of you are working with DCG, but not all of us have or are willing to spend the money for that sort of equipment.

I had mentioned that the liter of red-sensitizing dye made from a single gram of pinacyanol chloride would produce 400 of the 100ml sensitizing solutions noted in Jeff's DIY procedure, and that the per-unit cost was low enough that giving it away to foster the use of and experimentation with the method sounded like a good idea. I'd like to see the process developed and improved upon at a user level (and it seems that Jeff would like to see the same), and what better way to get that done than to have other people do the work? What's holding them back? Is it the lack of less than twenty five cents worth of dye?

Certainly the prospect of cooking gelatin and coating plates is a deterrent, but is it possible to use emulsions that are already in place? What about that old expired roll of Tri-X in the back of a drawer, could it be fixed out and resensitized into a holographic emulsion? I don't know yet, but I plan on finding out. Given a little dye, someone else may figure out an even better way. If the method were simple and inexpensive enough, and the quality equalled or surpassed that of commercially available materials, would you try it & use it?

Hmm, is this even vaguely inspiring to anyone? I guess it was to Jeff, because I didn't ask him to put up any cash, he must have got inspired

I've proposed that there be two requirements to receiving the free dye:

1. It should be used as intended (at least in part) rather than just being a holography related novelty.

2. Results, especially experimental results with differing methods, regardless of whether good or bad, must be reported, preferably publicly. Links to information not posted within this forum would likely be appreciated.

I'm also considering packaging and selling the small quantities of chemistry required and perhaps a few handy accessories. If anyone has a problem with the profit concept, I'd have to ask which of them works for free (because I have jobs for them).

Ok, read enough? While it'll probably be a few weeks before the dye goes out, I've put a signup form and a questionnaire here:

[http://www.9000shops.com/holodye/index.html](http://www.9000shops.com/holodye/index.html)

Yes, there's a little humor in there. Please excuse my inability to take anything completely seriously.

The amount of free dye will be limited, so requests will be filled in the order they're entered until there's no more "free" dye. That may or may not be a long time, depending on what happens.

Gary Deal Wed Sep 14, 2005 2:27 am #21
Danny, sorry to take so long to get back about this.

The dye solution I sent is the same as called for in Jeff's stock solution, one gram per liter of methanol. In use, as per Jeff's posted method, you should use 2.5ml of the stock solution per 100ml of KBr stock solution (4% w/v in 3/2 methanol /water).

Since 100ml of sensitizer may be a bit small to work with, the vial I sent has 10ml of stock dye solution, which is enough to make 400ml of finished sensitizer. The little graduated vial was included so that anyone without much for lab supplies could fairly accurately measure out 2.5ml of dye stock with only an eyedropper.

I haven't seen any results posted yet, and I've been off on other projects while it's still pleasant to be outdoors but I'm working my way back into the darkroom again.
