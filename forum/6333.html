A few months ago I started experimentation with a laser cannibalized from a DVD burner.

I shot several holos but all shown rings due to mode-hops. I tried to stabilize the temperature with a generous heat-sink without any improvement.

This week I've finished to build a closed-loop TEC control (based on MAX1968 integrated circuit and a NTC) that allows laser temperature stability within 0.01C.

The question is:

What is the minimum wavelength stability for holographic purpose?

Attached you find the laser diode behaviour (constant current, Pout=80mW) without TEC and with TEC in closed-loop configuration.

Measurements are done with a Anritsu MS710C Opt. Spectr. Analizer. coupling the free-space optical signal to a single mode 9um fiber.

Hans Sun Jul 08, 2007 10:39 am #9
Wow, that looks nice and compact. Did you mount the bottom of the laser unit to a large heat sink? You need a thermal mass that is several times larger than the mass on the laser side of the peltier.

Also, I noticed that you don't use shielded/grounded cables for feedback of temperature and feed-forward of laser diode current. It's important to use shielded cables in both cases to make sure there is no cross-talk from the highly noisy high current that goes through the peltier and the temperature feedback.

I noticed that you use only two terminals of the laser diode. So no power sensing feedback. That is very good. But just to make sure, you do supply the diode constant current, yes?

If you have good temperature stability and shielded cables, you should be able to get coherence length of several meters with powers up to about 40mW.

Ah, I just noticed you use a constant current source with a single transistor. Please bear in mind that they are quite sensitive to changes in ambient temperature. Did you measure the current through the diode for a longer period of time?
