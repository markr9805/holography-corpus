I was careful to say I don't know if this will work, and I realize the dyes on this site are blocking dyes for laser beam safety. I am going to reiterate what I said above based on the following:

Take a look at [http://photo.net/learn/optics/edscott/ir000020.htm](http://photo.net/learn/optics/edscott/ir000020.htm)

You will see senstivity curves for Kodak HS IR and Konica 750 film. The former has nearly uniform sensitivity out to about 925nm and the latter a hump that peaks at about 750nm. Both are silver halide films. So, Jeff, until you can make your photon energy argument stronger, perhaps show there is an excitation energy edge at, say, 950nm that prevents latent image electrons from being created in silver halide I wil continue to think there is a dye out there that will do the trick.

For polymers it may well be another matter. I now realize I gave a chloroform soluble dye from Epolin, but what was asked for was water soluble. Epolin has those too. In fact for the original question of the exposure at 763nm, either of the above sensitizing systems might do as they must be water soluble. But you need to get the dye and the problem is both are likely trade secrets and not obtainable from the film makers. But there are specialist dye makers who subcontract to Kodak et al who have a huge range of dyes. The added problem is that in my experience it is tough to get them to supply sensitvity curves. But who knows??? If you want a supplier name, ask me and I will look for one this weekend. It came up in my Lippmann photography work some time ago.

So far, I think your best bet is to try a company in the US called HW Sands, located in Jupiter, Florida. Their phone number is -for you- 001-561-743-8090, and they are on eastern standard time. The URL is:

[http://www.hwsands.com](http://www.hwsands.com). HW Sands is a large supplier of dyes, which used to do mostly visible photosensitizing dyes, but now does mostly IR photosensitizing dyes. Under the wavelength range of 751-775nm, they have several dyes that have absorptivities over 200 that might be useful to you. These include:#SDA 7590, abs max at 759nm, peak abs. 373 L/gm/cm, solvent methanol; SDA 2009, abs max at 761nm, abs 351 units, solvent methanol; SDB 5491, abs max 774nm, Abs units 369, solvent methanol, and also SDB 6906, abs max at 774nm, abs 409 units, solvent methanol. I know that the solvent is wrong but they tell me they can easily modify the dyes to be water soluble and are confident that they can be used to sensitize acrylamides. The web pages show absorption curves for each of their products.

They are fairly expensive: at 1 gm quantity they charge around $US 150/gm, at 10 gms about $US 50/gm, and at 1 kg they charge about $US 5-10/gm.
