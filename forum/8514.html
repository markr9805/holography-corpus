Ed Wesly Thu Jan 28, 2016 11:20 am #3
Why not cut them yourself? The cutter is usually available at a hardware store, they might even cut your sizes them for you. A local stained glass artist might also do it, but if you can go to a stained glass supplier, or check on-line, you will find a variety of devices to cut shapes consistently. I have a Glastar Glass Stripper and Circle Cutter that allows me to hack up a 30 by 40 cm plate into bite-sized test pieces.

There's not too much more satisfying to hear the zip of the cutter making a fissure on the glass, and the snap as it separates. If you go the DIY way, you should also get a pair of breaking pliers to make life simpler.

Glass cutting is a good skill that can come in handy and impress your friends. Plus you can use any thickness glass, or recycle window glass.
