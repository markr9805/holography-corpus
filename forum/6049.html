Ed Wesly Mon Feb 12, 2007 4:20 pm #2
SM-6 is a developer I discovered. See my article, [http://holographyforum.org/files/sm6.htm](http://holographyforum.org/files/sm6.htm)

It doesn't necessarily work that well when used with the relatively long exposures of CW He-Ne lasers. It was designed for the extremely short exposure times of pulsed lasers.

It's kind of a surprise that they would recommend that developer for the PFG-01 plates. Or maybe they know something I don't, as I never have used SM-6 on PFG-01 plates with a He-Ne. So give it a shot if you have the time and materials!

Hi Kiffdino, (and all Germans holographers out there),

Pyrogallol should be available at a good price from Brenner Fototechnik: [http://www.alles-foto.de](http://www.alles-foto.de) . Potassium dichromate (=Kaliumdichromat) for bleaching can be replaced by Potassium Permanganate, which is less dangerous and more easily obtained. I saw that ErichRose used the Pyro developer with very bright results, but on Agfa plates, not on PFG-01. There is also the option to order a set of "Ultimate" plates from Yves Gentet, France, together with a ready-to-use chemical processing kit. I guess, thats better than trying to pull bright reflection holograms out of PFG-01 (I am still trying).

Best regards,

Max

I don't think image brightness is limited to Pyrogallol developers only. We've made bright H2 copies on PFG-01 with a couple of different developers (among which Neutol and several ascorbate developers).

Neutol (being discontinued due to Agfa's disappearance) is/was a simple paper developer based on Hydroquinone and Phenidone – some time ago I posted its formula (don't have that link at hand now). Anyway, there's nothing special about it. I assume a great many photographic developers might perform equally well on emulsions with relatively large grains. The problem seems to be finding appropriate commercial developers – since the whole market is on decline. Best might be to turn to smaller “niche” producers like FORTE, CALBE, MOERSCH, MACO, EFKE, ADOX etc.
