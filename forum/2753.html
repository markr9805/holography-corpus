Dinesh Fri Oct 01, 2004 9:10 pm #8
[quote:4bab1f3510]Care to share your plate preparation, exposure and processing regime?[/quote:4bab1f3510]

I poured approx 1 ml onto a glass slide 2.5in x 2,5in. I then capped this with another glass slide. I set up a two beam grating with one beam at zero degrees and the other at various different angles. I then shot for various different times. To process I put it about 6 in under a 20W halogen lamp for 10 minutes, I believe and then 2 minutes under a long wave, low power UV source (essentially a small "black light we use for DCG lamination). I'm not completely sure about the times under UV and halogen because don't have my notes with me, but I think these were approx times.

For the one I quoted, one angle was zero and the other at 20 degrees. Exposure was 200 mJ/sq cm at 633. It was tested with 514 with an input power of 16.2 mW/sq cm. The first order diffraction measured 12.00 mW/sq cm - thus giving an eta of 74.07%.

The plates sandwich was held on one edge with a clamp with the other end free. Since the emulsion was still runny, when clamped like this, it continued to move and squeeze towards the unclamped end. This may have cause some motion during exposure which may make the exposure figure worse than it actualy was. Martin tells me that the emulsion was meant for 660 and so it may be faster at that lambda.

I received my order of Litiholo film this week shipped DHL. I tried it out this morning.

First, let me say my web order was quickly acknowledged. Additionally, when it did ship (a few weeks later) I got an email from Paul as follows:

" We do not have detailed specs on the film yet. It is a

red-sensitive, transmission hologram film. We usually advise a 2 minute

exposure with the diode lasers in our Hologram Kits. The film does not

need any developing. Once you are finished with the expose, it is

sometimes nice to hold the finished hologram up to a bright light (such

as an ordinary light bulb) to bleach the remaining bluish color to clear.

Let me know if you need anything else. "

When you order just the film, they want to charge you an extra $15 if you can't show proof of purchase for the $99 kit. Seeing as how I already had a bunch of lasers and stuff, I didn't want the kit. I just wanted to experiment with the film. So OK, I don't mind paying the extra $15 if it keeps a small holography business alive. I guess I hoped that for my extra $15 I might have gotten some info on how to use the film, other than what I read here and on the website. But all I got was a box of film - nothing else. So I was on my own, so to speak.

The 'film' appears to be glass plates, with a sandwich layer of polymer, covered with a plastic backing. The polymer layer is sticky. I couldn't figure out if the plastic backing is supposed to stay in place or be removed. I tried one with it removed, and that didn't work, so all the rest I did with the plastic in place. My guess is you're supposed to leave it on.

The film is bluish, and turns clear on hard exposure.

I tried several shots, with no luck until the last one. I have an 11 mw Hene, which I expanded to about 3 inches and made a single beam transmission setup, exposing for 3 minutes. Nothing. If I hold the 'film' in front of a lightbulb, it turns clear but no hologram appeared. I then tried 5 minutes exposure - still nothing.

My last attempt was with a 1" beam spread - very intense - and 5 minutes exposure. From this, I did get a slight, dim image, even though the area covered by the 'reference' portion of the beam had turned clear. The image was dim, and very angle-dependent as far as reconstruction.

I'm sure I have more to learn about how to use this stuff - I wish there were more complete details on exposure, etc.

So far, it's just a curiousity. If I could get it to work, it would be great for teaching holography in my middle-school classes - you know hwo kids are today with their demands for 'instant gratification'...

Paul at Liti Mon Nov 01, 2004 5:45 pm #22
Well, hello everyone. I thought it might help to get some information straight from Liti.

I have left phone messages and e-mails for both Tony and John, to see if we can fix their problems. I assume they will continue to post their results here, so this will hopefully keep everyone informed.

In the mean time, here is what I can tell you so far. The Litiholo "Instant Hologram" Film is red-sensitive material with peak sensitivity at 668nm, although this may change with future formulas. We currently use two different laser diodes with our hologram kit, with outputs at 650nm and 632nm. Both have worked well with the film. We have found the average sensitivity of the film to be about 60 mJ/cm2. However, because this is a fairly new material, we know that there is some variability in the sensitivity. We have been making holograms with the basic kit and film on our conference room table with very good results.

We hope to offer more film possibilities in the near future, including larger sizes, reflection hologram film, and other wavelength sensitivities. As we all know, holographic film is desperately needed right now, and we hope this is only the beginning of what we can provide. In the mean time, I hope you will all support and help us through the early stages. I probably cannot respond to postings here every day, but I will try to respond frequently. Thanks.
Tony Tue Nov 02, 2004 1:33 am #23
I'd like to thank Paul of Liti Holographics for taking the time to call me and give me some pointers on their new film. With his help, I'm now able to report better success. Some of the problems turned out to be stupid cockpit errors, like weak batteries in my wireless shutter!

Tonight I made a single beam transmission of a small nonglossy china rabbit. I expanded the beam from my 11 mw laser to an ellipse 2.5 x 3 " at the plate. I exposed it for 5 minutes. I got a decent hologram, but not too bright. I think with more experimentation I can get the brightness up.

Here's some interesting stuff I've figured out:

The hologram has a very narrow viewing angle on reconstruction. Maybe this is due to lower spatial resolution of the film, as well as a very thick 'emulsion' layer (compared to wavelengths).

I was making the hologram with hene but reconstructing it with a diode laser. Because of the narrow viewing angle and the slight difference in wavelengths or optics, it's hard to reconstruct the entire image this way. I see a vertical stripe of the image moving across horizontally as I change the beam angle. If I reconstruct with the original hene, I can get the entire image at once.

Also, after making the hologram, I hardened the plate with a white light bulb. I think the image get a tad brighter. This also had the effect of shifting the replay angle a little bit.

Wish I had more time tonight - I'll post more later if I learn more...
