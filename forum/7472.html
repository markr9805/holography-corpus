JohnFP Wed Aug 13, 2008 3:45 pm #17
> 1) I used my extra beam from my 457nm laser and illuminated the object from two sides. I verified that the polarization was random (which it is). So is that a problem? Can the kicker light be randomly polarized? After all it should randomly polarize once a beam hits the object any way.

No, having randomly polarized object lighting is not a problem if you are using a diffusing object. You are correct a diffusing object randomly polarizes the light anyway. Now if you were trying to make a hologram of a gold plated object and wanted the specular reflectons (the twinkling gleams) then it would be a problem.

> 2) In the area I lit up, there did not seem to be any extra brightness. I lit just a small section of the object (like the face only). I did get noise or should I say, a bright haze on the plate, but I get that as well when I user kicker mirror and don't block the beam well. Is there something I'm missing here, why is it not brighter?

Couple of things to watch out for. If you are increasing the energy at the plate by adding additional object lighting then you may have to decrease your exposure slightly. Especially if you are getting the "halo" noise around the object. Are you also getting burn in at that extra lighted spot (burn in is seen as a white silhouette on the plane of the plate when viewing the plate in such a manner soas the hologram itself is not visible, usually looking through the plate is a good way).

Also, you have to try to make sure your object lighting is not brighter then your reference or one part of the object will try strongly to make a hologram with another part of the object. The theoretical 1:1 has a lot of consideration to be taken into account.

> 3) If my fiber (or kicker light) moves during the exposure, for what ever reason, what happens? Does it act as if there was no kicker light at all?

Yes, exactly. But if it moves just very slightly then you may see some faint destructive fringes overlapping the object just as if you did no kicker and the object beam moved slightly in a two beam set up. But again, these destructive fringes would be transposed on the "good" object and may be very dim (gray not black).

BobH Mon Aug 18, 2008 6:47 am #25
I have lots of experience pushing the coherence length of my lasers to make holograms. For example, my hologram on the opening page ("Rasta Dream") is a 250mm tall transfer from a 300mm tall master. The reconstruction beam for the master and the Reference beam for the transfer were both collimated and at about 52 degrees. I used a Spectra Physics model 907 (35mW He-Ne) with a coherence length of maybe 300mm to do the shot. If I remember correctly, the path length mis-match was approaching the coherence length. This made the bottom of the image dim when viewed from the top of the viewing zone.

What I did was to adjust the path lengths very accurately to minimize the variation across the viewing zone. With this set, I would still see a variation of image brightness in the plane of incidence, which I figured was caused by the diffraction efficiency variation as a function of the average spatial frequency of the fringes along various perspective views within the viewing zone. After moving the location of best path length match about 50mm to help out the weakly performing fringes, my image stays reasonably bright and even throughout the viewing zone. Upon close inspection, however, you can still see some image dimming at the extremes of the zone.

Fill light also helps, but you really need to measure the beams out carefully to avoid weird lighting effects as you move around the viewing zone. When coherence length is very short, you get bands. Working creatively with the coherence length is uniquely holographic, something few artists have explored. Most technical holographers want the coherence to be as long as possible, which has its own problems (like ghost images and glints off equipment). If you really want to play with coherence length creatively, I'd recommend a Spectra Physics Model 125 He-Ne laser without an etalon. The 2 meter long monster with a coherence length of around 150mm. Or a long tube He-Cd laser if you want to do it in the blue.

This is a huge topic. I could go on and on and on about it but have been writing all day and me fingers are sore.
