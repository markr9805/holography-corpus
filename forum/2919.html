I'm new to all of this; and would like to order the shoebox holography kit, and get started making my first holograms. Although I'm on the second floor of an apartment building, I really don't think that in itself will be a problem.

But I CAN hear the noise of crickets chirping, 24 hours a day.

That concerns me, because I have been researching holography, and understand that even the smallest sound or vibration can ruin a perfectly good hologram. Will I even be able to make a hologram under these conditions? Or will I have to do something drastic - like building a vacuum chamber - pumping all of the air out of it - and eliminating the noise in that manner?

Seriously, crickets should not be a problem. Generally airborne vibrations are difficult to exclude mainly because they come from the top down. Isolation tables, sand boxes etc can stop ground vibrations going from the ground up. Luckily, most airborne sounds are the wrong frequency. The setup cannot be vibrated at such a frequency that any component moves by more than about a quarter to half a wavelength of light over the exposure period . Generally for an exposure of a few seconds, this translates to sound frequencies at around 1 or 2 Hz. Crickets are probably above this range. Unfortunately aircraft aren't. If you do want to be super-paranoid about airborne vibrations, you could put a black cloth around your table suspended off the tabe. Maybe a scaffolding or something surrounding your table. If you move on from the shoebox and do more advanced stuff, the second floor of the aprtment may need to concern you, depending on various factors. Michael has a table on the second floor and he's made holograms on it, so it's do-able.
