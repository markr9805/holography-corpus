JohnFP Fri Jul 28, 2006 12:24 pm #13
Ok, I am going to have to interject.

I believe we could all learn from what he did with the "touched up" photographs. There is no physical way possible in the world to portray a hologram with a single still picture. What he did, give the viewers (holographers and non holographers) a sense of 3Dism.

The touched up Vase almost looks like a hologram for those of us lucky enough to have seen a hologram in person. For a person whom has never seen a hologram, a flat ordinary photo of a vase really doesn't do the art justice.

As holographers, we know that the image cannot go past the film. But how else do your represent 3D without a foreground reference?

So with that I have to stick up for Terry and think it's yet another excellent technique in trying to show what a hologram is with a flat picture.

BWDIK?

Well then I guess the questions are; Should the picture not be touched up to represent a 3D image and thus the unsavvy will never be interested, never care about and never know what a hologram really is. I wonder how long it will take him/her to realize when looking at the hologram from the side, that part not in front of the film disappears. I don't think this will disapoint that person too much.

Now on the other hand, if one was selling a hologram instead of just photographiing it as Terry is doing, I would say it is the reponsibilty of the seller to explain what is happening. If the picture is touched up as Terry's is and the hologram is trying to be sold. Then an explaination as to what photography does to a hologram, why the photo was touched up and what they potential buyer can expect should be made very clear. Ie. - This photo was altered to simmulate the 3D properties a hologram has as the 3D properties are lost when a regular 2D photograph is take of it. This hologram image does stick out in front of the plate by 2 inches but only when the film plate is directly behind the image. Or something like that.

I guess why I am defending his work is when I look at the vase, it actually makes me think I am almost looking at a real hologram. Then when I look at the beautiful work done by Fred U or Jon M as photos, they look just like beautiful flat spirographs of colors with no depth and no indication of what they truely are. It is only my experience with holograms that allows me to mentally envision what they may look like in real life.
