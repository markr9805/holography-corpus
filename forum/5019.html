Hans Thu Sep 01, 2005 12:17 pm #1
I am new to holograpy, so I don't know if everyone knows this already. I have found that the easiest way to detect of your laser diode mode hops is to reflect the beam of a cd. If you then look at the second reflection (from the center reflection) on the wall, you can see it make a distinct jump when the diode hops from one frequency to another. I found this much easier than looking at an interferometer because this way you know that you are only looking at the frequency of the laser and not at other phenomena such as vibrations. I have tried this with my D&S laser and found it does not mode hop in most power positions. I can make it hop when I adjust the power setting. The spot will then make a very distinct jump, depending on the frequency.

If the laser mode hops super fast, just rapidly move your eye. This way you can detect hops with a frequency up to about 70Hz. (Like the flickering on a tv screen.)

If you don't have the lens in front of the diode, the cd trick still works. Just look at the projected fringes on the wall, they make a distinct jump when the diode hops. My diode shows some fringes at the most outer edges of the beam. I can see the jumps in these fringes also without a cd, but they are a little harder to recognize.

Pls comment.
