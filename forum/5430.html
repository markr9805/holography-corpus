BobH Tue Mar 14, 2006 11:20 pm #2
Drift of fringes in one direction indicates something is settling or on its way to stability, not vibrating. Maybe the the table is heating or cooling. Maybe there is a grain of dirt under a component, which is gradually changing. Maybe a mirror is not properly attached to its mount, and is very gradually falling off.

I suggest you leave everything alone for a few days and see if the fringes are still moving. They may stop when everything has settled down. You can also put a small spot of 5-minute epoxy on everything. Between optic and mount, mount and post, post and base, base and table. Do one component at a time and you may find the unsettled critter!

JohnFP Wed Mar 15, 2006 2:25 pm #7
From your statement I cannot tell how thick your concrete is, but concrete, although it seems dry on the outside, takes quite a while to cure. During curing it is still releasing water and producing heat thus its dimensions are changing. I would let it set for up to a couple of weeks before jumping to any conclusions. But by all means, try the other suggestions. I personally don't use epoxy to stabilize my optics; hot glue works just as well and is easier to remove if the optic needs to be repositioned.

Also, if your table is just concrete and not reinforced concrete (with wire mesh and rebar) it is possible you will see that drift if one of the tubes has a small leak as the table, yes even concrete, will want to sag (bend). With reinforcement, the table should move as a whole entity and you will not see drift if one of the tubes is leaking air but you will see vibration when the tube is flat enough to allow the top to touch the base.

Try to set you interferometer up right on the floor if it is concrete. You should not see drift as you describe it but you may see vibrations. If you see the drift as you describe it, that is in one direction slowly, then it is probably from a drooping optic if you eliminate laser drift by Colin's points. If you don't see the drift on the floor then it is your table. Not that the table is vibrating or even moving, just that one part of the table is moving ever so slightly in one direction relative to the other part of the table.

Another test, set up the interferometer with the optics all as close as you can possibly set them up. If the table was causing the drift, then you should see less drift.

Play and have fun! Think how an interferometer works, and you should be able to move it around, fiddle with it, etc. to narrow down the source of the drift.

Thermal effects will not be smooth continuous movement, not usually anyway. Usually thermal or air movement affects on the fringes will be movement in on direction, maybe for a while or a short period, then a stabilization for a short time, or a reverse of movement, not a continual move in one direction. I have to believe that is sagging of something.

Some questions:

How thick is the concrete?

How old is the concrete (when was it poured)?

Is there any kind of reinforcement?

What is the length and width of your table top?

How close to the corners do you have the optics for the interferometer that is where do you place the optics for each leg of the interferometer?

Does the table rest on concrete or wood or other type of floor?

Which floor are you on?

Are there any windows or doors open at the time you are viewing your interferometer.
