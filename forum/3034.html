BobH Mon Jan 17, 2005 12:44 am #4
It will be a long time before ion lasers get cheaper because of DPSS lasers replacing them. There are very many in the field and it's much cheaper to buy a new tube than to replace the entire laser. Also, the flexability of wavelength choice with an ion laser will be very expensive to get with any other technology. The beam quality is usually better as well.

Right now I'm using both Sabre Fred and I-328 argon systems from Coherent, both having all the automatic alignment controls. They still need constant attention, but nothing like ion lasers without the bells & whistles. Thank you, Coherent, for Powertrack!!!
Dinesh Mon Jan 17, 2005 1:17 am #5
I used to use a Lexel 95 which was supposed to be "cheap". I suppose it was, relative to a new laser, but it cost roughly the equivalent of 1500 pints of half-way decent beer! It also required approx two hours to warm up, attention every two to three hours after that and a constant fight against mode hopping. Eventually it had to be taken down to the fourth shoulder. A green lamp with a pinhole would have made life easier! I then bought a Spectra 165 that was half the price when bought. This worked great - for a while. Then it died, resurrected itself - died - resurrected itself - etc.. Finally I threw it at Cambridge Lasers, along with a great deal of money. They fixed it fine (apart from an initial problem which was more my fault than theirs. Nobody told me thay clamped the d**mn tube for shipment ). It's now working great. Thank you, Cambridge. But, it ennded up being costlier than the previous "cheap" laser. I must agree thast the ability to switch wavelengths is greeat. It means I intend to stick with Ar for the time being.
