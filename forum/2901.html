Ed Wesly Fri Nov 19, 2004 1:47 pm #2
This looks like the new improved version of what I have used. The old one you could take off the center post. The only trick is to use just enough pressure from the spring-loaded side to hold the plate without putting stress on it.

It was designed for PCB's, and worked fine for the old 1.5 mm thick Agfa Glass, but the grooves on the sides of the blades might have a problem with thicker glass.

aghisays Fri Nov 19, 2004 2:08 pm #4
I use one of those in my H2 copy setup for holding the master with good results. When I was using it with film-sandwiches I got missed results because the grooves are too small and with long exposures I got film movement. Now that I'm using film laminated to glass (2mm glass) it works really well even with long exposures (2 min or more).

Andres.
eric343 Fri Nov 19, 2004 8:58 pm #5
It's great for use with plates -- it's incredibly stable. You do need a sturdy way of mounting it; it won't work on sand tables unless you're willing to accept some shifting around while inserting and removing the plate.

It can be used with sandwiched film, but I wouldn't recommend it. It's a hassle.

MichaelH Tue Nov 23, 2004 6:11 pm #8
I use the OPCLR material from Kapco and laminate film to glass for my transmission masters. I generally haven't done much with the reflections because of paranoia about dust getting between the film and glass. Once the film is down, you can't remove it without ruining the film and image.

For masters this is less of a concern because a single speck of dust generally won't ruin a master.
