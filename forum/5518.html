Ed Wesly Mon May 01, 2006 10:55 am #8
Yeah the $10k is really hard to understand, since you can get complete projectors for a 1/10th of that. Plus what is not so clear from the blurbs is that you need the other card, the high speed one if you want to do continuous tones! For a total of 16 grand! And they don't help you with the projection lens design!

Why does TI do this I don't know. I guess if you were a big projector company and wanted to investigate the technology without ripping apart the competition's boxes it might make sense. But we got one of the basic packages at CFC and it became a political nightmare, because someone thought they were just downloading information but they placed an order for a 30 day trial. And the management didn't care, but we never got to playing with it within the 30 days, so a bill came. Then they went nutty.

Then playing with it it was discovered that this developer package was not capable of grey scale. Why on earth would they ship something like this? Anyone making a projector would want grey scale. But I guess that the $6k auxiliary board would make those people happy, but still, that's 6 projectors!

Recently I took apart a Compaq projector and it didn't work after I reassembled it. But doggone it, I never plugged it in before disassembly to check how well it worked! So I don't know if I screwed it up or not.

The reason why at CFC we got a the developer's package is that we wanted to got to the next larger format of chip, from the 600 by 800 pixels to 720 by 1024 or whatever. The lower res is still in use to this day (even though I am not working there anymore) and it is fine for images up to about 6" by 8", when the pixels start to be really evident.

If anyone here wants to attempt using one of these devices from a projector go for it! The only thing that might be a stumbling block is the projection lens. What comes with the projectors is a zoom lens, and I am afraid that there might be bad artifacts from internal bouncing especially with a coherent source. But I haven't tried putting a laser beam through one, but a couple of achromats would make a decent system for the holo lab, per what was used at CFC.

The reason I am dubious about the projector lens is that when we built a stereogram system at the School of the Art Institute that was based on16 mm movie film, not digital screens, we always got nasty coherent artifacts using Canon or Nikon Zoom lenses for 35 mm cameras. Although these optics have lost of A/R coatings on them, they aren't necessarily tuned for He-Ne wavelengths. We finally settled on fixed focal length Canon lenses, 50 mm for 16" wide landscapes, 35 mm for 16" tall portraits.

If anybody needs help on such a project I am available.
