Ed Wesly Mon Aug 22, 2011 9:27 am #6
> They both work in the same way anyhow since they're both reverible bleaches

The Pyrochrome is a reversal bleach, which is what I think you are trying to say, while the Fe EDTA is a rehalogenating bleach.

Eventually the Pyrochrome dichromate reversal bleaches (and there are dichromate based rehalogenating bleaches, the Kodak R-10 series for example) get so loaded with dissolved silver compounds that they can't hold any more. Dropping some table salt in them will precipitate some nice chunks of silver chloride. Store in the dark or you can watch them print out in the light! And maybe you can still keep using the bleach!

The Ferric EDTA bleaches like exposure to air! It regenerates the oxidizer. What kills these bleaches is too much alkali developer getting carried over from not washing well between them. Maybe some more acid to lower the pH might help if the bleach slows down. And I doubt if all of the Br ions would get used up with the relatively small amount of material that gets put through the solutions in a typical holo lab. I've got some made up that is a few years old.

The organic oxidizer based rehalogenating bleaches do go bad after a day or so, like the beloved PBQ and the PBU series, although with the latter the persulfate will keep regenerating the oxidizer for longer life.

Yes, that's true. I never used a Fe EDTA bleach so I always assumed it was a reversible bleach. I just looked it up and it is a rehal bleach. I use PBU, which I put in a fridge (a separate fridge in the lab just for chemicals!) I generally make up a litre of the stuff, use it 200 mls at a time, and throw it out at the end of the day. At the next shoot, I replenish from the fridge. Once or twice I've had to develop very large plates for which I've had to use 2 litres of bleach and developed just two or three plates.
