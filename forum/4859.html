With those Tmax plates, plain fixing and reloading produced heavy fogging starting at the ascorbic acid bath (last step in the Blyth process). Exposed and developed, density was higher in the exposed area than in the area masked by the holder. This led me to believe that there was a sensitizer remaining in the emulsion.

Redoing the same process with less exposure, less development, and reversal bleaching produced a dim but visible hologram with extensive white haze, apparently a result of the fogging.

Thinking that the problem sensitizer might be destroyed in normal development processes, I subjected a plate to fixing, development (D76, 15 min), rehalogenating bleach/fix (farmer's reducer), more fixing, reversal bleaching (R-9), with washing between steps and extended washing at the end, and then a soak in a carbonate based "dehardener" bath, followed by more washing. Fogging was substantially reduced, but still present in the final plate. A portion of the plate had been treated with a very dilute copper sulfate/potassium bromide solution (leroy halogenating bath) after sensitization, with no appreciable difference in result. The plate produced good density and looked promising, but no holographic image is to be found in it. The culprit may be excessive grain growth or one of the many abuses I subjected the emulsion to, or that I may not have allowed the plate to dry long enough before exposure.

I have two plates up for my next test. They were bathed in a fresh solution of plain thiosulfate, washed, bathed in a borax solution (mistake on my part, Ed Wesley mentions sodium metaborate and I grabbed the tetraborate[?]), soaked in methanol (re somebody here, Ed?), washed again and well rinsed in distilled water.

On those last two plates the water sheeted off the emulsion rather nicely, and as the water receded it could be seen that the emulsion thickness was changing as it was uncovered. I'm taking that as a positive sign, but only the final results will tell.

I'm thinking that clean fixation and a dehardening bath may be the way to go for now. A washed-out swelling bath might be good too, to try to remove any physical "memory" of the preexisting silver grains (i.e., "holes") in the hardened emulsion. Gotta kill off residual sensitizing dyes too.

Yes, I know that I should be starting out by following the instructions and coating a plate or three before I try other stuff, but I'm just like that.

> Gary Deal: This led me to believe that there was a sensitizer remaining in the emulsion.

I guess that's highly unlikely – given the sensitizing dye is attached to the silver grains (and those grains being fixed out). However, having read a couple of photography/lithography related patents I understand some emulsions to have additional non-sensitizing dyes to lower scatter etc., matting agents and alike.

> I'm thinking that clean fixation and a dehardening bath may be the way to go for now.

Unless you want to carry out special techniques like SHSG, I guess hardened layers don't pose any problems for the diffusion method. Perhaps on the contrary, hardened layers tend to slow diffusion speed, favoring hence smaller grains...
