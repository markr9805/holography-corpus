Hi all

Up to now i've only ever used Colorholographic BB plates to make Densiyuk holograms. Sadly I don't have the space or stability required for split beam work. However, i'm quite satisfied with Denisyuk SBR's for the time being.

I fancied having a go with some photopolymer film and note that Sergio sells green sensitive film photopolymer (available in 1m x 20cm samples) at $150.

Has anyone ever used this in a 'Hobby' environment and if so what were the results? Is it suitable for Denisyuk SBR holograms. What problems am I likely to come across if I decide to give this a try? Is it really as easy to process as it sounds (expose to strong white light)?

Perhaps i'm missing something here, but I can't understand why there's so much interest in DCG when Photopolymers seem to provide an ideal solution. Why aren't more people in the hobby sector experimenting with developing photopolymers? I appreciate that DuPont & Co like to keep their formulas a secret and restrict it's use for security holograms, but surely if it's so good more people in the hobby sector would be using it for it's simplicity of processing.

Finally, if I decide to go down the photopolymer route and give Sergio's film a try can I expect to get good, clean and bright SBR holograms using my 100mw Compass 315m?? What exposure times am I likely to need for a piece of film say 10cm square?

Any hints, tips, advice would be much appreciated as i'm a little confused as to why no one else is going down this route.

Cheers

Jem, you said it all, the people on the hobby sector usually need use the film in a Denisyuk simple setup, this is the most difficult requirement for a photopolymer, even our model that bleaches upon exposure due the necessary dye absorption, so the model is more adequate at H2 level with some material tested at photopolymer printing machines in China. (actual sensitivity in green is around ~50mJ/cm², red one is ~3 to 10mJ/cm² index modulation = 0.006 - to be published by one Czech team)

200um is the upper coating thickness limit and we were using ~150um to get results, this is why we are changing the structure a little to ~20um due high costs. However the liquid emulsion did play another league with the first article in the photonics field (Photo - Prague 08) to be published in August, it will release the material into a solid base to be developed since the dry one uses the same principle as SM-TR series.

We are finding our way into scientific community and holographic printing marketing but still aiming the valuable hobby people to simplify the holographer life, however nobody can hurry holography as Jeff said, and we are passing first the science evaluation tests to turn our material a serious promising for the Industry.

I would state that this route is difficult, but rewards are waiting for those with time and money to expend.
