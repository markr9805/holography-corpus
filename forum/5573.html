Stability? Ease? Table size restriction?....Not sure but here it is.

I just set this up last night as I wanted to start playing with H1 transmissions in DCG. I hope the illustration is clear enough.

The beam is an overhead beam that comes in from the left and hits a nice size front surface flat mirror. I am using no collimator at this time but the spatial filter is about 9 feet away from the plate via this mirror. I will see how much distortion is had from not using a collimator for the H1 reference beam and H2 object beam. Anyway the expanded beam hits the overhead transfer mirror at the top right.

For the H1 creation the light is reflected down and only the parts of the light that are marked with a green colored arrow is used. Top to bottom describing the green arrows...Top-object beam light via another mirror, Next down-Reference beam, Next down-Object light via another mirror, Last- Direct object light. All other light is carded off.

For the H2 creation, after the transfer mirror in the upper right, view the red arrows. Top red arrow is the H2 reference beam and the Bottom red arrow is the H2 object beam that illuminates the H1. All other light is cadred off.

So, I can make an H1, process, flip the plate and place it back into the plate holder and make an H2 reflection from the H1 without any table modifications. Also, the entire set up is only as wide as my plateholder (about 16") and only 6 feet long.

Ratios are controlled by slightly moving the upper right transfer mirror such that the center of the beam is directed slightly up or down, thus decreasing or increasing the ratios.

The piece that looks like a bridge from the H1 to H2 is simply a modified flat piece of aluminum that rests on the L beam at the top of my H1 plateholder and then rests directly on the glass plate of the H2 for a very nice static placement. It is not perfectly represented but this static bridge allows me to move the H2 plate closer and further from the H1 by about 3 inches total.

JohnFP Sun May 28, 2006 3:09 pm #7
Yes, I am lucky in that I never even think about matching path lengths. I think my coherence length is in the meters.

Dave, I am very pleased with the first tests. I only had time to do a few exposures. The tranmission holograms came out very bright but the had a hazy glow around the outside of the object for the longer exposures. For the shorter exposures the glow was gone but the object was dim. I am assuming that the ratio of reference to object was low. For this first set of tests I did not alter the ref/obj ratio as I wanted to see the effect of exposure times. I think I have narrowed down the exposure so now I will modify the ratios but moving the beam. Of course a different ratio will mean a different exposure time. But I believe I can zero in on what is best.

What I think will work really well if I can produce DCG masters is that covering the master with a glass plate will not produce advers effects in a Tans H1 to Ref H2 as it would with a Ref H1 to Ref H2. My Ref H1's have to be used with a cover plate and the emulsion facing the H2 otherwise the reflection off the glass produces undesirables unless exactly at Brewsters.

I normally don't like Brewsters as that angle of replay is to sharp for me.

Also, I used 200 bloom Bovine instead of 250 Bone.
