That looks like a photopolymer hologram to me. They can be pretty bright. There are lots of tricks to making bright holograms. One is to only use the field of fiew needed. In a hand held hologram the field of view can be very small as you can move the hologram till you see an image. In a stationary hologram you need more field of view so tall and short viewers can see an image.

> koliaco: Sergio wrote:Seems a photopolymer hologram for me, if it's a film\Yes this hologram is a film, so probably photopolymer. So is photopolymer hologram generally brighter then DCG and has wider viewing angle? what are the angles of view should I expect from red DIY DCG and diffraction %?

MB-DCG holograms can be very bright.

Diffraction efficiency is only one part here. Bandwidth would be another thing to consider. The self-processing (“real-time”) photopolymers currently available, have a relatively limited bandwidth. So the perceived image brightness may look far less bright than DCG. However, there have been a couple of photopolymer systems that required liquid processing similar to DCG – Polaroid's DMP-128 for example. They used to have very high index modulation. Such holograms may be fairly broadband and appear very bright.

p.s. Now that I realize we are talking about Jeff's MBDCG in this thread. To answer your question: yes. MBDCG is very bright. It is the brightest formula that I have ever tried. But it does require some trial and error to get it just right. But once you get it right you will have holograms that are so bright that there is no need to paint the rear side black.

Jeff's diffusion method is also very bright (as bright as the brightest commercial films) with the added advantage that you can make a stock of plate and store them in the fridge for later use. But no Silver Halide film will ever be able to compete in brightness with DCG or MBDCG.

Once you fix a plate all of the silver is removed. The idea is to use PFG-01 as a piece of gelatin on glass without having to learn coating. Coating is a lot harder than one would think until you have all the bugs worked out. After the bugs are worked out it is not too bad.
Hans Fri Jan 30, 2009 12:15 pm #23
> you did mension about using PFG-01 plates as shortcut. So it still did not imply that I could use silver halide emulsion for diffusion method to make brighter holograms?

PFG-01 are very nice candidates for the shortcut method where you pre-load the AgNO3 into the emulsion that has already been coated. Because PFG-01 has such a very very hard coating it can be forcefully squeegeed well without scratching the gelatin. It is a little more difficult to make your own coating that is both very flat and hard. PFG-01 is not expensive and it is a good way to get your hands wet with the diffusion method. It is a great feeling to first make a hologram with normal PFG-01 and then see the intense brightness on a upgraded PFG-01 from diffusion method.
