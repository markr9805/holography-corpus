MichaelH Sat Jan 15, 2005 2:41 pm #5
I use a sheet of glass for all my squeegeeing.

I attached rubber feet to one side to keep it from sliding and use the other side for my working surface.

After taking the film out of the rinse or TEA bath I place it emulsion-down and wipe across it once or twice with the squeegee. I then wipe it down with a paper towel to dry it out.

I carefully lift the film off the glass and while holding the film, squeegee the glass, put the film down emulsion up, squeegee the film once and then put it someplace to dry.

I've been meaning to do it for some time so after I get my lab clean again I'll take some pictures and do a full writeup of the process. I've been using this method for more than a year and haven't had any squeegee problems when I've stuck to it (got careless a couple of times).

Dinesh Sat Jan 15, 2005 7:30 pm #9
I've squeegeed BB520 and the blue BB plates (440?) after TEA but before exposure. I lay the plates glass side down (I also clean the glass side later) on a paper towel. I then dip my finger into the appropriate liquid (photoflo/TEA) and run my finger along the edge of the squeegee. This gives it a smooth surface. I hold on to the edge of the paper towel just above the direction I'm squegeeingI then splay my fingers along the length of the squeegee so as to get uniform pressure and drag it across in one smooth movement, with a relatively light touch. If the movement isn't smooth I dunk the plate back into the photoflo for a few seconds and start again. I repeat for a second squeeging. The important things I've found is to smooth the edge of the squeegee with the "finger method" and make sure the drag is smooth, continuous and uniform. Since I drag with a light touch, I've found doing it twice gives a smooth finish with no scratching.
Tom B. Sat Jan 15, 2005 10:48 pm #10
> Pascal: Hello,But can you use ethylalcohol also after the pretreatment with TEA on BB640- plates and before the exposure or should this only be used after the development/bleaching?

In answer to the original question, I think an alcohol bath after pretreatment would also remove the TEA, eliminating any color shift, though the emulsion would probably remain sensitized. It might be, though, that the alcohol would also remove essential dyes or sensitizers from the emulsion, rendering it insensitive. The squeegee by comparison leaves a thin uniform layer of TEA+water which, when the water evaporates, leaves the emulsion swelled by a certain amount.

If no color shift was wanted, it might or might not work

Dinesh Mon Jan 17, 2005 5:10 pm #12
I'm not sure about the squegee theory. You'd expect to see an elongated dragged out smear, rather than a spot. It looks more like either contamination in the processing batths or lack of agitation in the developer. You may not see it in the wet plate because in the wet state the plate is expanded. These effects may only show up when the plate is dry. It could also be on the object. It could be reorded incomformities on the object that don't show up beecause they're clear but with a different index. Are there blobs of plastic covering your object? It could also be a mismatch between the various chemicals you use, causing "local reactions" that dump reaction products as specks all over the plate.

A couple of tests you might try:

Shake vigorously in the developer

Don't use TEA, dry with your usual meth baths and dry with a hair dryer.

Use a uniform white background. This way, you can tell if it's just on the object or the whole plate. Also, this'll tell if the distribution is continuous or varies towards the edge of the plate.
Tom B. Tue Jan 18, 2005 7:26 am #13
I just remembered reading some advice about always very carefully mopping up (and wiping clean with wet cloth) any chemical spills in the lab. The reason being that when the the spill dries, it becomes dust that gets picked up by air currents and can get deposited on emulsions, causing hard-to-diagnose spots. This is also why it's a bad idea to mix developers from dry chemicals anywhere near the lab.

I somehow doubt that's your problem (too many spots), but it's good advice anyway.

Dinesh Wed Jan 19, 2005 11:50 pm #27
I use a hair dryer for all holograms - tests and finals. With tests, I just dunk it in water for less than 30 seconds for the final water wash and blast it with hot air. I don't particularly care that the emuslion cracks, since I'm only testing for geometry and de anyway, and I don't care if it prints out, since it goes into the trash after a only a few minutes. For finals, I water wash for 5 minutes or longer and hair-dryer dry it more carefully from a greater distance. The ironic thing is that the reason I dry from a greater distance is to avoid dust being blown onto the film. I've never had as bad a problem as Andy so I didn't recognise the symptoms. Anyway, once I'm satisfied it's OK, I put it back into the water for another 5 miutes, into photo-flo for about a minute and let it air dry overnight. So, basically, the hair dryer for final holograms is confirmation that no baffle got in the way, that no motion occurred and that no object lighting moved. Since we spend the better part of a day (sometimes more!) to get the object lighting just right, I'd hate to take it all down and set up for the next object without a quick check!

In terms of the present problem, is it possible to hit the air flow onto a flat or curved object which will re-direct the air flow onto the plate/film. Hopefully all the dust will get trapped on the first surface. Maybe even cover the first surface with something to trap the dust. Perhaps spray-glue a piece of glass and dust on powdered Al(OH) or CaCO_3 .
