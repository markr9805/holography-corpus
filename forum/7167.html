JohnFP Thu Feb 21, 2008 10:31 pm #3
To get rid of most astigmatism, you need to use a reference beam that is the conjugate of the replay beam. Use a collimated reference beam for your H1 as you can mimic the collimated beam for your H2 Object beam.

Tom B. Fri Feb 22, 2008 4:05 am #5
Beautiful work!

Graham Saxby in "Practical Holography" 3rd ed. p.182 mentions (paraphrased summary) that shrinkage during processing (which skews the fringe planes) will induce astigmatism in transmission holograms. Shrinkage of about 10% results in 5 degrees of image displacement and size reduction of about 10% - but only parallel to the plane containing the object and reference beams and not the other way, hence vertical/horizontal astigmatism.

He mentions that one old-time trick used by portraitists to compensate for the shrinkage was to shoot the H1 with a pulsed ruby laser and do the H2 transfer with a shorter wavelength HeNe.

If shrinkage is indeed your problem, maybe instead you could slightly post-swell the H1 to restore the original fringe angles?

Hans Fri Feb 22, 2008 5:06 pm #7
You need a second parabolic mirror for your H1-H2 transfer. The object beam just needs to be parallel, just as the reference beam that you used for making the H1 master. The H2 reference beam needs to be the exact opposite from the beam that you will use to re-play the H2. So, instead of it being diverging, it needs to be converging with the point of origin (focal point of the beam) at the same orientation and distance as where the lamp will be that you will use to re-play the H2 hologram. However, if you preswell the H2 plate for re-play in a different colour, the beams need to be adjusted for that as well (or the lamp you use to view the hologram needs to be in a different place) because shrinkage after development will displace the focal point of your hologram.

A problem here is that your H2 reference beam parabolic mirror will need to be very very big. So a more simple way to minimize the problem is to use a slightly converging beam from a not-so-big H2 reference beam parabolic mirror and re-play the hologram from a point source much further away.

Or use a parallel reference beam in your H2 setup and somehow re-play with nearly collimated white light.
Ed Wesly Fri Feb 22, 2008 9:05 pm #8
Konrad wrote:

> I though that the problem introduce parabolic mirror with quite high angle between beams (about 40 degrees).

This is the source of the astigmatism. If you are using a parabolic telescope mirror, they are designed to work pretty much on-axis; coming in at 20 degrees off normal and reflecting the same amount will undoubtedly make an astigmatic output, like the vertical axis might be parallel and the horizontal might be focusing somewhere far from the mirror, either as a real image or virtual. The trick is to come into these mirrors as close to the normal as possible and have clearance for the optics.

I didn't really notice the tell tale signs of astigmatism in the images, like highlights becoming plus signs, but you are right, to see them in person will make them more apparent.
