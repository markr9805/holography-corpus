MichaelH Thu Mar 03, 2005 5:38 pm #6
> Grey: I thought that film might be difficult for a newbie like myself, due to stability issues. I figured that I might want to reduce the number of variables that might cause problems for me. You think not?

Yes and no. You'll get that answer a lot in holography. You'll also hear "it depends".

For a newbie I'd recommend starting out with small, say 2x2", pieces of film since you've still got the basic process to work through. That size really doesn't present much of a stability challenge when clamped between two pieces of window glass that are slightly larger.

Go to the hardware store (assuming you don't know how to cut glass) and get two pieces of 3x3" glass cut. Take some masking tape (the blue painters tape is perfect) and wrap it around the edges of each piece but don't overlap the tape (you can clean up the overlap with an xacto knife). If you look halfway down this page at the images of a new plateholder you'll see what I'm writing about (I hope). [http://www.dragonseye.com/holography/2004_09.html](http://www.dragonseye.com/holography/2004_09.html)

When you put the film in this holder, it will be held evenly and you shouldn't get any film movement (especially at 2x2" film size). You don't really need the tape now except to keep the laser light out of the edge of the glass, but you definitely will need it when you go to larger size film.

Dinesh Thu Mar 03, 2005 6:27 pm #9
The film vs glass problem is really dependant on your creativity and desire to play. So, I guess, "it depends" Actually, what it depends on is the plate/film holder. Film needs to be supported on all four edges, so when you do sandwich it between glass plates, you need to support and hold the glass plates on all 4 edges. Not particularly difficult, but something ot bear in mind. Glass is rigid, so a small piece of glass (say, the 2.5 x 2.5, which Integraf sells for student work) needs only to be held along one edge. A simple groove with a couple of screws is enough. However,glass is expensive and film is cheap. Film can be easily cut, so you can cut small 1" x 1" pieces to get exposure tests before going on to glass. You'll find that every hologram, 20 days or 20 years later, will always need a few exposure tests because every setup and every object is different. For what it's worth, I have a U-shaped plate holder with two screws along the bottom edge, and two along each of the side edges. (I believe Colin has a pic of it on his site). After setup, I cut small rectangular pieces of film (approx 2" x 3", but it depends on the object), sandwich it between two glass sheets and shoot exposure tests. Once I believe I have all the tets done, I "go to glass". I put in a glass plate and shoot one. Other points: film can easily be processed with a small quantity of chemicals, since it can be rolled into a cylinder and dipped in a narrow tube, glass doesn't curl in the developer and so the entire surface is developed. In the end, I think I'd agree with Michael that it's better to start with film fo exactly the same reason: economics. You'll be doing a lot of "playing" and it could get expensive!.

As for 01 vs 03, I'd say go with 01. there's no loss in brightness. I've made holograms with both 01 and 03 and actually the 01's are brighter. This depends on holographic parameters (exposure developing chemicals, ratio etc) and has nothing to do with emulsion differences. However, 01 is more forgiving and easier to handle. Get the Integraf kit, it contains everything you need to start.

MichaelH Wed Mar 09, 2005 10:46 am #24
> eric343: So is the consensus that PFG-01 is better for Denisyuks? That's quite odd, since Integraf says that -03M is better!

"better" is a highly relative and dangerous term to use around holographers 03 will give you a sharper and perhaps brighter image and so many would say it's "better".

It depends on what your priorities are. Here are mine for comparison...

o Cost. I'm not made of money, so I'm going to consider the lower priced option when purchasing film. PFG-01 on film is a highly economical option when compared to the alternatives.

o Sensitivity. I need something reasonably sensitive because I'm shooting in an upstairs room and want to keep my exposures short.

o Image quality. PFG-01 isn't the best in terms of final image quality but it is very good and when compared against the other requirements, it comes out on top.

o Ease of use. My time is limited and I need to make use of all of it. PFG-01 film is simple to cut, process and mount. PFG-03 has always given me problems (I've tried two batches) when processing as the emulsion is extremely soft. I don't particularly like working with plates as they aren't re-usable in the way that self-mounted film on glass is.

I recommend that you make a list of what's important to you and purchase based on those requirements.

Dinesh Thu Mar 10, 2005 6:12 am #35
[quote:8d9fe256b4="eric343"][quote:8d9fe256b4="Dinesh"]Indded, the concept of "ratio" is not well explained, since "ratio" is useless if the object and reference miss each other - a fact usually not covered in books.[/quote:8d9fe256b4]

What exactly do you mean?

If I'm making a Denisyuk, then the various books (Saxby et al) generally have the laser beam incident at >45 degrees to the plate; so that if I'm making a hologram of an object, the majority of the reflected light would indeed miss the reference beam if the object isn't right up against the plate.[/quote:8d9fe256b4]

Exactly! That's why I advocate a painting the object a flat white and not shooting metal objects. The object beam and the reference beam must meet in order to interfere. If you place a shiny coin an inch behind the plate and bring in the reference at 50 degrees, the object beam, ie the reflected light off the coin, will completely miss the reference beam and there'll be no interference. It's best to have a uniformly diffuse object close enough to the plate so that the brightest part of the reflected light from the object is co-incident with the brightest part of the reference beam. I'd use a card with a hole in it to test this ( [http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2122](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2122) ). The other reason to have the object close is to minimise the effect of coherence length.
