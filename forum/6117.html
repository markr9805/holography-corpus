Joe Farina Thu Mar 22, 2007 3:42 pm #4
> Colin Kaminski: I am eager to set up 3 color hologrpahy.

I decided not to worry about my lack of blue, at least for now. I'm glad I took than attitude, becasue just setting up two lasers is more of a pain than I thought. I think Kaveh mentioned that getting that third laser into place makes it even more difficult.

It would be very interesting to see more two-color holograms. They seem to be about as rare as three-color ones. I think our resident color-holography expert (Kaveh) mentioned that a great deal of color variation is possible with only two properly-chosen wavelengths, and the research I've done confirms this.

Argons are a triple pain, and power supplies can fail. The lab will need to be designed around an argon laser, considering requirements for water, three-phase power, etc. The solid-state Melles Griot 58-BLD-605 (~400mW at 457nm) would in my opinion be a better choice for dye-senstized DCG, but at that short wavelength scattering may be a problem with silver-halide materials (?), so a slightly longer wavelength may be better in that case, but it must be shorter than 488nm. As I recall, there is a line somewhere around 476nm for argon which should be good, but power is rather low.

So I would suggest just taking the plunge with red and green first, to get the hang of things. I'm finding that combining just two lasers (with a spatial filter in mind) is not nearly as easy as it seems.
