BobH Wed Mar 26, 2008 1:45 pm #3
I recommend the table design you originally used. Although good results can come from both, sand will definitely limit the complexity of your work, is bad for optics and less stable, and prevents consistency of results. A few will tout the acceptability of a sand table, but that's a few (literally two or three) among the thousands of optics workers who all use steel topped tables.

I don't think it needs to be "as heavy as possible" but it should have some nice mass to it above the inner tubes.

I think Colin did wonderful research on Mass vs. frequency dampening and hopefully he or I or someone can find the old thread. You may search for a few key words (mass, dampening, resonance, etc.). I dont remember if it is in the new forum or the old archived section.

Jeffrey Weil Thu Mar 27, 2008 2:25 am #7
Here's an often overlooked option. Buy one!

Really, search around with people in the area of the state your in and craigs list and ebay and whatever else you can think of. Lots of industries use these tables and when they don't need them anymore it's a huge problem to just throw them out let alone do something constructive with them. Selling them is very difficult for a few different reasons.

I have a friend in holography that did this and in a few months had a 2.5 foot thick 5 x 12 Newport table with active Newport legs and the best enclosure you've ever seen delivered to his house and installed in his garage for 3 thousand dollars. What a deal. Moving that table with a professional moving company would cost that much alone.

Tables are often just given away if you come and pick it up yourself. They are just happy to get it off their hands. I could go on with other stories but I'll let others do that. Hey BobH. I bet you have a tale or two like this.

I don't know your time frame but think about this approach. There is nothing like working on a proper surface. I've made many tables and have had seamless steel tops. Having it be stainless and tapped makes all the difference. Its magnetic stainless so you can still use your mag mounts, and having all those holes is good for more than just clamping stuff down. You can do all kinds of indexing tricks with them too.
