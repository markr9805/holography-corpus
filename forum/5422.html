I went to that site and then asked for a sample from the US distributor. It doesn't sound like a chunk is forthcoming.

Here is the US site:

[http://www.brookviewtechnologies.com/](http://www.brookviewtechnologies.com/)

There are a lot of pictures of these screens in use! But just vague descriptions of how they work, just punting about how HOE's are involved.

It's good to see this technology in action! About 10 years ago I interviewed at Physical Optics Corporation for a job, and they demo'ed a screen based on this type of technology. They had "the best screen we could buy at the camera store" and projected a video image on it. Then they turned on the lights, and of course, it got overwelmed by the ambient.

Then they dropped the diffuse screen, and behind it on the wall was one of their screens. And it was nice and bright!

The trick to how these things work is shown diagramatically in one of the HoloPro literature pages. It's kind of like a rainbow hologram in that the light from the reference beam is directed into a narrow viewing zone. What would happen with the POC screen and undoubtedly with these is if you are not in the viewing zone the image disappears, like when you get out of the viewing zone of a rainbow hologram.

I had seen the POC screen in 1996, so this company is the first to really take it to market! But what's strange is that they say that these screens are made on Agfa film, SkyPro it's called, and they have an exclusive on it! Kinda makes you wonder what will happen when these screens are exposed to direct sunlight for a while! Plus with AgfaPhoto filing for bankruptcy...
