Joe Farina Wed Dec 20, 2006 4:27 pm #2
It seems to me that, everything else being equal, that MBDCG would be far more sensitive at 670nm as compared to 633nm. (According to the absorption graph of Methylene Blue in the Sigma-Aldrich Handbook of Stains, Dyes, and Indicators. By the way I highly recommend this book, written by Floyd J. Green, 1990, and published by Aldrich.)

But my own experience with diode lasers on MBDCG (years ago) was VERY unfavorable due to mode-hopping during 5 minute exposures. It was frustrating becasue I was easily producing bright holograms (as you mentioned, Jeff's system "always worked") but the holograms were severely banded due to mode-hopping (i.e., wavelength instability or whatever). Hans has been able to do it, but my previous experience has made me extremely skeptical of diodes for long exposures. Others may disagree. Honestly, I would prefer using a diode if it could be relied upon, so I really have nothing against diodes in principle. But I have been following the diode phenomenon for a long time, and I don't think it's for me.

But according to the graph in my book, MB absorbs much, much more at 670nm as compared to 633nm. The lambda max is given as 661nm. Good luck, but personally I would get a more powerful HeNe if you need more power, and stick with a good thing!

Joe
Hans Wed Dec 20, 2006 5:22 pm #3
Here is a graph of the absorbtion spectrum of Methylene blue: [http://omlc.ogi.edu/spectra/mb/index.html](http://omlc.ogi.edu/spectra/mb/index.html).

From this graph it seems that it is more like one long mountain of absorption that starts at about 600nm and ends at about 680nm. So I think it should be no problem.

Several people have had great success with laser diodes. [http://www.analogtechnologies.com/products.htm](http://www.analogtechnologies.com/products.htm) sell a diode laser that they guarantee to be single line. Kris Meerloo makes most of his superb holograms with their laser. Opening their unit reveiled it to be nothing more than a diode kept at temperature with a TEC controller.

I have made 30 minute exposures on MBDCG with my TEC controlled laser (the diode is a HL6512MG) without problems.

From tests that others have done, it seems that at powers over 50mW, it becomes difficult to keep the diode from mode hopping. But below those powers it is actually quite easy to keep the diode stable. Before I made the TEC controlled laser I used the D&S laser as described by Frank DeFreitas. I made 30 minute exposures with that one also, without mode hops (as long as the temperature in my lab stayed constant).

Hans Wed Dec 20, 2006 11:17 pm #9
I only have two pics that I made in June. The photo's don't do the holograms justice because I have not mastered making good photo's of holograms yet. [http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4583](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=4583)

The exposure times were long because I had a problem with gelatin detaching from the glass at that time (I did not pre-treat the glass with Silane). Longer exposure times allowed me to make the gelatin harder and subsequently stay on the glass longer during washes.

For the D&S laser I used no kind of radiator at all. I used to find a mode hop free power setting and then make the hologram. The problem with that kind of setup was that once the diode started hopping, it would never stop hopping until a new power setting was chosen. It seems that this type of laser (with measured output power feedback into the power supply) has a tendency to want to stay in mode hop regions. Another problem with this laser was that in even mode hop free regions, the holograms would be less bright with longer exposures. I believe this has to do with slow temperature drift of the diode that causes a slow drift in the wavelength. I have had none of these problems with my TEC controlled laser. It is a fire and forget laser and needs only a few minutes to become stable.

Others have found that low power laser diodes are more stable, but the problem you would have here with MBDCG is that with such long exposures (1-1.5 h) is that the changes of movement become to large. Also, in the beginning you will need to make many exposures to get a feel for the process. With such long exposures it will take a very long time to become proficient with it.

I think that for MBDCG a comfortable laser power level is 20mW or more.
