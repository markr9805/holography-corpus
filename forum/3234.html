Are they uniformly scattered throughout the plate or just on the edges? We had someone recently who had spots uniformly scattered throughout the plate. Michael finally found the problem to be a dirty hair dryer.

Other possibilities are contamination in the developing chemicals, smudges on the film before exposure, or dust falling on the plate during development. Do they appear brighter at some angle, ie are they brighter when the plate is viewed at a glancing angle? Have you tried looking at the plate at a glancing angle in the safelight before exposure?

Gary Deal Fri Mar 18, 2005 8:41 pm #9
Ok, a totally different direction here, but those spots look a bit like someone at the factory was spraying lubricant into a squeaky part of the film transport machinery. Even if film wasn't running through at the time it could have gotten on the transport rollers and transferred front to back when the film was spooled. The random placement along the edge would make sense with that.

Oil spots on the emulsion would probably keep developer and/or TEA from soaking in and doing their job, and they could start as very small spots. Is there any repeating uniformity to the spots? Since they don't appear across the film, it's probably not the developer or treatments, though "mustard-on-fingers" is an age-old darkroom error ("just-ate-sandwich"?)

Carefully examine a piece of film for spots or oil before shooting and shoot with no pretreatment, if they still show up (and you don't have small amounts of a condiment on your fingers) there's a good chance that it's coming that way. The next thing to try would probably be a (pre-)bath in acetone if it won't eat the film base, as a degreaser. If it's oil of some kind I don't think you're going to be able to remove it, though you might be able to work around it. Maybe with some alcohol in the pre-treat and developer?
