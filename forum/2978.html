Danny, all you need to make color DCG holograms is a dichroic mirror to combine 457nm and 532nm. I assume you have a DCG emulsion with sufficient sensitivity to 532nm. It will also have a natural blue sensitivity (at 457nm). If you make a Denisyuk hologram with these wavelengths (of a colored object), you will get different colors in the final hologram. I'm not saying that these colors will necessarily bear any relationship to the object's real colors, but there will be colors. (And you will have to balance the amount of power at each wavelength by trial and error.) Controlling the colors is another story entirely.

If you want to include a third color (red in this case), I believe you will run into problems, because I understand this is very difficult in DCG. I have been told that there's a problem in making dye-sensitized DCG that has reasonable sensitivity to both red and green. The blue probably won't be a problem in your case, since you have ample power at 457nm. While dye-sensitized DCG emulsions do have sensitivity to blue, I believe it drops substantially when dyes for green or red are added. It seems that some people (on this forum for example) have had good results at 532nm with a "straight" DCG emulsion, with no dye (with substantial power, however, i.e., a 315M doing ~100mW). If this can be done, then perhaps it would be best to dispense with the dye entirely, at least for two-color holograms at 532nm and 457nm.

Silver halide emulsions are a totally different situation. If you're using PFG-03C, then yes, sensitivities will be different for all of the wavelengths. From what I have been told, sensitvity is by far greatest for 633nm (helium-neon laser). This is fortunate, since a lot of power isn't available there. An experienced color (PFG-03C) holographer told me that while he had practically infinite amounts of blue and green laser power at his disposal, he only used about 40mW of HeNe light. (I'm thinking I should be O.K. with my SP-127, which is doing 37.5mW.) Much more power is needed at 532nm, and still more at the blue wavelength (I can't remember exactly what this was in his case). Your 457nm laser should be excellent. If you go to the Slavich website you can see the spectral response curves for PFG-03C, but remember this is on a logarithmic scale, and a small "dip" means a substantial drop in sensitivity.

The good thing about silver halide materials is that all three colors can be used with relative ease. Of course, emulsions like PFG-03C are designed for it, and many holographers have shown it can be done. If the processing is done carefully, the colors can be very close to that of the object itself.

> DannyBruza: on some of the color holograms Ive seen have a strong blue overcast, in film (color film) they counter balance this with a mask (orange/red) because the blue region has a strong sensitivity ... also the mask lowers the contrast (gama)........

A lot of the bluish cast (for silver halide films) is due to scattering by the grains in the emulsion. It's very noticeable for PFG-01, less so for the finer-grained PFG-03. It seems that any imperfection in film, development, or bleaching shows up really well at this wavelength, and eye response is much lower, too, which is why bright, clean, blue holograms are very very rare.

You can filter out the bluish noise with a orange/red filter, but then you lose the blue, to.

Dinesh Fri Dec 31, 2004 6:28 pm #6
With DCG, this technique of using different wavelengths will not generally create good colour rendition because of the mismatch in swell rates. DCG is a "continuous" material, as opposed to a silver which is "particulate". this continuous deformation causes the fringes to behave in a differrent ways. I've never actually succeeded very well with using different wavelengths, all my colour DCG work is with only one wavelength (488, though some early work in the mid-80's was done with 514. At that time I was researching into holographic solar concentrators for solar-power detectors and was experimenting with multiple wavelength detectors on just one emulsuion)

As for Silver, I've found that PFG-03C has a 3mW/sq cm in 633 and 514. Hans has a paper in which he apparently uses 3 mW/sq cm for 633, 532 and 457. However, I got far better results with BB-Pan ( [http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/albums/al ... _gator.jpg](http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/albums/album02/color_gator.jpg) ). With BB-Pan, you don't get that instant-blackening efect. However, the sensitivtties are not equal. As Hans and other point out, the really imnportant thing is to use a processing scheme that has no swelling.
