The paper is called “Wavelength shifting and bandwidth broadening in DCG”

Here is the section on plate drying:

Influence of drying temperature Tcoat after processing

In a series of experiments the temperature in the climate test chamber was varied from Tcoat = 22C – 30C while the ambient humidity was kept constant.

Though the sensitization process was not changed, the transmittance of the holographic plates during exposure was higher for those which were at lower temperatures.

Increasing the bandwidth with increase drying temperatures and decreasing transparency was observed. From a theoretical point of view an increased modulation of the refractive index is supposed.

The laser intensity is the layer decreases exponentially due to absorption, thus the refractive index is modulated inhomogeneous throughout the layer. An almost linear relation between light intensity and index of refraction is assumed.

Low absorption leads to a distribution of Bragg planes with a uniform degree of modulation. Whereas high absorbing layers have a higher mean value of n decreases and the modulation increases as long as the constant value is in the linear region of the refractive index/exposure energy relation.

In consequence low absorbing layers have sharp reflection spectrum close to theoretical predictions using idealized Bragg planes. But they require exact exposure intensities because in case of underexposing only weak index modulation is produced throughout the layer in case of overexposing the mean refraction index increases up to its saturation point and modulation will be destroyed.

High absorbing layers usually render holograms with larger bandwidth. Their sensitivity to deviation in exposure energy is lower. Because of the decreasing mean refractive index throughout the layer, the optimal working point of the refractive index characteristics curve versus exposure will be met at least in part of the holographic plate.

After chemical hardening the gelatin is fixed and the following wet process with HO2 as solvent do not cause the swelling of the layers.

Well Martin, I did find it. I search for your key word "molecular". Page 4 of your second posting in that thread. This is very interesting indeed.

The forgoing discussion of the structural dependence of the film properties on the drying and heat treatment temperatures

may be summarized as follows:

1. High drying temperature: the number of triple helix structures in the gelatin layer is small; an interconnected

network of these structures is almost non-existent. The amorphous state is dominant. The film exhibits high sensitivity

and high modulation

2. Low drying temperature: the triple helix fraction is large (30 %) but consists mainly of small structures; the

number of crystalline molecules is large. The film sensitivity is high (small helices) and the modulation capacity is low

(large number of crystal-like molecules).

3. Intermediate drying temperature: the number of the triple helix structure is intermediary but they are well

developed; the sensitivity is low (the large structures are abundant) and the modulation capacity is intermediate.

Additional information on the subject of the triple helix structures and their distribution is given in References 7 and 8.

Hummm, well, obviously we will be on the cool end and are allow more crosslinking. This means again, a harder film and a lean toward a narrow band hologram. Now if you take it below 20C during colling, let's say 4C (fridge temp) and you see no adverse effects then it may be that there is a threashold that has been crossed and all naturally occuring crosslinking has already happened.

But it could shurely be that the rapid cooling just after coating and the extreemly cool drying environment (4C) may have a reverse effect and not allow the crosslinking to happen as much as if it were at 20C. You could make some non mold coat plates and allow one to dry at 20C and one in the fridge and expose both the same in 6 or so hours to test.

> Why is this occur?And what if you store a plate in cool temperature then take it into a room that is warm and humid. Does it change the structure?

Well surely the emulsion will swell some due to the humidity. And as long as the temp is not over 115F then I would say no crosslinking will be broken. So the hardness will remain. Once you get above about 115 then the crosslinking breaks down. This is one reason I always cook at about 110 - 115 to try to maintain the bloom strength. Otherwise if I try to use a high bloom and cook at 130F - 140F, I would have lowered the bloom. This also causes pits.

So for a nice narrow band I would use a high bloom, cook at 115 max, dry the film at a cooler temperature, use a full exposure and process in cool fluids while dehydrating very slowly. All the opposites for broadband.
