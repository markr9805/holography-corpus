Gantry for the Preparation of H1 Master Transmission and H2 Image Plane Reflection Holograms

Using size 6 Kee Klamp safety fittings a gantry has been designed and built that can produce both H1 masters and H2 reflection copy holograms. The changes necessary to convert from making H1s to H2s and vice versa are minimal with no changes or adjustments of the more massive components.

Two photographs are shown:

Photo 1. - Laser & Optics Common to both H1 and H2 preparation

A. 30mW HeNe laser

B. Electromechanical shutter assembly

C. Half wave plate mounted in adjustable rotary housing

D. Polarised Beam Splitter

E. Steering mirror for H1 reference beam and H1 to H2 reconstruction beam

1A. Half wave plate in rotary housing aligned with beam from E

2A. Steering mirror associated with H1 object illumination

Photo 2. - Gantry Optics for the preparation of H1 Master Transmission Holograms

The H1 reference beam (A) (B) (C) (D) and (E) passing through (1A) is cleaned up with Spatial Filter (1), straight to 8”Collimating Mirror (3), tilted backwards such the the expanded collimated beam strikes a 10” X 12” front surface mirror (4), which reflects the beam down to the plate/film holder (6). (3) and (4) are angled so that the reference beam strikes the plate/film holder at about 56 deg plate normal – the Brewster angle, to minimise internal glass reflections.

The object (5) is illuminated by mirror and double concave lens combinations, including (2) but other components used in illuminating the object have been excluded for photograph clarity purposes

Optics for the preparation of H2 Image Plane Reflection Holograms

With the H1 flipped and positioned in plate/film holder (6) then illuminated with its reconstruction beam (A, B, C, D, E, 1A, 1, 3, 4, 6.), the inverted pseudoscopic image from the transmission hologram at (6) is projected into the plane of H2 plate/film holder (10). Both plate/film holders (6) and (10) can be moved relative to one another along the gantry to position the image plane of the final reflection hologram in the required place.

The H2 reference beam follows the path A, B, C, D, through another half wave plate (not shown) through spatial filter (7). The expanded beam strikes 10” X 12” 1st surface mirror which is tilted to reflect the beam upwards to overhead mirror (9) which deflects the H2 reference beam down on to plate/film holder (10). The angles of (8) and (9) are such that the H2 reference beam strikes the plate/film holder (10) at about 56 deg plate normal

The 'Z' fold mirrors necessary to equalise the object/reconstruction beam and the reference beams and all the screens necessary to blank-off spurious light are not shown as they would complicate the photos
