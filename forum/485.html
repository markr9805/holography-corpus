Michael Wenyon Sat Mar 03, 2012 4:31 pm #4
You might want to consider using a gloss black paint, because it may actually look 'blacker' than a matt paint when painted on the back of the hologram.

Try painting a piece of ordinary glass with your different candidate black paints and see which looks blackest from the other side when dry.

Matt paints contain particles that create a 'rough' or matt surface when the paint dries: but those particles may be causing some scattering and some 'noise' when matt paint is painted on the back of the hologram. Even though the matt paint is effectively index-matched to the emulsion, the particles -- if they do anything -- may cause some light scattering.

On the other hand, a gloss black paint is also partially 'index matched' to the emulsion it is painted on, but suffers no surface reflection, either diffuse or specular, and then the black pigment within the layer of gloss paint absorbs all the incoming light.

As I say, you can test this yourself.

Gloss paint may in and of itself be 'stronger' than matt paint because it does not contain the filler particles that create the matt finish.

Painting with a brush usually puts down a thicker layer, which may be more durable over time than a thin layer made with an aerosol paint. For tips on brush technique, read a good manual on, for example, how to paint a door with gloss paint: a common recommendation is to paint vertical strokes, then horizontal strokes and end with vertical strokes, and to work quickly and confidently without over brushing. Your brush, materials and environment need to be as clean as possible, but a few bits of dust in the paint should cause no harm.

But a warning about brushing: be careful not to make your paint layer too thick: this may trap a layer of undried (liquid) paint in contact with the emulsion, which may 'wet' and sensitize a silver halide emulsion, changing its thickness and possibly causing print out if the hologram is illuminated with a bright light when the emulsion is wetted by trapped, undried paint below the top surface.

My understanding of black paints used for solar collectors is that a matt surface may be a better absorber when exposed to the air, or that they are used to avoid specular reflections.

Michael is right about the gloss black vs matt. I've seen some black matt paints that look brown or even slightly green from the glass side.

The classic is high heat bbq paint. Gloss, deep black when viewed from the glass index matched side and it drys very fast. With a hair dryer it's dry in under a min. Not hard, it needs more time for that but it is dry.
