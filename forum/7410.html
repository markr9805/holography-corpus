Hans Tue Jul 01, 2008 2:08 pm #1
Yesterday Kris Meerlo and I had a chance to cross sensitize the excellent HF53 film from Filmotec. This film is normally sensitive only to green light. Using this method (originally invented by DannyB), the film gets an equal sensitivity to 633nm as it already has to the 532nm.

The method uses a solution that was invented by Jeff Blyth for the diffusion method. Here is the procedure:

STOCK DYE SOLUTION:

- 1 gram Pinacyanol Chloride in 100ml methanol.

SENSITIZATION SOLUTION:

- 66ml Methanol + 33ml de-ionized Water + 2.5ml Stock Dye Solution

PROCEDURE:

- Dip the film for one minute in the Sensitization Solution and wash in de-ionized water for one minute.

- Optionally the film can be made more sensitive by immersing in a solution of Ascorbic acid (1%) made to PH5.0 with Sodium Carbonate.

For developing a solution of Pyrogallol + Sodium Carbonate works really well with this film.

Hey Hans ! I certainly did not "invent" that use of pinacyanol chloride (or quinaldine Blue) in methanol. That was done around the 1850 's by photographic pioneers as well as being used by Lippmann and Neuhaus and others .

On the matter of using additional sensitizing effects with ascorbic acid at "pH 5.0" I now find that I can take it higher still to pH 7-to 8 to get still more sensitivity. I originally settled on pH 5. because when I put the film away after it had been sensitized with pH higher than 5 , I got some fogging trouble later. But now I don't get that trouble. It is probable that this issue needs to be "trial and error-ed" for each individual make of film or plate.

As for their bleach formula, I assume that once those A + B solutions have been mixed, it won't remain stable for a long time.

I may soon post a new formula for a rehalogenating dichromate bleach that provides far better stability (no bromine or chlorine formation) and has good keeping properties.
