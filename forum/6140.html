Joe Farina Sun Apr 08, 2007 11:26 am #12
I've had good results with a spherical mirror, too. The larger the mirror, the better (if the narrow beam spread is acceptable in your setup) since you have more areas in which to find a clean spot. The same would hold true for a lens. I've had some very clean beams from a lens alone (as I recall, pretty much perfect). But the key parameter is having a larger lens or spherical mirror, so that a clean spot can be found somewhere.

I have often wondered if the spatial filter can be dispensed with for two or three-color holograms, since it's even more aggravating to use in that case. If a lens or mirror will work to spread the combined beams, then color holography would be a lot easier, and maybe more people would be doing it. I would think that if multiple co-linear beams were adjusted properly (diameter and divergence) then a lens or mirror could be used to spread the reference beam. I haven't seen this documented, though, and there may be a problem of which I'm unaware.
BobH Sun Apr 08, 2007 2:39 pm #13
> If a lens or mirror will work to spread the combined beams, then color holography would be a lot easier, and maybe more people would be doing it.

I doubt it. Of the very small number of people making holograms, those making multi-color holograms (or wanting to) have much bigger fish to fry than worrying about aligning spatial filters.

The mirrors and dichroics used to steer and combine the beams will put more noise into the beams. Finding a "good spot" on the spreading optic will not help with that. Also, the cost of multiple lasers, the panchromatic film (think about all that experimentation to get the colors right), and the cost of all the tilters and mounts to combine the beams require a budget so high that a spatial filter is not a big deal.

Sure, diverging a beam with a simple lens or spherical mirror works just fine for many systems. Its use will, however, only mean the holographer will be fiddling with that instead of the spatial filter!
