Hans Sat Jun 10, 2006 12:03 am #1
I thought it would be nice to be able to measure the diameter of a home made pin hole by measuring the diffraction pattern of a beam of laser light shone through it. When I do the geometry and the math, I come with the following formula:

L/nd=sin(a)

sin(a)=cos(a)D/Z

This can easily be re-worked out to the simplified formula:

d = nLZ/D; Here n is the order of the dark fringe from wich the measurement is taken. L is the wavelength, Z is the distance of the pin hole to the projection screen and D is the radius of the dark fringe.

Saxby writes in his book (Practical Holography page 93) that the formula should be:

d = 2.44LZ/D. I cannot determine where the 2.44 factor (that seems suspiciously close to the square root of six) comes from.

When you measure the size of the focussed spot, how will you position your film in exactly the focal point of your objective? I have been thinking about that problem, because if one can do that there might be a much easier way to make a spatial filter:

- Paint a thin sheet of alluminum black.

- Place the sheet at the focal point of your objective.

- Shoot the beam through at MAXIMUM power.

- As soon as a clean beam appears on the other side of the in-situ created spatial filter, turn off the laser.

- Clean off the black paint in a solvent bath.

Thesrot Thu Jun 15, 2006 4:17 am #4
The 2.44 appears to come from the equation of the Rayleigh limit. It is generally used to indicate the maximum resolution of a telescope. You will usually see the equation with 1.22 and radius as opposed to 2.44 and diameter.
