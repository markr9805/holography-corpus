I don't know if it's possible to make a practical visual telescope with only holograms, especially with limited means. If you can obtain a copy of Graham Saxby's "Practical Holography" book, he has a section that describes how to make simple Holographic Optical Elements (HOEs). The main problem I see is that HOE mirrors and lenses only work as designed at a single wavelength, and are useless for white light. There may be a clever way around this, but I haven't seen a working example of a pure holographic telescope reported anywhere.

Not to be too discouraging, there was a thread a while ago about using holographic optics to correct a bad telescope mirror, but it seems likely that you would need at least one very good mirror to make the HOE:

[http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1602](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1602)

I wonder if, using a narrowband H-alpha filter and a couple of HOEs, a simple solar telescope could be made? Maybe you could get some useful magnification before dispersion became too much of a problem. It would be cool if it worked, even badly.

I recently looked for some information about this topic. It is theoretically

possible to build a holographic telescope lens that would work for a small

part of the visual spectrum.

I found a few papers about this topic when I used the

search engine on [http://adsabs.harvard.edu](http://adsabs.harvard.edu) .

There are many attempts to correct aberrations of a telescope by

holograms (for a spectral range of about 10-50 nm) but even this is quite a

problem. This was the reason why I looked for some information. For this

purpose you can find an article here:

[http://adsbit.harvard.edu/cgi-bin/nph-i ... .274..983L](http://adsbit.harvard.edu/cgi-bin/nph-iarticle_query?1993A%26A...274..983L)

Building a conventional telescope is much better and easier and not so

expensive. You can purchase a Pyrex glass disc for a few Dollars that

is ground to the correct radius of curvature. After a few hours fine grinding

with (15 um, 12 um, 9 um and 6 micrometer) emery it can be polished

with Cerium oxide, tested and parabolized. I recently finished a 12.5"

telescope. Since last year I prefer to use my homemade telescopes

because I discovered that image quality is usually superior to many

expensive commercial telescopes.
