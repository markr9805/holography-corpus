I think the broadbandedness seems to prove something of what I have been saying on my "apples vs oranges " thread.

The water soak which you did with presumably mild agitation on the whole plate, was long enough to have removed virtually all the unbroken dichromate buried in the dark zones in your gelatin layer. This leaves the

light struck zones chemically holding onto the degraded dichromate and the dark zones swelling up in a virtually unhardened state. This has led to "chirped fringes" which are not sinusoidal but have a "top hat" profile

which makes for good brightness for HIGS (holographic image generators but bad for HOE's that are wanted for wavelength replay purity). I think the green areas in the left hand image are interesting too

as they look like a definite liquid involvement and not a temperature effect.

The green bits suggest to me that they were areas where the fix got to work on making all the unaltered dichromate ions turn into Cr+++ hardener while still inside the gelatin layer whereas the less green parts

were already loosing unconverted dichromate ions to solution. So what caused this differential effect ?
