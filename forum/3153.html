Here are a few thoughts:

As the Earth rotates on it's axis, there is about a 7 or 8 thousand mile difference between the angle that the moon is viewed - as it rises in the east - and sets in the west. There MIGHT be enough of a difference in the viewing angle, to make a stereoscopic hologram possible. This difference of course, could be amplified with a small telescope.

Other celestial objects could be even easier . . . .

Mars rotates about it's axis on roughly a 24 hour period.

So it should be possible to create stereoscopic holograms of the ENTIRE PLANET, since the viewing angle is changed on a regular basis. The only problem I can see with this - is heat distortion of the image from the Earth's atmosphere when seen through a telescope.

The Hubble doesn't have this problem however, and there are supposed to be some Mars images from the Hubble on file at NASA.

And since the International Space Station is rotating around the Earth . . . .

And constantly changing it's angle of view . . . .

In theory - it should be possible to image this also - and create a stereoscopic hologram.

Just a few thoughts . . . .

It's been done! At least, as a setereo pair. Some at the NSA (National Stereoscopic Association) meeting held in Riverside, California in 2002 showed the audience a stereo pair of the moon done exactly as you suggest ( [http://home.comcast.net/~jlballou/LunarStereo/](http://home.comcast.net/~jlballou/LunarStereo/) ).A hologram may not be as easy, since you need far more than 2 views. If you read the method of the stereo meeon shot, you'll see that he had problems with variations in brightness. So, I wonder if it's possible to get several views of the moon from different times of the night on a night of the full moon and combine them into a multiplex? I suppose it's also possible to make a hologram of each of the stereo pairs and incorporate some kind of HOE into the hologram that images the two pictures right at the eye. A sort of holographic auto-stereo image.
