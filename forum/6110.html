Tom B. Sun Mar 11, 2007 4:15 am #4
From Iovine's "Homemade Holograms" p. 154:

If you know the range of frequencies:

CL = c / (2 range in Hz) - c is speed of light in m/sec

For a range of wavelengths:

CL = (center wavelength in meters)^2 / (2 range in meters)

Example: laser diode at 670 nm with wavelength range of 0.2 nm should

have a CL of (670 10^-9)^2 / 2 (0.2 * 10^-9) = 0.0011 meter.

Joe Farina Sun Mar 11, 2007 12:38 pm #7
> info.crew: Yes, there are just lines across the object

This is a common sight for people who experiment with laser diodes for holography. I've seen large (1/4 inch) and pronounced (dark) bands when doing long exposures on MBDCG and a 35mW diode, and very small (1 mm) and faint (dark) bands when using short exposures with a red pointer (3mW) on silver halide. So, the "lines" can manifest themselves in a number or ways.

All that it means is that the coherence length is not long enough for the depth of the object, for the duration of the exposure. Actually this has been used (on purpose) for precise measurement of objects. A "banded" hologram is made, and the depth contours establish the exact distance of various parts of the object in relation to the film plane. But if you want to make display holograms, it's a real pain, to say the least.

To give you my honest two cents worth, I would never bother with calculations of coherence length. I would rely on holographic testing only.

> Tom B.: From Iovine's "Homemade Holograms" p. 154:If you know the range of frequencies:CL = c / (2 * range in Hz) - c is speed of light in m/sec

This is a typographical error in Iovine´s 'Homemade Holograms' book. The number 2 shouldn´t be in this equation. The right equation is:

Coherence length = c/bandwidth

(bandwidth, or the range of frequencies, is in Hz)

Iovine´s exampel in the book is right. With a typical HeNe laser bandwidth of 1500 MHz the coherence length is:

CL = 3x10^8/(1500x10^6) meter = 0.2 meter
