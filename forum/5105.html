JohnFP Wed Oct 12, 2005 11:18 am #1
I thought I might start a new thread as there is more then greenies I wish to discuss.

It seems very apparent that the greenies I was struggling with was directly related to ambient air humidity. I made 5 x 7 plates with veil and spin at 78, veil and spin at 45 and veil and sit flat. I also made 8 x 10 plates with Myer bar and sit flat. Some were exposed 5 hours to 10 hours old and some exposed 24 -26 hours old. All exhibited no to very minute if any greenies. I am very pleased. Especially with the 8 x 10's.

As I was struggling with greenies for so long, I was not careful in cleanliness. I just wanted to see if I could get rid of the greenies so there is some dust etc.. Especially on the 8 x 10's. I used the run off emulsion from the 5 x 7's to coat the 8 x 10's. I think this is where the pits came from (dust or dirt). In the future I will re-filter the run off before using it.

With some of the 5 x 7's I made a tuned reflection for 514 and made a proximity copy (image planed) with about 2.54cm of the object in front of the plate. That too I am pleased with.

I decided to take the overflow water from the laser and use it instead of simply throwing it away, I simply had the discharge hose running outside. I made a large rinse basin and re-ran the expelled water from the laser to the basin. This allows me to use the discharge water from the laser for my 5 - 10 minute rinse bath without having to adjust temperature (it's pretty constant when the laser power is left alone) and I don't have to run additional water for those 5 - 10 minutes.

I tried to take photos this morning but my camera was too low on batteries. Boy I needed this session. I was beginning to get discouraged but I found that spark again.

Peace to you all!

JohnFP Thu Oct 13, 2005 9:12 am #4
What I do is cook my emulsion. Then filter it through a coffee filter back into another clean beaker which is back on the heater to keep it from jelling. I use the beaker to pour and coat the plate. I hold the plate over a clean plastic tray (which is like a Tupperware which has a sealable lid when not in use) which is on a heating pad, again to keep the run-off emulsion from jelling in the tray. After the beaker is spent, I take the run-off emulsion and pour it back into the beaker, in which then I can start the process over. I may do this a few times. This is all done under my positive, heppa filter flow bench. I feel I should filter the emulsion from the over-flow tray back into the beaker for re-use.

One of the biggest reasons I was/am trying to devise a better coating system is the fact that it takes about 20ml of emulsion to coat a 5x7 plate with the veil method. Of that 20 ml I presume only a few ml or so remains on the plate and a few ml remains in the beaker and in the run-off tray. The Myer bar and pre-scored coating process is much better but still has waste, this is waste that is not recoverable.

Colin, Yes, I believe the pits are due to dust or dirt specks.

I get good results when I take my time, but having done a half dozen sessions with terrible greenies, my main concern was getting rid of those, not really worrying about dust or imperfections. Had I know I was going to have a good session I would have been more careful.
