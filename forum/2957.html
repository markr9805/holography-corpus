Colin Kaminski Thu Dec 16, 2004 4:28 pm #4
Technically this is a clean air supply. There are no provisions to laminarize the air flow. If it works well then thats great. A better design would have the "screen" on the back wall laminarize the air flow. If you look up the Reynold's number formula you will find that for a given diameter passage and a given viscosity you can define a length that will laminarize the air flow. I made a small wind tunnel this way once. I used a bunch of straws to laminarize the air flow.

Without laminar air flow all the way to the edges of the back wall you risk having eddies that will allow dust to accumulate. I don't really think this is a problem for coating but for clean room applications as well as biology applications it can become a problem. The one I bought has the entire back wall as a HEPA filter.

I believe the coating yo are talking about on the MDF is Melamine. It is a white hard plastic that is somewhat brittle. Make sure to have it down when cutting with a skill saw or up on a table saw. Better is to have s table saw with a scoring blade ahead of the main blade to cut the melamine. Cabinet chops have these saws if they use a lot of this stuff.
