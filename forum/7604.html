jeff-blyth Mon Nov 17, 2008 5:04 pm #11
> BobH: Perhaps you're seeing some real time interference between the object light and the image, which should be present in real time after a short time of exposure.

Yes, I guess you are on the right track. There is the question of a transport mechanism or diffusion going on. I know that a monomer

species as it polymerises increases its density (usually but not always) . An increase in density means a decrease in volume , that means the missing volume must draw in material from the dark fringe towards the developing light fringe and this process maybe going on on BOTH sides of the developing light fringe simultaneously therefore the spatial integrity between the developing fringes must be being maintained , --otherwise the fringe structure would self- destruct.

I have now managed to reproduce this effect now in my lab using a difunctional monomer and the dye methylene blue. So far the effect is not as strong as I have seen on the Bayer stuff and it is also very dependent on not having too much dye present.

Jeffrey Weil Sat Nov 29, 2008 10:08 pm #12
Hello Everyone,

Sorry it too me so long to get back to you about the poly I had another trip for the holidays.

At the Holopack conference in Toronto there was the Bayer Guy. David Jurbergs. He gave a speech about the stuff. This is a business conference so there was very little tech discussion going on. Jeff Blyth has already said more about using it than what was presented. I did get to see a half dozen or so pieces. It looked fine, I think they were all H2's, IP. The objects they used were mostly natural crystals. The lamination had lots of dust but thats not the fault of the material itself.

It will be available in a year or two, he would not nail it down and it sounded like he really didn't know. It will be openly available, he did say that clearly. Only pre coated onto film. Bayer materials usually sells raw materials in 50 gal drums but Jurberg said it would be too hard for outsiders to coat it themselves. They would think about it for special cases and only for large users. Any companies like that would also have to be trained by Bayer.

So, this is exactly what everyone is waiting for. Fast polycromatic volume stuff! Bright and easy to develop. A short wait but not too bad.

Jeffrey Weil
