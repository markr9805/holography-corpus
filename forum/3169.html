[quote:73b13c4a93="relax"]But are there more people (non-professionals), making full color holograms?

I understand the high Yves quality will not be reached, but maybe they still look great![/quote:73b13c4a93]

Professionally, I make DCG. However, I have several "play" interests. One of these is colour. I've managed to make colour DCG's with only one laser wavelength ( [http://www.triple-take.com/holo_photos/ ... _gecko.jpg](http://www.triple-take.com/holo_photos/photos/Gallery/opal_gecko.jpg) ). Colour in DCG has always been done with two or more seperate wavelengths, since DCG is sensitive to a range of wavelengths from the 400's to about 530. I believe so far nop one has made a colur DCG with only one. I've also played with colour in silver ( [http://www.triple-take.com/holo_photos/ ... _gator.jpg](http://www.triple-take.com/holo_photos/photos/Gallery/color_gator.jpg) ). This was made on BB Pan at 633 and 514. The problems with colour in silver are the number of lasers required. While you can get reasonable colour rendition with two lasers, the more the merrier! Also, there is a problem with pan emulsions. The only commercially available one now seems to be Slavich PFG-03C. The problem is that this emulsion is very soft and needs to be pre-treated. Also I and someone else I know of, find that the stuff goes instantly black in the developer so there's no feedback about exoposure. The exposure itself needs tests. Some of the literature says it needs 3 mJ/cm^2 across the board, which I've found is too much. I've heard good things about the Ultimate pan, but I've not been able to get it. Colourholographics will make pan, which is excellant material, if you ask them, but don't on a commercial scale. Finding info on exposures and development for BB pan was not easy.

Anyway, assuming you have the material, my experience has been that the first step is finding the appropriate exposure for the film at the various wavelengths and then finding the best developer. Obviously, you need a developing scheme that does not swell (otherwise all your carefully orchestrated colours will shift). Once you have exposure and development, the best way is to hit the object with the various lasers. Manipulate the powers of the lasers so that the object, under illumination, looks "good" in a colour sense, ie the colour rendition under laser light is about right.. Sometimes killing the green a little will bring out the yellows and pinks more etc. Then it's pretty much like falling off a log. Measure energies, determine exposures, pre-treat film (if required as it is for PFG-03C), develop. Of course, it's a little trickier, as is all holography. Like all holography, first results will not be great and you'll have to fine tune exposures and development, a necessity despite some who claim to get perfect holograms every time with no testing!. But essentially, providing you get the equipment and tune your procedure, there's nothing that's any more intimidating in full colour than there is in any hologram.
