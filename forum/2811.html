eric343 Tue Oct 19, 2004 4:23 pm #1
I recently salvaged a Philips color-separating prism from an old LCD projector, and it turns out that it works as an excellent variable beamsplitter. (The ratio depends on the angle at which the light enters the prism.) As a result, I decided to try using it to improve the beam ratio in Denisyuks, having read that the optimal ratio is 1:3 to 1:1.5.

The configuration I tried was a modification of something I'd read about in the Holography Handbook; the reference beam hitting the plate as in a standard Denisyuk configuration, and the split-off beam diverged and illuminating the object from the side. Using a large first-surface mirror in the sand, I tried to get the reference beam to hit the plate at what I judged to be a 45-degree angle (up until now I'd simply been hitting it straight-on).

After setting it up, equalizing the path lengths, and checking the beam ratio with my handy S&M photometer, I let everything settle for awhile and made an exposure. Unfortunately, the only thing visible in the plate was a series of horizontal bands with an odd character to their bottom edge (not straight as would be expected from interference fringes). So I carded off the object beam and tried again -- still nothing.

Now, I don't think that external vibration was a factor (my table is the sand tension table as described in the Holography Handbook and also in Saxby), and I let everything settle for about an hour. I'm wondering whether there's anything fundamentally wrong with this setup, or whether it's likely that I simply got the exposure way off. I don't think I massively underexposed it judging by the speed at which it developed... unfortunately, I haven't figured out how to translate the readings from the S&M meter into exposure times.

Eric if you has just loaded a plate and then it was 45 seconds till exposure, your effective settle time is only 45 seconds not 1 hour. In a Denisyk, plate movement to object is the main factor you want to elliminate. Let the plate settle for longer and what I have done before in stablizing a plate held from the bottom only is lay a long object of a couple of pounds across the top of the plate and to the table. I used a hunk of steel about 18 inches long and 3 inches wide. This keeps the top from moving to and fro.
