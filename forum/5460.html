Hello,

I am pursuing a project on using medical images (NMR or PET) to shoot a multiexposure hologram as described in Saxby's book.

Actually I see two different ways to make an hologram from these images:

a) use "slice cuts" and shoot them at the appropriate distance as plane images like Saxby describes, obtaining 3d stack of two dimensional images

b) use some imaging software to recompose a 3d model from the medical images and then shoot an stereogram from the 3d model.

I was wondering if anyone has ever tried any of the above routes, and could display some results. Any comments are welcome!
Kaveh Fri Mar 31, 2006 11:15 am #2
I worked on the slice shots (a) many years ago. One of my colleagues, Steven Hart, is still pursuing it, and trying to commercialize it through Holorad (formerly Voxel):

[http://www.holorad.com/](http://www.holorad.com/)

[http://www.apple.com/science/profiles/voxel/index.html](http://www.apple.com/science/profiles/voxel/index.html)

Advantages of (a):

• Full parallax

• All visual depth cues

• No software needed

• No software manipulation of image, so no danger of computer misinterpretation

• Physician can measure surgical tools or fixing elements like screws, by putting it inside image. Full parallax allows this.

Advantages of (b):

• Single transfer exposure means no overload of recording medium

• Software can interpret image, add/remove elements, enhance contrast, etc.

• Hidden line or surface removal possible with software

So no clear answer as to which is the better route, but I favour (a) as a safer method for medical interpretation.
