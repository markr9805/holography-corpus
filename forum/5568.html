Dear Holographers,

WARNING WARNING WARNING ......

I have been confusing a lot of terms, and some of my logic is therefore wrong. It seems I got longitudinal mode mixed up with TEM00.

There are 2 modes of a laser:

1)Transverse Modes such as TEM00 have to do with the field intensity distribution in a plane perpendicular to the beam. For some reason, you need TEM00 mode which is a smooth gaussian profile for holography, but don't know why, or if it is really critical. Transverse mode is related to smaller beam divergence and beam diameter. And TEM00 has the ability to be focused at a tiny point. A fresnel number < 1 supports a single TEM00 mode, and is related to rod geometry, especially with an internal small aperture. I conjectured that the central widening of the passive q-switch "aperture" acted as an internal small aperture.

2)Longitudinal Modes which are numbers of closely spaced wavelengths around the Nd:YAG 1064nm that spread its "color" out. They therefore have closely spread frequencies. We want a single pure "color" for holography. Single longitudinal mode of operation, good for holography, also gives the best coherence length and narrowest linewidth. You get it with internal etalons, and slow build up of laser energy as in a passive q-switch.

Sorry for the confusion. I am rethinking the whole thing as I learn all about it! I am no expert by any means !

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen

Dear Holographers,

I was thinking about the passive q-switch's known ability to favor a single longitudinal mode, based on the Solid-State Laser Engineering book and article in Applied Physics Letters.

I had placed an adjustable etalon in the SSY-1 cavity (very tough to do) with passive q-switch installed, and did not detect any improvement in the holograms I took. This may mean that the passive q-switch is doing the job of generating a single longitudinal mode all by itself. A high coherence length was also seen in the holograms both before and after the etalon was installed.

Now that I have unmounted that laser for photography, I will show you how I installed the adjustable etalon inside the SSY-1 cavity.

The first picture shows the side view of the laser. Note the 4-40 threaded screw on the left. When you screw it to the right, it moves the whole cavity to the right. The cavity has a spring on the right side that forces the cavity to the left, and against the screw. The screw has a rounded tip. The cavity has 4 mounting screws that are slightly loose, and the four holes in the cavity are elongated horizontally by filing. The spring is quite stiff, and in compression.

The second picture is a closeup of the etalon, which is a thin parallel plate of quartz. If we can vary the tilt of this etalon, we can control which wavelengths around 1064nm get selected, and approach single longitudinal mode operation of the laser. To do this, the etalon is mounted on one edge with a very thin layer of flexible silicone cement to the cavity. In the picture, that is the edge of the etalon on the left side. The right side of the etalon rubs agains the metal of the resonator housing. In that way, moving the cavity a fraction of a millimeter will change the tilt of the etalon. I found that maybe 10 degrees turn on the screw made it go through the maximum to minimum output level. Tapping the metal for the screw was difficult since the metal is very hard and brittle. Care must be taken that the cavity is not moved so far as to touch the brewster window that is installed in the cavity as well.

The pictured SSY-1 laser is the most highly modified laser I have made to test what optics are absoltely necessary for taking pulsed green holograms. It has a brewster window, replaced high power Cr4+:YAG passive q-switch, and adjustable etalon, all integral to the standard SSY-1 cavity. It seems that the etalon and replaced q-switch are not necessary for taking pulsed holograms. Using the normal PFN1 (pulse forming network) means that you can just keep the standard dye passive q-switch in place. This means substantial savings in money and construction time.

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen

JohnFP Wed May 24, 2006 9:25 am #7
The reason a Tem00 mode is desirable for holography is only that you will not have blotches of high intensity and low or no intensity or in the case of Tem01 a missing intensity in the center.

Bill, I don't think you need to do anything to change the Transverse mode. As long as you have a somewhat even energy level across the plate you will be fine. As far as trying for a gausian profile, that is not desirable. A gaussian profile has a higher energy level in the middle and lower toward the outside. A "flat top" beam is more desirable where the beam intensity remains constant across the plate.

Your test in the regular photographic paper is the best to measure beam profile (trans mode, intensity distribution etc..). Repeat that test with the new lens and larger KTP.

[http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_mode](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_mode)

I wondr if a normal digital camera can see 1064nm? I know mine can see the IR diode in my remote controls! Aim your digital camera at a white card and fire a pulse in preview mode. You should see a purple spot. If you have a IR lens you can expand the beam to see more detail.
