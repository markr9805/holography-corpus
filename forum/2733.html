Colin Kaminski Thu Sep 23, 2004 7:25 am #1
Does anyone have expewrience with redeveloping holograms after bleaching? At CCAC Don is using 10 grams of ascorbic acid to 1 Liter of water as a redevelopment solution. After bleaching he soaks the plate for 20 seconds and then turns on a 50 watt halogen bulb. After about f minutes the hologram turns red and he washes it off. He does not need to further darken the hologram to see a reflection image. I am sure this is his method for avoiding print-out.

I would expect the image would be brighter before development. What are the pros and cons of redevelopment?

In my view applying this additional step really does make sense for reflection holograms on "large grain emulsions" like PFG-01, VRP, 8E75 only. Of course, light absorption at the thin colloidal layer will cost you some percent of DE.

On the other hand, since it cuts most radiation below 500nm, such a colloidal silver layer may provide fairly good printout protection...
Tom B. Fri Sep 24, 2004 6:01 am #3
I tried it a couple of times with PFG-01, and didn't really care for the effect. As expected, it did eliminate the bluish scattering noise, but the subjective brightness of the hologram was not improved much if at all (probably the absorption loss offset the improved contrast). Also the image became quite monochromatic and boring, having lost the interesting brightness-dependant wavelength shift that CWC2 (JD-2?) development can give with this emulsion.
MichaelH Fri Sep 24, 2004 8:12 am #4
JD-3 includes an ascorbate acid post-treatment bath. I haven't used it in some time now and found that I prefer the results from JD-2.
