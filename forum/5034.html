Well, I finally got my laser up and running. It has been quite some time. I tried a 5x7 DCG plate that was made in early June and has been in the refridgerator. I came out very poor. So it looks like they may be just too old. It was nice to see the 457 and 514 lines again.

My next batch of plates will be an attempt on 8x10" DCG format. Hopefully tomorrow.

I remember now why I don't use the score, coat and break method of making plates all the time. I would then be wasting a lot of glass. I will use the method for making plates with fresh glass but if the holgorams on the fresh glass do not turn out it will be back to coating one at a time (to reuse the already cut glass).

Yes, Dave, I have read that thread and your input and disclosure was greatly appreciated. The veil method is one I have used extensively and still use. It produces great results but is slow. I recoop the run off in the veil method in a pan that has a heating pad for your back under it. This keeps the run-off emulsion viscous and allows me to put it gently back into the coating container for subsiquent plates. But what I was thinking is more on the lines of a tin "matting" that is hinged. I place a plate in the jig, hinge down the "matting" (which is basically a piece of tin with a retangular cut out 1/4 smaller then the plate). This would allow me to use the myer bar but keep the run off from getting on the edge and creaping around the back of the plate. It is just a thought. SO far the score, coat and break method works fabulous.
