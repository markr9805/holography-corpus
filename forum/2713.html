tjohnson Sat Sep 04, 2004 3:59 pm #2
This is just my opinion, but I would recommend for your first hologram to use a Denisyuk single-beam geometry. Since your power is limited and you don't have access to really decent mirrors yet, this is a good way to make a successful first hologram. It won't be as bright as a two-beam hologram, of course, but I know I was amazed and delighted when my first (single-beam) attempt color-shifted into view as it dried.

If you have success with this configuration, then you may be encouraged enough to find some first surface mirrors and other such niceties.

You could also "harvest" good mirrors from something like a broken or obsolete scanner or laser printer.

Best of luck to you.
tjohnson Sat Sep 04, 2004 5:20 pm #3
Regarding your question about polarization: A polarized laser is nice but not absolutely necessary to make a hologram. Linear polarization allows one to reduce internal reflections inside the plate, or filmholder glass. If you were to orient the polarization horizontally, you would reduce the reflections from the first surface of your glass (assuming it's tilted horizontally). If your little HeNe isn't polarized (actually "randomly polarized") you might consider a laser pointer. Those are inheirently polarized at about 100:1, not as good as an internally polarized HeNe but better than nothing.
Dinesh Sat Sep 04, 2004 6:57 pm #4
[quote:c410afc8f9]Can I somehow reduce this interference? I read somewhere that you have to put the mirror at 60°, and make a special polarization to the light, is that right? [/quote:c410afc8f9]

The best thing is to get a front surface mirror. These are pretty easily available from many different optics suppliers and can cost a lot or a little. You can also get front surface mirrors from most optical equipment.

Putting a mirror at 60 degrees may effect the fringes if your polarisation is what's called 'p' polarisation. This means that if you're using a horizontal setup, so that all the beams are in the same plane, then your polarisation needs to be horizontal. In this case, the reflection off the front surface is killed off and you only get reflection off the back surface - theoretically. In practice, the front surface reflection will not go to zero and you'll still get some interference.

[quote:c410afc8f9]And my second question: do I have to use polarized light at all[/quote:c410afc8f9]

No. Polarisation helps kill off internal bounce between the back and front surface of the film, as tjohnson says, and also reduces the possible brightness of your hologram. The bouncing off the light between front and back surfaces of the film is known as "woodgrain" by holographers and is seen like a woodgrain pattern on the hologram. At this stage, I think it's better to get a hologram and then fine tune the parameters to get it better.

Also, consider the coherence length of your laser. The 1mW laser may only have a coherence length of about 2 - 4 cm and this determines the possible depth of the object. The difference in paths between the beam to the film and the beam to the back of the object and to the plate has to be within this 2 to 4 cm.
