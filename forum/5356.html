Following Graham Saxby in "Practical Holography", 1st and 3rd ed. (probably in 2nd too).

Index of diffraction is approx. 1.5

Focal length is approx twice the radius of curvature

Cement : Tensol n° 12

Check the Optics Guides by Melles Griot or the Newport Catalog for the Lensmaker's formulae.

1/f = (n-1) {1/r1 -1/r2}

If one side is flat, r = 0, so that term drops out

Ed Wesly Fri Feb 17, 2006 11:44 pm #10
It would be all too cavalier to just say plug the numbers into the equaton I posted above. That is what you would have to do, but the big problem is measuring the radius of curvature of the piece of plastic.

I would suggesst to trace the arc, then try to find a best fit using some sort of compass to draw similar sized circles. Since the lens will be filled with an almost index-matching liquid, the outer edge of the curve would be the one to measure.

But there must be a more elegant way of finding that parameter. Then it's a piece of cake to find the focal length, since thickness and second radius of the outside drop out if the added on side is flat.

Ok, I traced the curve of the curved glass frame on paper. I then connected both ends to make a cord of the partial circle. Then I found this formula on the internet.

[http://www.algebra.com/algebra/homework ... .6681.html](http://www.algebra.com/algebra/homework/formulas/Geometric_formulas.faq.question.6681.html)

r=[c^2 + (4*(x^2))] / 8x where

r = radius

c = cord length

x = distance from cord to circle.

The glass plate has a cord length of 8.5 inches and the distance from cord to circle edge is 1.5 inches.

This give a radius of 6.77 inches if I am correct. I took a piece of string and measured it about 6 3/4 inches from the arc and it traced the curve pretty close. So I think I may have calculated correctly.

This yields a focal length of about 13.5 inches using a flate side (thanks Jean and Ed).

Additionally the curved glass is 7 inches high.

So the square backing will be 8.5 inches wide by 7 inches high. Even with the beveled edge it will be pefect for the LCD I have.

I think I bought a few of them locally (possibly K-Mart) for about $5.00 US each but see quite a few on the internet. The plastic gold framing that holds the picture was easily removed with a razor blade. I originally bought them to do some curved DCG holograms but haven't gotten around to that yet.

Again, it is GLASS.
