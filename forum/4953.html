Hi,

I remember there were posts about this, but I couldn't find them...

The question is about a proper glue to fix lenses and other optical components with coatings. It just happened again to me that I was fixing a lens around its edge (laser diode collimator) and all went fine. But a few days later there had appeared some kind of crystallic film on the lens surface, rendering the collimator useless.

This seems to have to do with outgassing of "instant" glue; I had encountered this in the past, and had been able to remove the film by a solvent - in the present case I rather don't want to touch the lens surface.

So, what are the kinds of recommended, and anti-recommended glues? More specifically, would a dedicated glass glue from the home improvement store be any better? I'd rather avoid loosing another of those collimators.

Thanks !

-W

wler Mon Jul 18, 2005 6:01 pm #3
Aha, I will try that, thanks !

In the mean time, I found the following on the [Optima](http://www.optima-prec.com/notes.htm) site:

> Cyanoacrylate Adhesive Precaution – "Super glue" should not be used anywhere near laser diodes - or near any other optical component - outgassing may fog windows and other optical surfaces. The amount of fogging, or the time required to observe the fogging, varies with different products. If you're in doubt, test the adhesive over time at an elevated temperature and in a sealed container. For example, place a drop of the adhesive in question on a piece of glass, something like a microscope slide, then place the sample in a plastic bag and seal the bag.
Ed Wesly Mon Jul 18, 2005 7:19 pm #4
UV Curables like those available from the source below are great to use because the hardening action doesn't start until it is exposed to UV. So you can administer the goo, position the part where you want it, clean up the schmoo mess you may have made, and then cure it.

Once these things are 100% cured they are totally inert.
