How does one calculate the percentage alcohol to water using the specific gravity number.

Example;

What is the percentage of ISO alcohol to water if the liquid has a specific gravity of .86?

Isopropyl Alcohol has a specific gravity of .785 at 20C.

Thank you in advance!
Ed Wesly Thu May 19, 2005 8:13 pm #2
Specific gravity of a material compares the density of that material to the density of water. Water has a specific gravity of 1.0.

The density of water is a kilogram per liter. Anything with a specific gravity < 1 will float, > 1 will sink. I would see specific gravity listed on the industrial sized cans of tomato sauce when I worked in a pizza place in high school, I guess to show how much the sauce was or wasn't watered down.

To solve your problem, let x = percent of alcohol in solution. Then (1 - x) is the amount of water. .786 is specific gravity of the alcohol.

.786x + 1.0(1-x) = .86

.786x + 1 -x = .86

-.215x = -.14

x = .651 or 65%

Alternatively, letting x = % of H2O

x + .785(1-x) = .86

.215x = .860 - .785

.215x = .075

x = .348 or 35%

I was trained as a math teacher and that is still one of the things I have to do to put the chops on the table!
