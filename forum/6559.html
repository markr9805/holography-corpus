JohnFP Tue Nov 27, 2007 2:25 pm #1
I have not had a chance to try it yet, but I wonder if there are any different effects of exposing DCG when it is young, but let it set for a day or so to harden the gelatin before processing?

I get really nice pastel colors when I expose young, but the trade off is the emulsion has a slight whiteness (milkiness) to it when processed. If it is balanced properly to rid the milkiness the pastel is not as vivid. So I was wondering if the exposure is done when the film is young, maybe a few hours old, but then left to cure, if the pastel color can be salvaged without the expense of the milkiness.

Has anyone tried this yet?
Colin Kaminski Tue Nov 27, 2007 4:46 pm #2
It has always been my opinion that milkiness is water. Perhaps if you dessicate the hologram before processing it will get rid of the milkiness. If you are getting pastells then you have very selecive bragg planes. They are likely very fragile. (All theory with zip for experience to back it up.)
JohnFP Tue Nov 27, 2007 5:25 pm #3
Well, there are two things I think important. One is for sure the water content but secondly, and I think more importantly, is the hardness of the gelatin. The harder gelatin will absorb less water in the fixer and subsiquently in the water wash bath. Whereas the softer gelatin will swell more in the fixer and water.

So getting rid of the water but not letting the natural crosslinking via dark reaction may not achieve the same results as letting the hologram sit for a day. It may remove water, but my thinking is that if the gelatin is still soft, minimal overall crosslinking, then the swelling in the fixer and water may put us back to where using fresh gelatin is.

But I feel, not knowing for sure yet, that if I expose young and wait a day, It should be simply like exposing the plate when it is a day old, except for the fact the gel was a little swollen when exposed and it will probably give a blue shift. Of course that will not achieve the goal either.

It seems what we need to visit here, is not the difference between and exposed and unexposed areas (light and dark fringes) but the difference between a strong image area (lot of AmDi used to create the fringes) usually near the plate where the object is closest and brightest, and the weak image area (very little AmDi used to create fringes) usually found around the outside of the main image area and near the edges of the plate.

I wonder if a variable desity filter of some sort could be used in which, in the simplest case, would allow less light to center where the object is and more light to the outside where there is no image and very little object light hitting. This would be used as a post treatment exposure to harden the outside of the plate more then the inside. I could probable do something by hand in using one of those stars on a handle they use in photography to tone down a hot spot, and just move it around keeping it mostly in the center.

Another thought, and I hope I posted it here somewhere, is to harden young film by exposing it to fringes instead of just light post exposure and pre processing. Basically, lay a mirror down on the table and place the exposed image hologram on top of it, and expose it again. If the angle is enough (Brewster's) and since we are using a mirror, no grating should be visible when illuminating at Brewsters and viewing from the perpendicular.

The simplest solution would probably be to create backgrounds for your objects such that all areas of the plate are getting enough light to harden them sufficiently. But in the case of the hologram I want to make, that will not work.

Now here I go. I was going to make a hologram I have been dying to make forever, and I may have to do some testing prior...again....LOL!
