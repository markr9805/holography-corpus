I have no particular qualifications to pontificate on this subject, but I think that when waves from phase locked 1064 nm and 532 nm sources are added linearly, no fringes will result because in every 1064 nm half-cycle there is a complete +/- cycle of 532 nm which would cancel itself out.

But if the recording medium is nonlinear enough, I expect that fringes should be seen (as in RF mixing) which would look similar to interference between same-frequency waves. I don't know about the readout wavelength, though - I expect there would be some funny optical effects from different reference/object wavelengths.

On the other hand, when I think about quantum interference between photons and their indirectly frequency-doubled offspring, my brain hurts and I doubt that it's possible at all...
Tony Sat Nov 19, 2005 12:38 pm #9
I'm not qualified in optics, but have some experience in second harmonic phenomena in ultrasound.

My opinion is it won't work. Here's my hand-waving reasoning:

Rememember that the interference at the film is not a static process. Over the time it takes for one fundamental wavelength to go by, both the object and reference beams go through 2pi radians. Because they are coherent wrt each other, the standing wave pattern stands still in space, but the sum of object and reference does traverse 2 pi also. In non-exposed areas, the object and reference are always 180 degrees apart, as they traverse 2pi radians.

Now consider the second harmonic doubled case. I postulate that whatever nonlinear process causes the doubling, will also cause a doubling of the phase. So as the fundamental traverses 2pi, the second harmonic will travers 4pi.

When the object and reference interfere, the standing wave pattern is no longer stationary in space. So no fringes will be formed in a medium that is sensitive to integrated intensity over time.

Another way to look at it is as a frequency mixer. If you mix two frequencies of f, you get two products, one at 2f and one at 0 (dc). It's the DC term that we record as a hologram.

If you mix f and 2f, you get f and 3f out. No DC term, no hologram.
