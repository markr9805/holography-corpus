Has anyone found a cheap and reliable method to measure coating thickness? (For a dry DCG layer)

I don't have a lot of confidence in my cheap micrometer, though more expensive ones could probably do the job. But I was wondering, for thicker DCG layers, say 20 to 30 microns, how can you be sure when the plunger of the micrometer stops, i.e., can you "press" it too hard into the layer and get the wrong measurement? Or will it be obvious when the plunger contacts the surface of the gelatin? I don't know....

I was thinking of a different approach using a single-edged razor blade. To one end of the sharp edge of the razor blade, glue a spacer of known thickness. This could be a feeler gauge .0015 inch thick (38 microns). Then hold the blade perpendicularly to the surface of the layer and forcefully scratch it. Since the blade will be at a very slight angle, the "low" edge will cut into the gelatin, but the high edge will not. It would be necessary to scrape some gelatin away beforehand so that the spacer rests on the glass, and not on top of the gelatin.

If the blade is 1.5 inches long, and the gelatin is disturbed over a 0.75 inch distance, then the thickness would be 19 microns. If it's disturbed over 0.5 inch, the thickness would be 12.7 microns, etc.

Hans Sun Sep 24, 2006 6:13 am #3
I have tried with a very expensive digital micrometer, but found that the variations in the thickness of the glass prevent a reliable measurement of the thickness of the gelatin.

I have found an article in "Selected papers on Holographic Recording Materials", but have not tried it yet. Here is an abstract:

![Image](http://hologramhans.hopto.org/pictures/Thickness.jpg)

The flatness uniformity and the thickness of the layer can be evaluated in an interference device shown here. A point source of white light (Osram 12V 20W lamp) is directed on the layer by a beam splitter. The lens L forms an image of the layer on the entrance of a small spectroscope (holographic grating spectroscope manufactured by R.Collard, Liège-Belgium).

Black lines corresponding to the extinction condition 2 ne=kl (l=wavelength, n =refractive index, k is an integer, e=thickness).

By counting the number m of black lines between two known wavelengths, the thickness is equal to:

e=ml1l2/(2n(l1-l2))

For example, the emulsion Agfa 8E75HD displays 7 lines between the wavelength 0.5um and 0.6um wich leads to e=6.7um, taking n=1.55. The contrast of the black lines is also a measure of flatness and uniformity.
BobH Sun Sep 24, 2006 7:23 pm #4
I use the technique above with an Ocean Optics spectrometer to measure cell gap thicknesses to 3 microns. It's very accurate and easy to use. Haven't tried it with an emulsion on glass.
