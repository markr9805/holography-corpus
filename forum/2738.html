Tom B. Sun Jun 19, 2005 2:31 am #23
> John Klayer: Okay, now that I have this laser what somewhat simple tests can I do to see that it doesn't mode hop and verify the coherence length? For the mode hops, I guess the thing to do is set up an interferometer and put a silicon cell at the interference pattern and listen on an amplifier. What would be expected to be heard? Maybe nothing but the amplifier's noise floor hiss?

Re coherence length- smoothly vary one path length in an interferometer and observe the fringe contrast. Multiline behavior produces a periodic coherence function (seen as contour lines in holograms) so you can't just see fringes at, say, 6 feet and assume that the coherence is OK - it could have dropped to zero many times between 0 and 6 feet. A deep hologram is a good test.

Mode hops can be observed as small discontinuous jumps in amplitude as the laser flips between adjacent modes. No interferometer setup is needed, though an interferometer can be helpful in confirming mode hops. For rare events they sound like clicks and pops, but in the middle of a mode-hop zone it's more of a chaotic rushiing waterfall sound. To get some idea of what to expect and to help set your gain levels, try observing the output of a laser pointer as you cool it with an ice cube and warm it with body heat or a hair-dryer. This should produce some easily observed mode hops.

wler Tue Jul 05, 2005 5:19 am #30
> BobH: Do you have a scanning Fabry-Perot interferometer I could borrow?.. I wonder if there is any interest from those here in collaborating on such a project. ...

I would definitely have an interest. But things are not easy.. I already tried and gave up at some point. The main problem is, when using ordinary mirrors, that what you see on the scope is a mixture of the laser modes and the multiple longitudinal modes of the scanning interferometer - a total mess in other words. That's why the commercial ones use spherical confocal mirrors (ie, the resonator is like a cylindircal cut-out piece of a of a Xmas sphere - sorry, I don't know the proper word in english for those shiny balls you hang on the tree). Only then the frequencies of all the resonator modes coincide with each other and do not disturb the picture.

See the discussion in LEOT and also the discussion in alt.lasers I had some while ago [here](http://groups-beta.google.com/group/alt.lasers/browse_frm/thread/a918740310f33d24/b1bee8ef60742fe5?q=scanning+goldwasser&rnum=1&hl=en#b1bee8ef60742fe5).

That is, one needs very special mirrors and I wouldn't know where to get these.
