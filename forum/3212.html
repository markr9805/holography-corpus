> Dinesh: Yes AH would help. It helps because it kills the glare coming back off the plate. Unless your plate were super clean, the reference beam would cause glare which reacts with the incoming object beam and creates spurious fringes inside the emulsion. These fringes are reflective fringes, but there are still surface fringes and they can be seen in transmission mode as an overall haze.

I've been meaning to give home-made anti-halation a try (burning styrofoam?) and was wondering if there's a blue dye that readily bleaches out in white or UV light which could be used to create an AH layer in the masters I use with Kapco laminate.

I'm going to try wet-mounting my Kapco (just to see how it works) and I thought that if I could wet mount the kapco to glass (prior to putting the film down) then perhaps I could use a red-light absorbing dye in the water that would act as an AH layer. The kicker is that once the plate has film on it, there'd be no way to remove the dye unless it bleaches out when exposed to white or UV light.

Can anyone think of a material that might fit the bill or have suggestions on how I'd go about looking for such a dye?
Ed Wesly Wed Mar 16, 2005 1:18 pm #2
Here are a few things that I know of that have been used for antihalation backings.

Universal Photonics (495 West John Street, Hicksville, NY 11801, 1-800-645-7173) makes two products, #33 Metal Blocking Spray and X-59 Strippable Coating that are good AH backings. They spray or brush onto the back of the plate, dry quickly, and after exposing but before developing you can peel them off like the coating on a virgin front surface mirror! In fact, that's what these products are typically used for. And they can also be used to clean FS Mirrors, by spraying on the aluminized side and then when removed they take off most of the dirt (hopefully)!

#33 is blue, so it might not be useful for blue lasers, but it's great for He-Ne. X-59 is black, so it's broadband usable.

These things are pretty smelly. To avoid this type of nastiness, I have used Krylon Spray Acrylic (not enamel or lacquer) Flat Black as the AH backing. If applied to a plate that is used in a few days after spraying, this acrylic paint is somewhat water soluble, and can be removed with a well-drenched paper towel, being careful to not to get the front of the plate wet.

I have spun photo-resist on architectural black glass, not stained glass black because it's pretty ripply, and that also keeps the woodgrain suppressed. However an H1 on black glass is not such a good idea since the beam is attenuated, but for the production of gratings that were to electroplated it worked real well!

If anyone would like a copy of an article I wrote for the late great holosphere, the Advocate of Holographic Art, Science, and Technology, please e-mail me. There is a great picture in there of a low-frequency grating pattern showing the interference pattern, with one half AH'ed and the other not, with the latter having the dreaded woodgrain pattern superimposed on the grating lines.
