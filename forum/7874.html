Hi John

It's quite possibly I.R., I can't recall whether or not the c315m has an I.R. filter fitted though. The I.R. on a DPSS tends to diverge very quickly so unless you're looking directly into the beam path from a few inches away you should be o.k.... I think

This is why you should always measure the true power of a DPSS laser after an I.R. filter, especially if using something like a Lasercheck pen. The I.R. can play havoc with the power readings and give you a false impression of true power at the wavelength you're measuring.

Jem
Ed Wesly Mon Jun 15, 2009 3:10 pm #3
Probably the 808. I know you can see the 780 nm of CD players, and if here's a lot of 808 pumping away, the weak sensitivity of the eye might still catch it.

I once was using 1 Watt of 780 nm from a Ti:Sapphire. The undiverged beam looked like less than a 5 mW He-Ne, but when you put your finger in it, YOW!

Once it was diverged, it became practically invisible. We had to tune the spatial filters by pointing an old B & W TV camera at the equipment, and look at a monitor to see what was happening.

Agfa 8E75HD still had some sensitivity at 780, and we did get some decent foot square HOE's out of the project.

jeff-blyth Sun Jul 26, 2009 8:20 pm #6
Hi John, I've noticed a few times some coloured plastics lying around (usually red plastics) actually fluoresce a bright deep red in a fairly strong but somewhat diverged beam from my "20" mw 532nm laser pointer. The effect is interesting and makes for a good question for newbies ---- How can this "reflect" red light when there is nothing but pure green light in the room? This is though a well known effect where the excitation wavelength is almost always shorter than the fluorescing wavelengths . We cannot of course use this in holography because the fluorescence is incoherent light. Although I suspect it can be possibly helpful in unusual cases to increase the sensitivity of a recording plate very close to the fluorescing object when you are struggling to get to a threshhold level of photons to kick start your fringe recording. I have certainly gained from this when using a frequency doubled pulsed YAG laser.

jeff
