I would like to recommend that you make a transmission hologram of a simple beam of light (a hologram optical element, or more specifically, a holographic prism) and measure its diffraction efficiency as the reconstruction light changes angle. This would be a simple setup easy to make stable, and you'd learn all about making an accurate measurement.

If you just fill the box with water, you won't see any difference. The reason is that the light hitting the front of the empty box will reflect from the front and continue on to the back of the box,reflecting from the back of the box. Thus, reflections from both the front of the box and the back are at the same angle. If you now put water in the box, the light entering the box will refract to a new angle, but, on hitting the back of the front, the angle will be the same as before. Again, no difference in directions (see figure below).

Joy (wife) has suggested that if you put a coin (or some small object) attached to the back of the box, the coin will appear nearer when there's water, than when the box is empty. Think of seeing a coin at the bottom of a swimming pool, where the coin appears nearer. The difference in position of the coin will give you the refractive index. Another suggestion of hers is to make one hologram, double exposure, not two separate holograms. In this case, there will be interference fringes on the coin. Measuring the fringes will give you give you a measurement of the refractive index

By the way, it's 'refractive index', not 'diffractive index'. Also, by the way, it's going to be a difficult hologram because transparent scenes do not reflect much - the hologram will be dim.

Din Tue Nov 05, 2024 2:27 pm #4
A better idea is to record a hologram in Denisyuk geometry at some angle (say 45 deg). Now, place the hologram at the bottom of a box filled with water. In order to reconstruct the hologram, the beam must hit the surface of the box so that it refracts to the angle you originally referenced. So, if you reference a hologram at 45 deg in air, when you put the hologram at the bottom of a box filled with water, the light has to enter at 67 deg. (see below)

More generally, if the hologram in air is referenced at θ(r), then the recon at the top of the water-filled box must enter at an angle:

θ(i) = arcsin(n*sin(θ(r))

Using this equation and two angles, you should be able to calculate n
