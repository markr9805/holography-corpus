JohnFP Mon Jun 21, 2004 1:09 pm #1
Ok, I am looking for something creative to make with holography and I think this is what I may do next time. I want to make a jig that holds four pieces of glass with the edges toughing and into the shape of a cube without a top or bottom, just 4 sides. Then I will coat four pieces of glass and put them in the jig with the emulsion facing outside the cube. Then I will cut three blockers that block the three faces not getting exposed. The object will be inside the cube. The whole thing will set on a base that can be turned 90 degrees. I will expose one plate, rotate the entire jig, move the three card blockers, expose 2nd plate and repeat for all four sides. Then I can process and seal the outside of each plate. Then epoxy them back together in their original location and have a 4 sided hologram of an object. This will allow viewers to try to reach into the box and try to touch the object that is not there.

What propted me to want to make this type of hologram is the statement from the gentleman that asked if I cut the Vodka bottle in half while he was actually looking behind the hologram and frame. I figure if they want interaction, then I should give it to them.

For an object I want to use something that has great detail on all four sides and invites the viewer to want to touch or pick up the object. Not sure what that object is yet. It should not come close to any of the plates as I do not want the object to roll and distort from one view to the next. This is something I will have to give thought on how to get a smooth transition from one plate to the next. Reference beam properties and object size and selection will all play a role in the final effect.

Andy Dean Tue Jun 22, 2004 1:07 pm #7
My first thoughts are to think about the subject (object) and the viewing position first as this will have an effect on the successfulness of the subject matter.

Will the object appear to be one colour? If so this will affect the viewer’s perception as soon as they see the object. Will the viewer be looking at the box at eye level of at a slight angle looking down? One position will make it difficult to reach in the other might give away that the box is empty.

If the hologram was full colour of (maybe) green, then an interesting object would be a pile of Money $100 bills. The temptation would be too great for most people and they would just have to try and pick it up.

Or maybe a nicer object would be something most people never get to touch, but this is when the holographic conundrum comes into play as objects of this type, will just not stay still enough. That’s why I like stereo holograms because the subject limitations are removed some what.

A final thought is the possibility of filling the box with water and suitable objects so it is a fish tank (but no fish, which brings is back to the conundrum again)? Then shoot the hologram remove all the objects and water and see if people want to put their finger in the tank just to see if the water is in there? This I know will have massive technical difficulties but I do begin to wonder how someone looking in the tank from the other side would appear and how interesting the appearance of foreshortening would be????

What I like to consider is the possibility of building a narrative into the journey around the object, so that the movement around the box reveals an alternative perspective of the object within the box, not a visual perspective but an understanding perspective. Like walking around the back of a film set. This is a separate topic really, so I will end my rant here.
