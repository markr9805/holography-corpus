I'm thinking of using a 50/50 beamsplitter cube instead of a 50/50 beamsplitter plate.

The laser i'm using is a 100mw C315m.

The question is should I get a polarising cube or a non-polarising cube?

Cheers

MichaelH Thu Aug 16, 2007 1:41 pm #2
It depends.

Do you have two (at least) half-wave plates so you can optimize polarization of the outgoing beams? If not, go with non-polarizing as you won't gain anything with a polarizing cube.

On the other hand, if you can think toward the future and either afford to buy or wait until something cheaper comes up, get a polarizing cube and three 1/2 (not 1/4!) plates so you can fully control the ratio between outgoing beams as well as control the polarization angle.
BobH Thu Aug 16, 2007 1:58 pm #3
I recommend a polarizing cube and two half waveplates. You need one before the cube to determine the beam ratio, and one for one of the output beams to manipulate that beam's polarization. You really don't need a third waveplate if you build the system so that the reference beam is polarized as you wish. Then, the output beam with adjustable polarization will be the object beam.

With a 50/50 cube, you'll be non-adjustable, at least easily. That would be like working with one hand tied behind your back. Just my opinion.

JohnFP Thu Aug 16, 2007 5:18 pm #5
Got to agree with BobH on that one. I only used two 1/2 wave plates per polarizing cube. And to tell you the truth, in with what Bob stated, if you are going to buy a beam splitter that is not adjustable, the cheapest way is to do what I do. Buy a small 30/70, 50/50 and 70/30 antireflection flat beam splitter. Then glue them next to each other. It's not like having a varible beam splitter but it does give you some adjustment room. The downfall is if you switch to a different ratio, you will surely have to readjust your downstream beam paths. But the good side is they are really cheap.
