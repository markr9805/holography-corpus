Here is a suggestion to reduce the amount of hands on clearing or voltage monitoring of the HV circuit.

If before powering up the circuit, you designed a voltage monitor( a properly rated for the wattage and voltage, voltage divider and chassis mount lcd voltmeter) for it and it is built into the chassis along with your HV power supply that would provide safety to you as you would only have to look at your LCD display to see the voltage.

Additionally, another add on to the circuit is to use a properly rated power resistor(s) from the cap to a relay to ground, both of which are rated for the peak wattage and voltage isolation. A dpdt switch rated for 250vac that powers the HV tran and breaks the 12vdc or what ever the relay's energized requirement is, can be used to switch off the HV power supply feed, the relay will go back to a normally closed position and discharge the caps automatically. By allowing the voltage monitor's own power feed (5vdc for most single ended voltmeter models) to stay active you can monitor the discharge voltage. Of course, even if this is designed properly, the single points of failure of the voltage monitor or discharge circuit can leave lethal voltage on the caps. Therefore always perform a clearing with properly rated equipement before work on the circuit and short the caps to ground to prevent residual cap dielectric stress to return any voltage to the caps. This is very brief suggestion and of course their is still much information not included regarding safety when working around HV equipment. Wearing of rubber gloves and shoes and not working alone are a few tips.

yes, class 1 for your application would work. Never bought from these folks. [http://xpresscartcentral.com/petl/thumbnail.asp?cid=30](http://xpresscartcentral.com/petl/thumbnail.asp?cid=30)

Better to have many redundant circuit features as already mentioned plus add resistors across the cap to bleed off the charge as well. So to recap, you have bleeder resistors that if they fail, the voltage dividers resistors of the lcd voltmeter will eventually bleed off the charge and you also have a discharge resistor and HV relay doing a fast discharge. These measures will clear the caps before you try any manual procedures. There is much danger in doing manual only without the above protections.

For example your discharge wand tip with a HV resistor to ground could come into contact with some chassis part causing an intervertant arc-short and molten metal burns or worse instead of the normal resistor to ground discharge.

After the voltage is reduced above by the circuit items, then to manually clear make sure, wear class 1 gloves, rubber sneakers, one arm only holding the discharge wand/resistor combo to ground. No other body contact with tables or other hand touching chassis or other devices are permitted. A white pvc sch 40 pipe about 18-24 inches long makes a good insulated handle for a wand if you are DIY.

I just thought I'd throw in a very simple idea for messing about with high voltages that I used to do.

I used two ordinary cheap identical step- down transformers wired up back to back. These had to be standard isolation transformers with infinite resistance between the primary and secondary windings. So by then wiring up the 2 low voltage terminals from the transformer pair to form a circuit (with maybe a safety fuse in the circuit just in case.) one obtained a completely isolated mains supply . So there was no potential between you and the ground . (It is as bad as a 340V peak on this side of the pond.)

If you then stepped up the voltage with a third transformer (HT type) connected to the second one and you kept everything well isolated from earth you still had that extra safety. One does have to choose the right size for the transformer pair to handle the maximum current requirements of course.
