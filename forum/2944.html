Ok, quick question here...

Can I get away with regular mirrors for aditional fill in what would otherwise be a single beam reflection hologram?
Colin Kaminski Wed Dec 08, 2004 6:27 pm #2
In a very odd way you can. Use the mirror at 56 Degrees and you will not get a reflection off the first surface. If you use the mirror at another angle you will find in make an interference pattern that does not illuminate evenly.
tpark Wed Dec 08, 2004 8:03 pm #3
I got some hack first surface mirrors from anchor optical, and they work pretty well. Also, if you're in a rush, you can sometimes first surface mirrors at auto parts places as rear view mirrors.
jski Thu Dec 09, 2004 5:32 am #4
I hadn't thought of the auto parts store as a source of first surface mirrors. So far I am managing to stick with mirrorless setups, but I see that I am going to have to get some first surface stuff in the near future.
Ed Wesly Thu Dec 09, 2004 12:47 pm #5
Since these mirrors are only used for fill light and not for imaging or in the reference path you could just use some shiny metal to direct the light to the object.
JohnFP Thu Dec 09, 2004 12:54 pm #6
I used non first surface mirrors extensively and do not notice those fringes. They are very very dim. Especially if the light from that mirror is for additional lighting of an otherwise already illluminated object from the reference beam.

I think the best think would be to set up your set up and see what you have. Good call on the brewsters angle thingy if your poloarization is correct with respect to that angle.
