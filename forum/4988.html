MichaelH Sun Aug 21, 2005 4:57 pm #3
1949? It was barely a gleam then.

I suppose it all depends on what you want. If you want the world-sweeping phenom that holography almost had in the 80's then yeah, you might think it's dead.

It hasn't lived up to the hype any more than AI did in the early 80's but that hasn't stopped researchers from plowing on through and making small but steady advances. I think the unrealized goals of both will be realized slowly such that when the grail arrives most people will say "oh, haven't we had that for years now?" and accept it as a normal part of their lives.

Holography aint dead but it's also not going to change the world overnight like people thought. As for the part that I'm particularly keen on, there's still plenty of room for artistic expression using holography.
