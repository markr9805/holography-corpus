JohnFP Fri Jan 09, 2009 5:50 pm #2
Hummm, without an expensive piece of equipment....

How about looking at the cross-secion under a microscope with a reticle? We used to view 1um pinholes under a microscope with a recticle to check diamter. So you shoud be able to see 25um quite easily. You may have to remove the microscope table and build a jig to hold the plate. But it should work if your coating is even across the entire plate.

Another way might be to put an anti reflection coating on the back glass. Then bounce an expanded collimated laser beam off the plate at NOT Brewsters angle. Using that angle and the distance between the fringes of the "wood grain" you should be able to calculate it. In fact that wood grain would probable tell you how evenly your coating is.

It's amazing how apparent the flaws are in a piece of glass. Simply set up an expanded collimated laser beam. Then put the glass (with no backing) in the laser beam at about 30 degee angle and look at the reflected image on the wall (or ceiling taking into consideration polarization of couse). I usually see nothing close to parallel lines. Not with cheap window pane glass anyway.

Hans Fri Jan 16, 2009 5:10 pm #4
There are three easy ways to do it:

1] Micrometer: Scratch gelatin off the glass on certain places and then with a micrometer measure the thickness of the clean glass and the glass+gelatin right next to it.

2] Microscope: Most advanced microscopes have a fine focus with a micrometer scale attached on them. Make a scratch in your gelatin and focus on the top of the gelatin and onto the glass where the gelatin has been scratched away. It's best to look through the microscope with a web cam as your eyes will try to focus together with the focussing knob of the microscope which makes the test too subjective.

3] Spectrometer (my favourite): Look at the reflection of your gelatin from a halogen light bulb that is some distance away and count the number of dark bands that are between 500nm and 600nm. You can then calculate the thickness with this formula: T= mL1L2/(2n(L2-L1)); L1=500nm, L2=600nm, n=1.5 for gelatin. Please look at this link for photo's done with the spectrometer: [http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewt ... ess#p41567](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?f=20&t=5189&p=41567&hilit=thickness#p41567)

Spectrometers are cheap. You can buy any children's spectrometer as long as it has a nanometer scale on it.
