I think it's not so much an issue of secrecy on part of the SHSG process.

The main difficulty seems to be the lack of a silver halide emulsion that fits SHSG. For example, to have an emulsion whose parameter are subject to change (gelatin hardness mainly!) within a few weeks or months may not lead to consistent results. Furthermore, it might be necessary to know what kind of gelatin hardening has been involved in the manufacturing process. Frankly, I don't see any manufacturer sharing this kind of information.

So at the end of the day we may be stuck with making the emulsions ourselves.
Joe Farina Wed Dec 19, 2007 12:08 pm #15
I really regret spending so much time on SHSG. It was a huge mistake.

As Martin pointed out, that basis for SHSG is a manufactured product (silver halide emulsion) over which we have no real knowledge or control. If we make the emulsion ourselves, then another level of difficulty is reached. It's true that Jeff's diffusion method can produce good results, and this is probably the best way to begin if someone really wants to get into SHSG. Personally, I require that coatings be perfect, more or less. It seemed to me that this would be difficult with the diffusion method. After discounting that, I was left with traditional precipitation techniques, and then again, a new level of difficulty was reached.

When I was doing my last SHSG tests, I was weighing all these factors, looking at the results I was getting, and also looking at the sample SHSG hologram by Shevtsov/Usanov which I have. My results on PFG-03C were lackluster to say the least. Moreover, the chemical processing was more complicated than DCG. The Russian hologram, which is the only thing I have to judge my work against, is good, but definitely inferior to DCG in terms of brightness. So I asked myself: "why do SHSG if it's much more complex than DCG, yet is only capable of inferior brightness?" The only answer that I could come up with was increased speed (i.e. larger holograms) but this does'nt seem so important anymore, once I consider measures that can be taken to increase the stability of the setup (passive means), as well as the possibilities of fringe-locking (active means), so that exposure times can be increased.

Now, the research group with Bjelkhagen is reporting near 100% efficiency with SHSG on PFG-03. But after taking a close look at the patents, I see how complex their methods are. In other words, I see how difficult it would be to duplicate. In addition, I've never seen (or heard how I can see) one of these holograms. To me, a patent disclosure is just another form of literature, and the actual results need to be seen. (It would be kind of like judging a hologram based on a photograph.) I also have a feeling that, if they must use rehalogenating bleaches, there will be a fair amount of noise.

Having said all that, it would be fantastic if someone could reliably produce some good SHSG holograms.
