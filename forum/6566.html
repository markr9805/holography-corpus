Regarding mold coating it's possible to replace the RAIN-X treated cover glass with a plastic sheet. Polycarbonate (PC) proved to be excellent – given it absorbs very little water. PC is a rather rugged stuff – roughly the same material is used for the manufacturing of CDs and DVDs. I've got 0.75mm thick MAKROFOL film (in the US you might look for LEXAN for example) which proved to be very convenient for mold coating.

Since this material is highly flexible, it's much easier to remove once the gelatin layer has set. I had no problems making coatings with 2% gelatin solutions only.

PC has good stiffness. So it provides a fairly even surface. It's worth mentioning though that glass definitely is superior in that respect. PC mold will probably introduce larger thickness differences than glass. However, since most people here are dealing with reflection holograms that issue might not be relevant.

Martin Mon Dec 03, 2007 8:17 am #5
> dave battin: Martin wrote:Since PC is flexible, bubble formation becomes muss less of a problem.Martin do you mean, you chase out the bubble by pressing the mold?

Actually I noticed no bubbles at all. The PC sheet can be bent – so essentially it resembles index matching a piece of Agfa holo film on a glass plate.

With a glass mold I used to pour a line of gelatin close to the edge of the “substrate plate” and apply the glass mold subsequently.

In contrast, when using a PC mold, the gelatin can be poured at the center of the substrate plate. The PC sheet then is bent (concave side facing you) in order to contact the gelatin on a very narrow area. By gently applying the PC towards the substrate glass, the gelatin is spread out evenly and bubbles are easily prevented from forming.

> I have a an SPIE early article about casting hoes on to helmet visors , and they talked about using PC for the molds and how the gelatin released easily from it

That's interesting. I think PC is very convenient here since it's a pretty stiff material that shows relatively good heat resistance and doesn't absorb lots of water. It's far better than PMMA (“plexi”) in that respect.

> I suppose you could just the keep the mold waxed/polished?

You mean leaving the silicone oil release layer on the PC sheet? I didn't notice any problems so far.

One thing to watch though after use is to carefully rinse and dry the PC sheets. A fair amount of surfactant in the final water rinse may help avoid both drying marks and dust.
