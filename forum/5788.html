BobH Sun Sep 17, 2006 12:40 am #1
Tony was worried that his table and recording system wasn't stable enough for a three minute exposure. He did it anyway and just reported that it worked! 8) I can just see him totally Zen'd out ... sitting in a dark room, trays all full of meticulously mixed chemical baths, watching a clock, listening intently for low frequency noises that might excite a component, waiting for just the right moment to open The Shutter of Commitment.

We all know how many tiny little things can mess up a hologram recording. I'm just amazed that it works at all! It's the time from just before I open that shutter to its closing I like best about the practice of holography, though. It's theatrical, scientific, and a gamble. It's de-stressing and spiritual.

I like the little necessity of taking a break for the settling time. I like the focus on background vibrations in a quiet moment. I like the paranoia that comes about a third of the way into the exposure, about helicopters and motorcycles comming by.

I was just wondering what it is about holography that motivates those who practice it. What's your hook?
