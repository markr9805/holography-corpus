I've been doing some tests with dye-spot diffusion for checking the hardness of DCG layers, and bought a video microscope to see if it helps. I wanted to mention that these new video microscopes are very interesting, and not expensive. They have software which enables measurement. So basically, if you put a ruler to the side of the object, the microscope captures a jpeg image, and you draw a line along the length of the ruler (say for example going from the 1mm to the 2mm marking), the software will establish this as a 1mm distance for future measurements. Then, you can draw lines, squares, circles, etc., and it will display the size in millimeters or fractions thereof.

I bought a second one, this time with a higher magnification (from 20X to 800X), eBay item 380455621989 for $38.91. I'm assuming this could be used to measure the thickness of a gelatin layer also. The software takes a little getting used to, and the first one I bought had the standard chinese-translated-to-english kind of instructions that take an extra effort to understand. It seems that these microscopes can easily be made more (or less) powerful by adding a lens or two in front to change the magnification ratio. The photo below shows a 15mm circle drawn with a sharpie pen, which would be a typical result for a dye-spot diffusion test.
