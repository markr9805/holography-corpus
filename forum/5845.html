JohnFP Fri Oct 20, 2006 11:21 am #3
I once stated that the DCG plated needed to be hardened for one reason, among a few, is that the fringe structures needed to be supported. But this comment I made was criticized. Although, I still believe it. In trying to envision a hologram made of two collimate beams, both hitting the plate at the normal and both from opposite sides of the plate I see some very nice, evenly spaced parallel planes running parallel to the glass surface of alternating partially crosslinked (light hardened) and un-crosslinked (non hardened) layers. When the hologram is placed in water, I believe most of the unhardened layers will have their uncrosslinked gelatin washed away. This would lead to almost a total collapse of the fringe structure as without crosslinked areas the water molecules cannot get trapped in the partially formed triple helix structures. Thus in the uncrosslinked areas the water molecules are not trapped and the alcohol cannot replace the water thus preserving the structure of the fringes in the dehydration process.

Also the hardening is needed to allow a higher temperature processing. If the film is too soft then the higher temperatures cause the amino molecules to stretch too much (the spring analogy) and break the weak bonds that are making the fringe structure.

This is evident in that if you simply cool process a DCG hologram without hardening, you get a very blue shifted hologram (fringes collapse). If you use a higher temperature processing without hardening then some of the gelatin molecules have broken apart and cause scatter (milky appearance).

This is the balancing act of DCG. How much harden do you want for what temperature and dehydration rate baths to produce what kind of replay. Then couple this with the amount of exposure which also acts as a hardener, the age of the emulsion (softness), the water content of the emulsion (ambient humidity) and which phase the moon is in and you get quite a lot of variables you can play with. That’s why it is so much fun.

But, the good news is, is that if you control all your variables for a desired effect, it is pretty easy to reproduce it. In other words, if you like a gold DCG hologram and get to a place where you get a nice gold DCG hologram and make note of all the controls on the variables, it is easy to reproduce that hologram.

Hans, I believe you can harden a plate with light but let’s think about that Jeff has taught me. You need two things, hardening and conversion of CrVI to CrIII. Now this explanation is for DCG not MBDCG because of what you told me that MBDCG goes right from CrVI to CrIII very quickly. This is not the case for regular DCG. For regular DCG, when you expose to light the CrVI converts to CrV very quickly but the CrV converts to CrIII much more slowly (does this sound like what happens in a laser???). Any way, if you make a hologram, which has light and dark fringes, then expose to a light (not an interference pattern) the CrVI in the dark areas are quickly being turned to CrV, much faster then the CrV in the light areas are being turned to CrIII. Thus you will get a hologram where the dark areas’ amount of CrIII is catching up to the amount of CrIII in the light areas making a dim hologram. With fixer the CrVI is being washed out before a lot of it can be converted to CrIII in the dark areas. I don’t fully understand the heat process but I imagine through testing that the heat acts on the CrV to allow it to convert to CrIII but does not affect the CrVI in the dark areas very much.

Now with MBDCG, if what you told me is correct in that the MB allows the CrVI to be ultimately turned to CrIII much more quickly then I would have to say no Sodium Metabisulfite, or heat or fixer or light is needed to help with the final conversion to CrIII. But we do need some type of hardener. So possibly a diluted concentration of Alum in water would work prior to water washing and alcohol drying. Balance the exposure of the hologram, the concentration of Alum, the length in the Alum bath and the temperatures of your baths and see if that helps get nice clean gold holograms.

Hans Fri Oct 20, 2006 2:37 pm #7
I want to use the SM to remove all traces of Chromate and MB out of the plate more quickly. I have found that if I keep the plate in the washing (water) bath too long, the gelatin dissolves. If I keep a plate that has not been pre-treated with silane in the washing water too long, the gelatin separates. This usually happens if the plate is submerged longer than two minutes.

If I have something that removes the chemicals quickly enough out of the plate, I can use plates that have not been pre-treated with silane. That makes things simpler. Also, then I can use my coating machine. (Silanated plates are slightly hydrophobic, which makes bar coating more difficult.)
