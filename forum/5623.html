ron olson Mon Jun 12, 2006 3:26 pm #3
Thank you for changing the tone of this forum section away from "Dumpster Diving for Pulsed Lasers". If you have some extra time and a little money - have you given thought to augmenting your laser (actually replacing the final amplifier ) with Nd:Silicate Glass? The problem with using a small diameter (6mm) Nd:YAG rod for the power amplifier is that it becomes an unwanted aperture in the beam. As a rough approximation: 5-10% of the energy reaching the rod edge will result in ~ 30-40% spatial modulation. Getting rid of these rings will require spatial filtering or (easier) using the beam in the true far-field : allowing several meters for the beam to propogate back to Gaussian. This is costly with respect to energy. I see 1/2" diameter and 5/8" diameter (typically x 8" long) amplifier glass and ruby heads/power supplies from time to time on Laserresale.com and with your experience, integration shouldn't be a problem. You may have to keep the gain down to avoid free-running (lasing outside the Q-switched window) but with ~200mJ input (from your first Nd:YAG amp stage) you should be able to get at least 1J output without stressing the system and this should translate to a nice 300-400mJ single shot green output capable of exposing 24" x 20" plates.

As an aside for holographers using Q-switched Nd:YAG or Nd:YLF- I recently tested both the new Fuji film (FH L) and the new HF 55 film from Filmotec. Both produced good initial test results in reflection using ~500mJ in15ns @532nm with SM-6 developer and PBU Amidol bleach.

Hi Ron,

I agree with you the final stage has the limiting aperture of the 1/4 rod and was a known issue before design. But was a demonstration of some available parts. The parameters around this laser was to work within an integrated head/power supply unit which Nd:yag afforded some efficiencies regarding size but lower energy storage than the silicate. Nd:yag oscillators also was known to have it's own issues related to depolarizaton so this was an attempt to quantify a moderate output system using such rods. The system is still undergoing revisions to achieve it's goals. But as such, it's design criteria still is a small chassis system. If other design factors like limited life electrolytics are totally incorporated it may still be possible to use a glass amplifier in this system. No calculations yet have been made regarding that although some consideration already had been given to larger aperture Nd:yag rod 2nd stage if the part become available at a reasonable price.
