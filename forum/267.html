kyodai Thu Jul 14, 2011 8:47 am #1
OK so here is the summary of my first hologram. I must admit although neither my experience nor my preparations were superb it still came out quite well. SO let me tell you what i did and you pros let me know what i can improve (Begging for comment here, hehe!)

First i read the instructions of the holokit. They were like "Get a cup of sand" (Or sugar or salt), destilled water and gloves. For development refer to the developer instructions.

And that was my first error as i went shopping without reading the developer instructions, i thought it was like "add water and you're fine". Big mistake.

So i bought gloves, 2 Liter of destilled water and some beer (Thats why you won't send men shopping).

It was already about 1 am when i decided it was quiet enough outside and inside to start with the hologram and i slowly ran out of beer. So i decided to prepare the developer trays, labeled them and opened the devloper bag. Big surprise, like 8 bags of chemicals and more instructions about how to prepare them. Big shocker - you need at least 4 liter of destilled water while i only had 2. As here in germany there are virtually no "24 hour supermarkets" i decided to make half of the developer. My scale isn't too precise, it counts in 1 gram steps, which is a little bit insensitive to weight 2 gram of some chemicals. SO what i did was making weighting trays out of folded paper and did an "optical" weighting aided by the scale, so for small amounts like 2 gram or 2.5 grams i made sure that it also optically looked like 2 even parts as the scale simply isnt sensitive enough. Worked quite well, i resealed the remaining chemicals in their original bags with sticky tape. I did not have the sodium hydroxide touch the paper trays as it is rather "wet", so i just did tara on the bag and made sure to have somewhere between 7 and 8 grams directly into the developer bottle.

I used coke bottles that i thoroughly cleaned with tap water dozens of times. The i did tara on the scale (´German PET coke bottle with labels and without cap = 82 gram) and just weighted the distilled water into the bottles. Came out 499 gram, 498 gram and 499 gram - fine enough for me. (Originally you should make 1 liter each).

Making the developer took nearly most time due to my weighting session and me being a bit slow. Be sure to take time for that.

Making a proper table was a big problem for me as most suggestions i read were just not practical in my small bathroom (Like 6 by 8 feet, some space occupied with stuff like bathtube, toilet and so on). So i had to go for a limited space solution.

A "high" table is easy to work with in a dedicated garage, but without a ton of sand and concrete it seemed to be less practical to build a high tower, so i looked around for material that would be sufficient. SO what i did was using my old bathroom carpet as the base, putting my mousepad on it. The to stabilize the whole "table" with weight i took a heavy old glass plate (used to be the top of a fish tank) and put it on the mousepad. The weight of the glass ensured that the table was already rather heavy and stable. To get rid of even the last vibrations i poured a small peak of salt on the glass and placed a small dining plate on it. I just arranged the coins on the plate and finished was the object.

The laser had to be on an elevated postion to properly light the dining plate. I took an old vase with a wide bottom and placed it upside down. On the vase the cup with salt and the laser on a clothespin in the salt.

Mr. Iovines holography book served as the shutter - i hope he can forgive me using that.

I prepared all development trays (Developer, rinse, bleach, rinse, wetting agent) in the bathtube.

My safelight was a walmart Wii card. Sounds stupid but the "card" is actually including 3 blue LED lights with spreader windows that make it emit loads of bright blue light in 3 directions on the push of a button. Tested against a CD surface - yep it's indeed true blue light.

I warmed up the laser for about 30 minutes and turned off all devices that could produce any sound including my computer. I turned off all lights as well. Probably rather unnecessary, but i was wearing a mask (Like a doctor in the operating theater), simply as they were like 4 cent each and i still had 20 of them. That way my heavy breathing could not blow the object away.

I put a latex glove on the right hand for handling the plate later in the developer.

I unpacked the plate in the darkroom and tested with a moisturized finger which side the emulsion would be. After placing fingerprints all over the emulsion i found out which side it is and placed it emulsion downside on the coins. I gave the setup 5 minutes to settle down. I slowly lifted the shutter like a half inch, still blocking the beam and allowing everything to settle down again. I held the closed shutter in mid air for like a minute, then i gently put it up for around 12 seconds and put it down again.

I was a bit slow, i think normally you directly put the plate in the developer as soon as you shut the laser again, but due to my setup i was taking a bettry out of the laser first to turn it off.

Then i developed the plate, making sure the emulsion side is upwards to not rub it on the bottom of the developer trays.

I finally put the plate on a soft tissue leaning against my shaving kit. I let it rest there for like 30 minutes then looked at it.

I actually thought it was finished and just a bit dim as i read you can not see reflection holograms before they are completely dry. it appeared a little bit like a clear hologram, you could see all details, but some kind of milky fog in the plate when you hel it in a specific angle. I thought it was already dry as i could see it against the light and i was like "Damn i thought you could view these with a light in the background as well" and wondering what i did wrong.

What i did not know was that it was still soaking wet and the warm lightbulb of my nightstand lamp was slowly drying it. So what i noticed was that my translucent hologram was slowly getting dark at the edges and i was like "OMG where is the manual again? Probably i should have sealed it!" and before i understood what was going on the hologram was dried and turned into a beatiful classic reflection hologram with superb sharpness and contrast. Wow talk about being shocked!

I will make pictures of my setup later on. Thanks in advance for all comments. Without all the helpful input, comments, links and opinions from all of you i am sure i would never have come this far.

lobaz Fri Jul 15, 2011 12:52 am #4
Hello,

congratulations to your first hologram! There are two points that can be made better. First, check spectral sensitivity of the plates. I believe you have a holokit with PFG-03M plates (check it on Integraf site). The spectral sensitivity is available at [http://www.geola.lt/lt/holography_materials/colour/](http://www.geola.lt/lt/holography_materials/colour/). You can see that they are quite sensitive to blue light so using green safelight might be better.

Also, get some box with a sand as recommended in Integraf manual. It will really allow you to make more complicated setups quite easily. I have used 70x50x20 cm box successfully.

One tip: if you want cheap single surface mirrors, just disassembly any old and obsolete harddisk. The plates from inside work quite well.

Dinesh Sat Jul 16, 2011 8:31 am #8
> kyodai: I unpacked the plate in the darkroom and tested with a moisturized finger which side the emulsion would be.

No need. There are better ways to test for the emulsion side. If it's film, the film will curl in the direction of the emulsion. Hold the film in the middle of the top of one side with just your fingertips and notice which way the film curls. The curled side (the concave side) is the emulsion.

> kyodai: Mr. Iovines holography book served as the shutter - i hope he can forgive me using that.

Use something lighter than a book. The reason is that as you move an object, it generates air currents. The larger the object, the more air currents it generates. You can get stiff, black paper or cardboard from any art store, cut it up into a strip about 12cm by 7cm and place it in front of the laser. To expose, simply lift the paper/cardboard. Shutters have always been a bit of a problem because professional shutters are very expensive. A lot of hobby holographers have posted electronic circuits for shutters, if you have the talent for building electronics. Simpler shutters have been numerous and ingenious. Get an old fashioned voltmeter ( the one with a needle that rotates) and putting a black tape over the tip. To expose, simply pass a small voltage through the voltmeter. Cut a disc from a black paper and then cut out a sector of the disk. Place the disk on a spindle. To expose, rotate the disk so the 'open' sector' is in front of the laser.

> kyodai: I was a bit slow, i think normally you directly put the plate in the developer as soon as you shut the laser again

Again, no need. The image will stay in the film for days. Some people swell the film (for a technique known as 'pseudo-color') and leave it overnight.

> kyodai: i was taking a bettry out of the laser first to turn it off.

Cover it with a black cloth. Get a few metres of black velvet and, when you're ready to process, cover the laser with the cloth. Might save a few hours in the laser's lifetime to simply cover it than turning it on and off every few minutes.

The important factors are the exposure and development times. You should expose in such a way that the film develops in about 2 to 3 minutes. By 'develops', I mean that it goes about 80% dark (for reflection Denisyuks) in about 3 minutes. Different people do this in different ways. I pull the film out of the developer every 30 seconds or so and look at the safelight through the (dark) film. When you can just barely see the safelight through the film, then it's about 80% dark. You're exposure should be such that this happens in about 2 to 3 minutes. If this happens in less time, you're exposure is too long, if this happens in too long of a time, you're exposure is too short. One way of getting the exposure right without wasting too many films is to put a black card in the way of half the film. Expose for what you think is about right (you said 12 seconds, so start there). Then put the card over the other half of the film and increase the exposure by 50%. When you develop, notice which half develops first and how dark it gets. If both halves get dark too fast, decrease exposure and try again. Of course, the opposite if both halves are too slow. There are all sorts of machines to give you what are called "density numbers" etc and these are important as you progress. But, in the beginning, when you're trying to get a feel for the processing - an intuition of how far to process - using simple methods will start you off.

Coins is not a good object. The reason is that in the Denisyuk arrangement, the light goes through the film, hits the object and then must be reflected back onto the back of the film. Objects that are very shiny will reflect the light away from the film, not back to it. The best objects are small porcelain figures (start with cats! Holographer's joke!). If you can buy a can of matt white spray paint, spray the porcelain figure with the paint.

Anyway, glad you got a hologram!
