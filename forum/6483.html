JohnFP Fri Oct 12, 2007 12:05 pm #2
Dust on the mirrors is negligible if you have a spatial filter. If you don't then you will see them at the plate as tiny specks with rings around them. You will get holograms, but they will also record what you see on a white card at the plate including the dust and rings.

Most likely your odd shaped beam is from the double reflection of the beam splitter unless it is coated and used properly (the correct side facing the beam). Also some beam splitter coatings are make for a specific wavelength. If incorrect then a partial reflection from the second surface will be reflected along side the first and cause interference, wich will produce an odd shaped beam. Just a guess.
BobH Fri Oct 12, 2007 2:38 pm #3
One or more of your mirrors are probably not flat, or they're stressed into a non-flat surface because of how you mounted them. Another problem may be that the lens you're using to diverge the beam isn't good, and you're seeing the result of an aberrated beam going downstream. Use a microscope objective to spread the beam.

I had a table set up in a metal sculptor's studio a couple years ago, and had a super dusty environment. I dealt with that by putting plastic bags on all components whenever I wasn't working on the table, and building a pretty well sealed enclosure out of thick black matte board to keep the dust out. There was also a black plastic curtain around the table. Without spatial filters, you're going to have to accept all the swirls and rings on your images and plates. You really want to find or make a couple of spatial filters if clean results are important to you.

A spatial filter is probably the best prize that can be given in a contest for holography!
