Tommy Wed Jun 30, 2004 9:23 pm #1
I'm pondering H1 H2 copies, from transmission H1 to reflection H2, to make some image plane holograms...

Looking in Practial Holography, the two plates are put a couple of inches apart (depending on the location of the image), and then a reconstruction beam is shown on the back of the H1, at a 45 degree angle, and a reference beam on the back of the H2, again at a 45 degree angle.

If the plates are less than the width of the H1 plate apart, then the reconstruction beam for the H1 will shine on the H2, and form an "extra" transmission hologram, or a grating with the reference beam.

Since the reconstruction angle of the extra hologram is 90 degrees off from the intended hologram's reconstruction angle, it may be ignoreable. But the reconstruction beam is going to be quite a bit of light, and may over expose that part of the H2 plate.

How is this addressed? One can't card off the extra light, because the card would have to be between the H1 and the H2, blocking the H2's view, or between the laser and the H1, preventing that part of the hologram from being reconstructed. Is the only solution to have a space of at least the width of the H1 between the plates?
MichaelH Wed Jun 30, 2004 9:58 pm #2
there are several ways to solve what you're trying to do. You just need to apply basic geometry to figure out what your distance needs to be.

Barring that, if you know what reconstruction angle you'll be using and the size of your plate holder you can use a ruler, string whatever to just brute force work it out. Here's a quick drawing...

![Image](http://www.dragonseye.com/holography/images/H1H2Layout.jpg)

In the top image you have a basic full-aperture copy with the H1 on the right. You want to design your layout with the copy setup in mind so, start by figuring your reconstruction angle (probably somewhere between 45-56 degrees depending on what you're doing). Now set our your plateholder and figure out how far from your master the H2 holder would need to be so that the reference beam won't hit the H2 plate. That's where you need to put your scene (assuming the "front" will be in the plane of the final copy).

The black squares are light blocking cards which will prevent any spillover from hitting the other plate.

If you're doing a restricted aperture copy the H2 holder can be much closer and will result in a hologram with greater parallax.

This has been a really quick overview as I'm as work and attempting to save my sanity by thinking and posting about holography. If anything's unclear just shout and I or someone else will surely fill in the blanks.
Dinesh Thu Jul 01, 2004 3:06 pm #3
As Michael says, think backwards. There's no restriction on the H1 reference except that you need the same angle to reconstruct. This depends on how much space you have on your table However, one thing to bear in mind is that the reconstruction of the H2 is usually from an overhead source. If the angle of the H2 reference is too low, the reconstruction (white) light source will reflect from the hologram into the viewer's eyes and cause dazzling unless your H2 is better than about 70% efficent. This angle depends on the size of your plate. If it's of any use to you, we use a 40 degree H1 ref and a minimum of 50 degrees for H2 ref. The scene is usually placed about 8 in away, that is, the intended image plane is 8in from the H1 surface. The 'low' H1 ref is so that more light enters the emulsion than bounces off. As the angle increases, the reflectivity, and hence the glancing reflection, also increases. Too low of an angle causes reflections back into the object scene and is difficult to baffle from the edge of the object scene.
Jeffrey Wed Jul 14, 2004 5:52 am #4
forget 45 degrees.

Use 33, from the plate, and figure out your polarization so Brewster's angle works for you. all reflection dies out at that position, telling you exactly where the right angle is.

33 avoids the severe problem of the H1 illumination hitting the H2 plate, and allows closer inter-plate spacing, which gives a much wider viewing field.
