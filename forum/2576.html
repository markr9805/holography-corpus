tpark Tue Jun 29, 2004 2:33 pm #1
What methods do you use for determining optical density of holograms? My current method consists of developing the plate, looking through it in safelight conditions, and guessing if it seems "about right". Not very scientific. I had though that maybe a very low intensity filtered green LED/photocell combination, calibrated against some neutral density filters might provide a more quantitative measurement of what's going on, but I had some concerns that even this energy may cause fogging of the hologram.

To measure how close the exposure is to "ideal" I've been making gratings, and measuring the intensity of the diffracted beam. This may not be quite correct though, as this measures the diffraction efficiency of a 1:1 ratio, as opposed to the 4:1 or greater ratio of actual holograms.

There seems to be a fairly wide range of exposure that produces good holograms, but I'd like to optimize this as much as possible.

I've been exposing so that the plate is darkened, but I can still see through it pretty well.
BobH Tue Jun 29, 2004 2:48 pm #2
I seldom pay attention to photographic density, except as a "reality check" for the exposure time and developer activity. The method you're using of making a series of gratings (best if they have the average spatial frequency and beam ratio of your intended geometry) to easily measure the diffraction efficiency is much more accurate. For example, over-exposure with under-development can give you the same photographic density as under-exposure with over-development, but the resulting holograms could be very different.
wler Tue Jun 29, 2004 3:53 pm #3
I have a couple of test film strips which I had carefully calibrated and with which I compare during development (holding up against safe light). But it's not even necessary to know what the absolute density is - just keep a few sample strips of which you know by trial and error that they have the right density for the emulsion/process in question, and compare to those.

Well, that's for Slavich materials - for Ultimate, there is almost no change in density whatsoever, so all depends on achieving the proper illumination beforehand.

wler Wed Jun 30, 2004 5:34 am #6
> Dinesh: No change in density? You mean that when shot as a reflection and developed, it doesn't go dark in the developer?

Yes and no - he (G) has two different emulsions, U08 and U15, and in fact also sends different versions of those with different behavior. So either the emulsions do not get dark at all, ie at most a pale yellow or pink tint, or they get totally dark (OD3 or so). As getting total dark is in contradiction of what is written on his site, I wasted a couple of plates as I thought I did something wrong. Then I emailed him and he said he is playing with the formula, and getting totally dark was OK with the version of the plates I had gotten.

At any rate, the not-darkening-at-all is independent on how long you expose and develop, and the blackening (for the other variant) is independent on the exposure, even an unexposed plate turns pitch-black when following his developing instructions. He emphasizes that the exposure is the only factor to play with, and this makes adjustment quite easy. On the other hand, since there is no visual guide, all depends on accurate intensity measurements before shooting.
