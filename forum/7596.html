Danny and i just got our hands on this nifty little blue laser and i plan on giving it a few tests to see how well it performs

![Image](http://www.mi-lasers.com/stock/VioletlaserA_300.jpg)

So far all seem good, nice fringes on the Michelson interferometer, with a coherence volume af at least 6".

The MTF or tem00 mode seems a little flat on top with an oblong beam.

After a good bit of shenanigans i got what i would call an "acceptable" beam spread, this is how i did it

i first turned laser on side and bounced the beam w/mirror to @ 90+ deg.(this helped to round beam).then i placed an iris downstream and set it to tiniest opening, and then put the beam thru it, adjusting laser's focus to send the most amount of energy through tiny hole in the iris . Now place SF just downstream in front iris, focus SF and remove/adjust iris.

i had to clip the beam as it passed thru the iris, and used a 40x objective and 25micron in the spatial filter.

Now I’ve achieved a decent beam ill try a dcg holo for the real test ............

ok testing continues, iv been able to see it does work for dcg holography, how ever there are some issues;

the hologram i produced came out very green, and i was rushing a bit as I’m traveling over the weekend, and film was not totally dry, i did notice a nice gold patch running down side of the hologram, telling me i need a thicker film (i used veil coating method), mold coating will correct this. there was absolutely no banding at all

the lens this laser came with for focusing beam works very well if you need to focus all the energy in one small spot, which is not the case here, so my thought here is to change the lens to a better beam spreading lens. Danny also came to the same conclusion.

Another way to spread the beam was to use the raw beam (without lens) and place a positive cylindrical lens to stretch out the narrowest part of the beam, i then placed a 6" dia spherical mirror to "capture the beam and now direct this collimated beam to my plate. i could have easily shot an 8x10 inch plate in 60 seconds.......

Joe Farina Mon Nov 10, 2008 12:14 pm #12
> dave battin: the dog basically came out black

I wonder if the dog was fluorescing. At such a short wavelength, I would imagine that many materials fluoresce, and of course that light would not be coherent.

I did a quick check of the pigments listed in The Artist's Handbook of Materials and Techniques(5th edition) by Ralph Mayer. It was interesting to see the low reflectance for many common pigments at 405nm.

For most purposes, I would just stick with Titanium White, in a reputable grade of artist's color (acrylic, oil, gouache) such as Grumbacher, Windsor & Newton, Liquitex, etc. I would expect such materials to be completely free of dyes that could cause fluorescence.

One possible way to check for fluorescence is to look at the material in the dark with a common blacklight which I believe puts out UV predominantly around 365nm. If it seems to glow a little bit, then perhaps the result at 405nm would be similar. If you just looked at the sample illuminated with 405nm, it might get difficult to visually "separate" the fluorescence from the reflected light, though I would expect fluorescence in many cases to appear white, while the reflected light would of course be 405nm.

In watercolor, titanium white is not available, because it's an opaque pigment (watercolor of course being a "transparent" technique) but zinc white, called "Chinese White" in watercolors, appears to have nearly the same good reflectance (around 85 to 90%) as titanium. A couple other colors which show good reflectance at 405nm are Cobalt Violet and Ultramarine Violet.

I put a little more time and I would like to discuss my observations,

This is only 15 mw of 405 light; the light is VERY powerful and should be handled CAREFULLY! By using a florescent orange poster board cut into squares I was easily able to observe the expanded bean making alignment less difficult.

I got a very clean beam (for Denisyuk holography) by de-focusing beam slightly on to a frosted glass (Polarcoat), then take the illuminated spot and project it using a positive lens , and then capture it using a 6” mirror, this method gave a nice clean beam (while eating up a good amount of light as well) , 2 min exposure for 4x5 @ mixture of .25/14/100

Response to objects color is critical, trial and error is necessary to optimize.

I think this laser has very good potential for one beam holography .

I now need to put this little laser back on the shelf (sadly)and get back on track and start to make silver holograms again …………
