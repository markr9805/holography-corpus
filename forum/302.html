John Sonley Sat Aug 20, 2011 2:06 pm #1
Jem kindly gave me a 15 micron pinhole to use with a X60 objective in my spatial filter and that just nicely covers a 5" X 8" film with smooth collimated red light. The first time I used it, whilst bringing it into alignment, there was no evidence of Airey Rings and the adjusted beam was blotchy - my diagnosis .... a dirty pinhole.----------------- so!

My original cleaning technique was to violently shake the pinhole in a 50% absolute ethanol/water mix in a poly bottle. This I did and the re-assembled pinhole showed evidence of Airey Rings - but very indistinct and very broken ----------------so!

On to eBay and for a surprisingly small sum I'm now the proud owner of an ultrasonic cleaner

About 10 minutes of US cleaning in disilled water improved the Airey rings - but then a quick chat to Dave Battin and he suggested that if the stainless steel pinhole film looked corroded - give it a rub, both sides on 600mesh wet&dry.

This I did and then re-cleaned with distilled water in the ultrasonic cleaner

The final result is a pinhole that does not give continuous concentric Airey rings - but they are pretty damn good and the reference beam is smooth and even and I've just made a really bright and even H1 of a lump of dessert rose

Just thought you might like to know how one guy tackled a dirty pinhole

John

Johnfp Wed Sep 07, 2011 7:43 am #5
How to clean a pinhole:

First try compressed canned air with a straw. Hold it as close as possible (dont touch the hole) and give it a 1 - 2 second shot. Check cleanlyness and repeat from other side if necessary.

Second, try the ultrasonice bath but don't just drop it in, make a jig so that the hole itself is not touched. If still dirty repeat first step with air.

To really understand how to clean a pinhole, you have to know how it is made. A 15 micron pinhole is made one of two ways. Either blasting the SS with a pulse of laser light or blast with laser and then ream. At 15 microns it could be either way. Less then 15 then doubtfull their was reaming. Above 25, almost probably reaming.

Now to get to my point. Reaming a hole usually produces a burr on the hole's edge. If you touch that edge then you can bend the burr into the pinhole. This is almost impossible to clean. Even if you break the burr off by air or whatever, it usually takes some material way from the edge where it was connected. Not a round hole anymore. You may get lucky and bend the burr back outsid the hole but I doubt it.

So never ever let anything touch the hole itself. It should always be stored in something that supports it from the outside and leaves the hole suspended in air.

Just my experience.
