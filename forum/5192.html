Making one be an amplifier is a good idea as well. There are many options but one is to take the second one and remove the optics and the Q-switch and place it ahead of the frequency doubler. This may make the beam have a significant amount of divergence however. One would have to try it to know.

Bill Jensen Tue Nov 22, 2005 11:28 pm #9
Hi Colin,

Looks like you are having fun !

The SCR is the one on the red "firecracker" board, and has the Hexnut. Shorting the indicated places with a Normally Open switch will fire the trigger fine. Cap voltage is measured from the side of the resistor next to the cap. (The other side of the resistor goes to the + of the HV power supply.) The ground of the voltmeter goes to the black wire with the metal lug with round hole in it.

That HV supply is nice to be a variable one. A variac with a transformer and bridge rectifier can be an inexpensive option. I bought parts for an inexpensive power supply for this laser SSY1 to make sure it works. If so, I can sell some that are tested, maybe for $100.

Bill Jensen Wed Nov 23, 2005 4:31 am #11
Hi Colin,

The caps' red wire goes to the flashlamp. It also goes to the 3.9 K ohm resistor (10W) which then goes to the positive of the charging power supply. It serves to limit the current from the power supply to the cap.

Here is the sequence of discharge, after the cap is charged over about 1/2 a second, you are ready to discharge by triggering. Upon triggering the cap dumps all the charge in it through the smoothing and limiting inductor in the PFN. In the meantime the HV source tries to charge up the cap again, but can't overcome the massive drain from the flashlamp conducting. With the 3.9K the lamp then extinguishes and then the HV power supply can charge back up the cap. If you didn't have it, the power supply would deliver a lot of current (possibly burning it up) and keep the flashlamp glowing. Not what we want.

I hope that helps.

Sincerely,

Bill Jensen
