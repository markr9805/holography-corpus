Tom B. Thu Jul 01, 2004 3:17 am #5
It's a curious paradox that in holography, unlike other graphic arts, pure abstraction (of the non-trivial variety) seems (and is) MUCH more technically difficult than realism, whether sur or super. I'd LOVE to be able to make amazing non-referential light sculptures, but for now I'll have to settle for doing the best I can with what I have. Personally, I tend to favor art with some sort of easily graspable emotional context anyway, so it's no great loss at present, but it would be nice even for realistic works to be able to provide an interesting abstract 3D setting ....
JohnFP Wed Jul 07, 2004 9:34 am #6
I too love the abstract and that is why I created the broken mirror hologram. I like them so much I am going to do another session of them, hoping to get a good 1/2 dozen or so. DJ, if you would like and you are patient I would be glad to send you or anyone else one that may want one. What I want to do with the next session is raise the plate a good 1/2 inch or inch off of the object and get more depth when it is viewed from the pseudoscopic side. I was also thinking of increasing the size of the clear glass ball from the 1/2 diameter to two or 3 inches in diameter.

I had all the lights off in the room, it was night but the hall light was on outside the room. As I walked by the wall, whereas most of the other holgorams were barely visible, the cracked mirror hologram reflected very nice flashes of bright color and beauty.

I was also thinking about trying to add a two way mirror (partially silvered) on the reference side to get multiple reflections from the partially silvered mirror and the cracked glass.

Peace and have fun!
