I expect the hydrometer is calibrated for ethanol? There is a slight density difference: ethanol = 0.789, isopropanol = 0.785. Here though, the error is in the wrong direction - the float should sink deeper into the isopropanol, indicating more alcohol. Was the alcohol stored in glass bottles? It's very hygroscopic. Do you get a reading of 0% with pure water? What accuracy do the hydrometer makers claim?
Joe Farina Sat Aug 11, 2007 11:13 am #3
This morning I tested the hydrometer with distilled water, and the reading came in at -1% (after temperature adjustment). As for the accuracy of the hydrometer, they didn't say. The IPA was stored in sealed plastic bottles, so perhaps there was some permeability of the plastic.

I don't think an alcoholometer is useful for this kind of measurement, as it's probably not precise enough. I have learned a couple of things from this, though. The only way I currently know about being really sure about IPA concentration is to buy from a reputable chemical supplier, in glass bottles, and be sure it's well-sealed at all times. I think it will be worth the extra trouble to do this. Even though expensive, the really pure stuff is only needed for the final bath, and this can be in a special container which holds the minimum amount of IPA, to avoid waste. This wouldn't be needed for any of the baths preceding, of course. The IPA from CVS (as Dave mentioned) would be good for the next-to-last bath, and I seem to recall John saying that 70% and 91% IPA is cheaply available (in large containers) from K-Mart. Alfa Aesar has a number of grades in the 99.7+% range (with water contents in the 0.1 to 0.05% range) for $44 to $73 for 4 liters. So I think it will be worth it, if I can make it last.

There is no way it remains at 99% for an extended exposure to the atmospheric moisture.

I will say using two baths will help in maintaining a longer life, but after just a few soaks of a plate in the bath prior to the fist 99%, the fist 99% will rapidly dilute a lower concentration thus causing more water in the second 99% bath. I doubt the 99.953% stays at 99.9 for very long.

I will agree that there is a point where the dilution of the last bath causes undesirable drying effects, but my rule of thumb is, if I can see the image develop in the last bath, the alcohol concentration is ok. After while, I found I could use the meter for all concentrations below 99% and thusly never had to buy alcohol other then the 99% (99.953%).

Colin Kaminski Tue Aug 14, 2007 2:09 pm #11
A safety note should be added here. Heating 99% IPA is dangerous. It is critical that the heater is thermostatically controled.

> According to NPIRI's Raw Materials Handbook which referenced numerous other chemistry reference books, the flashpoint of IPA is 12oC (53oF) and the boiling point is 82oC (180oF). This data is for 100% IPA and the 99% values should be virtually identical with any differences in the 0.1 or less range as the boiling point for a 91% IPA pure material is 80oC (177oF).

A flashpoint this low tells me that an open flame is not an option to heat IPA. Also it tells me that there is a large amount of IPA in the air in these processing rooms and ventelation would be required. (I used one with a small bathroom fan and it was way too small.) (Think explosion proof fans).

What are you guys using? I think I used about 110F bit it was so long ago I don't remember I would have to check my notes.
