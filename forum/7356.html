In the "Focused image suing LCD" thread I alluded to the fact that typically holographic recording materials do not preserve the polarization states of the beams used to make them. But there is the exception that proves the rule.

I remember at the 1988 Lake Forest Symposium Jeff Blyth showed a hologram using something he had cooked up that did preserve polarization! jeff-blyth Mon Jun 02, 2008 2:04 am #2
Hi Ed ,

Yes you are right I did have a polymeric liquid crystal Denisyuk hologram illuminated by a spot lamp shining through a slowly rotating piece of Polaroid filter.

The image showed some shiny 1 Mark coins lying among some peppermints. As the Polaroid sheet went round , the coins darkened down almost to black disks but left the randomly polarizing peppermints still white and virtually unaffected.

As one does so often with the benefit of hindsight I kicked myself for having so stupidly chosen a really slow motor to turn the Polaroid sheet , it meant you had to stand there for a full 30 seconds to witness the coins going through a full light- to –dark- to- light cycle. I should have had it going 50 times faster so that they were flashing on and off among the white peppermints of course.

So why are these effects not out and about in exhibitions and advertisements?

Well there were problems with this recording material., …technically very bad ones unfortunately. The polymer recorded the hologram in the side chains hanging off the polymeric backbone. These side chains had an

N=N double bond arrangement (a “diazo” group) which in its stable form had the other bonds attached in a “trans” position and after irradiation

to 488nm it changed over to a “cis” arrangement. While you yourself of course know about cis and trans arrangements across double bonds, other readers may not.. So I need to mention that the trans arrangement is symmetrical but the cis form is bent and strained and can be in some arrangements optically active. ( Cis-trans is discussed here with respect to C=C bonds:-

[http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_isomerism](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_isomerism)

Anyway asking light to do work by bending chemical molecules about is asking for a lot---- and the exposure requirements for an area of polymer covered by a beam of about 3 inches in diameter from a 1 watt Argon laser at 488nm required more than a half hour's exposure!---(Although no post exposure processing of any kind was required .)

But that exposure requirement was’nt the worst of it, those diazo molecules were very unhappy in their bent over positions , they naturally wanted to straighten up all the time and go back to the trans arrangement (actually to be pedantic the cis and trans forms of nitrogen double bonds are called “syn and “anti”) .--- Every night during the symposium I had to leave the hologram in a container of iced water in my hotel room (it was at least totally unaffected chemically by water) but it wasn’t stable at room temperature and by the end of the week following the symposium it was too weak to see anymore.

However a typical bit of the law of unforseen consequences for all holographers came out of this , the polymeric material was made by chemists at Rohm GmbH and I volunteered my services to Rohm to try making holograms out of it by working with them for a few months in Germany. Meanwhile this type of material was having its physical behaviour investigated for a physics PhD thesis by a student at the Deutches Kunststoffinstitute in Darmstadt West Germany. That student was the marvellously talented guy Richard Birenheide. Richard caught the Holography bug from me and after he finished his thesis he invited me over to work in the basement of his mother-in –law’s house so that we could make our own silver halide plates. Richard then resolved to set up a manufacturing unit . He brought in his great natural engineering skill and it must be said much family money to bring this to fruition. The result was the “BB” plates now made by Colour Holographics Ltd in the UK.

I have still got some of those plates which are 12 years old now,-- never kept them in a fridge at all-- and once they are given the old TEA treatment they are as good as new! Unlike others I could mention, they are there for the real long term.

Jeff

BobH Wed Jun 04, 2008 12:31 am #16
I remember seeing that hologram demonstrated then as well, and was always somewhat mystified by it. It was certainly the first hologram I ever saw that had liquid crystal in it. In '99, I started working with HPDLC (Holographic Polymer Dispersed Liquid Crystal) materials at Retinal Displays. Holograms made with it will only diffract p-polarized light.

The Denisyuk hologram of peppermints and coins Jeff showed in '88 had the objects right up next to the plate, if I remember correctly. I suspect the specular reflection off the coins created the simple gratings needed for HPDLC recording locally, and that area of the plate was polarization sensitive upon reconstruction. The complex gratings forming above the areas with peppermint information resulted in conventional photopolymer recording and was therefore not polarization sensitive. Just a guess.

In any case, that was one of the most amazing holograms I ever saw, and it's gone pretty much unnoticed for the past 20 years. Kind of amazing to me that I ended up working with that kind of thing for the past 9. All I can say is "whodathunkit"!
