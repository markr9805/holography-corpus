What processing chemicals would be best for developing VRP-M as a high resolution, hight contrast, black and white photography film?

I need very high resolution with maximum contrast. The exposure would not be holographic although may be made with a laser. If made with a laser the exposure would not have a reference beam, just a projected image thus it would record as a standard photography image.

Any help or experience in this would be grateful.
Ed Wesly Fri Jan 27, 2006 12:32 pm #2
Kodak D-19, D-11, or D-8 would be good places to start.
JohnFP Fri Jan 27, 2006 12:44 pm #3
Thanks Ed, I was leaning toward D-19 as it is a high denisity developer. I guess I should stop and fix, right?

It's been a while since the old B&W photography.
Ed Wesly Fri Jan 27, 2006 4:56 pm #4
No and yes. Stop bath with these high contrast jobs sometimes gives little pinholes that would have to be opaqued out. Use running water instead.

Yes, use fixer, any kind.

D-11 and D-8 are even higher contrast and density than D-19. But I am sure you will be fine with any of them. It depends on what is available at the camera store or what ingredients you have on hand.

Ed Wesly Wed Mar 22, 2006 12:54 pm #10
The D-19R is the replenisher needed to top off the working solution of D-19 whenever a plate is developed in it. It would be a much faster acting developer since it has twice as much of the developing agents plus a higher pH value with the addition of the sodium hydroxide. I probably wouldn't use it for the developer since it might just fog the highlights.

I would usually develop Agfa plates in D-19 for two minutes when making masks for 2-D/3-D graphics etc. Probably do the same for Slavich. But you could go possibly go as long as 6 minutes before getting fog in the highlights.

There is no way to change from energy measurements to ISO precisely, as the ISO takes into account broadband visible wavelengths and the Joules per cm^2 is wavelength specific.

As a sort of guideline, I made a graphic on both photo paper and an Agfa 8E75HD plate. The photo paper, the lamented Agfa Brovira, needed a 1" exposure under my enlarger. The Agfa plate, same circumstances, 50"! So that should give you a feel for how much light you might need to expose the holo plates to white light, considering that photo papers were regarded as having an ISO of 1. (Basic Daylight Exposure for photo paper in a 4" by 5" view camera was 1" at f/16.)

Hope this helps!
