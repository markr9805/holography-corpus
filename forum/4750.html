How difficult would it be to produce Automated Holographic Stereogram H1's with a pulsed laser?

Set the subject on a turn table or have the subject perform some movement. Have a slit cover the plate which is attacted to a worm gear translation stage and a stepper motor. Time the stepper motor movement with the PPS of th laser. Stand back and watch the hologaphic movie (stereogram master) being made.

What is the maximum PPS of a holographic pulsed laser? How many times can a stepper motor stop and move a few mm in a second? 5, 10, 25 times a second? These limitations would dictate how fast the object can move. An optical feedback strip sensor on the slit translation stage would insure absolute position instead of relative position of the slit.

This has to have been tried, no?
Steve Wed Apr 13, 2005 11:42 am #2
My small lathe and mill with a 130mhz pent computer will step a little over 20 inches a min while cutting and some where around 50 inches per min in rapid while not cutting. In my case it's mostly the computer limiting the speed. The machines at work are all servo, but hit 600 inches per min while holding .0001 inch through the movement. They are all moving a lot more mass then you are probably talking about.

I had heard that the Royal College of Art had a stereogram contraption illuminated by a pulsed Ruby laser. But this thing was used to turn film footage into an H1, not shoting directly from an object. This I know from hearsay so there are others that have had experience with this device, unfortunately long since disassembled.

Since ruby lasers that I have worked with have really slow rep rates, like seconds between pulses it wouldn't keep up with a mechanism as fast as this proposal is suggesting. But the article in holosphere that describes the holographic cinema of the Russians mentions a ruby doing 24 fps. Yag sounds like the better choice.
