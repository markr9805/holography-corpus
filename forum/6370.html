Hi, I was reading an article by Jeff Allen on Fresnel lenses

"Optical projection systems utilizing Fresnel lenses can produce images without the use of screens. The images are optically focused by the Fresnel lens system to float in front of the viewer."

Does anyone have anymore expertise in this area? Can anyone point me in the right direction?

Thanks
favalora Thu Aug 02, 2007 12:51 am #2
Hi -

Yes, that sounds right. A good convex lens or a concave mirror can re-image an "object" behind it into a real image in front of it - between you and the lens. A Fresnel lens might work, too, but will probably result in something a little lower quality.

Of course, you can only see the parts of the image that lie along the line of sight between you and the lens, etc. etc. In reality things need to be set up just right so that your brain is tricked into believing it. Also, "deep" objects won't work too well because the magnification is different along that direction.

Check this article out. (If I had a nickel for every time I cited this paper...) [http://web.media.mit.edu/~halazar/autos ... tereo.html](http://web.media.mit.edu/~halazar/autostereo/autostereo.html).
