Ed Wesly Fri Mar 06, 2009 5:59 pm #5
Here’s that old fiber in holography nay-sayer once again rearing his ugly head. Actually for single beam work there are not so bad for delivery of a beam, but trying to wrassle two fibers into stability submission is not my idea of fun.

We did attempt to make some multi-wavelength recordings at LFC using a trio of fibers in a single chuck. But even the small single mode fibers bunched together created color shadows around objects, like a red shadow on one side of an object and a blue one on the other side, an effect not unlike what I’ve seen in some color holograms by Hans Bjelkhagen. Small alignment differences over long distances do show up!

Something TJ had run across was an evanescent wave coupler for fibers. It was like two fibers were laid next to each other, with the jacket and cladding removed from the adjacent sides of two fibers, so that the cores barely touched, an you could get the light to “jump” from one to another the closer you got them together! So a pie in the sky true color set up might entail the three lasers individually fed into fiber optics, and then all combined into one fiber by a couple of these evanescent couplers.

There was a guy, I don't remember his name but Ed might after I bring this up and remind him, that made a full fiber setup.

It was a small setup of machined alum boxes with controls. About the size of a shoe box. In it was a fiber launcher and a few of those beam splitters Ed mentioned. Putting raw fibers close together. There were mic screws to adjust all that.

It was single color but still a very nice rig if you want to go that way. Fibers delivered everything. 2 object beams and a ref. He cleaved the fibers at brewsters and because of the small apt didn't need to further spatial filter.

He was at Lake Forest trying to sell it. I think he wanted 10 to 15 thou for it.
