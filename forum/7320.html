BobH Tue May 06, 2008 1:50 pm #6
I see a problem with your plateholder plans. Even if you were building your system on a Newport type table top, with flatness +/-0.004" over the whole surface, and the mag bases were top of the line, the upright posts would still not be adequately parallel. When you attach the plate sandwich, you'll probably clamp a stress into it. To overcome this, attach little offsets to the posts. Two on one of them and one in the middle of the other. Then, clamp the plate sandwich to these spacers with the minimum force needed to hold it from falling off.

I don't have one of the posts anymore but I have 20 or so of the bases. I remember the uprights as being much smaller than 1/2" like 10mm. The thread in the block is metric. I made up 50 studs that had the metric thread on one side and 1/4 by 20 tpi on the other. Then I made a bunch of uprights and cross pieces from 1/2 drill rod with 1/4 20 holes on the long pieces and #8 32 holes on the smaller pieces. I made some cross blocks from aluminum that have two 1/2" holes and a pinch bolt. I have found them to be very useful and quite stable. They are also easy to configure into new configurations. I did this so long ago that I take having them for granted.

Jeffrey Weil Thu May 15, 2008 1:30 pm #21
Hello Everyone,

In the past I've also made plate holders out of mag mounts. I went into the closet to find my old stuff and took some shots for you.

These are just flat pieces of steel that have been ground flat. One has a square hole in it for reflection work. In the shot you can see one of the back edges has been ground with a hand grinder on an angle to let the ref. light in.

To hold the plate or sandwich you put magnets around the edges of the steel half way on the glass. You could also use clamps, toggle clamps, etc.... A design like this lends itself to adaptation easily.

The plate is very close to the ground with this design. My mirrors were very low too. If your optical axis is higher you can just build more metal on the bottom, or put it up on blocks. With this design you can just do whatever you need pretty fast and easy. You can even tilt it if you need for some reason.

If you have a milling machine this will be very easy to make. If not find a small shop. You should be able to get these made these pretty cheap.

I've made many reflection pieces with these holders. They are very stable. The entire edge is resting on the table or a block. They are also registered holders. One of the spring pins goes into the mag mount to hold it but the other's are for registering your glass or plate.

Colin Kaminski Fri May 16, 2008 2:49 am #23
I route openings in aluminum plate all the time. If you think about the jigging first it is quite easy. Aluminum cuts better as a "climb cut". That is backward from how wood cuts and means you need to have a firm hold of both the part and the router. You can regular cut aluminum but the finish is not as nice. (I also table saw aluminum with very narrow kerf wood blades.)

Jeffrey's Holders are very nice. Setting them on the table is a great idea. I often hang 4x5 holders off of a single 1/2" rod on a mag base. Two is better but if you don't have room you can make one work. These cheap bases are not always flat so it is good to kiss them flat. I have a course diamond stone that I pass them over when I buy them to check if they are flat. If not, then I belt sand and then use the stone again. Also, make sure the table is clean when you place them.

Michael Harrison cast some epoxy onto the bottom of his and that might be less work.
