> DannyBruza: have you ever used the dye Rhodamine B in dcg gel? or are you familiar with this dye?

No I have never used it in DCG work Danny.

I have used it a while back as the dye of choice in a Argon ion pumped dye laser. I do remember reading something about the properties of a good dye for lasing being exactly the wrong ones for generating free radicals . So that suggests to me it is not the best one for your experiments with dichromate.

For your 514nm line I would recommend trying Erythrosin B , that is the one that is the sodium salt and not the "spirit soluble" one. Erythrosin B is not a nasty one nor is it particularly expensive. I once used it successfully with my TMG/chromate system with a 1mw green HeNe to get a 1.2 inch diam Denisyuk.

Yes you are probably right about that triplet state difference.

With regard to comparing it with Methylene Blue it was difficult to make quantitive estimates because of the very different laser ouputs.

It was very clear though that Ery B just did not photobleach easily like MB in the presence of TMG or TEA amines (leaving dichromate out that is.) Ery B is an anionic dye whereas MB is as you know cationic. So if you try using them together to get panchromatic results you get complications because they can mutually precipitate out of aqueous solutions . You can get away with it switching to alcoholic solutions (EryB "spirit soluble grade") but then the gelatin does'nt like alcoholic solutions.

However the Microscopy people don't need the chemistry subtleties we need.

Rose Bengal is active around 550 nm --rather far from your 514nm requirement.

(It is also a bit toxic compared to Erythrosin B.)

The Aldrich catalogue shows the chemical structure of Rhodamine B (Lmax 555 nm) compared to "6G" (Lmax 528nm).

If wavelengths are far from your 514nm it is not helpful and undermine undermine what you are trying to do.

There is an old photographic dye phenosafranin (that used to be used to desensitize photographic papers or plates so that you could process them in normal light. MB works the same but stains the gelatin too much.) That one has L max at 519nm. As you could probably get it from photographic specialists I think worth a try.
