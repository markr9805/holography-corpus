JohnFP Wed Apr 06, 2005 3:09 pm #1
Since for some reason I aquired a 1/4 wave plate I was always wonder if it could be usefull in holography. I just read a post about making a hologram of an object that may have specular reflections. If this is the case the using a 1/4 wave to circularly polarize the object beam may cut down on the amount of specular reflections give off by those points on that object which otherwise would be polarized in the same direction as the reference beam.

Then I was reading the Glossary at the Melles Griot site on 1/2 wave plates and it never dawned on me that if you had 2 - 1/4 wave plates you could identically orient then and place them back to back to achieve the same results as a 1/2 wave plate.

[http://www.mellesgriot.com/glossary/wor ... sp?wID=170](http://www.mellesgriot.com/glossary/wordlist/glossarydetails.asp?wID=170)
MichaelH Wed Apr 06, 2005 4:58 pm #2
Yep, that does work. The PCGG-I netsuke hologram you have was made with a 1/2 wave plate made from two 1/4w plates stacked. You lose some light from the extra surfaces though so if you can get a cheap 1/2w plate, it's worth it.
Colin Kaminski Wed Apr 06, 2005 10:23 pm #3
I have one that I laminated with index matching adhesive. Norland of some number or other. It works well.
