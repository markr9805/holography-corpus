rzeheb Fri Apr 03, 2009 12:15 am #1
My lab (at work, not my holography setup which can be called a lot of things but not a lab) used to perform electron microscopy. Recently I ran across some EM film that was destined for the trash. I rescued it in the hopes that 300 sheets of 4 by 5 film might be useful for making holograms. The film "expired" in 2003 but that's not THAT old, probably just lost some sensitivity ![:pray:](https://holographyforum.org//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f64f.svg) . It was stored in a refrigerator the whole time. The film is Kodak Estar Thickbase 4489 (emulsion No. 27200201). As far as I know, EM film is typically ultra fine grain and sensitive at the blue end of the spectrum. I am using a 532 nm laser. Anyone know what the spectral sensitivity of that film is, or else, can anyone suggest a resource where I might go to look it up?
Colin Kaminski Fri Apr 10, 2009 12:05 am #2
I was hoping someone would awnser. I think you are going to have to shoot some trials. Set up two beams spread to fill the test piece at a 15 degree angle. Find the exposure time that causes the film to go dark in 60 seconds in the developer. Then try increasing the angle till you find out if you can have the beams at the oposite sides of the film.

Also, take a bleached piece of film and see what the base does to the polarization of a laser. Once you have that data you can plan some shots.

salvee Sat Apr 11, 2009 9:35 am #5
From a quick web search, it seems as if the resolution of the film is 200 lines/mm. You need 2000 lines/mm for a transmission hologram and 5000 lines / mm for a reflection hologram. My advice is to stop wasting your time trying to make this film work for holography.
Jeffrey Weil Sat Apr 11, 2009 1:24 pm #6

I have not looked up that film but the above info makes it pretty clear it won't work. BUT, its probably a nice film to convert to Jeff Blyth's diffusion method for home made film.
