Dinesh Mon Sep 06, 2004 12:31 pm #2
It all depends on the quality and intent of the finished holos.

[quote:0b25e71fdc]I am wondering what would be the cost per unit[/quote:0b25e71fdc]

It'd depends on what medium you want to put the H2 and how quickly. If the piece is intended to be put up on diosplay with a light on it, you could shoot it in Silver or photopolymer. If photopolymer, I suspect it'll be expensive. I know Du Pont will make H2's from H1's but they need certain requirements on the H1. I've never seen an 8x10 photopolymer. If on Silver, Slavich does not make 8x10 plates, as far as I know. I've been trying to find 8x10's myself. Colourholographics makes series BB 8x10's. They cost about $650.00 and you have to wait 2 weeks or so for delivery ( [http://www.vinteq.com/price.htm](http://www.vinteq.com/price.htm) ) . This is if you intended to shoot it yourself. You can always shoot on film, which is cheaper, but holding an 8 x 10 film poses it's own problems. You can try DCG, we do DCG H1 to H2 copies on 8x10 ( [http://www.triple-take.com/](http://www.triple-take.com/) ). The cost would depend on the H1's, but it's very much cheaper (and brighter!) than Silver. If you want to go that route, call us. JohnFP, on this forum, also does DCG as an amateur. I know John doesn't do H1-H2 copies, and I don't know if he can do 8x10's in Denisyuk. John? You can see both John's and my DCG's on the PCG Photo Gallery ( [http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/album01](http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/album01) )

[quote:0b25e71fdc] Do I need a model for the shots or can I use photos or even an original? [/quote:0b25e71fdc]

You can pretty much use anything you like. Be aware that a photograph is a 2D object. You can take it out of the image plane, so that it receedes behind the hologram, or put it in front so it floats above the hologram, but it's still 2D. When we first started, a lot of people wanted a picture of family members "as a hologram". We pointed out that a picture is a 2D object and so there was no point. It turned out that just shifting the image plane a little made them think I'd made "3D" out of their picture! If you want a holograms of a 3D object, you'd need to have a model from which you make an H1, from which you make H2's. You can make Denisyuk's of the model, depending on it's size and depth and so make an H1 unnecessary.

[quote:0b25e71fdc]Who would be the best to help me with this project? Company or individual...[/quote:0b25e71fdc]

I think that's a very individual decision. It depends a lot on your resources and the intent of the hologram. If it's for yourself, making it yourself would give you exactly what you wanted. if it's for someone else, it depends on how this someone else intents to display it. There's all kinds of "backwards thinking" that goes on in making a hologram and in a lot of situations people have bought holograms in stores and then have been disappointed because they didn't light it right and complained they couldn't see it. Commercially, this lighting problem is the number 1 problem of holographers. Holographers go halfway around the world to set up pieces for customers because, if left in the customers hands, it's usually badly lit! A company would help you with lighting and composition based on their past experience.

If you need any more help, feel free to call me.

BobH Mon Sep 06, 2004 2:34 pm #4
> We pointed out that a picture is a 2D object and so there was no point.

This is a very strange sentence from a physicist, Dinesh!:P A 2D object? We must be in Flatland! If someone asks a holographer if they can make a hologram from a picture, the answer ought to be a strong "yes" (then start talking about price!).

A picture can be "photoshopped" and cut up to make "cardboarded" objects. The photo itself in some kind of framing is a 3D object. As you mentioned, simply recording the image "off the plane of the hologram" is a great effect. The photo can also be curved or bent creatively to make it more 3D. It can be cut up and made into sculpture. A bunch of photos can be grouped togehter to form a scene. A photo along with an appropriate small object related to the photo. The list can go on, but you get the drift.

The general public has a very different understanding of a hologram than we do. It's our job to give them what they want, not what we think they really want.

Dinesh Mon Sep 06, 2004 3:17 pm #6
[quote:14b26a53f4]This is a very strange sentence from a physicist, Dinesh! A 2D object?[/quote:14b26a53f4]

Actually, it's exactly what a physicist would say. An object, to me, is independant of it's dimensionality. A point is a 0D object, right up to 24D objects of various Unification schemes. Holographically, anything that generates a wavefront is an object. For instance a very small ball bearing used to generate an almost spherical wavefront is almost a 0D object. Of course, if you think of an "object" as a tangible thing that you can grasp, then a 2D thing is not an object.

[quote:14b26a53f4]If someone asks a holographer if they can make a hologram from a picture, the answer ought to be a strong "yes" (then start talking about price!).

The general public has a very different understanding of a hologram than we do. It's our job to give them what they want, not what we think they really want [/quote:14b26a53f4]

True. Very true. It's this damn conscience!

Neither Joy nor I could quite get ourselves to charge someone for a mono-colored picture, just because it was a "hologram". Many years ago (1981), I attended my very first lecture on holography given by Andy Pepper. Edwina was trying to interest (induce?) business types into applying holography and got Andy to give 1-day lectures. Since at the time I knew zilch about it, I thought this was a good way to "get my feet wet". Anyway, Andy said, at the time, that holography simply would not survive if it was nothing more than an analogue of 3D for the sake of 3D alone. The way he put it was: "Who'd hang up an exact image of a hammer in 3D inside a piece of glass, when for a tenth of the price they could hang up the actual hammer." This statement has stuck with me all these years. I thought that I couldn't in all good conscience sell someone a "2D hammer hologram". I must admit though, the price of beer being what it is, I may have unfortunately softened this stance Especially since time and time again, I keep seeing these "hammer holograms" selling.

Regarding Flyguy. If you wanted to contact copy an existing reflection H2, it may not be easy. It would have to have a lot of reflection in an appropriate laser wavelength. If you had the original H1, then it's easy to make copies in any format. I doubt any holographer would make an 8x10 H2 for $24 today.However, I'm assuming you want the costs of making the H2 yourself. Cost of materials etc. In this case, get in touch with Alec Jeong at Integraf ( [http://www.holokits.com/](http://www.holokits.com/) ). Glass is incredibly expensive, but film may not be. You also have more flexibility with film. You can cut it up to any dimension you like.

Origination cost would depend on who you go to and what you want. Some places charge from $1500 $2000 for an H1 master, but these are intended for the mass production market. I believe Du Pont have a fixed setup and require a particular H1 reference in a particular geometry. If you want to farm out the H2 on Silver, Id think an independant holography studio is the best option. Unfortunately most of them have packed up and gone home. We do Silver and I believe that BobH does (Bob?). Michael has a pretty good list of holographers ( [http://dragonseye.com/holography/links.html](http://dragonseye.com/holography/links.html) ) If you want it on DCG, email me.
