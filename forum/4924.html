It was done around 22 years ago by a student at the RCA. (Adrian Lines)

He did it as a death mask using plaster of paris. (He did his whole body if I remember rightly, yes I know I know this makes for mirthful comment but we best desist because I understand he then got killed in a car crash in South Africa.)

Anyway dealing with the technicalities I understand the face was the difficult part because the plaster took about 10 mins to set enough

and very uncomfortable it was during those 10 mins because it gets quite

warm during the reaction (calcium sulfate hemihydrate changing to the dihydrate Ithink)and you HAVE to stay motionless, eyes shut of course. Somehow you have to contrive to breathe so I think a stout tube in the mouth will do.

Also remember to put petroleum jelly on eyebrows and any other hairy bits. So after you have painfully pulled yourself out of the mold it looks as if you have a bland pseudoscopic deathmask of yourself smoking a cigarette or something , (the breathing tube.) You then need to leave this mask for at least a week before making a hologram of it because it continues to contract many nm and you cannot get a good result making a 10 x 8" master plate with a continuous wave laser until this drying out process is over. By making a single beam Denisyuk with the reference coming in at an angle from below ie hitting the pseudoscopic chin before the forehead you then flip the finished plate over and you have a real image right- reading deathmask. The brightest result comes from spraying the mould with silver metallic paint to avoid polarization effect losses.

You want it reduced size?-- More complications I am afraid. You have to process it be the right color to resonate as the object in another hologram

set-up where you use a large aperture concave mirror or convex lens to make a real image , I am afraid then you lose depth in the finished image because as has already been discussed in the forum recently and.....and......... I expect you have read enough and gone elsewhere by now.

Anyway, regarding making a mask. We recently had to make an 8 x 10 DCG piece for a showroom of a judge. We decided on a hand holding a gavel about to strike a bench. Joy made the gavel with parts from a chair leg. I then had to stick my hand into this ooze she found, hold it for about 30 seconds and then carefully draw it out. A perfect model of my hand! So, I suspect that this material that Joy found will work well for a face. You only have to hold your breath for about 30 seconds. The model was ready the next day, it took about 12 hours to cure well enough to shoot a 1 minute exposure.

Unfortunately I don't have either model or hologram, but I'll try to put up a photio of the model. Joy gave a talk on modelling materials at the last PCCG in which she talked about this stuff. I believe Michael has movies of the talk.
