Dinesh Thu Apr 28, 2011 12:46 pm #12
> 142laser: the "real" useable coherence length is usually limited by vibration.

No. All "coherence length" is limited by vibration, or none of it is, because vibration causes a displacement of the fringes, and so reduces modulaiton.The fringe modulation is dependant on the interbeam angle and given by the Michelson visibility function, which is independent of the coherence length. So, the amount of vibration that destroys a shot depends on whether the maximum of the fringe moves into an area where the the minimum of the fringe was. In other words, whether I_max - I_min is itself maximised. Whether you have a coherence length of millimetres or kilometres, so long as you're within the coherence length, a blur creating a displacement of x will alter the fringe position by kx (k=2pi/lambda). If kx<pi, you're OK, if not, you get weak holograms. As you depart from exactly equal path lengths, the fringe modulation drops until, as the path length difference veers towards the edge of the coherence length, the fringe modulation reduces to zero. The actual modulation fall-off is a sinc-squared curve, however, for all practical purposes, roughly being off by about 1/3rd of the coherence length will give you good fringes.

Taking a concrete example, let's say you have a laser of coherence length 10cm and you're shooting with a HeNe at an interbeam angle of 30 degrees. Then the fringe density is 790 l/mm, or the fringe maxima occur at 1.2 microns. In this case, k=2pi/lambda ~ 10, so to move a "light" part of the fringe into a "dark" part of the fringe (total loss in modulation), you'd have to have a motion of 0.3 microns. This is completely independant of the coherence length. The coherence length depends only on the bandwidth and is given by

L = (lambda)^2/delta(lambda)

and so long as your path difference are less than about 3 cm, the fringe modulation, or the distances between maxima and minima of the fringe structure is independent of the coherence length

One curious fact that is not well known is to remember that the modulation curve of the coherence length is a sinc-squared curve. This means that the modulation falls off to zero at some interpath difference,then rises again, then falls again and then rises again. It never rises to it's full height, in fact the second maximum is only 0.04 of the first maximum. I actually exploited this little known fact in a project many moons ago.
