1. Can I make Denisyuk type holograms on the VRP-M material with an Argon laser?

Ok, Just some simple things to look for.

Try a single beam hologram.

The plate will be clear when you take it out of the box in the safelight. (Use plates in the beginning as it is much easier.)

Expose the plate.

Drop the plate in the developer. The plate should take about 1 to 2 minutes to turn black. If it turns black too fast shorten the exposure time. If it turns black too quickly lengthen the exposure time.

Rinse the hologram in distilled water.

Drop the plate in the bleach. The plate will turn clear. Add 10 seconds of bleaching time after it is clear.

Rinse the plate.

Dry the plate. (this step takes a lot of practice to make a great hologram but you can just use a hair dryer on cool in the beginning.)

No you should be able to see the image. Use a hologen or the sun. There is only two angles you will see the image from so you may have to play with the hologram for some time to figure out which direction the reference beam was coming from.

If you see an image, then spray paint the back of the hologram black.

Now if you don't get an image at all, it is likely because there was motion of the plate in relation to the object. Try to make sure the object touches the plate so if there is movement at least they move together.

Don't give up. It took me 7 exposures to get a complete image. Some people get one on the first try but not everyone.

You can view the hologram before bleaching. Bleaching simply makes it brighter. To view the hologram: develop, rinse and then dry. It will be about 1/3 as bright as after bleaching and it will not survive very long so you can't keep it this way for very long. (a few days is all.)

JohnFP Wed Jan 04, 2006 3:00 pm #17
I have to add that is sound like you are doing your test strips incorrectly.

The way you explained it, is that your cover you hologram with strips and remove one and expose for 10 seconds, remove another one and expose for 10 seconds etc.... This would be NOT GOOD.

This would indicate the first part of the plate is exposed 5 times for 10 seconds each. Which is 5 seperate holograms.

I suggest if this is tried again - to make the first 10 second exposure with all but one strip covered. Then whey you take the second strip away put it on top of where the first strip would be such that only the second strip is showing. Then expose for 20 seconds. The take the third strip away and place it where the second strip was thus opening only the third strip area and expose for 30 seconds ...Repeat for each strip.

Basically, you want each strip to be exposed ONCE for the exposure time desired.

Finally, if your best test thus far was 10 to 20 seconds.

Make your exposures 5, 10, 15, 20 and 25 seconds....

Paulos Fri Jan 06, 2006 2:57 pm #20
Samuel,

It seems that the reason of your problem is rather "physical" than "chemical".

I propose to first try a denisyuk of a plane object touching the emulsion side.

Denisjuk configuration is very stable.

It is very rare, not to see any image, even if you have over-, or underexposed.

Although VPR is a very bad material for reflection holograms,

some of your experiments should give a fade image,

regardless of the type of developer/bleach you use.

>>I made an interferometer to to check the coherence of the laser i had some lines<<

>>of interferences and i found about more than 5cm coherence so i think my laser is ok<<

1. What was the distance between plate and your object?

If the coherence length is 5 cm, objects farer than about 1.5 cm

from the emulsion side of the plate, will not be imaged..

2. What type of laser you have?

The worst case is that the laser has not enough coherence length, or the laser mode is not stable (mode drifting).

Are you sure that the interference lines are from the two different paths of your laser,

and not caused by e.g. some internal reflections.
