Not sure exactly how new they are, but they look very interesting, and I've ordered some from Hong Kong via eBay.

The good thing about these is that they're just regular 8mm or 10mm LEDs like we're all familiar with, and they don't need any special heatsinking. This is unlike the more powerful types of LED which are a pain to deal with, because they need both high current and adequate heatsinking.

The specs are as follows:

1 Watt (360,000mcd) white 10mm LED, viewing angle 45 degrees, 120mA at 3.4 to 3.8V, price $27.99 for 20 pieces

and

0.5 Watt (100,000mcd) white 8mm LED, viewing angle 140 degrees, 100mA at same voltage, price $19.99 for 30 pieces

I put the viewing angle for the last one in bold, becasue it implies that the LED could be installed very close to the hologram. The eBay seller is Light of Victory (eBay name: lvehk). Shipping is free.

Wow, Excellent find on a light source and a fantastic hologram. I am very subjective to white broadband holograms. It that a SBR or copy?

The reason the background is dark in your photos is the way the light meter works on digital cameras. It usually adjusts the aperature and exposure time based on what is close to the center of the photo. If you hold your shutter button down half way while focusing on the table just left of the hologram and let the camera lock into the darker table, then move to the right and center the hologram in the photo (all the while still holding the button half way down) then shoot. You should get settings more normal to what the rest of the room's lighting is. The problem with this is, since your hologram is so bright, it will proably get bleached out and be too bright.

Joe Farina Tue Oct 13, 2009 10:24 am #15
Thanks a lot for posting that, Tony. One interesting thing is that the blue spike is rather small; I thought it would be larger. Maybe they are using more efficient phosphors to convert blue light into white light. It seems that they are still using that basic principle, due to the presence of the blue spike. Also interesting is the steepness of the slope between 500 and 600, this would correspond to the maximum sensitivity of the eye/brain.

I ran some tests also with the 8mm LED (with the 140 degree viewing angle) to see how long it would last with four AAA batteries. Since it's only drawing 100mA, it lasted a long time. The interesting thing that I disovered is that these LEDs, if held very close to the hologram (which is possible due to the wide viewing angle), can still be quite sufficient even if they lose ~80% of their power. Of course, this only holds true if the hologram itself is bright (I used the hologram shown above). So even after 16 hours continuous use, the LED was still bright enough to light the hologram effectively. The batteries I used were not special, just ordinary Duracell alkaline. At the 17 hour point, the batteries seemed to have lost power rapidly, and the light was no longer bright enough.

But 16 hours seems like a very long time for continuous use. I'm working on a timer/sensor circuit to be inserted into a base (with the hologram placed on top of the base, with the LED -- and reference beam for the hologram -- coming from the bottom left). If the sensor can sucessfully detect a human (either through touch or by infrared detection, i.e., a PIR circuit) then I can connect that to a 555 or 7555 timer and the light can go on/off only when needed, say for 1 minute or so. So, if the batteries last 16 hours, that would be 960 cycles before the batteries need to be replaced.
