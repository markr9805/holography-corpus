Steve M Fri Aug 18, 2006 9:31 am #1
I ran across this laser on Ebay several months ago and decided that if I could win it at a reasonably good price, I could test it for making holograms. You can view the results of a transmission hologram at [http://www.3dimagery.com/GBS100/GBS-100.htm](http://www.3dimagery.com/GBS100/GBS-100.htm) (10MB file - I've corrected this link!). The results were awesome. Over 12 feet of coherence and bright enough to do a 2x3 foot architectural model. You can usually win with a bid of $300 or less. Output power is around 100 to 110 mW. Polarized >100:1. Nice round gaussian beam too.

The laser is a little quirkie though. You shouldn't operate it for longer than 2 hours so I use the first 2 hours to setup, turn it off for half hour, then use the next 2 hours to make the exposure. The company says the crystal can get too hot after 2 hours and possibly destroy it. No TEC. But I have run it for 4 hours with no problems. Also, you may only get half power any particular time you turn it on. If you turn it off, then 30 seconds later turn it on, you'll get full power. Some times you have to do this 3 or 4 times. If also noticed interference fringes roaming around very bright white areas in the hologram which I believe is due to secondary interference between the glass plates I use to sandwich the film. It also occurs with non-antihalation plates. I don't have any anti-halation plates or film to test this yet. The fringes really don't distract much from the effect though. I think the higher power makes these types of fringes more visible where as with a 30mW He-Ne, they may still be there, but aren't as noticeable.

I've also successfully made a multi-beam reflection display hologram with the laser. Extreme Lasers out of Seabrook, TX bids out the lasers. Even with the quirks, I recommend the laser if you want to get into the green spectrum for a reasonable price.

The reason of the problem could be

1- a mode hop during the exposure,

or (and I am afraid this is your case)

2- a second relatively weak mode.

In this case your laser is not strictly single mode.

The lines are expected to be more pronounced in the bright flat areas.

In order to avoid a mode hop, I would suggest to try a very short exposure.

If the problem persists, the reason is #2.

Hans Mon Aug 21, 2006 3:22 pm #22
It's difficult to detect a second line with a DVD. But not difficult to detect mode hops. Mode hops can also be detected with a cheap silicon photo diode, because the power drops during the actual hops. When hooked up to an oscilloscope one can measure the frequency of the hops. If you hook up the photo diode to an amplifier, you can hear clicks if the frequency is within audio range.
wler Mon Aug 21, 2006 3:32 pm #23
For laser diodes, a DVD or almost any other grating is fine, because the mode spacing is so large ie the cavity is so short. For DPSS pointers, it may be possible with a DVD too, but for the GBM-100 I don't yet know because I don't know how large the cavity is. Most likely one would need a setup with a better resolution, involving narrow slits, a large path length, and a parabolic mirror; probably something useable can be relatively easily cooked up with parts a holographer has anyway.

wler Fri Aug 25, 2006 11:52 am #41
Right - if there are two modes and they are like 100Ghz or so apart, then this corresponds to a distance of 3mm. This means that the two modes are in phase or out of phase every 3mm (or mabe 6.. I am never sure whether to double or halve something, but never mind . Dark should stay dark, and white stay white where the two modes interfere constructively, but white becomes dark where the mode interfere destructively - all this repeats every 3mm or so.

And if the modes don't have the same strength, which is usually the case, white becomes dim and not completely dark where the mode interfere destructively, and this seems to be the case here.
