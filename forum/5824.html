Hi,

My name is Tom Ralston, a final year degree student at the University of Derby in England. I am currently undertaking my initial research into my dissertation for my BSc Hons Degree. The course that I am on is called 'sound, light and live event technology' and so my dissertation is to be related within the field of entertainment lighting.

For this paper, I am planning to write about holography and in particular hologram projection. My aim is to look into the possibility of having a holographic plate at the focal point of a projector (well more precisely a moving head light fixture...however the optical principles are the same) and to project an image through a field of smoke out from a stage.

The holograms I wish to project are not things such as detailed images, but more like sharp images of star shapes and spheres. My theory is that it could be possible to have the 'star' image be projected out from a stage so that the audience can see it from where ever they are sat.

My aim is to produce information to show that it is possible to use smoke as a projection medium where the ‘star’ can be seen to be a 3D image within the smoke. When images are projected from a moving light, such as a circle, then a 'tube' is seen in the smoke, where the circle is seen on the back wall of the theatre/hall. I wish to explore the possibilities in the theory behind having a 3d object be seen actually at different points of the projection. It is for the development of '3d without glasses'.

Since it is not possible to actually stop light at different points in air, assuming the atmospheric conditions are the same throughout the room, I wish to look at projecting onto a fine screen of smoke at specific points in a room, thus creating a separate screen and 'change in atmosphere' in comparison to the rest of the room. I was still wondering, is it possible to actually project a hologram?... by using a plate with a light source behind it, much like a general LCD projector with a DL source.

If you have any information that could help me either on the hologram projection or anything you feels would be relevant to my understanding into holography please let me know. This is a project and area of science which I have a great interest in and so any help would be greatly welcome.

Proposals for my research dissertation are to be presented in the next few weeks; however the paper is to be published to the university in May 2007.

Thank you

Tom Ralston

University of Derby
Colin Kaminski Thu Oct 05, 2006 3:25 pm #2
Hi Tom,

A transmision hologram will make a real image if illuminated buy the complex conjugate of the reference beam. There will be color smear if you use a white ligt source. This could be used as an artistic advantage. In order to get a feel for what you can and can't do I wold recoend aquiring a small trasmision hologram of some point sources and playing with it.

Usually you can only see a holgram by looking into the plate, however making a smoke screen will work. The power will decrease at the square of the distance from the image however. I often project real images from holograms on to ground glass to find focal points for parts of the image.

This will give you some interesting background:

[http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... ons_Part_I](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Holography_Transmission_Equations_Part_I)
