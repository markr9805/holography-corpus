Hi,

we are doing a project on holographic datastorage for a competition at the moment. Having studied the theory for about 2 months now we want to start and take our first hologram. For that we obviously need a set-up which is free of oscillation in the order of a tenth of the wavelength. Our plan is to use car tyres and a marble plate of some old table.

An expert on holography (who we actually get our laser safety goggles from ) told us that it might be necessary/advantageous to secure our components (we have got two optical rails on which everything will be mounted) in some way to the basis.

Now the question: is it really necessary to glue/screw/etc. our components to the plate?

Do any of you have any tips about holography for us?

Cheers,

Dom
Ed Wesly Tue Jun 12, 2007 9:09 pm #2
Movement is the Number Killer of holograms! Glue, screw, clamp or whatever to make sure no component can move!

Don't fill your inner tubes up so that they are hard; you want them to be floating but still soft.
BobH Tue Jun 12, 2007 9:52 pm #3
Make each of your rails have three point contact with the marble base so they cannot rock on it. Use glue to hold them in place, but not for support. Overclamping can produce stress in the mounts, which often results in hyper-sensitivity to airbourne vibrations or slowly drifting fringes.

Tom B. Thu Jun 21, 2007 2:55 am #7
It would be worthwhile to set up a simple Michaelson interferometer on your bench before you start going overboard on vibration isolation - or underboard, as the case may be This will tell you how bad your vibration situation is, and will help you to evaluate the effectiveness of various isolation methods you may try.

In my case (a concrete basement floor in a quiet neighborhood) no isolation seems to be needed except on washdays or during other minor earthquakes.

Another factor to consider, especially for longer exposures, is temperature stability. The periodic temperature variations and air movement caused by heating/air conditioning can be problematic both for laser stability and for optical bench setups. Ideally you want the temperature to be stable and the air to be still.

Oh - and magnets and bits of scrap steel are good for cheaply mounting components if a steel table is used. You can also epoxy small magnets or steel washers to non-metallic components as needed.
