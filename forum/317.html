cnewswanger Mon Sep 19, 2011 8:27 am #17
I've used these tubing clamps successfully many times.

I've built some large structures as well.

Here's a link to McMaster Carr:

[http://www.mcmaster.com/#catalog/117/1914/=e4p5z4](http://www.mcmaster.com/#catalog/117/1914/=e4p5z4)

Note the actual pipe diameter for "1 1/2" pipe is actually 1 7/8".

The clamps are expensive but you will likely reuse the clamps for many set-ups.

If you use "adjustable angle tee's" make sure you can tighten the swivel joint with a bolt. Some have a pin that allows some wiggle.

Wiggle = bad.

When i've had moving parts on the table I often filled the tubes with sand to help dampen out vibrations.

I made wood plugs from dowel which I epoxied into place.

Cheers,

Craig

John Sonley Tue Oct 04, 2011 6:47 am #22
Hi - here's my version of my overhead reference beam made from KeeKlamp fittings. The set-up as shown is for H1 master transmission holograms. Also in view is the large plano-convex collimating mirror I use in making 5" X 8" masters.

The overhead mirror is set so that the reference beam strikes the film plate at 56 deg plate normal, this angle together with a half wave plate in the beam gets rid of woodgraining in the glass/film/glass dry filmholder 'sandwiches' I use to support my film for exposure.

I've now made some very bright and successful H1s, so this construction passes the stability test

I'll be re-jigging the whole set-up quite soon to turn my H1s into Image Plane H2s
