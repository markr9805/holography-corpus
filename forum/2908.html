Tom B. Sat Nov 20, 2004 7:08 pm #4
Most currently available green LEDs have some red in the spectrum and will require some additional filtering. As John pointed out, it is easy to check with a diffraction grating. I use 2-3 layers of green theatrical gel (colored filter sheets used for spotlighting etc.) with my OptiLed safelight, a "festival" green lamp (screws into regular light socket). [http://www.optiled.biz/products/products.html](http://www.optiled.biz/products/products.html)

Newer Indium Gallium Nitride LEDS, such as the Osram "true" green and other manufacturer's "pure" green seem to have little or no red and will not require filtering.

I must say I'm surprised at this. I've never actually looked at a green LED with a grating so I can't dispute it. However, I've always assumed that a LED has a bandwidth of about 50nm. So if "green" is anything from 500 to 550, the bandwidth would be anything from 475 , clearly blue, to 575, yellow. To have red, assuming this to be about 600nm, the "green" LED would have to be about 575. I would have thought this was a distinct yellow.

I do have green LEDs at wrk and I'm curious enough that first thing Monday I'm going to look at them with a grating. I am assuming that the wavelength profile is Gaussian. I suppose it's possible that if the wavelength-energy curve is not symmetrical there'll be a peak with a lot of green with a long tail into the red and a short tail into the blue.
Tom B. Sun Nov 21, 2004 9:01 am #6
> Dinesh: I suppose it's possible that if the wavelength-energy curve is not symmetrical there'll be a peak with a lot of green with a long tail into the red and a short tail into the blue.

Yes, that's what I usually see, though very little extension to the blue end and often a bit of a bump in the red. Just checked the green LEDs on my cable modem and router with an audio CD (the 1.6 micron track pitch makes an adequate and very cheap diffraction grating) and it was very easy to see the red. Hold the CD shiny side perpendicular to the LED and experiment with viewing angles and CD tilt to see the spectrum.
Martin Sun Nov 21, 2004 10:04 am #7
Having no access to Optileds, I can refer but to Lumileds. Some simple Rosco absorption filter put on the LED makes a fairly monochromatic light. Thus a – filtered - red Lumiled centered at 610nm produces a reasonably sharp image of a transmission hologram.

I have just installed a filtered 505nm Lumiled which makes a very bright safelight for 8E75 and PFG-01 materials...
