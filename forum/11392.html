I've thought of this, but never actually done it. Bear in mind, you have to double the amount of dichromate.

The problems I see are:

1. Interface scattering. If warm gel is poured onto cold gel there will be some melting and warming, at the interface, of the cold gel (lower level), with perhaps expansion, while there will be cooling, with perhaps contraction, of the warm gel, at the interface (upper level). In other words, there will be differential warming/cooling and density in the three layers: upper, interface, lower. All these factors can be calculated, but, at any rate, there will be density differences between the interface and the upper and lower levels. There may even be air bubbles due to the various contractions and expansions. This will cause scattering of light at the interface, and noise in the hologram.

2. Light penetration. If the densities of the upper, lower and interface are different, then there will be variations in index in the three levels. The index difference may not be severe, but may cause refraction and reflections at the interfaces: air/upper layer/interface/lower layer/air. This will affect the Bragg fringes. It will also affect the efficiency because as the light passes through these various levels, it's being absorbed, with the amount of absorption depending on index. This will affect the ratio at back.

3. Dichromate absorption: As the dichromate penetrates the gel layers, there will be differential absorption and so different gel/dichromate concentrations. This may cause colour shifts, since the colour of an image depends on the dichromate concentrations.

There was a paper by Kubota where he did try multi-level gel layers, but I can't lay my hands on it. I have Hans Bjelkhagen's anthologies of important techniques in holography, and I'll see if I can find it there.

However, one of my mantras is: There's only one way to find out.

PS I mentioned earlier that gelatin will cross-link all of its own, without dichromate, albeit weakly. Therefore, it should be possible to coat only gelatin - no dichromate - and get Bragg planes, ie make a hologram. We did this, twenty unsensitised plates, intending to send them to the Olson's to ask whether they'd zap them with their pulse. Unfortunately, we never got around to sending them to the Olson's.
