This is especially true for those just starting out and using the cardboard shutter method but also applicable to those doing more complex setups.

You may only be doing single-beam holograms but there's still a very good chance you're transmitting vibrations to your setup just by standing next to it.

Rig a remote shutter or buy one so that you can trigger the exposure from across (or outside) the room. This can be something as simple as a [analog voltage panel meter](http://www.mainelectronics.com/panelmeter.htm) hooked up to a switch and battery. Just drill a hole through the meter behind the needle (when in resting position), glue a small piece of paper to the needle, hook some long wires to the meter and paint the meter flat black. Hook the wires to a battery (voltage depends on the meter) a switch and you've got a shutter you can trigger away from the table.

I still use this method both my table.

I was playing around with this this afternoon. I used a little multimeter I bought a long time ago. As soon as I taped a little paper or foil to the needle, it simply flopped all over the place from it's own weight. But, if you lay the meter on it's back and have the foil/paper bent in an L-shape, it seemed fine.

Two ideas: put the meter in Ohms/resistance mode and plug the leads into a long extension cord (WHICH ISN'T PLUGGED INTO THE WALL, OF COURSE!) and use a screw driver or something to short the other end. Or, put it in a high voltage AC range to measure regular wall voltage, and use a home-automation module (like X10) to remote-control it. Unfortunately, the X10 lamp module doesn't work because it always is sending out high voltage. The appliance module works because it uses a relay which 'clicks', but obviously is causing some movement so it needs to be located well away from the table.
