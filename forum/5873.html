JohnFP Tue Nov 07, 2006 3:31 am #2
I am by no means an expert on Silver but I had a similar problem years ago. This is not a cure but a "work around". Try to soak your already processed green or blue hologram in a solution of sugar and water. Maybe 3 to 5% sugar to water by weight. The idea is to leave sugar in the emulsion and thus keeping the fringes expaned slightly to shift toward the red. If you get the right concentration of sugar they hologram may shift to a lime green or yellow and look brighter. Somehow your fringes are shrinking.

Just something I tried, it worked, and I thought I would throw out there. But others on the forum, I am sure, can tell you what works best for what you are doing to get the brightest holos.
Tom B. Tue Nov 07, 2006 8:47 am #3
You don't say what film/plates you are using - PFG-01? Also if you have the JD-2 bleach, have you tried the JD-2 developer with it? Does the Dokumol work better?

A green image means a lot of shrinkage has occurred - the bleach only removes developed silver so I think it's self-limiting. Overexposure could cause this, or if the film had been fogged by accidental exposure to bright light. A bad safelight (one with some red in it) could also be a cause. When you develop, how dark does the film get? For Denisyuks you want a density of about 2.0 (1% light transmission).

My favorite test object for Denisyuks is white (fake) pearls of various sizes against a dark background close to the plate. Even when the process is working poorly, the bright highlight reflections are visible from almost any angle so at least you get SOMETHING.

Uli Tue Nov 21, 2006 2:28 pm #8
I have very rarely used a reversal (or solvent) bleach for reflection holograms. The first effect you get is the shrinkage you get due to the solvent effect on the metallic silver, therefore affecting the thickness of the emulsion layer, which is related to the diffraction efficiency (brightness) of the image. If you want to get this effect on purpose is better to pre-swell the plate using a TEA solution bath, which will also help with the sensitivity. The second effect you get with the reversal bleach is a diminished amount of silver in the hologram, and silver is what you need for achieving a good index modulation, since at the end the hologram is stored as a refractive index pattern with lower value for the zones with gelatine and the higher value in the "silvered" zones. You need an emulsion with a high silver content for achieving a good image using reversal.

That is why I think that is far better to use a good combination of developer and rehalogenating bleach. In our webpage there are some suggestions for Denisyuk holograms, like using Pyrogallol and EDTA bleach. This is in fact the one that gives the best results with our BB plates, but I think it will work with others as well.

Hello Holographers,

just wanted to let you know that I succeeded in recording a bright (compared to me previous ones) Denisyuk on PFG-01, with Dokumol processing and Potassium-Dichromate Bleach! It was only because I followed Jeff Blyths advice about the Reversal Bleach: [http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... sal_Bleach](http://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki/index.php/Reversal_Bleach)

Thank you a lot!

So, I was really enthusiastic about the stability of my "table"! It's just a metal frame, lying on a grand piano, protected by a thick blanket, and I clamped the plate with a simple "clamp" ("Schraubzwinge" in German). Another hologram i recorded had a depth of about 6cm, and it was still sharp, so I was quite satisfied and will do further testing of the coherence length.

Anyway, as Dokumol is not the best developer, I should be able to achieve even better results with a "proper" Holographic developer (TJ1, or Metol+...).

So, what was the problem?

Exposure time and stability was not the critical point, but obviously the development procedure, which I solved by just sticking exactly to the details by Jeff. The important thing was to use sufficently acid acetic solution (i used 1%) and distilled water for rinsing, because:

Jeff wrote: "Before the dichromate bath is used you have to have two pre-baths of de-ionized water (DI) to remove all traces of dissolved halide ions. If you don’t do this then some of the developed up silver fails to be removed from the light-struck fringes and deposits itself back in the fringe as silver halide. This causes scatter in the finished hologram and reduces diffraction efficiency because the light struck fringes have failed to be properly cleared of AgBr . (...)".

So, probably my development went on for too long (guess >3min), because the acid was too weak, and furthermore I didnt use distilled water at all.

And here's the result: The format is a bit unusual, because I clamped the plate at the corner, not at an edge, so I turned the image 45°.

Exposure t=2sec

HeNe-Laser, P~6mW

Development: Dokumol (Tetenal) diluted 1:5 with dist. water, for 1min

Stop 1% acetic acid for 1min

Bleaching: Integraf-Recipe: (5g Potassium Dichromate + 80gSodium

Bisulfate for 1L aqua dest.) until clear (~30s)

Objects (for testing): Chess-piece (King), a transparent marble - only visible by the circle-shaped shadow in front, and a white plug-adapter, all in front of a small concave mirror - giving the strong rainbow reflections left and right of the King

Additional illumination: a small mirror from left above, some artefacts in this corner visible

![Image](http://kingservants.de/x/holo_2511_1.jpg)
