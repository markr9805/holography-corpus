Solarenemy Tue Feb 03, 2009 3:59 am #4
IPA is not a fixing step. It is a drying step. All water must be removed from the DCG emulsion and that is the purpose for the various alcohol baths at various concentrations. You still have to use an actual fixer before the alcohol or a process that works like a fixer before the alcohol. Regardless from what i have read and seen in most processes listed here the alcohol baths are a necessary step if you are going to play with DCG. There may be other processes but I am sure they are going to complicate things much more and again the alcohol is not a fixer but a dehydrator. You still have to use a valid fixer before the alcohol baths.

Here is the chemical compostion of the Kodak Rapid Fixer with Hardener.

[http://www.holograms3d.com/Safety/Kodak ... er.jsp.rtf](http://www.holograms3d.com/Safety/KodakRapidFixer.jsp.rtf)

But the important thing here is after I peformed a few experiments, Jeff Blyth found things interesting with the formulation of the Fixer and since then has indicated that only Sodium Metabisulfite and Aluminum Sulfate (Alum) are all that is really needed for fixing DCG. I have played with some formulations of SM and Alum but quite a few other variables come into play. I recently suggested that one might try 5 grams of SM and 1 gram of Alum to 500ml of water to start. Take the exposed plate and put it in the solution agitating slightly. Note the time it takes for all the blue precipitation to be removed (The SM turns the AmDi blue as it exits the emulsion). The emulsion will then be clear and may have a slight blue tint. If this is longer then 1 minutes increase the concentration of the SM. If it is shorter then decrease the concentration of SM. You want all the blue out in about a minute and no orange left in the emulsion. Once you have that then work on the hardener (alum). Here is where bloom, exposure time, age of emulsion etc. all play a role on hardeness. The goal is to get a hologram that is JUST not milky white. If the hologram comes out milky white then increase the concentration of alum. If the hologam is clear then decrease the Alum until it is every so slightly milky white, almost non noticable. You actually want to be as close to the emulsion being milky without any milky noticable. So once you have all these concentrations and your hologram is just slightly milky incease the concentration of alum till it is just clear.

Then you should be able to use this formula as a standard and if you need to vary something, you can vary an easier parameter like exposure time or age of film.
