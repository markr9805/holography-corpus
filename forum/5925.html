Colin Kaminski Tue Jan 02, 2007 1:40 am #17
> dave battin: A lens and a mirror will also do the trick...

Yes, that is a newtonian telescope and works as well, remember in demanding applications that it will introduce coma when used off-axis with a parabolic mirror. Also, remember that spherical aberations are a problem with radius mirrors but they will work better off axis. Note: This is not really a problem for display holography more a thought to keep in ones mind when designing setups.

I use a parabolic mirror and try to minimize the off axis component as much as possible. Now that I am designiing an overhead reference I have been paying close attention to how close to on axis i can get since once I set the mirror it will remain in the same place.

I have taken some 1 1/4" plumbing pipe and filed it with sand. Then ends are capped with epoxy or silicone. (Thank you Jonathan for the sand idea and Micheal for the silicone idea.) This is assembled with traditional plumbing fixtures except where I need pieces that slide. They are composed of cheesbouroughs from theatrical rigging.

I'll post some information to the wiki when I am finished and include a photo.
