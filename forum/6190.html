I am seeing silver halide reflection H2’s color-tuned using Sorbitol green-shifting (drying out) when exposed to the dry desert environment of Las Vegas. As much of my work is now targeted for this locale, I’m looking for advice as to the best way to minimize this effect. My current technique is (following Sorbitol swelling to the desired color): to paint the emulsion; let the paint dry for 24 hours; and seal the emulsion with a second piece of glass using a continuous bead of Silicone. In particular I’m wondering if there is a technique for either red-shifting or plate-sealing that would be less susceptible to drying out than what I‘m currently doing? Obviously I can start out redder than I need to be, but then I run the risk of having images going off the visible chart when moved.

Ron you need of course to minimize ANY color shifting with respect to

your display location and the suggestions of our forum colleagues so far

would deal with the immediate problem OK possibly but then would cause red shifting when humidity happened to rise or if the sealing up suggestions start to fail.

The answer I think is to thoroughly post harden the gelatin for ever. So put a test hologram in a solution of ~5% ammonium dichromate,

squeegee off excess drips, blow dry it ,and leave it out in sunshine all day.

If you leave it like this without rinsing it will be extremely resistant to any

future print out but it might be prudent to rinse out any soluble salts.

This post hardening procedure will cause some final but permanent contraction so you would just need to do tests to compensate by adjusting the swelling state for your initial laser exposure of course.

My tests on this method allowed the pictures to be viewed in or out of water. So your customers could then view the holograms underwater in their swimming pools !

Putting it metaphorically, I thought that Ron needs to stop his holograms from breathing !

One way to stop something breathing is to strap it up tightly.

The easiest way to do this is to strap it up with chromium oxygen bonds.

Inevitably strapping up leads to contraction. A bonus is that using dichromate can lead to a more hydrophobic gelatin.

To get a longer replay wavelength than the 532nm pulse I suppose this is a bit awkward. Shooting the plates in a very dry state helps. The processing chemistry can also help but I appreciate that this must not

I tried the 5% dichromate + sunlight treatment today on a few-month-old BB-640 deep red reflection holo intended originally as a reflection master at ~650 nm. One unknown was whether or not it had had the Philips 1% acetic acid anti-printout treatment. I tend to think so as the gelatin seemed to have the same water repellant properties I'd noted before from this treatment and it took several minutes of swishing the dichromate solution around on the plate for it to be absorbed, creating the familiar orange-brown stain.

Exposed to sunlight the stain soon changed to a more greyish brown. I was able to give it only 3 hours because some clouds rolled in. Checking the reflection holo, I did not see any significant shrinkage - it was still deep red. I tried soaking in water for several minutes to see it the stain would go away (it didn't) and found that it must have swelled somewhat as the hologram became invisible. I then blasted it with a hair dryer for a while and it became visible again at perhaps 630 nm while hot. The interesting bit was that near the edges where the dicromate had not soaked in, the color was greenish. After cooling, the hologram was maybe slightly oranger than the original.

So I conclude that the treatment greatly reduces sensitivity to humidity but I still had some residual sensitivity for various possible reasons. Also

the lack of shrinkage from the treament was surprising - I did not see much if any shrinkage.

Yes I am glad to report a successful experiment last night that left sunlight completely out of it. (Perhaps this should go on the Holowiki section Colin.)

I took 2 bright bleached Denisyuk holograms, one on a BB640 plate and the other on a Slavich PFG-03M

The plates were first dipped vertically into a solution of 7% ammonium dichromate in DI water so that they were only partially covered .

After about 2 minutes they were wiped and the dipping solution had 1 % glycerol added to the 7% ammonium dichromate so that other sections of the plates were then treated with this solution.

Finally a fresh 7% ammonium dichromate solution was prepared with 2% conc. sulphuric acid.

So I finished up with 4 striped sections on a plate.

1) Untreated

2) 7% Ammonium dichromate in DI

3) 7% Ammonium dichromate in DI + 1% glycerol

4) Ammonium dichromate in DI + 2% conc sulfuric acid.

Plates were blown in cool air till touch dry and then placed in a preheated oven at 110 C for 12 hours.

Then plates were both cooled and washed in cold running tap water for 2 minutes and then dried.

Results:

Dry plates

Stripes 1,2, and 3 showed bright images with no indication of loss of brightness except perhaps stripe (3) may have lost a little.

Stripe (4) was completely ruined and out of the running.

Plates soaked for 10 minutes in tap water at ~20C.

Stripes 2 and 3 showed good clear images still.

The BB640 plate had changed color from yellow green to light orange.

The PFG-03M had changed hardly at all!... from light yellow to golden yellow.

(In view of the notorious softness of this brand it was a surprise to find it had hardened up even better than the BB640 )

So this hardening method does effectively strap up the gelatin film so tightly that it cannot “breathe” and change its color with ambient humidity.

Note: This was done on bleached finished holograms. It cannot be used on unbleached reflection ones, the colloidal silver would have to be converted to AgBr first.

My thoughts have been (and mind you I am not an expert and have only tried to think of the mechanics of Gelatin) that if the emulsion has any gelatin that is not crosslinked in unexposed regions then it should be possible to swell the gelatin slightly and add additional crosslinking in the unexposed regions which should allow the dark fringes to be slightly larger/thicker thus allowing a red shift. After completely processing a DCG hologram I would imagine most of the gelatin in the exposed area is already crosslinked and not much of the gelatin in the unexposed area is crosslinked. Thus I assume that in water (or a dichromate solution) the dark fringes would accept water and swell and the light struck areas may not. This would separate the light fringes by a greater distance due to the dark fringes swelling even though the light fringes themself do not thicken. Crosslinking more in the swollen state would, I assume again, allow a greater separation between light fringes thus allowing a red shift.

Most of the research I have done indicates that the gelatin strands are in an entangled glob of random coils. Being an Italian I envision it to be like cooked spaghetti that has drained and set in a pot with no sauce for a couple of hours. You can push on it and it will spring back until you push too far, then you break the spaghetti strands and it will not spring back. If you allow this spaghetti to stand overnight some parts will acually fuse and get hard (crosslinking ananogy). If you then put it in cool water the rest of the strands seem to be free to move and the mass expands except where the dried out, crusted together strands are stuck together. If in this this swollen state there was a way to stick together (crosslink) the free strands then when the water was taken out there would be support structures allowing the mass to be larger then prior to crosslinking in a more swollen state.

This surely could apply to Silver Halide but it may be that both the light and dark fringes are swelling slightly and then getting crosslinked to allow the red shift. A nice experiment would be to soak the silver halide finished hologram in the concentration of Dichromate you indicated and then while it is in the solution, expose it to light the Dichromate is sensitive to. Then bake and see if you get more of a red shift. It may be that the gelatin is too swollen and the Dichromate solution has to contain a certain concentration of IPA to get just the right amount of swelling to get addequate crosslinking at jus the right swellness or the "spring" tension is exceeded. Might it be possible to tune this way?
