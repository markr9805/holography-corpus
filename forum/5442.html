Yes I have used it but not on Slavich stuff.

I found it needed to be in high concentration to have any effect (~40%!)

The crosslinks are rather weaker I think.

It does'nt have any nasty vapour though. Whereas

Gluteraldeyde (glutaric dialdehyde) is a VERY effective as a hardener even in low concentrations and its solutions are

potent biocides. It does have a harmful vapour but not as much as formaldehyde. However put all that on one side because...........

When it comes to Slavich material I found that a developer containing "metol"

has a double bonus in that it causes the plate to develop up so quickly

provided you have normal exposure levels, that it is in and out of the developer and into a stop or acid bleach within 40 seconds and also it has some hardening action as can be proved by doing a scratch test on 2 bits of plate one wet with dev containing metol and the other one wet with same dev but without metol. So you can avoid messing about with hardeners because the material is developed and stopped before the soft gelatin is damaged.

Such a dev is given below.

Jeff

A good Developer is :

Solution A

40 g ascorbic acid

6 g “Metol” 4-methylaminophenol

Made up to 1 litre in distilled or deionized water

Solution B

100 g anhydrous sodium carbonate ( Na2CO)3

30 g Sodium hydroxide (NaOH).

Made up to 1 litre in distilled or deionized water

Mix equal volumes of A and B for the developer.

The mixture should be used in a plastic dish with an identical dish floating on the top so that there is a minimal air gap. If you use it like this the same developer can be used again and again for many days (depending on how much usage of course) otherwise this developer left in an open dish is more vulnerable to oxidation from the air than standard developers because it deliberately does not contain sulfite ions. So the time the developer spends exposed to the air is low when you use a floating dish and it is only while immersing your plates or taking them out that it is fully open to the air. Unmixed solutions A and B keep indefinitely. A yellowing or browning of solution A is OK . ( Badly oxidized solution A is BLACK).

Jeff
