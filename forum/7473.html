Wow, I have been wanting to play with this for quite some time. You may need to be creative but here are a few things I wanted to play with.

1. Take a plate ready for exposure and place it in the vacuum but have half the plate covered with a black card. Expose as normal. Then uncover the unexposed half and cover the exposed half. Turn on the vacuum (i have no idea how much is enough or how much is too much) and bring it to a stable vacuum percentage. Then expose that half. Process normally. Note any differences, the hope is the vacuum causes a slight swelling or shinking of the emulsion to change color.

2. Take an already exposed but unprocessed hologram and harden it by light or heat. Then place it in the vaccum and increase the vacuum slightly while monitoring to see if an image appears at any point. Or try to liquid fix the hologram just after exposing, squeegy and then try the vacuum as just described skipping the water and alcohol baths.

3. Take a completely processed hologram but not sealed in glass and place it in the vacuum. Gradually increase the vacuum and note if any color changes become apparent. If so, then release the vacuum and see if the hologram returns completely to its original color or retains some of the color shift.

4. Take a plate and expose it and process it for a base line. Then take another plate and expose it. Then put it in vaccum chamber and bring vacuum up slowly. If you found a vacuum percentage that worked for number 3 above then stop at that vacuum and expose the hologram to light (no fringe creation) to try to freeze it in a swollen or shrunken state. Then process as normal.

salvee Sat Aug 30, 2008 3:39 pm #18
1. expose hologram

2. fix hologram

3. develop in water bath (see below)

4. remove from water bath, pulling up VERY slowly and evenly so that the meniscus pulls any water droplets with it

5. place plate in a vacuum and vacuum dry as fast as possible

The above methodology is used by some commercial HOE makers to test the hologram to see if the water bath temperature is too low.

6. adjust water bath temperature if HOE does not meet efficiency and color requirements.

7. Develop normally- i.e. use above mentioned water bath followed by IPA baths

8. place in 80oC oven to fix the fringes.

9. protect hologram by cementing a cover piece

Note that in order to fix the fringes one needs to use IPA baths and an 80oC oven. Vacuum drying as set out above will lead to the fringes (and therefore the hologram) eventually fading (over time) to nothing.
