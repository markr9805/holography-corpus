JohnFP Wed Mar 05, 2008 7:52 pm #2
Well, it kind of depends on the recording geometry of your H2 rainbow hologram. But simply put, take a point source light, the sun works adequately for rainbows and hold or mount the hologram such that it is between the sun and your camera. Then move the hologram up or down until the you see the object image in rainbow colors. Click your shutter. You should be able to see the hologram scroll through the rainbow colors as you move it up and down. Then when you see the hologram (in mulicolor rainbow) move it right to left in that vertical plane and you should see the 3-D effect.

If the hologram was made with bottom lighting then turn the hologram upside down and perform the above.

If you only see flashes of color across the whole plate then flip the plate side to side.

Hope this helps. The key is rainbows are transmission holograms so the light source has to be on the opposite side of the plate of your eyes or the camera.

If you want to really try something cool. Once you find the virtual image side orientation (the one when the light is on the opposite side of the plate of your eyes and the image looks normal and in rainbow colors, not flashes of light across the whole plate. Then take a nice smooth piece of aluminum foil and place it on the light source side of the plate directly flat on the plate. Now you can view the hologram as a reflection but if when viewing as a transmission the light was overhead, when viewing as a reflection the light will have to come from below. The thought is, the foil reflect the light back through the plate from the opposite side, so the transmisison rainbow hologram thinks the light source is still on the back side of the plate.

If you have problems, don't hesitate to ask again and post what your are seeing in detail. I am sure we can help. I hope you post the photo when you get it.

ErichRose Wed Mar 05, 2008 8:22 pm #4
A few more tips:

if you can suspend or mount the hologram in a fixed spot and then move the camera it is best. If the hologram is moved around in relation to the reconstruction light the image will sway and swing making the perception of parallax confusing.

Also having a dark background will give more contrast when exposing the image. It doesn't have to be a black background but if you shoot in a darkened room where the only light is that illuminating the hologram it will look it's best.

Try and avoid capturing your reflection (or the camera's) in the plate or film. One way is not to shoot dead on so that you avoid the reflection completely. The other is to stay back far enough so that you are not being illuminated by the light source. Another is to wear black and even shoot through a hole in a black card or fabric.

Also I believe adjusting the distance from the hologram and the f-stop will alter the amount of "rainbow" that is visible. Many well done rainbow holograms are designed to play in specific color(s) at a specific viewing location.

JohnFP Mon Mar 10, 2008 12:07 pm #7
If you are using a digital camera, take a piece of paper and place it next to the hologram at the depth of the object. Hold your shutter button half way down while allowing the camera to focus on the paper. When it locks in, and in keeping the shutter button half way down, move the camera to bring the hologram into view, then click the shutter the rest of the way.

I have also found that I need to mount my camera on a tripod or something similar or I always get a blurry image.
