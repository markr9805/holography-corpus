Hans Wed Dec 03, 2008 9:07 pm #1
I have some trouble figuring out what will be better for making a panchromatic emulsion:

1] A mix of two emulsions (A+B) where emulsion A is sensitized for red light and emulsion B is sensitized for green light.

2] An emulsion where all grains are equally sensitized to both red and green light.

In the attached photo there are two emulsions, both with the same amount of AgBr grains. In the left drawing half the grains are sensitive to red and half of them are sensitive to green. In the right-hand drawing all grains are equally sensitive to red and green.

Both emulsions have been exposed to the same two fringes: A red fringe that goes from left-top to right bottom. And a green fringe that goes from right-top to left-bottom.

I would imagine that there will be a much greater index modulation in the bottom emulsion because of the greater density of grains that are sensitive to the exposed colour. But there is competition going on and perhaps a fringe that should be less defined (because of lesser bright fringe), would be over exposed at area's where this fringe intersects with fringes of the other colour. But would that be a bad thing?

A third possible emulsion I was thinking about would be the combinations of two emulsions (A+B) where emulsion A would be strongly sensitized to red and weakly sensitized to green, and emulsion B would be strongly sensitized to green and weakly sensitized to red. In this case one might get the best of both worlds. However contrast of the mix would of course be diminished a great deal. Now, is it necessary for a holographic emulsion to be very high contrast?

Which one of the three options would be the best... That is the question that has been haunting me for the last three weeks. I cannot figure it out by thought alone.

I find 1,1 diethyl 2,2 –cyanine iodide dye a bit expensive (almost 300 bucks for 500 mg)

The cheapest green dye is quinaldine red, BUT it is not very sensitive , but then on the other hand it is a good grain growth inhibitor.

A really useful thing to point out is that the red sensitizer dye Pinacyanol Chloride also has sensitivity extending to 532nm (but not 514nm). So if you have a stronger green laser than a red one you could be lucky---it may just compensate for the stronger sensitization to red light . Pinacyanol has the great bonus of having excellent grain growth inhibiting power. BTW , this not the case with dyes that contain sulfur atoms in their structure .
