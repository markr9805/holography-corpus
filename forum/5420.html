I'm from China and a fresh man here. Now I am engaging in contact-copying of 360 degree cylindrical multiplex rainbow hologram. I used to use xylene as index-matching fluid between film H1 and H2. As you know, xylene is toxic. So I also have tried saturated cane sugar solution.But it's very hard to clean and worse is that there is water in it, which make the emulsion swollen. So the reconstructed light beam's angular excursion of copying hologram,H2, was flagrant.

What kind of index-matching fluid I should choose? That must be easy to clean, can't dissolve emulsion and, as I've mentioned, can't be water solution.

Please enlighten me. Thanks!

I recommend decahydronaphthalene. It's toxic so don't let it touch your skin, but it cleans up real nice with methanol (another nasty poison). Don't let either of them touch your skin or evaporate into your nose. When used responsibly, however, they make a nice system for index-matching.

BobH Thu Mar 09, 2006 4:54 am #6
Decalin's refractive index is 1.475, like mineral oil and glycerine. It's thick enough to be stable on large pieces of film, and evaporates fast enough to clean up easy with methanol.

Index matching oil from Cargille is less toxic than decalin, and cleans up easily with acetone. I think decalin is cheaper in larger quantities. Cargille makes a wide range of immersion fluids and any index oil you want!
