I have a big rainbow type multiplexhologram (similar to the holograms made by Lloyd Cross, maybe it's one from him). This hologram has an mark on the right half of the hologram. The area is about 2x7cm where the image is damaged.

The hologram is between two thin plastic plates to get the right geometry for viewing (bent to a half circle). I assume that condense water between this plates has swollen the emulsion , i think it didn't dry equal so the image is damaged in this area. Some parts are visible equally bright to the normal picture but somewhat distorted, and you can see the contours of the water mark. Some part of the area are also a little bit milky with the image being dimmer there.

So the question is what to do

Do you think this mark could be repaired with rewatering and redrying?

Or with rebleaching? Or is it better to leave it as it is? Is the risk to high to damage it even more? Any suggestions?
Colin Kaminski Tue Oct 11, 2005 11:24 pm #2
I have heard of re-bleaching to save holograms that have printout but I don't think this would be helpful. I have often soaked holograms in water and redried them. This is pretty safe. If you can get the film separate from the plastic this would be a good choice. Make sure to post the results.

pearlv Wed Oct 12, 2005 2:11 pm #4
Just to add my bit - I would really NOT recommend trying to re-bleach an old hologram - I saw a 30 x 40cm reflection hologram that was about 10 years old disappear that way - only because we didn't know exactly what chemistry was used to bleach the hologram in the first place.

Unless you are quite prepared to let the hologram go I wouldn't try doing anything to it. If you do want to try recovering it I would rewash with distilled water and dry naturally.

Pearl

JohnFP Wed Oct 12, 2005 4:09 pm #7
Now this is purely speculative but why try to "fix" then entire hologram? If just a small part of the hologram is damaged, why not just take an eydropper or a cotton swab and just wet the damaged area and let dry? Basically, just try to fix the bad spot or at least just experiement on just a very small portion of the bad spot. But I also would be careful not to damage the hologram by simply taking it out of it's plastic plates either.

Again, I agree, personally I would never attempt to do anything to a vintage hologram, I have heard horror stories as Michael pointed out. I just thought I would share a thought if you were fixed on the idea of trying something.

One reason not to try to fix one spot is that generally it won't dry back to the same thickness as the rest of the hologram. That's why wetting agents and squeegees are used as the final step. To ensure that the emulsion dries evenly.
