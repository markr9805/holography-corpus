Joe Farina Wed Jun 27, 2007 1:29 pm #2
As far as color holography on PFG-03C is concerned, Hans Bjelkhagen has a great deal of experience, probably more than anyone else, and he has published a great deal on the subject. I suggest finding one of his recent papers, and following his processing schedule, at least for your first attempts.

Your lasers are the right wavelengths for color holography. It's good to have that much blue power available, but do you know the coherence length of your HeCd? It might be pretty short. You didn't specify the kind of 532nm laser you have. Likewise, have you tested it for holography? In my opinion, HeNe is the best choice for red, I would use the higher power one, as your coherence length might already be determined by the HeCd. Everything else being equal, having more red power is better.
floh Wed Jun 27, 2007 4:44 pm #3
Use a pyrogallol developper and EDTA bleach. It works fine. Be careful, PFG-03C has a very soft gelatin! Don't use too high temperatures (not above 20°C) to prevent it from coming down the plate. There is a hardener receipt on slavich page, try it.

Since the gelatin is that soft you have to be careful with room humidy above 50%. It could make unwanted color shifting.

Joe Farina Thu Jun 28, 2007 12:41 pm #6
> holography4fun: HeCd Laser has coherence length more than 1m

I don't think there's any chance of the coherence length being that long. Even a single isotope HeCd laser has a coherence length of only 30cm or so. But these lasers are very expensive, and I think it's far more likely that your laser is of the multiple isotope variety, with a coherence length of about 10cm.

If you want to make colorholograms, as your post implies, then eliminating emulsion shrinkage really needs to be your #1 priority, unless you don't care how the colors turn out. There are limitless ways to process PFG-03C, but how many are specifically designed to eliminate shrinkage? That's why I suggest taking a look at one of Bjelkhagen's recent papers. Maybe that's not a "fun" idea, but if your interested in fun only, then I think you should try something less demanding.

Colin Kaminski Mon Jul 02, 2007 7:17 pm #11
Remember that coherence can have a period or beat frequency if you prefer. So if your coherence length is 1mm then every 1mm you will find a place that can show fringes. I had this happen with a green laser pointer. Even though I could get good stable fringes with a 1 foot path length difference the holograms were sliced bread.

So you need to be able to adjust one mirrors length while observing the fringes. If you see a spot where the fringes blur out you are past it's usable coherence.

Tony Tue Jul 03, 2007 11:32 am #13
Spatial coherence is a periodic function, so it repeats every coherence length. In other words, as the path length difference increases, the grating contrast will degrade, then improve again, then degrade again, etc. You may have found a second (or third) peak.
Colin Kaminski Wed Jul 04, 2007 1:01 am #14
In my case I was finding the 1000th or so peak. Making a hologram of a deep scene shows all. So will a scanning fabry perot interferometer. Look at laser sam's site for a do it yourself version for those who have a oscilloscope.
