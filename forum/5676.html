This topic was discussed in another thread but I thought it best to start another thread to further discuss it.

I have heard of exposing DCG plates to humidity just prior to using them if they are old.

I have heard that high humidity does not affect DCG holograms.

I have heard that high humidity causes greenies.

I believe.....humidity affects a DCG hologram in two ways. The first being the humidity level of the plate when on the table and being exposed. A higher humidity level here would result in a more swelled emulsion thus the replay would be slightly towords the bluer end. Now is crosslinking directly effected by the higher water content of the emulsion at time of exposure. That is, does the higher water content separate some of the amino chains such that they are too far apart to crosslink or does the higher water content allow the amino chains to actuall move and curl during exposure such that more crosslinking happens at the exposed sites? Remember the amino chain is only about 0.3 microns in length if not curled and much much thinner. I don't believe movement of a chain would destroy the fringe structure and if it does happen it may allow the chains to curl toward or migrate toward the exposed area.

Then we have the humidity consideration during processing. I think this may be the most important area, thinking about Jeff's idea to cover the plate prior to placing in the first alcohol bath, uncovering in the bath, recovering in the bath and repeat for each subsiquent bath. It may be that if the humidity level is very high then the 1 second it takes to transfer from one alcohol bath to the other may be long enought for the partially dried emulsion to absorb additional water. This could very well lead to differentials in swelling in different areas of the holograms. I will test out the cover method during my next session. If it proves to help in high humidity conditions, then to further refine the process it may be that the alcohol processing trays should be in an enclosed environment, such as the sealed chamber glove box.

Generally, I believe more water in the emulsion would allow easier differential dehydration thus broadband, but I am not sure additional moisture at time of exposure guarantees this. In other words I believe you could subject the emulsion to a high humidity before exposure, keep it there during exposure but still process for narrow band with lower alcohol temperature and an extended time in the alcohol baths.

Humm, now this is making me think.

Assume a DCG emulsion has 10% water content at time of exposure. Then exposed to green light. Then the emulsion is swollen with water. Depending on the dehydration process the fringes can be either the same size, smaller or larger. Let us assume the playback is narrowband green thus the fringes are the same size as the original recording fringes. The key to this is even though the emulsion was swollen in water after the exposure the dehydration has to be such that the water is taken out and the fringes start to shrink but at some point, in this case the point at which the fringes are the same size, the water is taken out but the fringe structure remains the same size as the recording fringes. I believe this is due to the alcohol concentration replacing the water (thus the tuning bath).

Now if the same DCG emulsion has be exposed to high humidity and has 30% water content at time of exposure. Then exposed to green light. Then the emulsion is swollen with water again of course this time the fringes swell less as less water can be absorbed due to the higher amount of water already in the emulsion. At this point the fringes have to be smaller (spaced closer together) then the fringes in the water bath of the above scenario. Now if the same exact processing is done I would have originally assumed the emulsion would shrink by the same amount from water to final dry thus causing the fringes in the 40% original emulsion to shrink more and yield a blue shift. But does the emulsion really shrink more or does the tuning bath still lock in it's percentage of alcohol causing the same fringe spacing as the 10% although in thicker resultant emulsion?

Finally, how would this same thought experiment work taking into consideration the emulsion thickness. That is an emulsion that is 10 microns thick with 10% water compared to a 10 micron thick with 30% water? The above thoughts simply add additional water to an existing concentration thus the emulsion thickness must be greater
