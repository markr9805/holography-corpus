ron olson Wed Jun 28, 2006 3:35 pm #5
Wait just a second. If adding R6G to your water coolant improves the pump efficiency, you're probably using flashlamps with the wrong spectrum to begin with. No one I know in the commercial laser industry has ever gone with this appoach for a water-cooled system. My recollections of Rhodamine are that it neats replacing quite often - this would prove a real mess for what has to be a negligible gain in pump efficiency. You should think about the logistics of a scheme like this before recommendation.
Hans Wed Jun 28, 2006 5:22 pm #6
I admit that I am no expert at this subject. I am still collecting litterature. It was not my idea. I got it from: [http://www.americanlasertechnic.com/pulsebook.htm](http://www.americanlasertechnic.com/pulsebook.htm). The author claims a 30% increase in efficiency using Rh6G. He uses Xenon flashlamps.

He writes: "There are two basic gases used for flashlamps --xenon and krypton. Krypton can be used at low power densities for Nd:YAG systems and is more desirable for such systems because of the more suitable match between the krypton spectral lines and the YAG absorbtion bands. Xenon however will be our choice for this project even though its output does not match any of the YAG absorption bands. Xenon overwhelms the laser rod with intense energy throughout the spectrum. Nd:YAG absorbs almost all visible and near-infrared wavelengths and absorbs a good portion of the xenon output. You simply couldn't deliver this much absorbed energy with a Krypton flashlamp despite it producing energy in the Nd:YAG absorption bands."
