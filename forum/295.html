Jeffrey Weil Sat Aug 06, 2011 1:11 pm #2
Hello Joe,

That doesn't look good to me at all. Without a mirror or beam splitter in there somewhere to get the angle between those beams much smaller it won't produce visible interference at all. You need visible fringes to get good locking.

The beams should be pretty much superimposed over each other to get nice fringes for locking. Maybe a few degrees difference to get them a little smaller sometimes, but not much at all. Nothing like that diagram. That looks close to 20 degrees difference between the two beams.

I bet Bob H could give you more exact information on this. I think he uses a locker on a regular basis.

I bet you have "overlap expanded beams at a "small angle" to produce fringes" even though you don't realize it. You've made a Michaelson interferometer right? There you go. That's all your really doing with a locker. Taking some of the two beams AFTER they have passed through your actual recording setup, getting them to line up so the fringes are large enough to be visible. From there you can stick the sensors of the locker (there are two of them in that one head) over the bright and dark parts of that interference pattern.

For many setups you need another mirror or beam splitter to get those two beams coming from the same direction. The Saxby illustration is missing that component.

Jeff W

Jeffrey Weil Sat Aug 06, 2011 2:57 pm #4
Hello Joe,

Exactly, he had that extra mirror downstream. A beam splitter on a stand will do the same. Either way, your trying to get the beams pretty much overlapped.

If you can use a simple mirror in your setup that would be better as the locker would have a stronger signal. It all depends on the setup and the coherence of your laser. Mirrors usually take up more coherence than the splitter but both beams need to go through the splitter giving you only half the power at the lockers detectors.

I wasn't saying to pick off beams and make a Michaelson. I was talking about taking the run off beams after the plate, just like in the diagram, and making THOSE beams over lap just like the final output of a Michaelson.

On the Saxby diagram all you need is a large beamsplitter, so no small cubes a plate is the way to go. A 50/50 would be best. If you don't have one just try a regular piece of glass but make it thick so your not looking at the fringes created by the first and second surface.

Take the beam splitter and put it right where the two beams cross after the object and plate have been passed already. Right near where the diagram shows the fringes. Set the beam splitter so the beam lighting up the object is now on the same path as the beam lighting up the H1.

The beam splitter would be tilted like a forward slash /, tilting from 1 oclock to 7 oclock in this diagram. It would bend the object beam towards the left as it progresses downstream. Now the two beams are right on top of each other going the same way. Visible fringes.

You could also do it the other way. Have the splitter leaning like a back slash \. That way the H1 beam would now be following the same path as the object beam. Some setups it easier one way or the other.

Jeff Weil

Jeffrey Weil Sat Aug 06, 2011 4:25 pm #6
Hello Joe,

If you have a combined beam you don't need to read both freq's with the locker. In fact it would be better to lose one of them anyway. You really want only one set of fringes in the lockers sensors or you'll get something similar to cross talk between them.

If the beam is combined then it's going through many of the same optics. Locking the fringes of only one freq will remove the motion problems for those optics no matter how many frequencies are hitting it.

BobH Sun Aug 07, 2011 11:22 am #9
That illustration is completely useless. You'd need a beamsplitter where the two beams overlap to get them nearly collinear to get fringes to lock. The fringe locker also wouldn't see the object or the plateholder, and the fringe locker's additional beamsplitter must be the most stable component on the table to avoid it from making everything worse. Fringe lockers are very difficult to use in pictorial set-ups, especially with more than one object beam. Not worth the hassle.

Johnfp Mon Sep 12, 2011 6:12 am #18
Hey Joe, there are many disadvantages as well as advantages to fringe locking. The biggest draw back is if your holographic system is nice and stable and you put a fringe locker in and any of the optics inserted in the system exclusively for fringe locking moves, then the locker actually destroys your hologram by purposely introducing movement into the tranducer mirror. Also, it's almost impossible to get the fringe locker to acually be the beam coming off the object and the beam being reflected off the plate to insure those two are stable also. Usually fringe locking is done just after the spatial filters which in itself are expanding beams, thus to set up an "interferometer" while catching those lights, proves to be tricky.

Not to sound to negative on Fringe Locking, but I have found that just insuring my set up is stable is enough and forget about the fringe locker. Just my opinion.
