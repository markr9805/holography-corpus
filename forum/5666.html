I noticed a strange effect with MBDCG Denisyuks when exposed at certain angles close to 45 degrees off the normal. This effect happens with three different types of laser diodes. The image appears bright when illuminated at all angles. But when looked at from the left, the background appears brihgter then when looked at from the front. When looked at from the right, the background almost disappears. The subject that is close to the plate remains of the same brightness.

I think it is not a coherence length problem, because when looked from the left, the images are deep. I also don't think it is a second mode problem because this behaviour shows with exposure from different laser diodes at different temperatures.

I had to use spot metering because otherwise the holograms over exposed my digital camera. This is why the floor next to the holograms becomes brighter as the background in the holograms disappears.

These pictures don't really do the holograms justice. In reality the holograms are much sharper and easier to view.

The left hologram was coated with the veil coating method (top of the veil).

The right hand hologram was coated with the mold coating method.
