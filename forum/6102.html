Ed Wesly Tue Mar 06, 2007 5:49 pm #2
Pretty much so. As always, it takes some practice to get you own system worked out, like maybe 5% would have given green on Agfa but it gives orange on PFG. (PFG is a silver halide plate.)

The main problem is getting the knack of squeegeeing the plate streak-free!

And don't be afraid to ask for more tips, we have the originator of the technique, Jeff Blyth on board, plus Bob Hess, one of the cleanest working proponents of the process!

Colin Kaminski Tue Mar 06, 2007 7:00 pm #4
Mostly. You will find ther is batch to batch differences as well as differences in your TEA from batch to batch. If I remember right with pfg-01 I was using 3% to get gold, 5% to get green and 8 or 9% to get blue from 632nm.

It is very important to practice Squeegee technique as any streaks left behind will replay a different color.
MichaelH Tue Mar 06, 2007 7:30 pm #5
> Kiffdino: so there is no difference between the PFG-01 Holographic Plate and the PFG-01 Holographic Film ?the emulision behaves the same ?

In gross terms, yes. There are slight differences. I've some evidence that the film does pass some water that results in differences in development time.

Unfortunately it's the nature of the beast that you have to tune the process to your combination of emulsion and developing regime.

ErichRose Wed Mar 07, 2007 1:21 am #7
Yes the Trieth is mixed with water. I can't remember if it's a liquid or a powder but if its a liquid it is in mililiters and water is added to equal 100ml.

example: 10ml Trieth + 90ml H2O = 100ml total; 7.5 ml + 92.5ml = 100ml; etc.

Soaking time for me was always 2 minutes (found my notes!) but that probably varies depending on the emulsion's ability to take it in. I was using Agfa 8E75HD plates back then.

I'm not sure what the safe light issue is other than the possibility that since Trieth increases sensitivity it might also push the sensitivity into different wavelengths. Anybody have some knowledge/thoughts on that?
Colin Kaminski Wed Mar 07, 2007 2:39 am #8

Ed Wesly Wed Mar 07, 2007 9:17 pm #12
The processing you want is Kodak formulae D-8, D-11, or D-19; CWC2 or similar developer, but not the Pyrochrome developer; Fe EDTA, Copper Sulfate (JD3 or 4) bleach, or the dreaded PBQ. Anything that keeps the color of the laser.

Since writing that TEA article many moons ago, I had found out from Greg Cherry and Nancy Gorglione that they spun dry their plates to avoid the squeegee problems. They had a honking huge 208 V three phase motor to hold 8 by 10 inch, 30 by 40 cm and even 50 by 60 cm plates and spin them real fast!

Since readers are taking lasers out of DVD players, those motors or from hard drive or CD players could be used for te spin cycle!

MichaelH Thu Mar 08, 2007 2:59 pm #14
While squeegeeing does have its risks, with some care you can get consistent scratch-free results every time and it doesn't require the same clean-up and safety considerations that a spin-box would. Plus, PFG-01 is a nice relatively hard emulsion that is suitable for squeegee work (unlike PFG-03 which is likely to come off the plate when looked at by a squeegee).

Here are the methods I use. I only have problems when I don't take care to make sure my squeegee is clean.

Ed Wesly Sun Mar 11, 2007 12:48 pm #19
The developer can be found here:

[http://holographyforum.org/files/pyroab.htm](http://holographyforum.org/files/pyroab.htm)

The bleach is just one of the variations on Fe EDTA, this one makes it in solution, and instead of liquid sulfuric acid the powdered form is used.

Both work well, although I have never used both in conjunction. Read Pyro AB to find out why.

He probably did this to counteract some shrinkage from the Grman made BB plates he was using, if I know the holograms he is talking about making, circa 1997.

What I did do to counteract the shrinkage I found with the BB-640 plates was to use their pyrogallol based developer, see their web site, because it's a bit more energetic than the basic pyro, and PBQ bleach, because I'm still trying to use all I have left up!
