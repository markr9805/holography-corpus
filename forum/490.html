Ed Wesly Tue Mar 06, 2012 2:32 pm #3
The Norland optical adhesives are great, but they have on fatal flaw; they need UV to cure them, and while they are curing, the hologram may darken from print out. But if you are going to display your hologram in sunlight, it’s going to make that switch to the purple-brown colloidal silver state sooner than later! And will improve signal to noise ratio, but at the expense of brightness.

The biggest problem I had with sealing holograms with Norland is getting rid of the bubbles! It’s pretty viscous stuff, and takes a lot of manipulation to get the bubbles out. But it was the standard of the industry, as some of the New York holographers of the Godlen Era like Dan Schweitzer, Rudie Berkhout and maybe even Sam Moree used it on their pieces, along with some DCG’ers. A cat named Peter Rod Finlay told me that they farmed the work out to him, as it was not an easy task.

The beauty of the UV curing stuff is that it is liquid until cured, so you can work out the bubbles, and align things perfectly, etc. There might be a cheaper alternative out there, but Norland is another brand that is the standard of the industry. Tell them what you are doing, they may send some samples your way! They used to be so benevolent in the past! Too bad all my samples are hard as a rock now.
