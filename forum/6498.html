Ed Wesly Tue Oct 23, 2007 6:14 pm #8
5 is the reference, 1 is the object. Standard nomenclature is reference to object beam ratio. You can get usable transmission holograms with just about any ratio, just as long as the reference is higher.

When you send your beam through the LCD, is there a difraction pattern projected? Something that looks like the pattern but with a lot of fringes around each feature? This will localize the interference pattern of each pixel, and I am guessing that since you mentioned that you are going into information storsge, then you might be reading out the hologram with an undiverged beam, so you will not be seeing the whole LCD from that one spot.

The diffuser as John suggests will smooth out the diffrection pattern, and make the hologram more redundant; anyplace the undiverged read out beam hits on the holo will read out the whole screen. The diffuser can be as simple as a piece of ground or sand-blasted glass, which doesn't de=polarize the light.

Also on your screen there might be some polarizing sheets you can remove. but then you might have to rotate the polarization of the laser to match, with the addtion of a half wave plate.

Isn't it funny how it always seems that you need more equipment?

Good luck with your endeavor.

Ed Wesly Thu Oct 25, 2007 9:13 pm #11
Yes, you are correct about the low ratios where reference and object are equal are giving fringes of the highest contrast. However there is an effect called intermodulation noise where an interference pattern is formed by every point on the object interfering with each other. this unwanted interference pattern will cause a glow around the object. A higher beam balance ratio will keep the intermodulation fringes underexposed, and the fringes that you want will be stronger.

This is especially true in transmission holograms. People shoot for 1:1 ratios in reflection holograms and get away with it becasue the Intermodualtion fringes from the object points, all being on the same side of the plate, are more or less perpendicular to the plate, while the reflection hologram fringes are parallel to the plate. So the noise fringes diffract light in a different direction than the object reference fringes.
