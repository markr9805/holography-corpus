Just because I could, I've started experimenting with a different design for autostart adapter for Coherent C315M and kin analog controllers. My goal is to end up with a unit with more features than the more elaborate designs out there, but with minimal parts count and a total cost considerably lower than the street price for Coherent's relatively simple adapter.

The key components are (1) a 2x16 character LCD display panel and (2) an Atmel AVR 8-bit micro-controller. The AVR uC has a surprising number of functions, including a set of A/D converters, so I can use it for proper sequencing to start the laser, continual sensing of the laser state signals, and the ability to convert the power level signal from the Coherent controller to milliWatts and display it on the LCD panel.

I have some preliminary software running on a crude prototype. It hasn't been connected to a real Coherent controller, yet, but it does drive the display and respond to the digital status signals. A/D conversion is my next hurdle. The display would normally look something like this:

Code: [Select all](#)
`    +------------------+
    | Ready      141mW |
    | 00:14:37   241 D |
    +------------------+`
The "Ready" indicates the laser is powered-up and stable. The 141mW is the estimated power level (based on calibration data the operator previously provided using the two push buttons). The 00:14:37 is the hours:minutes:seconds since the laser signaled it was ready. The 241 is the relative setting (0-255) from the power-set potentiometer. Finally, D is which laser was specified, A through E. (I plan for it to remember calibration data for at least five different lasers.)

Pressing the <Enter> push button would to the expected thing--signal the controller to change power settings. Pressing <Menu> would put the device into a mode where you can select options and enter calibration data using the two push buttons.

R1  -- Laser power adjustment.
R2  -- Display contrast
SW1 -- Menu.  Cycles through menu options and suboptions.
SW2 -- Enter.    Selects current menu option.

The XTAL circuit could be replaced by a simple 16 MHz resonator.  The only
consequence would be the accuracy of the real time clock.  Similarly, the
ATmega328P could use its internal 8 MHz oscillator.  A few timing values would
need adjustment, but otherwise it would work and provide a real time clock
display at whatever accuracy the internal oscillator provided.

BL (backlight) is optional.  Some LCD display modules use LED light panels for
illumination that exceed the 200 mA power budget.  Such modules would require
separate power for the backlight.  Other LCD modules are better behaved
(~100 mA required) that can be driven from the Coherent controller through an
8.2 ohm resistor.  Still other models use a single LED or two (~20 mA needed)
and can be driven safely through a 56 ohm resistor.

AVcc can be improved by including a low pass filter.  0.1uf bypass capacitor to
ground and a 10uh inductor to Vcc.
