JohnFP Sun Nov 20, 2005 2:43 pm #2
Well, I had to give it a try and really saw no difference. I did a regular Denisyuk. Then I did the same hologram (50 second exposure), then blocked off the object put an additional mirror in that caught light from below the plate and redirect it back to the plate to interfere with the reference tranmission style with a 20 second exposure. For the third test I just left tha additional mirror on the reference side in the entire time of 50 seconds. Thus bascially having two refernece beams, on from above the plate and one from below and the object light from the back.

The regular hologram (overhead reference) was dim but I noticed the other hologram, the one made with the bottom reference beam was a bright as any DCG hologram I have ever made. The bottom reference beam, again was just overflow and was about half the intensity of the original referece beam. Because the angle was so great between the two reference beam, they did not bother one another, but of course the downside it the bright hologram needed to be replayed upside down.

So what happened was, not only was their additional lighting to the object through the plate but the second (bottom) reference beam had much less intensity. Thus the overall ration of object beam to bottom reference beam was lower. I know one can always add additional light to the object from the side but most of my objects are always so close to the plate this is not an option.

I may pursue this further by expanding my reference beam such the the lower intensity part falls on the plate and the higher intensity part misses the plate (below the plate) then put the mirror in at the bottom as before thus having the lower intenisty reference beam come in from the top and a more object lighting from the bottom.

Now looking at the difference between a very bright and efficient DCG hologram and a less efficient hologram I would have to state that there is either a higher density of gelatin in the crosslinked, exposed fringes (layers nearly horizontal to the glass plane) or a lesser density of gelatin in the uncrosslinked, unexposed fringes. I believe this to be true to a certain extent. I believe the real efficiency arises from the introduction of the gelatin matrix to remain uncollapsed in the unexposed regions and the water trapped in between that matrix to be dried out and replaced by air spaces. I may agree, I am not sure yet, that there are few of these voids but the fact remains that if the water is taken out of these spaces without the fringe structure remaining supportive the fringes collapse. This is evident in the fact that you cannot simply dry a fixed and water swelled DCG with air. It need to be dehydrated with alcohol with allows the water to be taken out while also providing support to the amino acid chains. As the alcohol is completely taken out, the amino chains remain relatively in the same place as when they were in the unprocessed emulsion.

But here is the part I don't get, still. What is it about the crosslinked sites that are different from the uncrosslinked sites? Is the water locked in by chemical bonding so that the alcohol does not replace it in the crosslinked sites? Is it that the crosslinking cages in the water? Why is the water replaced by alcohol in the uncrosslinked sites and not the crosslinked sites?

The only way I can imagine it right now is the DCG process may be a reversal process. That is, is there just a very small amount of water in the entire emulsion? Then it is exposed. The crosslinked sites are hardened in that they will not absorb any more (or very, very little) additional water. But the uncrosslinked site will absorb lots of water. The water, which is mostly in the uncrosslinked sites, is then replaced by some alcohol allowing the gelatin to remain stretched and swollen and less dense in those areas but it starts to shrink back to its original size. As the alcohol treatment continues the emulsion in that uncrosslinked site does finally get back to its original size but all the water is now been replaced by alcohol. This is evident as the hologram becomes visible in the 100% alcohol after some time. Then the final drying removed the alcohol from the uncrosslinked sites providing air spaces there. This to me seems a reversal process as it is the unexposed regions that the developing process works on, no the exposed regions.
