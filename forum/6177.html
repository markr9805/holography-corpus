Now knowing from your later info. Hans that you used KCr (potassium chromate) instead of KDi it makes for a reasonable explanation. 5% potassium dichromate is moderately acidic and it is there in sufficient concentration to override the natural buffering action of your gelatin layer. That means the gelatin is acidified and moves well away from its I.P. or iso-electric point where the amino acids in the gelatin strands all balance themselves out so that each acid bit is internally neutralized with its amino part . At the IP of gelatin it is at its worst point for us because it is at its weakest and lowest transparency and its ability to hold salts in a "glassy" state instead of crystalline state is at its lowest. i.e. its ability to hold salts in solid gelled solution when dried. (BTW. This is also important in the silver diffusion system where a moderately dried gelatin layer needs to hold silver nitrate without letting it crystallize out, before then putting it in bromide ion bath.)

However with potassium chromate (KCr) you have a mildly alkaline salt and you may be getting much closer to the IP depending somewhat on whether it is "limed ossein" or "acid bone" gelatin referred to as types B or A or Bovine , Porcine gelatine respectively. I guess you are maybe using

porcine gelatin which should be excellent if you are using acidic KDi but could shrivel off in mildly alkaline KCr.

Another separate issue is the crystals you described as "KDi" . If your

G307 formula is the one using Alum then aluminium chromate could form

and this is not nearly as soluble as aluminium dichromate. The alternative method in my paper of not using alum but a bit of prehardening with 1/4% dichromate and sunlight would avoid this.

I suppose it could be actual crystals of KCr which is slightly less soluble than KDi and the KDi at 5% is getting quite close to its saturation point

particularly below 20C.
