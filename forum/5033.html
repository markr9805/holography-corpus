I had always heard of the Agam holos but I really haven't ever seen any in person. Lee Lacey was one of those who did the work for him, and possibly Jody Burns.

It's funny how there are no shadows in the photo from the replay light to determine if it's front or back lit. It is obviously multi-colored, and I would venture a guess that it's a multi-slit rainbow. So I would give credit to Jody on this one, but it's purely an educated guess.

There are so many of these "mainstream artists dabbling in holography" out there it would be great to catalog them. The Dali stuff was linked to some post on this forum a while ago. He had multiplexes made, the ones I have seen in books are Dali painting a painting of himself painting a painting of Gala, in a very impressive cylindrical display, and the (in)famous Alice Cooper holo.

The Bruce Naumann stuff is really old, at least the ones I know of. He did a series, called "Making Faces", which was the artist making faces in front of the Conductron pulsed laser, way back in the era before transfers were commonplace, so they needed a laser to be viewed! I've got some article on it, but I am not up for scholarship right now as today is my last day of vacation before starting school again!

Dinesh Mon Sep 05, 2005 12:19 pm #5
If you look at the description:

Dazzling the eye with luminescent colors and the playful interaction of shapes, this work evokes a feeling of peace and joyfulness. Utilizing light as a medium, this piece takes on different appearances with the changing times of the day. This work also transforms with the viewer’s perspective, the translucent shapes dance amongst each other as the viewer moves around the piece with the changing reflection of light. Not only does this work reflect the variety of interpretations fundamental to abstract art, the artist’s intention was to represent the ever changing meanings within life itself.

The fact that it "...takes on different appearances with the changing times of the day." and changes with the viewers perspective "... as the viewer moves around the piece with the changing reflection of light." makes me think it's a single beam denisyuk, or perhaps a HOE, of just a mirror. Ed's observation of it being a transmission rainbow would mean a change of colour as the viewer chganges perpective vertically, while I read the statement to mean a change of perspective horizontally.

I rememeber Roberta Booth showing very similar pieces at the Austria 2000 conference.

BobH Mon Sep 05, 2005 3:21 pm #7
> So I would give credit to Jody on this one, but it's purely an educated guess.

I think Ed is correct. An article in "Holosphere", vol. 9, #5, 1980 probably describes the making of the hologram titled "Hologram I" for Agam. It says that "Agam worked with J. R. Burns of the New York Art Alliance, Inc., who made the recording and will print the edition."

Also mentioned is that it "is a white-light transmission pseudocolor hologram", the edition would be 250 copies, and that "The hologram and display unit will retail for $2,400."
