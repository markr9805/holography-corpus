I have some questions about laser diode systems suitable for making holograms. First, thanks for the links and information on the IR3C07 - this is very helpful.

Green laser pointers:

I haven't found a mar-free way of removing the laser diode assembly from the green laser pointer - what I did was grab the end of the thing with a pair of vice grip pliers and carefully wiggle the assembly out. There was some cosmetic damage to the end piece which was pressure fit into the pointer, but overall no actual damage to the diode assembly or the rest of the pointer case. With various tricks like cooling and reducing the drive current, I haven't been able to get the green pointer laser diode assembly to generate a beam stable enough for good holograms. I made another Denisyuk with it, and it showed the sliced bread pattern. The fringes on the interferometer can be made stable, but there are zones where the fringes are fainter, suggesting that perhaps there are other frequencies happening there. The beam has a gaussian distribution, but even so, there have to be other bad things happening in there.

I might try using a TEC cooling assembly bolted to an aluminium block

with a laser module sized hole drilled in it, but it looks like the green lasers aren't suitable for holography regardless of what adjustments/cooling tricks are done.

D&S driver/diode combination:

When I purchased the diodes from Optima Optics, I also purchased their laser diode holder. This is a fairly large (as far as laser assemblies go) assembly that looks like it could act as a good heat sink. This could be a factor in getting stable operation.

When I assembled the driver, I was careful to keep path lengths short,

and the leads to the diode are quite short, however these things might

not be enough to reduce RF interference.

I'll try the original ML1016R diode again once I get the second mounting assembly.

I think here's my next course of action:

1) Use lots of goop this time on the diode.

2) add a potentiometer to change Raj, and use a laser power meter to set the drive current. I suspect that diode/photosensor assemblies aren't a "one size fits all" situation - other drivers I've seen have adjustments for drive current.

3) add a 4700pf capacitor between pins 3 and 7 of the device - according to that PDF data sheet, this may be required to prevent oscillations.

4) shield/bypass the electronics. This will also give me the opportunity to mount the voltage regulator to the case as well.

I have done some further tests over the weekend.

I did get a sliced bread hologram with my D&S driver/LNCQ05PS - the problem may have been due to insufficient warm up, and the diode being too close to the shutter card (when I lift the card, it could have created a draft around the diode, causing a mode hop). A later image where I had let the diode warm up for 45 minutes, and had it placed further away from the card appears to be OK.

I'm starting to wonder if the optical feedback system which controls laser

diode current from the photodiode causes problems with mode stability - rather than applying constant current, it's possible that some hunting is possible in such a system. I note that the resistor limited systems seem to give the best results. I wonder if it's due to better current stability rather than better power stability. If the power is kept constant, the current could be moving around somewhat, and that may be what is causing the mode hops.

Tom B. Tue Jun 29, 2004 3:59 am #11
> tpark: I'm starting to wonder if the optical feedback system which controls laserdiode current from the photodiode causes problems with mode stability - rather than applying constant current, it's possible that some hunting is possible in such a system.--Ted

I prefer constant-current drive to APC for this reason. With APC, the drive current is sensitive to back-reflections into the laser and photodiode, one of the most problematic reflections being from the shutter. Open the shutter, less light reflects back, and the APC increases the drive current to compensate - just what you don't want to happen during an exposure.

I think I may have noticed some sliced bread with the LNCQ05 running at higher output power - around 40 mW versus the 30 mW I normally run at. I had noticed something similar wiith the ML1016 (worse behavior at higher power). Conventional laser diode wisdom has it that mode purity should INCREASE with output power, so I dunno what's going on.
