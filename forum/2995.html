Dinesh Fri Dec 31, 2004 4:18 pm #3
[quote:c9258965c9]What is the best-practices beam ratio with VRP-M? [/quote:c9258965c9]

1:1. This is the best ratio for any reflection, however, it's more important for VRP because VRP is more sensitive to variations in ratio.

[quote:c9258965c9]There is a grating right on the plane of the plate which distracts from the image... Is that from the infamous wood-grain (and addressed with Brewser's angle), or is it from not having a spatial filter?[/quote:c9258965c9]

No, it's probably from bad baffling (carding). A stray refelction (actually two stray reflections) from something shiny on the table, or perhaps from the plate holder, probably ended up interfering on the plate creating a grating.

Dinesh Tue Jan 11, 2005 9:39 pm #5
If light is scattering off the H1 from the H1 reconstruction (H2 object), so that the H1 is actually holographically imaging on the H2 then either something is pretty dirty or or the H1 is too noisy and too cloase. Try washing the H1 and gently rubing the film under the water with either fingers or a soft cloth. Be careful that you rub gently or you'll scratch the emulsion. If you have the coherence, take the H1 back. The H2 image will also be off the image plane and too far back or forward, depending which direction you take the H1, but you can judge whether the H1 image is better by taking the H1 back. Bleaching in JD2 (I'm assuming that's a Pot Dichromate bleach) may be noisy, which cause this effect. You could try not bleaching or not completely bleaching (leave it a little grey). The H2 will be dim, but again, you can see how much the H1 images. Another possibility is that a stray beam hit the H1 and caused scatter to the H2. So painting everything black and baffling well may help. However, remember that black objects also scatter
Colin Kaminski Thu Jan 13, 2005 4:57 am #6
