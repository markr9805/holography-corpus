Objective: Produce a Pulsed green Holography laser for under $300

I will be cannibalizing an SSY-1 (SSY1) commercially available surplus passively q-switched 1064nm laser with a Pulse Forming Network (PFN) capacitor bank for about $150 as a start. I will separate the OC mirror to gain access to the cavity to add optics.

I will add an intracavity Etalon on an adjustable mirror mount at nearly the Brewsters angle. I will add a KTP crystal intracavity or extracavity (whichever is better) for frequency doubling to 532nm green.

The supplied PFN has a 36uF non-polarized capacitor with very low ESR of 0.02 ohms and a 0.03mH inductor. Just supply a HV DC supply 645 – 700 V through a 1K 10W resistor to charge it up in a second. When this energy is dumped into the flashlamp when it is triggered, it begins to conduct till all of the energy from the cap is dumped into the flashlamp in about 100 usec. Then this formula will work: Joules into lamp = (1/2)*(Capacitance in Farads)(Voltage on caps squared). Then it stops conducting and PFN recharges. Hopefully the 1K resistor is high enough to stop the conduction through the flashlamp.

Experimental information on SAM’s Laser Faqs mentions that 645 V delivering 7.5 J to the lamp is the lasing threshold. A good value is 700 V delivering 8.82 J for a long lifetime of 100000 shots. I will be using 900 V for 15 J which is driving the flashlamp and q-switch moderately hard. Lifetime estimate 10000 shots. The PFN drains into the lamp in about 100 uSec in these cases.

The rod is 5mm x 60mm Nd:YAG laser. It has a dye (BDN?) passive dye q-switching cell to automatically get about a 10ns pulse without the need for any electronics. Assuming a 15 J lamp energy, about 16mJ of 1064nm will be emitted according to Sams data.

Experiments online have shown that just introducing a KTP crystal into that unpolarized beam gives a 1mJ pulse of green. I plan to introduce my Etalon in the resonator cavity to select a single mode primarily, which increases coherence length. It will be at the Brewster's angle to polarize the beam for improved efficiency of the frequency doubling with the KTP. Hopefully about 2-3 mJ of green output.

If 1 Joule can expose 1 meter squared area (Laser Reflections spec), I figure that I can expose a tiny green sensitive holographic plate. Subject might be a small flower on a 2.5" x 2.5" green sensitive plate.

The beam quality will be improved twofold:

Coherence Length is dramatically increasing by the addition of the Etalon. From about 3 cm to over 1 meter is my guess. This is caused by the selection of nearly a single longitudinal frequency by the Etalon.

Selecting the TEM00 mode by using the passive q-switch. As the energy builds up in the cavity, the TEM00 mode is predominant before the dye bleaches clear. This is the greatest intensity in the center of the dye. Then the dye begins to clear in this central portion, and the TEM00 mode begins to amplify, will little from the other modes. I am not sure of the actual mathematics of all this. I also have a Cr4+:YAG crystal coming which is a passive q-switch which has a much higher damage threshold just in case the dye one is damaged. Cr4+:YAG may also select the TEM00 mode in the same manner. It has been told to me that reducing the aperture to a smaller diameter intracavity will also help the TEM00. I will only try this if I am unable to make the holograms come out. The cavity is a flashlamp pumped with white Barium sulfate packed reflector. This diffuses the lamp energy for a more even rod illumination which also favors TEM00. The HR and OC mirror geometry may be a parameter to play with since some geometries favor TEM00, but I am not sure which ones.

I have most of the parts purchased and coming. The KTP is on loan. I lack the Green sensitive plates / film and chemicals, so I will need to wait till next month’s paycheck. I will try transmission holograms first since they are less sensitive to beam quality, and have a good exposure time.

I welcome any comments about the theory and execution of my upcoming experiment.

Thank you very much.

Dear Holographers,

I have taken my first pulsed Green Hologram ... SUCCESS !

The hologram is of my two fingers. The wrinkles are visible in an exposed area about 1" x 1"

Here is what I did. I took an unmodified SSY-1 1064nm unpolarized 4ns pulsed laser powered by the Pulse Forming Network electronics that comes with it on Ebay, and the trigger HV circuit that comes with it too. Cost $150.

I placed a 2mm x 2mm piece of KTP from a dead 3mW laser DPSS. This converted about a 1/3rd area of the 1064nm beam which is 5mm in diameter.

The green 532 nm output was filtered by a 3mm x 3mm piece of IR reflecting and 532nm passing filter glass from the same laser pointer.

Then it went through a 2cm focal length glass lens that acts as a beam spreader.

When it flashes from 900 Volts driving power, you are able to see a bright green blob about 1.5" wide by 1" tall. It is very irregular and spotty. This is driving the flashlamp and internal dye Q-switch pretty hard.

At a distance of 8" I held a piece of VPR-M Slavich hologram film 2" x 2" held in my bare hands without plate glass to hold it at all, at a 45 degree incident angle to the beam for transmission holography. I placed my two fingers as the object. I did a good flash. (The SSY-1 outputs irregularly because there is no polarizing Brewster to polarize the 1064nm which would make the KTP most efficient. Therefore you are at the whim of the SSY-1 to be polarized roughly at the proper orientation for the KTP to operate fully)

I exposed this undeveloped film to a 4 watt white frosted nightlight, 8 inches away, for 60 seconds. This is called latensification, and I found that it was a necessary step.

I developed in JD-2 developer for 3 minutes with my red diode laser as a safelight. I could see that there was an area 1" x 1" blobbish on one edge of the film that had darkened a bit, and fog all over slightly.

Wash in tap water 60 secs, Bleach JD-2 for 60 secs (it nearly all turned clear), Wash in tap water 60 secs. Blotted with kleenex, waved around in the air drying for about 10 minutes.

Unlike the first 3 tests which I performed, this hologram looked refractive in white light, and I could see diffraction lines in there by looking closely.

Using a green DPSS spread beam, I was able to reconstruct the image of my fingers, and look around them from different vantage points as a hologram should. Not a superb picture, but enough to know that:

Here is how to wire up the SSY-1, Pulse Forming Network, and the trigger boards that you get on Ebay for $150:

Starting from [http://www.repairfaq.org/sam/pfn1sch.pdf](http://www.repairfaq.org/sam/pfn1sch.pdf)

We run the + high voltage (700 nominal, 800 pushing it, 950 absolute max that capacitor can take) through a 3.9 K ohm 10 Watt resistor to P1-9. The negative goes to the black wire with the spade lug with circular hole in it, which is found next to the HV red output for the Flashlamp. All grounds go here.

The P1-5 gets +26.5 volts 100 mA but it works even at 16 v. The ground is P1-15. Keep it powered up as long as the HV is applied. When the power is shut off, remove this voltage source to quickly drain the cap voltage in about 2 seconds, its a safely.

P1-13 is ground and P1-3 is some sort of trigger voltage, but I was not able to figure it out.

Plug the 4 pin green connector between the PFN board and the red trigger transformer board.

The lamp positive is red FL+ and the neg of flamp is black to FL-. The white trigger wire of the lamp goes to the red wire from the trigger transformer. Insulate it for 30KV.

I shorted the case of the scr to the larger of the 2 vertical terminals that poke up from the SCR. Very little current / power throught it, but hundreds of volts so insulate well.

Insulate the Red trigger wire with thick insulation wire.

The caps charge to 700 volts in under a second. It flashes with a short on SCR. Sometimes you may get a double pulse if the switch contacts bounce. The current through the 3.9Kohms is not enough to support flashlamp conduction and it extinguishes.

Sources of 700 volts are at [http://www.repairfaq.org/sam/laserssc.htm#sscpfn1](http://www.repairfaq.org/sam/laserssc.htm#sscpfn1)

This part is relevant:

Converting a Lamp Pumped IR YAG Laser to Green?

Multiwatt Halogen, arc lamp, or flashlamp pumped solid state lasers are readily available at reasonable prices from laser surplus outfits, eBay, and elsewhere. What about adding a KTP (or other SHG crystal) and getting them to output nice 532 nm green? (Similar comments apply to higher harmonics but the difficulty level increases significantly.) While this would appear at first to be an easy project, the realities may be quite different. There are a number of issues all relating to peak power density available and shape of the beam inside the crystal. Maximum harmonic power will be generated when a narrow polarized collimated beam is properly phase matched to the KTP (Type-II critical phase matching). If the original laser isn't polarized (e.g., Nd:YAG without Brewster angled rod ends), adding a polarizing element will significantly increase performance. Since harmonic generation is a non-linear process, it is desirable to maximize power density so long as it is safely less than the damage threshold of the KTP.

Lamp pumped SS lasers often use flat-flat resonators to prevent hot spots inside the YAG rod. The intra and output beams both have a large diameter (almost equal to the rod diameter) and are massively multi (transverse) mode. Just inserting a KTP crystal into the beam (inside or outside the cavity) without taking into these factors and peak power density of the beam will likely result in either poor performance or damage to the KTP. With the multimode beam, it isn't sufficient to just consider the average power density in the beam cross-section - peak power in the hot spots due to the sub-beams must be taken into account.

CW SS lasers: External doubling will probably be very inefficient even with careful beam shaping due to the limited maximum power density available. Intracavity doubling may be possible but will probably require additional optics to achieve a narrow beam waist inside the KTP.

Non-Q-switched pulsed SS lasers: Intracavity or external doubling may be possible depending on peak power and beam structure. It may just be a matter of inserting a KTP crystal in the appropriate location.

Q-switched pulsed or quasi-CW SS lasers: Due to the extremely high peak power of Q-switched laser pulses, external doubling will probably be quite efficient but selection of the proper beam waist is most critical to avoiding destruction of the KTP.

In short, this may be possible but don't expect miracles, at least not unless the conversion is carefully designed and executed.

Hi there Holographers,

I am doing fine these days, and recently had some holographic activity and pulsed hologram success.

Before I was cutting up my 4" x 5" films in quarters, and using the SSY-1 with PFN1 at 900 volts.

I have now had success at quadrupling my power available for single green 4ns pulses which boosts me from 15mJ green to 60mJ of green to expose the full 4" x 5" sheet!

To let you know how I did it, I cannibalized four SSY-1 type PFN1's and robbed the caps and wired them in parallel. I used a 5 uH inductor (although 7 uH would be better) I ran it at 900 VDC and got 60 mJ of green out !!!

Developing the holograms with Jeff Blythe's Metol and fixing in Na thiosulphate (no bleach here) on transmissions, I discovered at first that the holograms were way overexposed! I backed off the divergent beam and took more shots and where the intensity was lighter, had very good holograms of my face. Pores, teeth glistening, glasses reflecting, etc.

Next I plan to reduce the voltage to reduce the mJ of green since they work better with about 30% dark exposure.

I have had some spare time since my consulting is between projects right now.

I recently discovered that all my other PFN higher power networks I designed were producing multiple pulses and did not give holograms at all. Now that I have four PFN1 caps with very low ESR, the holograms are back in buisness. I am really excited about having a lot of holographic quality green at my disposal! I estimate that I really only need about 30 mJ to expose a 4" x 5" film.

Has anyone else made pulsed holograms with the SSY-1?? I have sold about 60 of them so far to various experimenters. I have gotten up to 700 mJ multipulses out of them, even operating as a hand held battery powered laser gun.
