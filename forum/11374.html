jrburns47 Mon Sep 23, 2024 4:43 pm #2
Hi Alan,

I’m no expert re photopolymer, but…

1) thicker is probably better but if you’re laying the plate horizontally flat using 3 point stabilization and exposing with diverged beam from overhead, thickness of 3mm+ should be fine.

2) either UV or sunlight are fine

3) yes, the layer should be peeled off and the sticky side adhered to the pre-cleaned (with isopropyl alcohol) glass plate in VERY low yellow safelight. As far as keeping it on the glass, yes. Supposedly it can be peeled off and put onto something else but why bother![😊](https://holographyforum.org//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f60a.svg)? There are videos on the Covestro website that show proper glass plate cleaning technique as adhering technique.

If you’re on Facebook, you can check out Ferruccio Fabbri’s recent posts trying to make color Denisyuk holograms on the photopolymer and see that learning to adhere the film to the glass without dust or air bubbles is an important skill to master eventually![😊](https://holographyforum.org//cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/twitter/twemoji@latest/assets/svg/1f60a.svg).

Good luck.

Joe Farina Mon Sep 23, 2024 5:18 pm #4
I agree with what jrburns47 said above. Regular glass is fine, I used standard "double strength" 1/8 inch (3mm) glass. For curing, some people use sunlight, I've used a UV light that is used for pet reptiles. Adhering the film to the glass is a bit tricky, bubbles will typically form (all over the place) on the first few attempts. A double rubber roller ("cold laminating roller") available on eBay for ~$20 will greatly reduce bubbles, but you can also experiment with a cheaper single rubber roller (a "brayer") and maybe you will have better luck than I did. As for the angle, you can experiment with different angles for different effects. Eventually, and if your laser is polarized, you can use Brewster's Angle to eliminate most of the internal reflections that can cause "stripes" in the final hologram. The TAC layer is always left on (unless someone out there is doing something really fancy ). After UV curing you can peel the film off of the glass, if you want. At this point, the polymer will have lost much of its tackiness, and it won't stick as well, if you want to subsequently adhere it to another substrate for final display. I believe the final lamination is still something which hasn't been fully resolved, even by Covestro.

Martin Wed Nov 05, 2025 7:48 am #7
> sigintwrote:↑Tue Nov 04, 2025 7:42 am: Does anyone use water to apply the film to the glass?

Yes, you can do that. In contrast to hydrocarbon solvents etc. water will not interfere with the photopolymer layer. One advantage of water is that dust becomes less of an issue. The refractive index of water is a bit too low though. It is probably better to use higher index salt solutions or add glycerol etc.
