Joe Farina Tue Sep 12, 2006 1:56 pm #7
> Hans: I am already exposing 45 minutes with the current setup.

That's a pretty impressive exposure time, even remarkable if you are using a diode laser. Well done.

One minor drawback to MBDCG is (as you mentioned) that a lot of light tends to be absorbed by the MB, especially in thicker layers. In non-dye sensitized DCG, very thin layers are fine (say 5 microns) and are very good for bright, golden, broadband (but shallow in replay) holograms, just like the kind August Muth produces.

But I think the situation is quite a bit different in MBDCG, and I get the impression that thicker layers are best (based on the many papers I've read). Say for example in the 20 to 30 micron range. (A side benefit to thicker layers, according to Rallison, is that they are also the easiest to process uniformly, in contrast to thin layers.) So, I wouldn't suggest trying to increase transmitted light in MBDCG by making a thinner layer (I assume you're making unassisted, single beam Denisyuk holograms).

If you need better uniformity in terms of exposure across the plate, I would spread the beam out more, as John suggested. Of course a lot of light will be lost that way, but I would favor split-beam holograms in contrast to unassisted Denisyuk ones. Then the object beam could be increased as much as needed. This is where fringe-locking could be really helpful. Given your considerable skill in electronics, Hans, why not build a fringe-locker and see for yourself? In terms of project difficulty, on a scale of 1 to 100, I would say it's about 7.
