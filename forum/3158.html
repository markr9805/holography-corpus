> Stendec_Stan: 1. To be honest I don't know for sure, I don't really trust my safelight to I leave it off whenever possible, including most of the developing time.

Bad holographer! Trust your safelight you must!

To test it, just grab a CD or DVD (the commercial ones are best to use, blanks are a color that makes this harder) and while looking at the reflection of your safelight in the CD, see if your safelight shows any red. If it shows none at all, you're safe. If it shows just a tiny bit of deep dim red you can probably still use it, just keep the time down.

To really test it, cut a small test strip of film and expose the strip to varying amounts of your safelight by covering the strip and uncovering sections of it after 60 seconds, 1 minutes, 2 minutes, etc. Then drop this strip in developer and see if it goes dark.

Do a search of the forum on "safelight" and you should find a number of other recommendations including specific gels to use (from Colin) for making your light truly safe.

Tony Tue Mar 01, 2005 6:19 pm #11
For a safelight, get some of those plug-in flourescent green nightlights. ('Limelights (tm)'?) I have two of them in my 'darkroom' (actually the dowstairs bathroom). Never had a problem with fogging on the moderately 'fast' PFG-01 emulsion, even while 'in the soup' at a distance of ~ 1 foot.

As for your hologram, it looks like a pretty good Denisyuk on PFG-01. By that I mean that I have not achieved significantly better results, and yours looks typical of what might be expected from PFG-01.

As a beginner, sometimes its hard to properly set your own expectations. A trip to the Chicago Museum of Holography was very helpful in setting mine!

Some of the brighter stuff you see is either finer-grained emulsions, or H2 copies, or both!
