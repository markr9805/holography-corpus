JohnFP Wed Nov 08, 2006 10:40 pm #12
This brings up a theory I have in the alcohol processing. It is hard to find informaton on this.

Let's say we have some gelatin and it has hardened and unhardened layers or fringes. When you put it in alcohol I belive there is a certain acohol percentage thresehold in which water has been replace with alcohol to a point that the fringes retain their RELATIVE spacing. If you nearly hit this thresehold but are just under it at a certain alcohol concentration then there is still a little water in it just under this thresehold and when the hologram is placed in the next alcohol concentration that extra water causes slight shrinkage and you get a green shifted hologram. If you nearly hit this thresehold but are just over it at a certain alcohol concentration then there is less water in it just under this thresehold and when the hologram is placed in the next alcohol concentration that lesser amount of water causes the fringes to retain or lock in slightly expaned fringes thus giving a red shifted hologram.

In other words (this is hard to explain), at some concentration the fringes get locked. More water in this concentration more shrinkage, less water the the fringes are actually locked a little swollen.

With an older film, which is still gelatin and the gelatin alone has the capacity to absorb as much water as it could just out of the box, you get crosslinking via the dark reaction which inhibits the swell amount. Thus you may have "locked" in the fringe spacing to a degree. They don't swell as much in the water and thus the alcohol concentration thingy may not pertain as much.

So, if I wanted my young plates to act like old plates I could simply heat them, expose them to light or some other pre-exposure hardening method.

Just some thoughts in trying to understand this living creature.
