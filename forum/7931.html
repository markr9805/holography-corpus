Joe Farina Sat Aug 08, 2009 1:19 pm #9
> DJ Mathson: First I need some way to visualize what is going on; what does the original beam look like from the diode, what happens when the beam pass through the different optics and what will the profile look like on different distances due to the optics? I have a hard time trying to visualize this.

I have two aquariums just for this purpose. Take an aquarium of adequate size, and fill it with water. Then add a very small amount of colorant. I like to use a very tiny dab of white watercolor pigment, but you could probably use any number of different substances. The idea is to get a low concentration of light-reflecting particles in the water. It's important to add as little as possible, or else the laser light will be quickly absorbed, and won't pass through the aquarium. The colorant should be of such a low concentration that it won't be visible when looking at the water directly, but will be easily seen when the laser beam goes through the liquid. Then place your optics underwater, and direct the laser beam into the side of the aquarium and through the optics. The beam size and shape will be clearly visible in the tank. I don't know if this will solve your problem, but for people who haven't done it, it will definitely be a learning experience.
