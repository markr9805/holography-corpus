Not sure this qualifies or not -- but since brief pulses are one of the few time-tested ways to get portraits directly from the subject, and telecommunications (fiberoptics) uses lasers as a communications medium, I wondered if one could be adapted to the other's needs... more specifically, I ran across THIS:

New Focus 10 Gbit/s VCSEL

Model: 1780

SN: C0163

Qty: 1

Designed for use in laboratory applications or for test and measurement of 10-Gbit/s datacom devices, the Model 1780 is an 850-nm, 10-Gbit/s, directly modulated, multimode vertical-cavity surface-emitting laser (VCSEL). The Model 1780 uses high-performance microwave circuitry to ensure clean delivery of the electrical input to the VCSEL chip, and the VCSEL's 50-ým output fiber enables the use of either 50-ým or 62.5-ým fiber. The AC-coupled input and internal bias circuitry allows connection to any high-speed 50-W SMA-compatible signal source without the need for additional bias circuitry. A 10-position switch allows for optimization of the VCSEL bias for different datacom applications.

Can this equipment be modified (frequency doubling, eg)? What characteristics should I look for post-modification? Is it worth purchasing and saving for rainy day modifications? Any help appreciated, cheers.

Getting cheap crystals to double 850nm is not an option. Look for 808nm and pump Nd:YAG at 1064nm Then you can double to 532nm. There is a ton of stuff on this site that Ron Michael generated.
