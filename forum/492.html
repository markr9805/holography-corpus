Johnfp Wed Mar 21, 2012 9:20 am #8
Blow the surface of the mirror off with canned air. If you need further cleaning then try to rinse rinse with warm soapy water then warm water with a little photo flow. Try not to rub the surface unless you really have to but do it gently. Then blow the water off with air. Try to clean your front surface mirrors as little as possible as over the years, that extra wear and tear really starts to compound.

You may not even need to clean it. Make a hologram and see if you notice the blotchs.

Drying;

You said drying horizontal? You meant vertical, like leaning angled on a wall. The more smooth the drying process the less ripples and color variations, always dry my plates with the emulsion facing out. Hopefully others that know more about this will post.

Stray edge light;

Put a thin black tape along leading and trailing edge of plate to block this reflection, or you may choose to use black opaqe marker used in overhead projectors , your teachers proably don’t have any on hand but the local business product store should have a pen or two, just be sure to wipe it off with a mois towel (water soluable).

BobH Tue Mar 27, 2012 8:58 am #24
Vibration will not make the holographic image look "fuzzy". It will make it dim, or have dark or black lines moving through it.

The three lines could be light sneaking into the plate through the top edge. Block that light off with a mask, or piece of black masking tape. Never use electrical or plastic based tape on holography set-ups. The glue is terrible, and the base allows it to creep after being stretched to pull it off the roll.
Dinesh Tue Mar 27, 2012 10:31 am #25
Simon, it's important that you test your assumptions before you act on them. If your assumptions are wrong, you could be trying to fix a non-existent problem.
