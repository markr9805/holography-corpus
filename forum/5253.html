Hans Sat Dec 10, 2005 8:21 pm #1
I have done more than 80 experiments on pfg-01 film, changing exposure times, beam ratio's, tried kicker mirrors, brewsters angle, and many other setups I learned from this forum and books but never got any satisfying results. All were very dim, except for some metallic objects wich reflected almost directly back onto the film. In the meantime I ordered chemicals to try Jeff Bltyh's recipy and started building a fume cupboard, isolation table and TEC-coltroller/exposure timer. All are now nearing completion. But it felt so frustrating that my simple experiments were not satisfatory.

Finally I decided to ask Kris Meerlo for help. He is the only other holography hobbyist in Holland and within 400km of my house. He offered to help me. The idea was that we would try some exposures in his lab and to use his excellent ATI laser. That way we could check if the problem was not with my setup/D&S laser.

Kris is a true professional. He has converted is entire house into a holographic museum/laboratory. It was a great learning experience to watch him work. We made six single beams on my pfg-01/JD2 stuff and two on his pfg-1/(my JD2). All of them were of the same dim quality as my home experiments.

In a way I felt happy about this because at least it showed that it had to be something other than the quality of my home experimentation. Finally we tried to use the Pyrogallol recipy of Walter Spierings ([http://www.holoprint.com/articles/holos ... hrome.html](http://www.holoprint.com/articles/holosphere/pyrochrome.html)).

Wow, what a difference. The first one we tried was underexposed (we had mistakenly left the timer at the value for a previous TEA pre-shift). But already this one was much brighter than anything we had on pfg-01 so far. Then we tried a TEA pre-shift with a more correct exposure time. Finally a good Denisyuk on pfg-01.

The Pyrochrome truely works miraculous on the pfg-01 film.

Thank you for your help Kris.

Having spent several thousands of hours discussing issues like image brightness of reflection holograms, I wonder how much of the brightness increase you observed was due to a wavelength shift. I seem to recall that on 8E75 Spiering's pyro process (actually Van Renesse's) used to introduce a substantial wavelength shift towards the blue. I understand the JD-2 kit consists of a catechol developer (a la CW-C2) and a dichromate reversal bleach - right?

In my opinion both CW-C2 and most pyrogallol developers are very similar (pyrogallol producing a lot of stain though). Aynway, the wavelength shift in your processing schedule is basically caused by the reversal bleach - which removes all of the developed silver. That's why you end up getting a thinner layer - hence the replay wavelength is shifted towards a shorter wavelength.

When doing reflection holograms we all have come to learn that our eyes have a particular sensitivity to wavelengths around 540 – 590nm. The closer you get to those wavelengths, the “brighter” the image.

So in my opinion “image brightness” actually covers at least meanings like: diffraction efficiency, replay wavelength and bandwidth.

I somewhere gathered the notoriously bad results with Denisyuks were related to the high dye concentration of the PFG-01 emulsion. Since in a single-beam setup the object light passes twice that highly absorbing layer, losses are substantial.

I'd opt for a different approach. What about dissolving some of the dye prior to exposure? As a starting point you might try a very weak fixing solution, say a 1% solution for 1 min.
Hans Sun Dec 11, 2005 9:33 am #8
I don't think it is the replay wavelength that resulted in a brighter hologram this time. I had tried sugar pre-shifts before and with Kris yesterday did a TEA pre-shift. They showed no obvious increase in brightness.

Concerning dye absorption in the emulsion. I did try quite a few with the bypass setup (Saxby page 123, Figure 9.3) with varied beam ratios from 10:1 upto 1:1 and even 1:2. The first Pyrochrome hologram with Kris was severely underexposed, but even that one was much brighter than all of the non-metallic holograms before and there was no color shift. The second one we pre-shifted with TEA in order to reduce the exposure time because we sandwitched the film between two glass plates.

Because my table is not finished yet, I have not been able to test pfg-01 with proper path-matched split beam reflection setups. But I now convinced that at least the batch of pfg-01 that I have in my house does not work well with Denisyuk setups.
Hans Sun Dec 11, 2005 10:36 am #9
Actually, the Pycrochrome furmula we used (without sulfite) does not shift colors according to [http://www.holoprint.com/articles/holos ... hrome.html](http://www.holoprint.com/articles/holosphere/pyrochrome.html). This article states:
> The bleach dissolves the silver out of this shell leaving a void. The void does not shrink because its surrounding is tanned, so the gelatin keeps its original thickness, and no wavelength shift occurs.

The article further states:
> To produce holograms which reconstruct in green, add a component that stops the tanning so the gelatin will collapse. Joly found that when adding sodium sulfite to the developer the tanning effect is lowered [6]. The conjugate pyrogallol reacts faster with the sulfite, so it can no longer tan the gelatin. The voids collapse so the emulsion will shrink and the image turns out to be green for reflection holograms recorded in red.
