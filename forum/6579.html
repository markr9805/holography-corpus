JohnFP Thu Dec 06, 2007 5:07 pm #2
Hey Tony, You need to know the FL of the collimator. That FL will be fixed for that mirror curvature. Then place the pinhole (focal point of objective) at that length and you will get a collimated beam. Changing the objective changes the focal point of the objective, but once again, place the objective focal point at the Fl of the collimator and you will have a collimated beam. As you change the objective the beam will expand at different diamters and you can control diameter this way using a single large collimator while preserving light. If it is that you know FL of objective and you know FL of collimator and you want a final collimated diameter of "x" then I never had to calculate it, just place objective FP at collimator FP and change your objectives and see what you get. Then when you find the one best, just slightly adjust distance to collimator to compensate for differt FP of objective (very small, almost negligible).

To check for collimation take an opaque card with a nice size hole in it (couple of inches in diam) and place it in the beam after the collimator as close as you can to the collimator and measure the diameter of the light coming through the hole

projected on a surface some distance away. Now move the card with the hole further away from the collimator and see if that light coming through the hole is expanding (beam still diverging) or contracting (beam is now converging) on the projection surface. The futher away the surface is that the light coming through the hole shines on from the collimator the more accurate you can get with this technique. The light hole size should remain the same no matter what distance the card is from the collimator.

If you don't know the FL of your collimator, take it out in the sunlight an measure the distance that the reflected light is the smallest diamter. CAREFULL, don't start and forest fires...LOL. Same technique as a magnifying glass in the sunlight. This should get you in the ball park. Then use the above techniqe to home in collimation. Record the FL of the collimator and write is right on the back or edge of the collimator so it is never lost.

Colin Kaminski Fri Dec 07, 2007 10:10 pm #10
Yes, when you make a psuedoscopic image from your H1 you will need to flip the plate (for the mathematically inclined this turns out to be the complex conjugate). If you are using a diverging reference beam for the creation and reconstruction of the H1 then you end up creating a very large amount of magnification. So, in order to avoid this the reference beam for the H1 (Both during creation and replay) needs to be collimated. (The other option is to have an expanding reference beam during construction and a converging reverence beam during replay, of course this makes for a very large optic during replay so it is not practical.)

JohnFP Wed Dec 12, 2007 7:37 pm #19
Tony, I think you are going to find it quite troublesome to find a collimating mirror to meet your specs if you try to keep everything constant except the mirror FL. Unless of couse you have money to throw at getting a custom one made. The simple solution it to get correct diameter mirror you wish to use with the FL such that you know you have at least that much room on your table. Then simply change the objective to fill the mirror as needed using either a 4x, 5x, 10x, 20x or 40x.

I use all of my 8" mirror for my 5x7 plates by expanding the beam to about 10 - 12 inches at the plane of the collimator. This allow a more even intenisty of light across the whole mirror. No sense in buying a 8" mirror only to expand your beam to 8" and thus having a great fall off in intensity at the outside thus allowing only 6 or 7 inches of usablility.

> Ideally I was hoping for a plug and play formula where I know my angle (divergence) from my SF to the transfer mirror and the size of the beam diameter I want, like 7-10 inches, and determine the FL of the collimating mirror.

Well if you have the specs on the objective then simply find the distance from the objective focal point to the diamter beam you want and that will be the FL of the collimator you want.

Use tan Θ(div angle of objective) = opp(radius of final wanted beam) /adj(unknown distance away) for your objective to get the distance (adj) the spot size will be at your radius. Use radius not diameter. Then this distance (adj) will be the needed FL of your collimating mirror.
