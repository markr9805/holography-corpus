Ed Wesly Fri Apr 28, 2006 2:00 am #3
The corners always got the spin marks whenever I did resist. You just have to live within that central sweet spot.

One tip that goes against conventional or manufacturer's wisdom: we were coating plates at relatively low rpm, like 78 - 100, as opposed to the usual 1000+ as seen in the Shipley literature. I don't have experience with gelatin like I do with resist, but where I first heard of the idea of the slow spin using old phonograph turntables was from Rich Rallison, DCG'er extraordinaire.
JohnFP Mon May 01, 2006 3:10 pm #4
Mark, that build up at the plates edge never bothered me because I have found that even if you are using UV epoxy over the entire plate to fasten the cover plate, you still want an o-ring seal. That is, scrape away the emulsion around the edge prior to sealing. This is where that build up bead is anyway, so it is scraped off.

Ed, we used to do resist and spun our plates at increadible speeds. I think 1000RPM +. Needless to say I never experienced the "beading" until the slow DCG spin of 78.

SO with that, I believe you will alway have a bead a the edge unless the emulsion surface tension is exceeded by the spin force, which probably will never happen at 78RPM. I doubt 78RPM will break the surface tension of plain water.

Hummm, for you math geeks that like to play with numbers. What would be the speed needed at 5" from center to break the surface tension of water at an edge as described above?

Can the surface tension of a 10% solution of gelatin/water be calculated?

Maybe we can find that magic RPM number to get rid of the bead but still allow the emulsion to be wavelengths thick after drying...

One last thought, once that bead is broken the emulsion will fly out in all directions. Then you need to enclose the whole thing in a "mess container" container.
