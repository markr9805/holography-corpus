Dinesh Tue Jan 03, 2012 10:40 am #10
I wished I'd never used my name. People seem to react in funny, and very angry ways, when you try to be rigorous and exact. People also seem to dislike being disagreed with and get positively belligerent when you do disagree with them. In order to mute down the anger and (sometimes) viciousness on the internet, when you do disagree with someone, you have to qualify all your disagreement with statements such as "In my humble opinion". Why? I've never had an humble opinion in my life! When I do state an opinion, I assume that everyone regards it as my opinion, so why the needless addition. Of course it's my opinion! Personally, I'd rather be disagreed with because I may learn something. There was a time when discourse was an exchange of opinions enlivened with point and counterpoint, opinions and disagreements. In those times, no one faulted you for having a counter opinion. Also, in those times, debate and argument were carried out with high standards of exactitude and rigour. You were expected to be precise and you were expected to justify your statements with a high level of justification, not innuendo and metaphor. Pseudo-science, sloppy thinking and pseudo-intellectualism were revealed as such. Then came the internet!

As it is, I've probably lost friends in the community because I still insist on a high level of justification and proof and fashion all my statements accordingly. On the other hand, if I had called myself something like exp(i*cos(theta)), I'd be guilty of a lack of precision! There are somethings you cannot compromise on, and precision is one of them. Without this, you may as well reduce all science and technology to sitting around a campfire and telling ghost stories.
