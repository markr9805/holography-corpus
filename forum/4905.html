Dinesh Mon Jun 20, 2005 1:52 pm #4
Welcome to the forum!

Your talents are actually pretty useful to holographers. A lot of holographers are very adept in the process of making holograms, but, I think, are lacking in aesthetic vision. We need more artists! Also, you don't need "a great deal of understanding when it comes to all the technical stuff". Holography is a process and so long as you follow the process reasonably well, you need no more technical knowledge than necessary to be a photographer: an eye for exposure, an eye for the way light reflects and a feeling for "hologenic" objects. The rest is mechanical and knowledge of the best tools. This forum is pretty good at discussing the "best" tools in terms of lasers, developers etc. Like any tool, you don't need to "understand" it, as much as to be able to use it well. I suggest if you really want to make your own holograms, you get hold of the Integraf holography kit. It's pretty inexpensive and it's a good way of getting your feet wet ( [http://www.holokits.com/holography_kit.htm](http://www.holokits.com/holography_kit.htm) ). I notice there are three levels now, which seems to be a new thing.

If you're ever in San Diego, you're welcome to drop in and make a simple hologram. We use a material known as DCG and make pretty simple objects, mostly ( [http://www.triple-take.com/](http://www.triple-take.com/) ).

Actually, in most large holography companies such as Banknote, there is a seperate Art Dept. These companies make the silver foil stickers you see on cereal packets, wrapping paper, magazine covers etc. In these places, the Art Dept lay out the elements, colours etc of an image, creates transparencies, farms out for models and present the holographers with transparencies, models etc. and a layout of where each element is supposed to go. Display holographers (called "art holographers" because they're trying to create fine art using holography as a medium) make up they're own images, but I haven't seen one for sale in a long time. I rarely see any "art holographers" any more! Display holographers who do it for a (paying) hobby generally don't seem to do a lot of "design". You can see some of the stuff on the opening page of the forum. It's the technique that seems to fascinate more than any concept of aestheticism. Mostly today most professional holographers use holography for the technology. Holography for communications (FBG's), holography for commercial display ( [http://www.zebraimaging.com/](http://www.zebraimaging.com/) ), holography for technical imaging (Head Up Displays), holography for technical effects (diffraction gratings in spectrophotometers) etc, etc.

As I've sadi before, the public's perception of holography makes it a difficult sell. Either they expect Princess Leia's and Holodecks and are disappointed by the real thing, or they're lead to believe that anything "3D" (like those crystals with etched images inside them) are "holograms", or they're told that projection techniques like the ghosts of Disneyland are "3D Holograms". The word has been overhyped.
