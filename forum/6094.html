Geezertron Sun Mar 04, 2007 4:12 pm #1
Hello. Back in the early 90s, I spent a couple of years living in a holography lab that was cleverly disguised as a basement apartment. This was back in the day when Agfa not only sold a box of ten 8"x10" glass plates for 200 bucks CDN, but delivered it to my door for free the next day from Toronto (sigh). I had a 4'x8' concrete/steeltop table and made pretty much every type of monochrome holo there is to make -including 8x10 inch transmission masters/reflection transfers with my measly surplus Melles Griot 5mw hene.

Anyways, I've got a few design ideas for a micro-holo system for 4"x5" reflection/transfer work and wether or not I get back into it will depend on one thing: Finding or building a reasonably-priced diode laser with spatial/temporal coherence similar to that of my 5mw HeNe. I would have felt stupid even asking this a few short years ago, but the more I research on the web, the more and more I discover that people are recording holos with red, green, and even blue diodes, with pricepoints ranging from that of laserpointers to turnkey lab systems. One site, located very close to me, has me positively drooling, as they not only have frighteningly high-powered pointers but a specificaly SLM turnkey series (the most basic green slm model starting at $1700usd). They list holography as one of several applications for lasers in their red, green and blue product lines. [http://www.laserglow.com/](http://www.laserglow.com/) Does someone maintain an "A-List" of diode lasers or laser components that have established themselves as reliable for holographic applications (preferably in the higher powers). The thought of having anywhere from 20 to 500mw of coherent light available from a little box that's a fraction of the size of my HeNe just blows my mind. I wish there were a centralized list of diode lasers or construction/conversion plans that work well for holography. I suppose I could save up the 1700 bucks over the next couple of eons for a turnkey system, but the thought of accomplishing the same thing at a fraction of the cost certainly has its appeal.

From what I gather, even some of the cheapest keychain lasers can make holos, and some of the most expensive turnkey systems can be useless. It's basically a crapshoot when experimenting with pointers/components and their undocumented/unpredicted coherence properties. Trying out higher powers makes for an expensive crapshoot (but I'd love to get my hands on some of those high-powered pointers listed as tem00...even as playback lights for deep transmissions they would rock).

Can anyone in here specifically point out any high-output (20-500mw) diode modules/kits/pointers with or without modifications (at any wavelength...who knows, I'd go RGB if it were cheap enough, heh heh), that are well suited to holography? Browsing these forums, it's hard not to get excited reading about how you guys are using DCG and trying this silver halide recipe or that (I didn't have the internet when I made holos way back when...what a difference that would have made), but it'd be nice to find a big fat juicy article on available diodes and their costs and beam qualities so I can determine if it's still just a pipe-dream for me. When I think of the optics I might need to buy just getting the beam to pass through a spatial filter), I wonder if I'd still not be better off with a gas laser. I just don't want to set that massive pig of a HeNe up for a lousy 5mw, and even Meredith Instruments wants over a thousand bucks for a 25mw HeNe. I've looked into the D&S pointers as well as the ubiquitous "Infinity" pointers, and it seems they're a tad too low-end for serious work as I've read they suffer from mode-hopping. I've also seen alot of photos of expanded diode beams that looked so awful I doubted that even spatial filtering could save them, and alot of photos of holos showing telltale signs of uneven reference lighting, so as much as I want to make the switch, I feel as though I could be making an expensive mistake by not buying or building a system that has many holographers' stamp of approval.

So what would you get? Should I save up for a turnkey system? Should I take as gospel a company's claims when they state their diodes are suitable for holography? Can I modify a pointer to be as stable as a turnkey system? Is spatial filtering a diode still a royal pain? Am I silly for even thinking I'll ever get as clean a beam from a diode as I can from a HeNe (I can't stand artifacts of any kind from dirty or non-uniform reference beams). Is amateur holography with diodes still in the experimental stage with no diodes standing out as clear winners?

I've tinkered with laser diodes for a while and obtained some reasonable results but I have not yet seen any plug and play systems in the low end range ( < $2000) that I could unhesitatingly recommend as problem free for holography. Every now and then something promising comes along, but then reports eventually come in that the stability, beam quality, reliability and/or customer support are lacking. In principle it should be possible with temperature stabilization, maybe external grating feedback if needed to clean up the spectrum, and anamorphic prisms for beam circularization etc. but this seems to be difficult to do.

The only sure thing is a laser that has been in routine use by holographers for a while with few reported problems. The Coherent Compass and Verdi systems seem well regarded in this respect. Surplus Compass heads and controllers were going for <$2000 a while ago but are apparently getting much harder to find. I have no idea what new Coherent systems cost , but likely they would be 3-4 times more expensive. Melles Griot has some attractive items too.

I don't know about low cost DPSS - I've read somewhere (probably here) that some DPSS SLM lasers can have low-level, but still annoying secondary lines that produce faintly visible contour lines in holograms - note that while manufacturers might mention coherence length they almost never specify single mode purity. You would definitely want an unconditional return policy on a purchase like this. If's that's not possible - no sale.
