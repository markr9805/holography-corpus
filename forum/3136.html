Jim McPherson Thu Feb 17, 2005 11:16 pm #1
I've been looking for a cheap source for a high power laser diodes. Thor Labs and digikey sell the laser diode in DVD writers. I just bought a 16x DVD writer from newegg.com recently for $55.00 and free shipping, it works great. So I bought 2 more to pull the laser out of. I bought 16x writers, one was an AOPEN, the other was a Sony DW-D22A. The AOPEN had a samsung 4.8mm long can diode (12 pin, no feedback), the Sony had a SLD1236VL in a 5.6mm standard can diode. I use the Sony since it fits in common diode mounts.

The SLD1236VL pdf can be found here:

[http://www.sony.net/Products/SC-HP/cx_n ... 1236vl.pdf](http://www.sony.net/Products/SC-HP/cx_news/vol37/pdf/sld1236vl.pdf)

That's 80mW CW, and it is single mode.

Not bad for $57.00 including the rest of the drive.

In addition I got 2 nice lenses, and a couple beam splitters in each drive.

If you like experimenting, this might be a good thing to try.

-Jim

I use a direct current driver fom Roithner (EU-38 ) and i found no problems regarding current /wavelength stability, although the laser power is a bit less than battery operated diodes of low power.

I adapted a multi turn potentiometer into EU-38 for a precise current control in the diode, it works well, depending on the diode current limit, you need provide a resistor limiting current in serial with diode. The EU-38 output voltage is 4.5V, used only with constant current mode.

An aluminum heat sink is used as inertial heat control; with the help of a uncoated etalon, I see the interference fringes very stable, I think it will work.

Mode hopping vary from diode to diode, model to model and even between units, you need to provide a smooth current control (with temperature stabilized) to "fly" the mode hope regions and map it for each diode. there are required regions of current value that mode hop will not occur.

At high current value for each diode, most of them will be relatively less prone to form mode hop's.

See [http://www.ilxlightwave.com/appnotes/mo ... lasers.pdf](http://www.ilxlightwave.com/appnotes/mode_hopping_semiconductor_lasers.pdf)

Concerning the 80mW diodes, of course i tested them. They can work single frequency until 50mW about then they are multi-longitudinal mode until 80mW. They are not designed yet for single frequency operation at maximum power and will give fringed hoos. So until a new batch is available, better to use 50 or 60 mW diodes which can be fount single frequency.
