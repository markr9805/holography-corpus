This thread was sparked by a thread on the FB page, linking to this: [http://www.pinterest.com/pin/520095456940593924/](http://www.pinterest.com/pin/520095456940593924/) Some of the coolest pictures of manicures that I have ever seen!

Reverse engineering holographic nail polish was a project I was assigned to do when I worked at CFC Northern Banknote and you can have fun making the same observations yourself. As usual a client of theirs wanted to copycat someone else’s holographic glitter that was being used in nail polish. I had to figure out the details.

Looking at some nail polish applied to a black card under a dissecting microscope (50X) you could see the flakes, roughly 50 by 100 microns. I was surprised to see the flakes being rectangular, but then noticed that they aligned themselves with the brush stroke thanks to the shape.

The card with the polish was interrogated with a He-Ne beam incident along the normal. The center of a cylinder of paper was placed at that spot. The diffracted spots showed up at a certain level on the inside of the paper cylinder, and measuring the diameter of the cylinder along with the height of the diffracted spots allowed me to calculate the angle of diffraction, and from there figure out the spatial frequency of the grating, and then translate that to an intra-beam angle to be used in recording the copy grating at 458 nm into photo-resist. This technique also showed that the grating was single axis.

The flakes themselves are made in a clever way. The holographic grating pattern was embossed into a coating on polyester, and then metalized. The emboss coat was soluble in something organic like acetone, so the aluminum lifted off, but being wrinkled in a diffraction grating shape. Some step that I don’t recall chopped up the metal into the desired flake shape. The polyester could be recoated.

There are a few companies making the nail polish with these flakes, and attempts have been made but I haven’t seen it in the market place for automotive spray paint with the grating flakes in it, small enough to pass through a spray gun nozzle!

Another thought after posting the above: as long as you are headed for the cosmetics counter to pick up some holographic nail polish, grab a bottle of clear for a very cool optics demo!

Fill a black tray with H20, and drop a drop of the clear nail polish onto the water. A glob will sit there, and then bang! will spread out to wavelengths of light thickness, revealing the classic oil on water, soap bubble thin film interference.

But this lacquer is different, because you can make the pattern permanent by having placed a piece of black construction paper in the tray before dropping, so that you can pick up the pattern when lifting the paper out of the tray! A regular crowd-pleasing demo in Week 13 of the Physics of Light class that I teach.
