Now that I have mastered the MBDCG fabrication process, I want to start experimenting with polymers.

All references I find include the use of Acrylamide. When I read the MSDS about this stuff it appears to be really really dangerous. The MSDS's warn that even the slightest skin contact can cause neurological problems. But an old article in Scientific American states:

> "Even better photographic reproductions can be made in transparent plastic by means of another chemical system that demonstrates photopolymerization. The photographic resolution that can be achieved with this system is limited principally by the wave nature of light and the quality of the optical system used for making the exposure. The monomer is acrylamide, a solid organic material that dissolves in water at room temperature in the proportion of about one part monomer to four parts water plus 1 percent or less of the crosslinking methylene bis acrylamide. The solution is clear and becomes sensitive to light by the addition of a small percentage of a dye, such as riboflavin. This particular dye functions both as the light absorber and as the electron donor. The polymer forms an essentially grainless, three-dimensional image that does not scatter light. After the unreacted monomer has been washed away the image has the form of a transparent plastic film. The thickness of the film varies roughly in proportion to the square root of the intensity of the light. In effect the polymer becomes a device for converting the intensity modulation of light into optical phase modulation with obvious applicability to the production of holograms. The monomers are about as toxic as ordinary photographic chemicals, and the same precautions should be observed when working with them."

So, my question is:

Is this stuff safe to play with when normal precautions are taken (small quantities + mask + gloves + open door)? Or should I not even begin to think about using it?

jeff-blyth Wed Oct 25, 2006 6:02 pm #4
> Hans: ...........................So, my question is:Is this stuff safe to play with when normal precautions are taken (small quantities + mask + gloves + open door)? Or should I not even begin to think about using it?

I have been using the stuff in experiments for about 35 years now.

I used to use it in a converted garage /lab too. It is of course OK once it is polymerised (polyacrylamide gels have been the basis of all the electrophoresis industry.) But the monomer should be treated with respect and I do remind myself from time to time not to let familiarity breed carelessness. For small molecule like this it does have less volatility than you would expect compared to say acetic acid nevertheless

your "(small quantities + mask + gloves + open door)" policy is pretty safe. The standard crosslinker to use with it is methylene bisacryamide

and as such crosslinkers go, that is the least bad one that I have come across. Its solubility is rather low in water but more soluble in concentrated solutions of acrylamide in water. An alternative is dihydroxyethylene bisacrylamide. This has better water solubility but costs more. --You do need to pack the crosslinker in to get good holographic gratings as polyacryamide is intrinsically poor at holding holographic grating fringes as it is too soft without good crosslinking.

Jeff
