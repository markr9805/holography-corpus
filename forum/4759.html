I seem to recall someone saying that coffee-table holos (Saxby's term) work better with a converging reference beam. I tried one recently with a (slightly) diverging beam and the results, while interesting, had too much perspective distortion to be acceptable. Ideally, for any holo the reference divergence should be the inverse (conjugate?) of the divergence of the replay light source, but usually a collimated beam is used with OK results. It could be that off axis distortions are much more obvious with coffee-table holos and so they might benefit from more careful matching of the divergences. Not sure how this would affect the brightness vs. viewing angle, though...

As always, you need to think backwards to the lighting that's going to reconstruct the hologram. You're right that the reference should be the conjugate of the reconstruction and usually a collimated beam works and is easy to set up. However, the larger the hologram, the greater the distortions, or rather, the more the distortiomns are noticed.. So while a hologram on a 4x5 is pretty forgiving for a reasonable reconstruction mismatch, you need to be more accurate for a coffee table hologram of typical coffee table dimensions, say 48" x 20" odd. It also depends on the depth of the hologram. Most aberrations start at the plate and distort the image more as the depth gets greater, so the deeper the hologram, the more the effects of reference mismatch. However, having said that, it depends a lot on the image. The image I saw at Richmond was potted plants apparently set underneath the glass of the table. In this case, you had seperate images, each of which was small compared to the overall size of the hologram. Thus any small distortion in each image element (a single potted plant) would be small.
