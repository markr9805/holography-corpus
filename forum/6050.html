JohnFP Thu Feb 15, 2007 2:33 pm #5
One thing to remember:

Always assume there is a sharp piece of diamond particle lying on the surface to clean. Then use common sense. I think it was Edmunds optics cleaning solution and instructions that stated to place a cleaning cloth on your optic, then drop a drop of solution on the cloth, the genly pull the cloth across the optic...........OUCH!!! I never did this even after the first time I read it.

Blow with air first.

Then use any type of roll method that lifts dirt up and off the surface instead of dragging it across the surface.

BobH Thu Feb 15, 2007 3:54 pm #7
> The biggest pain is just that it is about an inch back from the end of the cylindrical housing and I have to do it with tweezers or a hemostat.

The first step of all laser optic cleaning procedures is to remove the optic from its holder! It will be impossible to clean it properly otherwise. You'll just be smearing the contaminant around on the surface with the tip of a cotton swab. Just be sure to do one optic at a time, and get the laser lasing after each attempt. See Michael's posts about his experience with his S-P 907.

There's nothing wrong with the "drop & drag" method. All laser techs use it. The only time the "roll & wipe" method (suggested by JohnFP) is used is for cleaning Brewster windows. In that case, wiping toward the tip is necessary to prevent picking up micro-chips of glass and dragging them across the face of the window ... not a good idea.
