> JohnFP: Joe, be reminded it is not the tuning bath alone (he uses the same for both) but is a combination of concentraion of AmDi, exposure time and processing.

Yes, I know, John. I'm just looking into insights for color-tuning of MBDCG. For two or three-laser color holograms on MBDCG, what we really need is something reliable and fairly precise. I don't know if that currently exists, but until my setup is ready, I'm trying to look at the literature for clues about color-shifting of DCG. Reducing dichromate content, for example, seems to favor a red-shift, but that's only one factor.

There may be a special problem (or complication) with MBDCG with regards to red-shifting, as it typically contains something else that washes out, namely TMG, glycerin, TEA, etc. What I really want to do is shift the wavelength upward by liquid processing, then try to shift is back down slowly (and in a controlled way) by non-liquid processing. This is just speculation at this point, but controlled non-liquid processing can consist of higher levels of dehydration (i.e., a vacuum oven, which I built) and high-level UV exposure (described in a rather obscure patent).

Joe, if i get in the lab this weekend, I have a couple ideas (some I presented a while ago) that may work toward that end.

The one I explained was the post process of soaking in AmDi and exposing to non coherent light.

The second is, after exposure, soak in AmDi solution and harden with light then process with fixer and alcohols. This latter idea is to allow the emulsion to swell before providing the overall even crosslinking and prior to the fixing working on the fringes. I imagine it will be a ballance of AmDi concentration and the amount of pre processing light exposure.
