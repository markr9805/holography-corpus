Ron Michael Sun Jun 11, 2006 7:44 pm #3
Hi Dave,

I have not personally done any Rainbow work so I can't speak about it. I can speak of my work with pulse lasers. The work I've concentrated on was split beam reflection. I found I could only get about a foot of depth of field with the Agfa 8e75hd. Even with single mode lasers this generally could not be improved and is a well documented limitation of this setup. I will note that single pulse is required for the best image since double or multiple pulses have created interferometer affects in the recordings of live subjects (moving objects). Of course the time to developer and it's chemistry was important to counter the effects of holograhic reciprocity failure. For the above film I found SM-6 a fast developer and I tried to get the film/plate into the bath within 3 to 4mins of exposure. I didn't feel my work with TEA or lantensification to be worth the trouble personally. Not sure if this experience helps...
Ron Michael Sun Jun 11, 2006 7:54 pm #4
Dave,

I'm not sure I made myself clear, It wasn't the limitation of the laser since

the documented limitation was regarding resolution requirements on the film in that particular setup. Of course transmission holograms can get much better depth of field accordingly. Pulse lasers can have many meters of coherence length and will be limited by pulse width, any frequency chirp etc.

> I’ll plan on doing mostly rainbow masters, wondering how deep ill be able to place my subjects behind the master plate.

If you want realism (as much as possible with a Rainbow), the distance from master to intended final image-plane should be the same as the intended viewer distance from the final hologram. Distances of objects to master is a creative decision. You may be disappointed if you're going for realism with a ruby laser originated Rainbow hologram.
