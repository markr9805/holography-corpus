John Klayer Sat May 12, 2007 9:04 am #1
Before I waste too much film on this idea. Why can't the beam ratio be changed by adjusting the "Z" axis of a space filter? When a space filter is perfectly set the stuff in the middle with no bullseye around is supposed to be as clean as the new driven snow. If it is off adjustmet a little, isn't the stuff in the Airy disk in the middle of the bullseye also clean but not as bright? Change the Z adjustment till the ratio is right. Block the rings around the bullseye.
MichaelH Sat May 12, 2007 12:17 pm #2
There's really no reason that I can think of why you couldn't do that. As you say, the beam at the center is clean and coherence is maintained. I don't believe you'll have very good control of the light ratio by using the spatial filter this way but there's generally a ton of leeway in ratios so it might work just fine.

One problem you will have is that the usable beam area will become smaller as you tweak the Z axis. You'll have to account for this in your setup most likely by moving the plate holder or mirrors.
BobH Sat May 12, 2007 1:13 pm #3
In my experience, a de-tuned spatial filter produces an unstable output. If the edges of the pinhole are in the beam enough to see diffraction rings, extremely small changes to beam direction (caused by beam pointing wander from the laser) will significantly change the power in the center of the pattern. As the distance between the laser and the spatial filter increases, this problem also increases. This is why I always use the largest pinhole I can, and keep my optics as clean as possible (to allow use of a larger pinhole).

What do you mean for "large"?
BobH Sun May 13, 2007 10:52 pm #5
"Large" is a relative term. If the laser provides a nice clean beam when expanded, no spatial filter is needed (same thing as a very large pinhole, say 100 microns). As components and devices are put into the beam before it's expanded, flaws and particles on their surfaces scatter the light, making the beam "dirty". Decreasing the pinhole diameter from (the ridiculously large) 100 microns will remove the scattered light from sources farthest upstream or smallest first.

As the diameter is further reduced, a point is reached where the beam is "clean enough" for use. Perhaps cleanest for a reference beam, maybe "dirtier" for subject lighting. Reducing the diameter farther will reach the point where calculations say the beam is "perfect". Decreasing pinhole diameter any more brings diffraction rings into the beam.

When the pinhole is at the "perfect" diameter, any wander of the beam rapidly degrades the quality of the beam. Such wander can be because of pointing drift, air currents, laser off the table or vibration of the laser. Using the largest pinhole available, usually 25 microns, makes aligning and working with the spatial filter waaaaay easier! You just need to keep the optics clean.
