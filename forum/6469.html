Hi all

HELP Please !!

Up to now I have pretty much guessed my exposure times when using my small HeNe laser. However, I now have a nice 100Mw 532nm C315m and the HeNe has gone into semi retirement.

Anyway, the other day I came across the exposure requirements of the BB520 plates i'm currently using. The exposure required is 150uJ to 300uJ. I wanted to 'experiment' with using an 8" collimating mirror in my simple single beam setup, so I figured a more scientific approach was necessary .

Would someone please be kind enough to check my calculation, as maths never was my strong point

I'm using a C315m Laser outputting 100Mw

The beam is expanded to the diameter of the mirror at its focal length in order to collimate the beam. Therefore i'm expanding the beam to cover an area of 50.272”^2 or 323.69cm^2

Next, i'm using 5" x 4" plates with an area of 20”^2 or 129.03cm^2

From the above (and for ease of calculation) it looks as though only approximately one third (ish) of the light is actually hitting the plate (using the cm measurements above).

So, my 100mw laser has the beam expanded to an area of 323.69cm^2 and only approximately 1/3 of the beam is being used.

100mw = 100,000uJ/second (I think)

I am only using approximately one third of this beam therefore 33333uJ/second

The plate requires (minimum) 150uJ^cm, therefore 150uJ (exposure required) x 129.03cm^2 (area of plate) = 19,354uJ

The energy reaching the plate is 33333uJ/second

Therefore 19,354 / 33333 = 0.58 of a second exposure required.

Now, bear in mind that I have never bothered to do this calculation before AND that maths really isn't my strong point, is the above correct? It seems an incredibly short exposure time .

If it isn't correct could you please point out where i've gone wrong.

Thanks

Jem
JohnFP Tue Oct 02, 2007 2:17 pm #2
The math looks correct.

I think this is a good starting point as due to the gaussian profile of the beam your more likely getting half the enengy not 1/3 at the plate which should make your exposure shorter, but you need to subtract about 10% of the total energy going into an optic to get what comes out, which will make your exposure longer. So again, I think this is a good starting point.

If your shutter will not respond to that short a time, increase the diameter of the beam with a higher power objective keeping the same fl from the collimator and recalculate so that you get a longer exposure.
