JohnFP Fri Jul 21, 2006 12:22 pm #15
Hello Chris and welcome.

The basic rainbow embossed foil hologram involved the following process and thusly why it is expensive overall but very inexpensive in a per unit eval.

1. Artwork needs to be designed. In your case simple litho masking would workeasily

2. A Master "Slit" tranmission hologram needs to be made of the artwork usually in Silver Halide and processed.

3. A Rainbow transmisison hologram needs to be made in Photoresist from the Master transmission hologram. Photo resist plates need to be made as I have never seen then available premade. Then the plate needs to be processed.

4. The photoresist hologram needs to be silver coated with a method like atomization.

5. Then the silverized resist needs to be place in a nickel electroforming tank and a nickel shim is made from the silverized resist hologram.

6. This shim is then pealed off the hologram and is remonted and again placed back into the electrforming nickel tank in which a shim is grow onto the first shim.

7. This second nickel shim is then pealed off the first shim an place in an embossing printer.

8. This printer presses the hologram into a plastic using heat and pressure which has a foil backing.

So you see, making an embossed hologram or even hiring someone to do it to make one will cost you thousands. But once the shims are made and put into the printer, hundreds of thousands can be pressed into the plastic in quite a short time and this reduces the cost of each embossed hologram to a couple of quarter or dimes.

Also, the nature of a rainbow hologram is if you move it such that you are past the red or blue ends, the hologram will not be visible.

BobH's idea is the best, simplest and most cost effective for a single one.

Do you want to do one or hundreds of thousands of the same artwork?

Does it have to be in foil or can it be in a piece of thicker plastic like a piece of film?

What do you mean by security? How will it be incorperated or attached to the product?

A simple hologram could be made in a piece of Silver halide film for a single unit. Although a little more costly not nearly as much as the embossing process.
