Colin Kaminski Fri Aug 29, 2008 4:13 am #7
I am hoping someone will pickup the chemistry side of the question as it is not my area of expertise.

> Colin Kaminski: The things that effect developer time for me have been time, temperature, dilution and exposure. Check all four. Perhaps the Wiki recipe has an error?

Development time = Longer allows for shorter exposures but creates larger silver crystals and can be noisy.

Temperature = Warmer shortens development time

Dilution = I don't know how dilution works but clearly more dilution make for longer development time. There may be other effects as well.

Exposure = Longer exposures shorten development times.

An example on how this can be used. if when Lon Moore shot bunch of daises. He kept the room cold to slow down the movement of the flowers. He then used a short exposure time and warm developer. All in an effort to limit the problems of the stability of something living.

So two things could have changed. If you evaporated liquid over time you may have concentrated the developer. More likely the developer is becoming oxidized.

Ed Wesly Wed Sep 03, 2008 2:26 pm #16
If you have undeveloped scraps of both batches of film, your old and suspected higher sensitivity newer, check to see if they are of similar cyan color. Agfa plates were all over the place with sensitivity from batch to batch. There was one batch of plates that looked much darker than others while they were being exposed on the object, and I did a d-log e sensitometric test on it this batch needed 1/3 the light to get to the same density as the previous batch. Placing them on a white card along with plates from other batches showed that they were definitely more cyan colored than the others, ostensibly because there was more sensitizing dye in them. I figured that it might have been a scenario worthy of the Three Stooges back at the plant, like "Did I forget to put the dye in? Maybe I'd better put some more in just in case I didn't!"

The holograms came out just as good as any others, and that 3 times sensitivity was a nice improvement! I wonder if there ever was any test program of how much dye to put in emulsions and still get decent single beam reflections?

Colin Kaminski Sat Sep 06, 2008 11:03 pm #18
There is a great range of exposures that will reveal an image with most systems. An over exposed hologram will be dimmer than a properly exposed hologram. So will an under exposed hologram! I have found no way to tell the difference with only the hologram in hand and no lab notes.

I have found PFG-01 to be very forgiving and simply by watching it go through the steps with no clock I can get very bright results. You start to get a feel for how dark you want it to go how quickly and how fast you want it to turn clear in the bleach. Exposure tests are a great thing to practice with.
