So I've got a Science and Mechanics A-4 photometer, and I'm trying to use it with the Slavich emulsions. (PFG-01, VRP-M, PFG-03M in this case.)

Now, I've found Frank DeFreitas' equation for exposure time:

[http://66.102.7.104/search?q=cache:S-YG ... agfa&hl=en](http://66.102.7.104/search?q=cache:S-YGj9wSorwJ:www.holoworld.com/holo/diode14.html+science+mechanics+agfa&hl=en)

> Single Beam Exposure Formula:Exposure times for single beam hologram using the Science and Mechanics A-3 PhotoMeter (emulsion facing object). You must take a reading of BOTH the reference beam AND the laser light reflected off the object. This will allow you to compensate your exposures for varying object reflectivity.Scale on Meter:(1) = 6(2) = 60(3) = 600(4) = 6000Equation:(Or + Rr) x .10 = nt= [(0r + Rr) - n] / (1), (2), (3) or (4) from above.Or = object reading.Rr = reference reading.n = total reading with substrate reflection adjustmentt = exposure time..10 = compensates for 10% (variable/avg.) initial reflection off of glass substrate (plate surface facing reference beam, emulsion facing object). This will be more or less depending on the reference angle and orientation of polarization. Put a glass plate into your plateholder. See the light reflected onto the wall? That light is never even getting to your emulsion. This value (which is sadly missing from many exposure formulas) makes up for that loss. If you're REAL careful with your reference angle and polarization, you can get down to nearly zero reflection (I'll save that for another page). You may wish to put a clear glass plate into your plate holder and take note of your intensity readings with and without the glass. Figure this number out as a percentage and use it as your precentage (ie: .01, .04, .10, .14, etc.) in the formula above to give you "n".\

However, I'm almost certain that the Slavich emulsions have different sensitivities than the Agfa series. Furthermore, for a combined object+reference reading of 50 on scale 2, the equation seems to give an exposure time of 0.75s -- which is a bit low to my thinking.

How do the Slavich plates (developed using JD-2 and JD-4 for the -03M) compare?

What power laser are you using? I'm using a laser diode from Integraf and PFG-03M and my exposure times are around 25 seconds. I did some simple tests exposing anywhere from 20 seconds to a full minute and it really didn't seem to make any difference. I'm probably even overexposing at 20 seconds, although I never seem to get the 99% darkness after developing (with JD-4).

Take a look at this:

[http://www.slavich.com/pfg03m.htm](http://www.slavich.com/pfg03m.htm)

And, use this formula to calculate exposure:

mJ/cm2   =   mW/cm2   x   seconds

With, say, a 5mW laser with only about 1/4 of the light hitting the plate (a plate 2.5" x 2.5", or 40cm.sq.) that is about 56 seconds for JD-2, if I'm calculating it right (1.75/((5/4)/40). And, about 6 seconds for JD-4 (jeez, maybe I am overexposing!).

And, here's another link worth looking at:

[http://www.holokits.com/a-teaching_holography.htm](http://www.holokits.com/a-teaching_holography.htm)

I think in any case, don't get too worried about getting the exposure exact. Start with something that works, then refine and worry about the details later. (That's my advice, anyway.)

-Phil
