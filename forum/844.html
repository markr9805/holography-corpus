In Lippmann photography, I was wondering if there was any discussion among the "old" authors regarding the hardening of the emulsion, after exposure, using a vigorous gelatin hardener, such as a chromium compound, alum, formaldehyde, etc. Or, did they rely mainly on pre-exposure hardening? (Lippmann, Neuhauss, Valenta, Lehmann, Ives, Cajal, etc.)

If anyone could answer this question, it would be Martin, being familiar with a vast number of Lippmann papers, and fluent in German, French, and English. By the way, is Martin's "Lippmann Collection" still available online somewhere?

The question of shrinkage/expansion prevention (and also the minimizing of emulsion deformation) in the final, dry emulsion would be, I assume, just as important in Lippmann photography as in color holography. Color holography in DCG seems more difficult if one is not able to pre-harden the emulsion prior to exposure (I have not been able to do this reliably using Jeff's MBDCG formula). Hardening after exposure seems much more practical.

In Bjelkhagen's new book, he does make it clear that the first processing bath for a Lippmann photograph should contain 1% formalin, when using soft PFG-03 (page 62). Likewise, for color holograms, he specifies the identical hardening bath after exposure (1% formalin plus potassium bromide and sodium carbonate), as shown on page 148.

Anything on the subject of hardening from "new" Lippmann photographers, including one of our own forum members whose recent Lippmann photos are remarkable, would of course be very welcome.

(Lippmann, Neuhauss, Valenta, Lehmann, Ives, Cajal, etc.)

The only explicit references on hardening I found were in Neuhauss' journal. He mentions both formaldehyde and "alum" (which seems to be chrome alum). He tried them in about any possible way: before coating, after exposure and prior to development, after development etc.

The reason why the other authors didn't mention gelatin hardening (that's at least what my quick overview suggests) may also be due to the overwhelming use of pyrogallol developers. Quite often these developers were prepared without sulfite, with an alkali (ammonia) only. This is known to provide very strong gelatin tanning.

> By the way, is Martin's "Lippmann Collection" still available online somewhere?

It's still there: [http://holoforum.org/data/lippmann/index.html](http://holoforum.org/data/lippmann/index.html)

> The question of shrinkage/expansion prevention (and also the minimizing of emulsion deformation) in the final, dry emulsion would be, I assume, just as important in Lippmann photography as in color holography. Color holography in DCG seems more difficult if one is not able to pre-harden the emulsion prior to exposure (I have not been able to do this reliably using Jeff's MBDCG formula). Hardening after exposure seems much more practical.

One key thing in my opinion is that the post-exposure gelatin hardening needs to not interfere with the photo hardening. That's to say, it should not compete with the chrome hardening - hence chrome alum etc. ought to be omitted. Formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde, glyoxal etc. seem OK though.

The MBDCG color shift may relate also to the removal of "material". TMG etc. being washed out during processing, may make it hard to preserve the original layer thickness. Have you ever considered post-swelling?
Joe Farina Fri Apr 25, 2014 7:24 am #3
Thank you Martin. That's interesting about the widespread use of alkaline pyrogallol developers in Lippmann photography.

I agree that the pre or post-hardening should not interfere with the photo-hardening, and I'm looking at hardeners which only work on the amino groups of the gelatin, as you suggested. Formaldehyde looks promising. Glutaraldehyde has a reputation for being more powerful, but I haven't tried it yet.

I too had always thought that the presence of TMG would cause some shrinkage, as it's washed out. Jeff certainly stated that in his papers. The funny thing is, I did some tests with two emulsion batches which were identical, one having somewhat more TMG than the other. I was getting much better color rendition with the emulsion with more TMG, plus it was considerably faster. Of course, I can't reach any definite conclusion, due to the large number of processing variables involved. But better color rendition suggests that the final layer thickness was reasonably close to the recording thickness. One thing which seems quite important (apart from the hardening issue) is the manner in which the plate is dried coming out of the 100% IPA. The IPA needs to be "flung" off the plate as quickly as possible. As this point, I prefer spinning with hand drill, with suction cups holding the reverse side of the glass plate.

I am keeping the post-swelling idea in mind, and will re-visit this possibility, if needed. (The old thread involving citric/succinic acid is very much in mind.) However, R6G+MBDCG (w/TMG) is quite a bit different from other recording materials (such as regular DCG or silver). So the results obtained in that thread may be difficult to apply. A while back, I tried a rough test with succinic acid in this application, and it seemed to basically destroy the layer.
