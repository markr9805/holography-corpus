> Colin Kaminski: Now the problem is the Micheal uses 632nm to make masters. The will replay with distortion on a AR+ laser. .........

Talking of wavelength distortion you might all be amused to hear of a hologram commission that a company I was involved with got from a TV manufacturer for a special promotion showing a new TV model. It was to be set up in a hotel for the press and folks in the retail trade. It was to be a large (1 metre square) hologram which was to be a deep image transmission type only viewable by laser.

This was almost 20 years ago (years before big LC displays) and the image was to specially feature the new TV's extra wide cathode ray tube and we had to show the set with its cover removed and the electronics and whole length of the tube visible to demonstrate its short length.

We duly made the hologram with all the painful rigmarole of getting the lighting right from a specially powerful

Krypton laser + etalon giving about 800mw of 647nm .

We found the finished result on the one meter plate with about 2 meters of depth really breathtaking.

However when it came to displaying the hologram later for the customer, for some reason I forget now, the only powerful laser available for the job was a 1 watt Argon giving a 514nm. spread beam. This was duly all set up in haste in the hotel with our thoughts only concentrating on getting the right reference angle so that the guests could see this magnificent bit of gee-wizardry when they walked in.

But then….. Oh dear! something didn’t look right at all.

Instead of the “new look” extra wide cathode ray tube with a specially short squat tube body what we all actually saw was a not- at-all-extra wide screen with a particularly long body . …….

No, the secret was never revealed why it did the opposite of what it was intended to show.

John FP wrote:

> Exactly what type of distortion can one expect from using a different color laser? Vignetting?What kind of distortions can one expect from using a diverging beam for the master and a diverging beam for illumination of the master to make a copy? Pincushion?This may be a good section for the Wiki.

It sort of is in the thing I started. You can see that the different wavelengths will come out at different angles, and will be focussing at different distances from the holoplate. My explication is looking at the virtual image, but it would be a sort of mirror image on the real side.

The magnification equations will tell how the size of the object will vary from front to back, but I am still working on that. A cube will acquire a tapered look.

The equations for the typical aberrations one studies in lenses applied to holograms are covered in a Benton paper in one of the LFC proceedings. Plenty others from Benton's MIT group are scattered here and there in SPIE Proceedings. These equations are real messy, more in the realm of the Forum Wrecker. (If he really does understand alll that!)

Tell us what the object looks like when you perform the transfer. I suspect the image will be smaller if you are going from red master to green replay.

The image will be complete, no vignetting. But if the image "rolls" as you move from side to side it would be spherical aberration; if flat planes in the object space become curved like a bowl then it's curvature of field; if straight lines in a grid become convex, then it's barrel distortion; turn concave then it's pincushion. If it seems horizontal lines focus to a different position than vertical it's astigmatism.

If anyone would like a 5 meg pdf on the aberations of lenses that I use as part of my optics classes please contact me via this forum.
