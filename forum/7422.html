See about using an established gelatin, that other DCG folks have tried (I am still willing to send you some Knox if you would like)

Consider light fixing as one of the forum members (Joe) uses. I believe he uses a 100 watt bulb although not sure of the time. Although I think it is great that you are try combinations of chemicals, I would recommend getting a good baseline established first. As I see it either you gelatin is too soft or your fixing is not adequate. By using a different fixing method, you might be able to islolate the problem. As all DCG'ers know, variables are abundant and interacting and is easy to achieve good results one day then crap the next.

Consider using a dull coin on the corner of your shots. Place the plate right on top of the coin. After processing, see what kind of level of "burn in" you have. If the coin image is deeply etched on to the plate, then you are most likely over exposed, if it is very dim, then you may be under exposed.

> what was different?If it is age of the film then of coarse your gel was too soft which makes sense right?I'm not a gelatin expert maybe John can comment on this.Can you explain your process from when you place the mold on top of the emulsion to how it drys?It could be a drying process issue.

I am not sure about the difference. Day before I exposed 10 and 25 minutes and last the time was 20 and 40 minutes. So the age affects the milkyness - probably the overtall hardening.

I use the follows mold process: wash the mold glass (common mirror) with WC cleaner, rinse it well, treat it with rain x (similar product which I get here), polish it well with cloth. Then I put the plates and the mirror into the hot air oven cca 50°C. After that I place the mirror on leveled surface, pour with gelatine and place the plate on in. I take in this step just one plate from the oven to get same temp. after puting 4 plates i leave it cca 30 min to thicken it and then i put it to the fridge for cca 1 hour. After separating the sandwich I place it oliquely across the wall. Usually I use the fan to dry it quickly to prevent re-melting the gelatine in this hot days.
