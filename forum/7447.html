Glenn Sun Jul 27, 2008 1:55 am #1
As you already know, 360nM is a very popular region of the UV spectrum. Is there a way I can make an extremely efficient HOE that diffracts light at this frequency? Gelatin like absorbs the UV, making the task difficult.
BobH Sun Jul 27, 2008 3:05 pm #2
I use the 364nm line from an Argon laser all the time. I've used it for photoresist and Slavich materials, PFG-03C I think. I got like 65% DE with the latter, on a single long weekend try. I'm certain it would be higher with more tweaking. DCG should love it.

The rehalogenated Slavich material doesn't like the UV for reconstruction. I tried KI in the bleach and as a final bath to prevent printout, but that absorbs ALL the UV. Perhaps SHSG processing, or colloidal development will allow its use at that wavelength. I'd also try Polygramma's photopolymer. They have one for that wavelength.

I'm also very interested in getting a small (25x25mm) high DE HOE for use at 364nm. Perhaps we can collaborate on some tests! I have 2W of single frequency 364nm on the table.
Sergio Sun Jul 27, 2008 4:37 pm #3
Yes, the 365nm is a 'classical' one for photopolymer UV curing including the cationic line, the Polygrama SM-365TR may work well, the characterization is [here](http://www.mediafire.com/?l1nmxonzdzl). Earlier PP versions were experimental but now the system works better, the series SM-365TR S may use the same WL for an extended aditional post cure for adhesion to glass and increase the hologram durability. No problem in obtain a high diffraction efficiency indeed.
