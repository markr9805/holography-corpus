Joe Farina Sun Apr 22, 2012 6:31 am #1
This is a fixture I made to hold a 10mm LED. It's lighting a cylinder seal impression, but should work well for 4 X 5 holograms also. The cable (neck) is somewhat self-supporting, it consists of a twisted pair of insulated 22 gauge (solid) copper wires, covered by a white braided (plastic) sleeve. The LED is enclosed by a brass tube painted white, and some white-tinted epoxy holds it together. The power is provided by a standard AC/DC wall-plug transformer. The warm-white 10mm LED is rated for 300mA, and is being run at 250mA. I have always assumed (?) that these 10mm LEDs do not require any special heat-sinking at rated current (300mA in this case) but they do get quite hot. I guess they are designed to run at a fairly high temperature. I'm doing day-long tests to see if there is any loss in power due to heat dissipation problems, after several days it's ok.
Dinesh Sun Apr 22, 2012 7:52 am #2
If you're running it off a transformer, presumably it's running at 12V and 1/5th A, so, yes, it would get quite hot. There should be no loss due to heating, though. The primary mechanism for heat loss in an incandescent is the vapourisation of the metal of the filament, which then coats the inside of the bulb; in this case, there's no filament. However, the heat generated will affect nearby items. I do notice that the subject you're illuminating seems to be about three or so feet from the floor. An object such as the one you're illuminating (which I find more interesting than the lamp, actually!) is visible from any point in the room, while a hologram is only visible from a point directly in front of it. So, to illuminate a hologram, the hologram has to be at eye level and the source - the led - has to be above the hologram; roughly 1.7 units up for every unit away if the ref was at Brewsters.

Bruno Mon Jun 18, 2012 2:48 am #8
LED efficiency drops off markedly with temperature. For today's LED's a rule of thumb is that an LED fixture ends up being approximately the same size as the equivalent incandescent fixture. LEDs put out less heat, but they have to run at a lower temperature so you can't translate the lower heat output into smaller fixtures.
