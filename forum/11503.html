jrburns47 Sat Sep 26, 2026 10:28 am #2
I’m no expert with reflection holograms or HX200.

It looks like you have good equipment.

Some suggestions:

1- do a Michelson Interferometer to make sure laser can create an interference pattern since the laser may not be linearly polarized in which case the laser is no good for making a hologram. It’s also possible that your HeNe doesn’t have enough power to initiate the HX200 but, since you could see the clamps holding the film, it’s most likely a movement problem.

2- put your object on a solid matte white surface (a solid piece of wood, metal or glass painted with matte white paint - this will reflect more light to the HX200 and shorten the exposure - let the paint dry for 24 hours before using the now white plate) flat on the optical bench (so the plate is horizontal instead of vertical) lay the HX200 plate right on the object, and bring your diverged beam from above at about a 35 degree angle from the perpendicular to the hologram plate). Let the setup settle for 20 minutes before making the exposure. Instead of laying the plate right in the object, you can preferably use three posts secured to the optical bench around the object to lay it on the posts just over the object (3 point stabilization). You should be able to see the image develop as it’s being exposed.

3- the bubbles are probably from dust and poor lamination to the glass substrate. I would be more concerned with 1 & 2 above and then, after getting a good exposure, learn how to avoid the bubbles.

4- if it’s a matter of laser power, try using a smaller glass microscope slide as a substrate for the film.

5- suggest that you check out [https://discord.gg/av7YZgGBU](https://discord.gg/av7YZgGBU) for a more active group of holographers who can make additional suggestions. However, based on your equipment, you’re way ahead of many of the beginners seeking assistance there. Good Luck.
