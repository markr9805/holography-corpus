What is the best steel to use for tables and where is a good source?

I had a local source till recently when the shop owner retired. What I need is 16 ga or thicker steel for the top and bottom plates of a larger hexcan table I'm planning to make. I'd like to use stainless, but not all stainless is magnetic. Some of you built honeycomb tables - what type steel did you use?
Tony Thu Jul 08, 2004 2:35 pm #2
I used plain sheet steel, not stainless. I keep it from rusting by wiping it with oil, then wiping off the excess. Cold rolled steel will have the smoothest finish, but may be more expensive. you can buy steel and other useful materials at:

[http://www.mcmaster.com/](http://www.mcmaster.com/)
Bruce Thu Jul 08, 2004 4:11 pm #3
I am using hot-rolled carbon steel sheet, I have absolutely no complaints at all.

Stainless is expensive and magnetic stainless is somewhat hard to find.

I live about 15 miles from the ocean with humidities ranging from 30 to 80 with averages around 60, no rust problems at all ( my table is in my garage).

Save your money for something else.

Bruce

MichaelH Thu Jul 08, 2004 6:14 pm #6
Hi John. Andres and I used 16ga cold rolled for our hexcel tables. I've painted the top to prevent rusting (I was seeing small patches).
Bruce Fri Jul 09, 2004 3:36 am #7
I am using 11 guage which is essentially 1/8 " . My steel is glued to a concrete slab and provides a rigid plate combination for a very heavy mirror and mount. I paid about $120. for a piece about 1x2 meters in size and it was shear-cut, not flame cut so edges are smooth.

Steel thickness should be chosen with the heaviest optic in mind so that it will not appreciably bend your table top. Do not use steel as a mass source as it is too expensive for that use, concrete is a much better and cheaper material.

B.
Colin Kaminski Fri Jul 09, 2004 4:06 am #8
I to am using mild steel. I painted it ultra-flat black. I chose plasma-cut to get the flatest piece of steel I could get. Since the plasma cutter was automated it left a very clean edge. I did have to grind a little slag of the ends but it only took about 5 minutes with a hand grinder.
