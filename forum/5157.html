Aligning all of these components is the tricky part and no book can teach you that. You will spend 100s of frustration hours getting it all to work. I am not trying to discourage this dream but be prepared for a long haul. If you finish you will be one of the few.

I was once talking to Ron and he said it would cost him about $100K to replace his laser in parts alone.

JohnFP Fri Nov 04, 2005 7:32 am #7
I used to build puled lasers for a company. They were Nd YAG's, not frequency doubled and no etalon. They were not used for holography. There were many variations and intra cavity optics used for different output beam properties desired.

When you go get around to building the cavity, just use the rod/lamp, the rear and front mirrors. Get it to lase before adding any intra cavity optics. Get the parallelism close by using another laser brought in from the partially reflective output mirror (when the pulsed laser is not running). Then add the intra cavity optics one by one.There is a component called a "Collimator" that has a light source, a few mirrors and an eyepiece used at the output end specifically designed for alligning optics nearly perfect. I will research and see if I can find that unit. Then fire up the laser and run it at its near max pulse rate to walk in the mirrors the rest of the way. Drop me an e-mail and I will tell you how to "see" the output very easily in the infrared and help you with any other tips I may know.

JohnFP Fri Nov 04, 2005 12:07 pm #10
Here is how I alligned the pulsed lasers. I used a HeNe and took a piece of flash card (card stock) and pushed a very sharp needle in it. Just enougth to let a squeek of light through. I then mounted this on the rail outside the output mirror. The laser should be on a gimble (x-y) mount also so that you can not only get all optic parallel but you want the beam to propigate down the center of the crystal and parallel to the rail. As you can see you will get back reflections off the output mirror, each face of the crystal and the rear mirror. My pump cavity (crystal/lamp) was also on a gimble. Align the crystal first, then the rear and front mirror. You can easily see which reflection spot (beam) is which by moving that optic independently. As you get close to parallelism, you will start to see more spots as the reflections start coming off of muliple optics (from back mirrot to card, from back mirror to crystal face to back mirror to card...etc.) but these will be dimmer. One you get all the dots to overlap you will be pretty close. Then use the walk in proceedure to find lasing.
