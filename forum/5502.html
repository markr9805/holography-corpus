I noticed some small (8"x8" and 6"x12") aluminum breadboards thorlabs had for around $100. Are these appropriate for making small transmission holograms? It seems to be a reasonable price to prevent some grief.

I made my first reflection holograms with a diode laser (Integraf kit) a few days ago. Why is it that the cheesiest one is the one to turn out the best? Orcs, dwarfs, Capt. America and a surreal Eeyore head all had issues, but Mickey Mouse was bright and clear. Sheesh. Though I think the orc was a lost cause from the start. It was my first try but I'm thinking green just isn't going to show up well is it?

I need to move on to transmission holos next. Are those breadboards meant for this type of stuff or am I looking at the wrong things?

What should I use for a plate holder (with or without the breadboard)? The ones I was looking at look like they would block a fair amount of the glass, but I guess it has to hold on to the plate somehow.

I'm specifically interested in making the 2.5" holograms. I'm not concerned if these things won't scale up easily.

Thanks,

Dave
Colin Kaminski Tue Apr 18, 2006 9:30 pm #2
Save your money on the breadboard. You can work just as well with a 12x12" paver.

The simplest filmholder is to make one out of 1/8"hardboard. Make sure to triangulate well and it will be stiff enough. I need to get my drafting software working again.
Thesrot Wed Apr 19, 2006 9:25 am #3
Ok thanks, I'll pick up a paver. I just thought the breadboard might allow me to get a way with something smaller and lighter and save some money and fuss on bases for some optics.

I'm actually using the 2.5" plates rather than film but I'm figuring the plate should be held pretty rigidly by something, shouldn't it?

Dave
Colin Kaminski Wed Apr 19, 2006 1:14 pm #4
With some quick set concrete, paper cups and 1/2" wooden dowels you can hot melt optics onto some simple bases. Make sure the dowels are only a few inches long.
