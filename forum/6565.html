Hello everyone... I've stumbled upon this forum in my frustration about my inability to create a hologram. I've purchased the standard integraf kit that contains PFG-03M plates, 5 mW diode laser with the cap removed and the JD4 processing chemicals.

Before I continue, I'll say that every hologram I develop is blank, whether its a transmission or reflection hologram.

Now, I've tried two different isolation systems. The first one consisted of a box of pea gravel rested on some foam. The second consisted of the good old box of sand rested on a half inflated innertube. The laser is attached to a sturdy clamp which is placed into the sand. I've also tried burying a paper towel tube in the sand so that it sticks out, filling it with more sand, and placing the laser in there for added stability. The room I'm working in is completely dark, and rather noise free(though it is in a building next to a busy road). I feel 99 percent certain that the problem is not caused by vibrations.

Since I'm concerned mainly with transmission holograms, I'll talk about my struggles with those. I've tried several different configurations. Mainly, I've worked with the configurations of the laser shining flat along the surface with the plate at an angle of 45 degrees with respect to the laser, and the object right next to the plate. The other configuration I've tried had the plate standing vertically with the object in front of it, and the laser shining 45 degrees down on it. The object used was a white bottle cap.

I used varying exposure times ranging from 10 seconds to 3 minutes. As I develop the plates they turn black, and turn clear, as expected. However, I notice that even if I do not expose a plate to light, it turns dark in the developer solution. Is this supposed to happen? Additionally, what causes plates to turn out foggy(this happens sometimes).

And I'm rather certain I'm viewing them correctly, so don't get into that.

I've tried shining the concentrated laser beam on a small patch of the plate and then developing it to see what happens... a bluish spot was observed there, which leads me to believe the plates are fine.

I was thinking about the polarization of the light. The light from the laser is vertically polarized. So that means the reference beam is vertically polarized. However, the wave front from the object is elliptically polarized. One thought I had is that the interference bands created on the plate are negligible compared to the light polarized in other directions. To test this idea out, I placed a polarizer vertically in front of the plate before exposure, and of course, nothing came out again.

I'm beginning to wonder if something is wrong with my kit, or if theres something I'm just completely missing.

Additionally, I've warmed up the laser for 5 minutes, and have it at a 30 cm distance from the plate and object.

Thank you in advance for any assistance.

> even if I do not expose a plate to light, it turns dark in the developer solution

How fast did an unexposed plate turn black in the developer?

> I've tried shining the concentrated laser beam on a small patch of the plate and then developing it to see what happens... a bluish spot was observed there, which leads me to believe the plates are fine.

Was the spot bluish when looking at the reflection of it or when you looked through the spot. If the spot looks bluish when looked through it and red when you look at the reflection, you have made a reflection grating at that spot. The reference beam being the beam from the laser and the object beam being light reflected from the back of the plate.

> I was thinking about the polarization of the light. The light from the laser is vertically polarized. So that means the reference beam is vertically polarized. However, the wave front from the object is elliptically polarized. One thought I had is that the interference bands created on the plate are negligible compared to the light polarized in other directions. To test this idea out, I placed a polarizer vertically in front of the plate before exposure, and of course, nothing came out again.

The light that comes back from an object is usually not polarized at all, unless you use shiny objects. But it does not really matter. Even with shiny objects you should see a hologram.

> As I develop the plates they turn black, and turn clear, as expected. However, I notice that even if I do not expose a plate to light, it turns dark in the developer solution. Is this supposed to happen? Additionally, what causes plates to turn out foggy(this happens sometimes).

I presume that you mean that they turned clear in the bleach bath, but not after turning black in the developer bath (while still in the developer bath).

If the unexposed plate turns black in the developer bath at the same speed as an exposed plate, it means that your plates are fogged. Either they are too old (which is a problem with PFG-03 plates when kept unrefrigerated) or they have been somehow exposed to light.

> Additionally, what causes plates to turn out foggy(this happens sometimes).

With "foggy" do you mean a hazy milky colour after the bleach bath? If so, that means that the plate has been exposed too long.

LarryD Fri Nov 30, 2007 4:56 pm #9
> pizmar2: And I'm rather certain I'm viewing them correctly, so don't get into that.

Keep in mind that if you are shooting your transmission holo with the diverging beam(from the lens-less laser-diode, and then reconstructing the real image with the same diverging beam, the reconstructed image will be HUGE and possibly not at the original image plane compared to the original object. You need to reconstruct with the "conjugate" or in this case, use a converging beam to get the image to replay at it's original size and location. I think this is why most people use a collimated reference beam, since the conjugate beam is still a collimated beam, thus the same beam can be used to shoot and recontruct.
