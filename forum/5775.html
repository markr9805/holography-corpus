> Does the laser have to be equipped with this ability from the beginning?In my opinion, yes. Hans has reported excellent results with his design, which is on the Wiki. (And I used a good controller, etc., from Thorlabs.) TEC-cooled laser diodes require a fair amount of work and expense. When it doesn't work out, it's a real disappointment.The biggest problem with homemade TEC lasers is that the temperature sensor needs a good thermal contact with the part of wich the temperature needs to be sensed. Just sticking the sensor against a part of the laser is not sufficient because too much lag will exist between the actual temperature and the measured temperature. Also, it is good to place the sensor very close to the Peltier element. Again to reduce lag (here between commanded temperature and sensed tempere). If these two conditions are not met, the actual laser temperature will swing slightly around the commanded temperature. This causes mode hops and wavelength drifts. In my sensor I drilled a little hole in the base plate, close to the Peltier and placed the sensor in that hole, and filled the hole up with thermally conducting epoxy glue.After you are able to keep the temperature of the laser constant, it is necessary to find the temperature at which the diode does not mode hop. This can be done by inspecting the light when it is reflected from a DVD.A good and stable temperature always improves the quality of the laser light. And I believe it is essential, especially for the beginning holographer, because one (very BIG) variable is eliminated from the holography laboratorium.If you follow my directions on the wikihttp://www.holographyforum.org/HoloWiki ... aser_Diode, you will have no problems at all to make a good diode laser.Does the laser have to be equipped with this ability from the beginning?

Not to make a hologram. But if you want to be able to analyze why some holograms are dim, striped, vague, or have little depth, the answer is YES. Temperature stability is essential for a diode laser. Also, while a diode may not be mode hopping before you start your exposure, it might start to do this during your exposure and an expensive plate is wasted. Somehow diodes love to mode hop. When they start, they never stop until you change the current or temperature.

Hans Mon Sep 25, 2006 5:56 pm #24
I have found that the best test is still to make a hologram. But to make sure that you are in a region where you suspect no mode hopping, it is wise to do the DVD test first: Adjust the power until you see a mode hop, then adjust the power further until you see the next mode hop. Then put the power in-between those two spots. Interestingly, at some power/temperature settings a diode is multimode, even in-between the mode hopping powers. But if you then choose the next higher (or lower) region between mode hopping power levels, most often at that power setting the diode does not mode hop I have found. (I use a HL6512MG 50mW diode).

Joe Farina Wed Sep 27, 2006 11:35 am #30
Not having seen the Integraf module, I don't know what it's like. I'guessing it's a diode mounted in a typical cylindrical housing with a collimating lens.

For use with TE cooling, it would be probably be preferable to work with the diode itself (i.e., removed from the housing), as Hans and Colin suggested. If you tried to use the existing housing as part of a TE cooled system, there would be more thermal resistance between the TE cooler and diode (i.e., the imperfect contact between the exterior of the housing and the metal cooling plate which is attached to the TEC). This gap could be filled by thermal epoxy, but even though this material is designed to transfer heat, it does so quite poorly.

So if you were working with the bare diode, the thermal transition might be like this: TEC, thermal epoxy, cold plate, thermal grease, diode.

If you tried to use the existing housing/diode, it might be: TEC, thermal epoxy, cold plate, thermal epoxy, diode housing, thermal grease, diode.

The housing would just add more complexity, and this would hold true for the thermistor placement as well.
