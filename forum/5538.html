JohnFP Wed May 10, 2006 9:49 am #3
If the end result is simply to be able to view the rainbow hologram as a reflection then that is quite simple. It is how they do it on most rainbow embossed hologram.

Make the H2 a rainbow transmission hologram. Then to display it, put a mirror behind it. The light goes through the hologram, reflects off the mirror and illuminates it from the back. Index matched to the back, there would be no additional reflections off a rear surface mirror. A front surface would not have this problem and it may also be possible to find a foil type reflective sheeting that can be attached to the back.

This led me to think about the following idea:

Suppose we make a reflection hologram of a mirror with the reference beam 45 degrees from above and the mirror angled so that the object beam comes from behind, also 45 degrees from above.

Now we make a transmission hologram with the reference beam 45 degrees from above. Would it be possible to view this hologram in white light if we place the reflection hologram of the mirror behind it? Or would the light be bent too much during it's first pass through the transmission hologram towards the reflection hologram behind it?
