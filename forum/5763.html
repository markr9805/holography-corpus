Being very new to Holography I can give a simple explanation of embossed holograms.

When a hologram is recorded on a glass plate coated with photoresist the image is an interference pattern recorded in the photoresist. To make a copy of this pattern the photoresist is developed, then coated with Silver. The Silver coated plates are then mounted on a panel or held in some sort of a jig that can be placed in an electroforming tank and plated with Nickel. When this is separated from the glass surface the Silver master can be used to produce Nickel replicas of the original image that can then be cut into a shim used on an embossing machine. Holograms are usually embossed onto a metallized film such as an aluminized polyester that will reflect light. The holograms produced are reflected light holograms rather than transmissin holograms. The embossing process allows for holograms to be mass produced at a reasonable cost.

There's more to be learned here...I'm sure there are others much more knowledgable than me to provide that learning.
