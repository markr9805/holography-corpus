Many moons ago when I was first doing holography we made excellent use of hot glue to hold things in place. Back then (mid 80's) there were only maybe two types of hot glue sticks available: ones that set up hard and ones that stayed kinda flexible. Of course the former were the ones we used.

Is anybody still making use of hot glue? The only draw back I ever ran into, other than the occasional burned finger, was that I have a few holograms that when looked at from a certain angle show a fine hot glue thread running across the image. They were there when the holo was shot but because of the vagaries of lighting could only be seen from a certain angle.

Martin Tue Jan 23, 2007 7:37 am #5
> ErichRose: Is anybody still making use of hot glue?

We stopped using it when we noticed that hot glue virtually never stabilizes. It's creeping all the time.

Fortunately we then switched to cyanacrylate (“super glue”) adhesives, which on curing become similar to glass. By the way, their action can be greatly accelerated by using one of those catalyzing/activator sprays. So curing takes a few seconds only. Optical components or holographic objects treated with cyanacrylates, can be used immediately (for a holographic recording).

JohnFP Tue Jan 23, 2007 12:03 pm #7
I would like to point out is that I use the hot glue as more of a dampener and to keep things in place, not to secure things. I alwasy let gravity do 99.9% of the work. Let me explain.

Let's say I want to mount an object to my table, I place the object in position, then use single edge razor blades to insure there is no rocking and the object is stable. This is a learned techniqe, to actually feel when the object is solid to the table. I then hot glue the object edge to the table right over the razor blade. I don't hot glue the object to the table. This was most important when I used the static, gravity held optic bases. They were round (see my new lab photos on my web site, although now it is old) and thusly washers, coins and razor blades had to be used to insure they were solidly sitting on the table. Again I would hot glue the base to the table right over the shim (washer, coin or razor blade) point.

This feely thingy is applicable to everything on the table. The spatial filter allignment screws, the object, the plate in the plate holder (most important) etc. I can feel when entire plate has even pressure in its mount. As a final check I do the twank tap. I tap the plate at the corners and center. If it twings it is not right. If it twanks it is good.

I too do what Dave shows below. Anywere I can I connect optics, bases, plateholder to one another with rods and bars using gravity to insure they are stable, then use hot glue to keep them in place.

[http://www.stewmac.com/shop/Glues,_adhe ... Glues.html](http://www.stewmac.com/shop/Glues,_adhesives/Stewart-MacDonald_Super_Glues/Stewart-MacDonald_Super_Glues.html)

I have also used a wide range of Loctite products. Accellerators will help harden many carazy glues. You risk turning the glue white if you try to accellerate too quickly.

A general rule of thumb, thin glue - hard setting. Thick glue - softer setting. Black Glue very soft setting. Cyanoacrylites harden by the inclusion of water so things like your fingers stick together very quickly but two pieces of plexyglass will harden more slowly.
