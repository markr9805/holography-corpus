Hi Michael, What's this "Bump" thing you are putting in these threads?

Anyway to answer your question, I was trying to make the formulation as user friendly as possible by using local -drugstore- purchasable Alum

instead of chemical company purchasable chrome alum.

(Yes indeed nothing can be done about the dreaded dichromate of course). ---Chrome Alum is more stable for photographic use with highly alkaline developers which make aluminum alum break down but for DCG

this is not relevant.).

If your gelatin is really high bloom strength (~300) and you keep the dichromate solution around 5C then there will be no lift-off from the glass and you can avoid using any alum.

I had to put it in because people may have ordinary culinary gelatin and I tried to keep things to work generally. (It would otherwise be better not to use it because you want all your cross-links of this type to be all laser light induced .

On Dan's point about definition of chem names it appears that "Alum" in the USA is aluminum sulfate and "Alum" in Europe it is the double salt

potassium aluminium sulphate. The former is more soluble and is the one which John FP helped me to decide is best to use instead of European "alum".

Jeff
