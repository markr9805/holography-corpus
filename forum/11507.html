Hello holographers from this forum!

Like many people who discovered ultra-niche hobbies, after watching few YT videos like the one from [3B1B](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EmKQsSDlaa4), and after discovering that ChatGPT can do any optical maths homework , I decided to try holography with almost zero background in optics, thanks to all the infos from this forum.

My setup consists of a 70mW PL530 green laser (the eBay one advertised for holography...), a plano-convex and plano-concave lens to expand the beam to approx 5mm (arbitrary chosen because of [this](https://www.holographyforum.org/forum/viewtopic.php?p=73307#p73307)), a 50/50 non-pol beamsplitter, a pair of FL10mm concave mirrors from Edmund for final beam expansion, Bayfol HX200, the cheapest silicon rolling pin I could buy, cheap 2mm glass plates, a small steel bench vice to lock the glass vertically and cover a bit the side of plate (because adhesive tape and plastic wasn't recommended in this forum), and for curing 3 modules in parallel of 9x Luminus SST-10 365nm LEDs from a german website (1 module is more than enough in fact). The table is a medium-density fiberboard 12080cm, on 4 Sorbothane 30D 4x4x2cm pads from eBay, where I drilled and put 6mm metal inserts to lock the mounting bases/posts, like a real optical table.

After learning how to do good-enough laser collimations, a DIY Michelson interferometer helped me to see if the fringes are stable. I tested various laser exposure times (30sec, 1min, 2min), various curing times (1min to 15min). For curing, process seems complete in a bit less than 1 min or so (@800mA), so this is now my reference for my UV lamp. With such a setup, I was able to see my first holograms almost at 1st attempt, using a simple white flashlight for reconstruction. I was impressed myself I could achieve that.
