Here's the quote from Sam's that has raised my concern.

> Getter: Make sure it doesn't have the "white cloud of death" appearance. The getter spot on these tubes is at the end of the cathode bulb where the negative lead attaches. The getter electrode - one or more round or rectangular rings - should be visible opposite the getter spot. A good getter will have a dark or silver metallic appearance. If it's milky white, brown, or red, then the tube is probably close to dead if not totally dead. A clear getter may or may not be bad - it could either mean the getter was never fired, or that a material was used that is clear rather than metallic, or that becomes clear when it combines with those bad gas molecules. Note that there should be NO metallic deposits anywhere else in the tube, particularly on the glass opposite the other end of the cathode can. That would be an indication of sputtering and an end-of-life tube.

It's been misbehaving a bit lately in that the output power fluctuates +-0.03mW as I sit and watch the reading on the power meter.

Am I likely to need a new laser soon?

BobH Wed Jul 26, 2006 4:02 am #5
It'll be close to death when it takes longer to start. I'd clean the brewster windows and re-align everything. Be very carefull around the anode pin which is very brittle.

The getter area is OK as long as there is any metal left. You may also be able to re-fire it (if I remember correctly). A blown getter is all powdery.

Treat it gently, though, because you may be very surprised at how long it lasts!
