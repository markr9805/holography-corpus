Hi everyone!

I'm new here and new to the entire holography field. I just finished the ISH holography course, and I’m waiting for my first films and plates to arrive in the mail so I can start my first experiments. I’m very excited and happy to have discovered holography.

Anyway, I have a dream project involving holography that I’d like to bring to life for an exhibition in April 2025. I plan to dedicate the next seven months to working solely on holograms, and I’ll try to learn and practice as much as I can every day.

My dream is to create a dollhouse where the rooms appear to have been destroyed by the aftermath of war, representing an abandoned house. Torn wallpapers, missing furniture, broken items ect. I would glue holograms onto the open wall of the dollhouse, and they would show a memory of what the home was like before the war started: cute little furniture, the warmth of a home, simple set ups of ordinary rooms. I’ve been trying to think of the most sensible way to bring my idea to life. Should I use transmission holograms (for individual display for each room with a diode laser from the back of the dollhouse), or reflection holograms (which would be much harder to display, as the light for each room would spread into other rooms, since they are very close to each other). I could also buy hardwood boxes and make separate rooms and display them further away from each other, room by room.

Is my idea completely doomed, or could it work? I was thinking of exposing the holograms first in a small open side box with walls painted black, the same size as the dollhouse room, hot gluing all the objects on the surface and trying to mimic the order of objects as they would be in the dollhouse, but I’m also hesitating about whether the dollhouse itself with tapestry walls could serve as a good display background for transmission holograms.

Any tips, tricks or advice? I just want to know if this could even work or be possible before buying tons of dollhouse equipment and then learn that it will not work in any way.
jrburns47 Sun Sep 22, 2024 6:36 pm #2
Interesting idea. Sounds like a lot of work both in terms of model making and arranging the lighting. A lot depends on your already existing skill set(s) and/or available local help from others. Suggest start with one very small uncomplicated and very stable object/scene in laser light and, when you get that to work, try a reflection. Good luck.
