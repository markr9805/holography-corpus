jeff-blyth Fri May 20, 2005 7:33 pm #18
> andy75180: Yes I always have the emulsion facing the object. And of course this means the the double-sided film does as well.

You must NOT let double-sided sticky POLYESTER-based film interpose itself between your emulsion and the object. --Not only do you put the returning beam polarization out of line with your ref beam but you get a double dose of it because it passes through twice when you make Denisyuk holos. So if this means having to put the glass sheet between your emulsion and object , then so be it.

It is worth perhaps mentioning my 25-years- ago experiences with Agfa's polyester base film used on their 8E75 HD product.

Some boxes were trouble free and then other boxes of same batch no. were causing real patterning problems in finished holograms from polarization twisting in a weird manner when exposing to laser light.

As I understood the position before Agfa switched over to triacetate base , the variability on polyester base was due to the following .

The manufactures of the 1 metre wide rolls of polyester film used heat and rollers etc. which put the polymer under a strain which was unfortunately imprinted into that material because it was potentially "anisotropic" which means its molecular structure added a bias to the light passing through it depending on

which direction the light's polarization (ie. its vibrating electric field direction) was in. The effect was however very variable across the width of the roller. So if your box of film had been cut from a strip down the middle of the roll that box was could be fairly trouble free depending on your set-up but if it was cut from sections along the outer edge it was likely to very troublesome, particularly if you were having to make pseudoscopic masters where you needed to reverse the film and make copies where the base material had to be between master and copy. It's a fine Murphy's law example that polyester is so excellent in all other respects compared to other polymers. But then polymers based on cellulose, eg the 'triacetate' base, are as far as I know just "isotropic" and do not have that polarization problem, but instead prone to wretched solvent problems of course and just not as tough. ...

jeff

MichaelH Thu Jun 02, 2005 3:01 pm #27
Not any more so than any other liquid. It all depends on developing a method that gets you the results you needs with a minimum of fuss and mess.

What I do is use as little as I can get away with, apply it with an eye-dropper and use newsprint-type paper to absorb the oil as it's squeezed out the edges by the roller.

I wouldn't recommend it as the first choice when getting started but there are times when index-matching is the only way to go (if you're stuck with film) such as when using a high % of TEA and the film sticks to the glass and never really settles.
