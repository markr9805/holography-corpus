Tony Fri Dec 31, 2004 7:01 pm #5
I think 'yes', the light from the LEDs will be visible on the object. It's a pretty good guess, since there's probably many ways that the light from the LEDs would be visible, and only a few (or one) way it would not be visible.

Here are two arguments:

1. The object is illuminated by some source illumination, say a flourescent bulb in the ceiling. The source illumination has some spectral energy response - energy versus frequency. The object also has its own spectral reflectivity response. The actual light being reflected off the object is the product of the two responses. By your experiment, this is also the same response (as perceived by your eyes) from the LEDs. But when you illuminate the object with the LEDs, the response would be the product of the object reflectivity response and the LEDs, which are (to your eyes) equal to the product of the illumination response (the bulb in the ceiling) and the object reflectivity response. So I argue the net result would include the effect of the object reflectivity response twice, and therefore not be identical.

2. On a pure energy level, there would be more energy coming from the object if it was illuminated by both the source (flourescent bulb) and the LEDs, than by the source alone. (You didn't specify if you turned off the bulb in the ceiling for the second part of the experiment).
