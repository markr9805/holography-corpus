zipsister Tue Feb 26, 2008 11:23 pm #4
Hi John FB,

Working big ain't easy; I enjoy the challenge. You may have seen on my website that for the nine metre hologram I had to design and build a camera that would expose the film as thirty individual pulse portraits. I've built these types of cameras before so it wasn't too difficult. The hard part in this instance was designing a film holder that would enable me to wind the film on it and process it in the confined lab of Holocenter; what I built was a bit like a photographic processing reel/tank combo. Again there are images on the website to show what this film rig looked like. Then I built a chemical container to put this rig in and process it. I used 100 litres of a modified Neofin Blue developer and the same quantity of bleach. Shame to have to throw all those chemicals away at the end. I'm still organising galleries to exhibit it, firstly in Australia then overseas. Shortly I will be exhibiting the metre square The Gamer hologram at an electronics/pc gaming shopping complex. I'll post images of that shortly. The one and two metre square holograms are simply processed in big trays. But the nine metre was a totally new ball game. I'm hoping to make another nine metre hologram this year.

Hope this answers you question satisfactorily.

Cheers,

Dave

Thanks Julian and Tom,

Persistence and a measure of sadism are the key ingredience for making holograms, as we all know. From the beginning I realised that holograms were very hard to make so if I was going to spend a lot of time trying then I might as well spend that time trying to make them big; that way the failures were bound to be more spectacular. And my experiences from travelling around the world mirrors this forum in that it constantly proves that the holography fraternity is made up of generous people who understand the difficulties of the medium and are always willing to help or offer unselfish support. I am always appreciative of that.
