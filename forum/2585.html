I have some reflection holograms where the back of the holograms are not yet painted black. Unfortunately they have collected some dust on the emulsion. Is there any way of getting rid of the dust? Can I put the holograms in distilled water again and try to rinse off the dirt?
MichaelH Wed Jun 30, 2004 9:17 pm #2
What emulsion is it? Some are softer than others. PFG-01 is what I'm most familiar with and it's a fairly hard emulsion to start with.

If the dust didn't dry onto the emulsion, compressed air will often remove it. If the hologram wasn't post-swelled you should be able to soak it in distilled water and gently remove the dust. An extra soak is also a good way to correct a bad squeegee job.
DJ Mathson Wed Jun 30, 2004 9:43 pm #3
It is BB640, PFG-01 and Ultimate emulsions. They are not preswelled or anything so I think it should be possible to rinse them in distilled water. Does the Isopropyl alcohol bath get rid of fatty dirt, or why else should I use that instead of water?
MichaelH Wed Jun 30, 2004 10:00 pm #4
> They are not preswelled

It's more important that they not be post-swelled as rinsing them in water will cause the post-swelling agent to be rinsed out and the final reconstruction color will be altered.
DJ Mathson Wed Jun 30, 2004 10:07 pm #5
They are not post swelled either. The last bath they where in before drying was destilled water with Photoflo.
Dinesh Wed Jun 30, 2004 11:45 pm #6
You can clean it in water, so long as it's not post swelled. You may need to wash in running water for about twice the time of your last water bath in the original processing, ie if you normally wash 5 mins in the last bath, wash for 10. The reason is that the emulsion is harder than when you initially transferred it from the bleach into the last bath. Distilled is not necessary, I've always just used tap water, the important thing is that the water must be running. To get rid of the dust, gently wipe the emulsion with a soft tissue, being careful not to scratch the emulsion. In all probability the dust has immersed into the emulsion and will not come out just by washing alone, especialy if it's an old plate. Dry as normal, photoflo in your case.

I often put a hair dryer onto the Silver plates to get an initial idea of the way its turned out, then throw it back into the water.

Tom B. Thu Jul 01, 2004 3:36 am #8
If the dust is not too deeply imbedded in the emulsion, you might try laying some 3M Magic Transparent tape or Post-It note sticky bits (or similar low-residue adhesive) on the emusion and peeling off the dust before resorting to more drastic methods. Helpful for cleaning black velvet, too.
