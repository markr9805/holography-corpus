After that, the next hologram I want to do is as follows.

Make an on axis single beam reflection hologram of an object with a converging reference beam. The converging beam is more for getting more light to the sides of the object (on-axis) then matching the reference of the reflection master (pseudo) but will help the H2 as in the copy set up the beam will be diverging. Make an H2 image planed reflection from the H1 reflection but use an indexing matching fluid between the H1 and H2 wich will be sandwiched together. I have had sucess with Reflection H1 to Reflection H2 transfers. I have had success with on-axis reference beam holograms. But I envision a back reflection off the H1 if it is on-axis, thus the indexing matching fluid.

Any thoughts before I proceed (again, will not be for a few weeks)?
Ed Wesly Thu Sep 01, 2005 1:02 pm #2
Sounds like what I would try in a similar situation. The back of the H1 would need to be blackened, too, with something that wouldn't dissolve in the index matching fluid. Even though there would only be about 4% Fresnel reflection along the normal, the combination of the two parallel holo plates would create a lot of ringing, kind of a thick air-spaced etalon.

But then there is the question of what will happen to the image since it's being refracted through the index matching fluid? Looking at dishes in a sink is different than looking at them underwater, especially where they break the surface. Might be a good idea to mock this up with a different on-axis hologram before committing to an oily mess.

BTW, those Holodisks were H2 reflections with an on-axis reference beam.

Ed Wesly Fri Sep 02, 2005 1:55 pm #6
The point I was trying to make about the effect of the index matching liquid on the image is analogous to how an object looks to our eyes underwater. The mental picture experiment I'm trying to paint is what happens when you're in a swimming pool, and you look at a pole first above the water, in air, then submerse yourself in the water and look at the same pole extended under the water. I think the pole moves nearer and gets bigger when the higher index material is between your eyes and the object.

What makes me think that something is going on is that a Nikonos, the underwater Nikons, used a 35 mm lens as their normal, to be equivalent to a 50 mm underwater.

I don't want to recommend putting your holo in the index matching tank and then sticking your face in the IM liquid to see what happens to the image. But not having a swimming pool and living too far from Lake Michigan I can't verify my gut feeling about the change in the image from the higher surrounding n. So what the H2 will be seeing from the indexed out stack is speculation until the experiment is done.

My only experience with Index Matching is in contact copying, which didn't seem to make any difference except to change the reference angle in the set up.

Give your experiment a shot and see what happens!
