Hey Tony,

If memory serves correctly, the 909 is one of those rare water-cooled mid-frame argons that has single-phase power. Is that correct? If so, that's a definite plus. It's rare to find one with an etalon. This may (?) be the same one that was on eBay a couple of years (?) ago, which slipped through my fingers. It had an etalon. It was purchased by someone who is a regular on alt.lasers (was it "Laserlover"? I can't remember) Anyway, whoever it was that bought it on eBay, I sent him an email afterwards asking if he wanted to sell it, but got no response. On the other hand, this may be a completely different 909. There are a lot of questions to be asked before buying an argon, John would definitely be the person to ask, not me.

458 and 532 is a perfect combination, along with 633, for color holography. I'm more knowledgeable about the 58-BLD-605, because that's what I use. If you want, send me an email if you have any specific questions, or post them here. Even though these don't use water cooling, the beam quality will not be as good as an argon, and you would need some additional beam-shaping optics to get the beam through a spatial filter. It would be essential that you know the ACTUAL output from BOTH beams before making any offer. The seller must be able to provide this. If it's doing 200mW per beam, which is the specified output, this laser would be worth about $5K, unless the price has come down recently. There are other factors to consider, so please send me an email if you are serious about this particular laser you're looking at, I would like to see some additonal information like power, price, date of maunfacture, seller, etc.

Ed Wesly Thu Jan 24, 2008 2:47 am #12
Every party has a pooper and here I am. If I remember correctly, American Laser was bought by LiConix (which has since been bought by Melles Griot) around 1990, so I would be a little concerned about how viable this unit would be.

It is a shame that they didn't continue production of this product. When I visited "the House of Blue Light" as they called themselves back then, they showed me some early production models. I suggested that they paint them the same color as their He-Cd's, that deep blue, before they went to black paint. I never saw any product like that.

Not only was I amazed by the low voltage (110) power supply, but they had the tube cooled by a recirculator! And in sotto voce they told me a "corporate secret"; the recirculator was nothing more than a box with a Honda Accord radiator and its fan to cool it!

They also had a prototype of a 10 or 12 Watt model, which was simply two tubes cascaded in a row in a common resonator!

I don't know the fate of these American Laser models made by Liconix, like how many are out in field or if they even made it there. But it seemed to be rather innovative for its day!
