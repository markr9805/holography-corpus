wler Mon Feb 06, 2006 7:25 pm #4
> Hans: A ring laser. I have read once before that a ring laser setup can even make a dye laser single frequency. How does a ring setup where the light travels only in one direction through the lasing medium enable one single wavelength to win?

Because there are no standing waves like in an ordinary resonator; for the latter, the resonance condition is satified by a large number of longitudinal modes, and those which happen to lie within the gain curve of the lasing medium, will be excited. This effectively reduces the coherence lentgh. Then there are other effects like "spatial hole burning", which makes it it easier for competing modes to become excited.

This is not so for a ring laser - there are no multiple resonance modes at all to start with, and there is no spatial hole burning but rather a "winner takes all" principle at work. There is only one thing to care about, namely there will be also a wave running in the opposite direction, which takes away 50% of the power; unles one makes it hard for this mode to come up (like using an acoustic modulator or somethng else).

Surely single mode operation can alse be achieved by an ordinary resonator, but things like etalons and other tricks are necessary. Unless one is taking care of this, there is practicaly no way to have single mode operation, and this applies to basically all DPSS lasers that are not specifically designed for single longitudinal mode.

PS: here's a brief [movie](http://argonholo.webhop.net/laser/pics/633modedrift.AVI) (3.8MB) I did a few days ago, showing how a few longitudinal modes of a HeNE laser drift while it warms up (the lower trace shows a 10x zoom). I plan to do more of such investigations esp for diode and DPSS lasers.
Bastian_S Mon Feb 06, 2006 7:34 pm #5
in standing wave resonators there is something called spatial hole burning. The standing wave leaves population inversion at its nodes untouched. The next longitudinal mode doesnt have its nodes at the same position and sees inversion left by the other and starts oscillating too.

in a ring setup there is no standing wave and therefore one single mode <i>CAN</i> dominate the others.

A good Developer is :

Solution A

40 g ascorbic acid

6 g “Metol” 4-methylaminophenol

Made up to 1 litre in distilled or deionized water

Solution B

100 g anhydrous sodium carbonate ( Na2CO)3

30 g Sodium hydroxide (NaOH).

Made up to 1 litre in distilled or deionized water

Mix equal volumes of A and B for the developer.

The mixture should be used in a plastic dish with an identical dish floating on the top so that there is a minimal air gap. If you use it like this the same developer can be used again and again for many days (depending on how much usage of course) otherwise this developer left in an open dish is more vulnerable to oxidation from the air than standard developers because it deliberately does not contain sulfite ions. So the time the developer spends exposed to the air is low when you use a floating dish and it is only while immersing your plates or taking them out that it is fully open to the air. Unmixed solutions A and B keep indefinitely. A yellowing or browning of solution A is OK . ( Badly oxidized solution A is BLACK).

No your ritual routine is correct from a purist point of view. There are large osmotic pressure forces at work between virtually pure water saturating the emulsion and high ionic concentrations in the developer outside. Over the ~10 micron thickness of the emulsion these osmotic pressure forces act faster than it would take dry emulsion to imbibe developer solution and swell up. With very fast development times I have found that by looking at the absorption hologram in just silver without any bleaching , a reflection hologram can look fine on the emulsion side but then you see almost nothing through the glass side.

----In other words the development is so uneven that the back end of the hologram against the glass has perhaps got no fringe structure . Longer development time would have sorted this but then the front of the hologram would be getting over developed ie your dark fringes are starting to develop up. So you can have a catch up problem and a more broad band result because of unequal fringe thicknesses. But generally with moderately short dev times it does not seem to make much difference if you do just put your dry plate in the dev withou the presoak.
