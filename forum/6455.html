JohnFP Wed Sep 26, 2007 11:36 am #11
Tony, I have been giving this some thought and have come to a couple conclusions;

First I think you can puddle emulsion as you state. When I coat with the Myer bar the top of the emulsion is open to the air and this produces great results. In essence it is the final stage of a puddling technique. I think the key here is to heat the glass prior to puddling to the temp of your emulsion, 115-120F. That way the emulsion has a good chance of evening out prior to getting less viscous. It may be that a higher vollume of water will work better for this technique. The only downfall I see is I assume you would need a precise amount. Not enough and the puddle will not reach the ends of the glass, just enough and the puddle will stop at the edge (I assume you have a little play here and can vary thickness due to the fact that the water tension properites of the emulsion at the edge of the glass will bead up and allow a thicker or thinner puddle depending on volume of emulsion), and too much and that water tension bead breaks at a point or few at the edge and you get a deflating at those points. I wonder if the plate should be put in a warm oven (about 90 - 100F for a few minutes until the puddle evens out.

Next, if you use too much water in the mold coating, I believe you may have a problem with getting the cover plate off before the emulsion has shrunk too much. A lot of shinkage in the mold, I assume, will cause uneven vacuum points at various spots around the edge of the plate. It may be possible to cool (fridge) or supercool (freezer) to allow removal of the cover plate, but I am not sure on that one. But again, please keep us posted if you do any tests.

I will have to say I have never heard of the puddle technique and it is very appealing to me due to it's ease after finding the correct volume. I imagine a syringe may do the trick in the volume measurement. Coupled with some heat to the plate and a little extra water, I LIKE IT.....

I may have to try that too.

By this puddle technique, will require VERY precise leveling ,im going to throw out a guess here and say the edges will be way thicker than the centers (that’s why they spin coat after puddling),I’m sure it will work it just not going to be the best way for uniform thickness across the plate.

As far as cooling after mold coating, i would not recommend the freezer as if you wait to long you’ll get some beautiful crystalline structures across your plate! Use the fridge, or do as i do using frozen granite slabs.....

I remember doubling my water content (for mold coating),in an effort, to try to produce a thinner coating and it worked, generally it seemed linear, double the water content and reduce thickness by a half, as you stated also needing a longer cool time.
