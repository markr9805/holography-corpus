Hans Wed Feb 07, 2007 12:34 pm #4
Here is the predicted replay wavelength when using the new formula:

- We have 12 g gelatin.

- The weight of the chemicals is calculated as follows:

--- 4,8ml TMG x 25% = about 1.2g

--- .77g Boric Acid = .77g

--- 1ml Potassium Chromate (at 5%)= .05g

--- 6ml MB at 0.4% = .024g

--- Total weight of chemicals: 2g

- This is 17% of the weight of the gelatin. Thus the gelatin should shrink about 17% after exposed and these chemicals are washed out.

- A hologram recorded with a laser diode at 650nm will then replay at (1-.17)*650 = 540nm

These calculations are not super accurate, but adequate for our work. I have confirmed this formula by making a hologram with these concentrations and then measuring the replay wavelength with a cheap spectrophotometer from ProjectStar. (In my humble opinion a valuable instrument that every holographer could use).

Hans Wed Feb 07, 2007 5:32 pm #7
> Could you please tell me a bit more about this, as I would like to get one.

It is a glass part that looks like a float (like one uses when float fishing). It has a scale on the side. You can drop it in a vertical container with the alcohol mix and then read on the scale how deep it has sunk in the mixture. The scale will then indicate the concentration of the IPA.

I think it can be easily made in the kitchen lab witha thin straw that is closed on both ends and weighted down on one end. Then drop it in 100% IPA and place a mark with a pen or make a little scratch at the IPA level on the straw. Next, drop it into say 70% IPA and place another mark. You can do this for all your IPA solutions and always be sure they are at the right concentration with this little tool.

Hans Sat Mar 24, 2007 10:18 am #26
> Can these calculations be taken as rule?

With MBDCG, I think so yes. It is an entirely different matter with classical DCG because there the hardening there takes place in a liquid (swollen state with hardening fix where the swelling seems to depend on the temperature of that bath). This may also explain why it is near to impossible to change the re-play colour of an MBDCG with different times in "tuning" baths.

> Does shrinking take place only after alcohol escapes from fringes?

I believe that is the case. I think two things take place here. Firstly, the alcohol takes the water out of the gelatin matrix. Secondly, the alohol stiffens the gelatin. If you start with a low concentration alcohol, the gelatin stiffens enough to prevent further deformation during the higher concentration baths. So, starting with a low concentration enables a narrow band hologram.

Also, when you start with a low concentration, the calculation of replay colour seems very accurate for my adapted MBDCG formula and for Jeff's original formula.

If you start with a very high concentration of alcohol, the gelatin stiffens while the alcohol is extracting water from the gelatin. Water is able to escape more quickly at the surface of the gelatin and the gelatin stiffens as a whole throughout the thickness. This creates a differential shrinking throughout the thickness of the gelatin. The result is a broadband hologram. The same effect can be reached with a warmer water bath. The gelatin is then able to hold more water before it is thrown in the first alcohol bath.

> I shoot some holograms that appears pretty bright when plate is still wet after alcohol bath, but image disappears when alcohol escapes from fringes, and no further thermal processing restores image.

I think this might be because your alcohol is not 100%. The result is perhaps that while the plate is in that bath, there is some equilibrium. The alcohol has stiffened the gelatin, and at the same time, the water that is still there between the fringes holds the distance between the fringes. But where there is still water, the gelatin is not completely stiffened yet. As you remove the plate from that bath, the water evaporates and the fringes collapse. The plate will change colour from it's original green/yellow to blue/violet and disappear.

But perhaps this will work:

Process your plate in baths of normal alcohol of concentrations of 30% and 90%. Then, as the last bath, use pure acetone to remove the last bit of water and stiffen the gelatin more. It's a wild guess, but perhaps this will work for you.
