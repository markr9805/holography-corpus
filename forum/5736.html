Tony Sun Jul 30, 2006 12:11 pm #2
On squeegeeing:

-Some emulsions are harder than others. Harder emulsions can take squeegeeing better than softer ones. I find BB-640 is pretty hard and sqeegees well; Ultimate is pretty soft and easily scratched.

-Squeegeeing works better when a wetting agent is used. Photoflo works well, but some think it may contribute to printout. Others use the Ilford product, or just a drop of liquid soap in the final rinse.

-You didn't say if you were using plates or film. For film, use a piece of clean glass to support it. Stick the film to the glass emulsion side down a la index matching and squeegee the back. The remove the film, squeegee the glass dry, and stick the film back onto the glass emulsion side up and squeegee that side last.

-For glass plates, the sharp edge of the glass will make cuts in the rubber blade, which will leave streaks next time. To minimize this problem, make a jig that keeps the wiper blade in the same position relative to the plate every time. This way the cuts will always be close to the edge of the glass.

If you've got any cuts or imperfections in your squeegee blade, throw it away. You'll only ruin any hologram you use it on.

I currently use a squeegee that I bought from the DIY shop which has replaceable blades. The blades come in 3' lengths that I cut up for my 8" squeegee.

Since I also make my own master plates, I make sure to sand the edges so they aren't sharp and won't cut the squeegee. If I were using pre-made plates I'd probably still do that since I always pre-treat my film to increase the speed and that process would allow me to rinse off the glass shards before use.

Wendy Mon Jul 31, 2006 6:01 pm #11
I'll ignore the comment about girls ...

The best squeegee I've used is a silicone rubber one that was designed for use on cars to wipe off the water - bought from Halfords (UK). It consistently gives me a good, clean finish on both film and glass. The glass might cut the rubber, but I found that the silicone blade is quite large and bendy, so just manoevering it carefully, can avoid streaks. You can always buy 2- one for film, and another for glass. The silicone also reacts less to organic solvents than the black rubber of ordinary rubber blades, so it is more resilient.

Try it!

Although my experience with Holo film and plates is limited I have been around film a long time and in my experience, the use of de-ionized water or distilled water and a wetting agent will give you a good result. Most importantly; do not over-concentrate your wetting agent. More is not better.

MikeJ Tue Aug 01, 2006 4:45 am #15
I have rewashed Fuji film in a dip and dunk processor and had good results. The final wash was DI water (mineral free) and a wetting agent. the film was vertical on a film hanger and went though a 30 minute room temp dryer cabinet. You should be able to place your plate or film vertically and let it dry slowly.

I do not believe that that Photoflow should have any effect on printout, it is merely soap that releases surface tension. Printout seems to be more attributable to wash and Bleach quality.You may be able to use half the amount of Photoflow because the distilled water is so clean.

The spots will tell you what works. You can always rewash.

Cheers,

Mike
