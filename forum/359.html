THought I would post this in the beginning section as it may help others that are starting up with a dual beam hologram set up.

Understand, I am not doing 3 second exposures in Silver like I could with my Argon Ion laser (boy I miss that baby). My exposure will be somewhere around 30 seconds to 3 minutes.

I loaded a plate and shot a hologram, NOTHING. So I tore the setup down again and set up and interferometer. Very bad fring movement. It wasn't a breathing movement but almost like a mode hop or shaking of the fringes then snap back in. I can only assume the laser, as directed by other on the forum here does not like to be mounted on it's side. It surely seems as if it was laser instability. Also, ground vibration were very evident if even a slight tapping of the floor. Soooooooooo

^Going to tear down the entire table and construct a complete box enclosure. Plan to make it out of wood framing and then cardboard stapled to the wood framing.

^Need a lot more weight. Easy enough with concrete pavers put on the table in between all the optic. I may even go a few levels high.

^Surely need to unmount the laser on its side and put it back on its base correctly.

I thought I would need two more mirrors to rotate the polarization, which I tried not to do as not only does it add optics but it makes the feed to my table incorrect. My laser table point directly to the holo table. Two more mirror for polarization rotation would have the beam go off that table at 90 degrees. But I did find I could do the rotation on the holo table with the first Mirror M1.

^ I will do the above and retest with interferometer.

^I am finding that the diffusing screen take ALOT of power away, I need to try and test some more types to salvage more power.

Jem Tue Oct 18, 2011 12:49 pm #4
Hi John

You really ought to try and pick up a halfwave plate instead of using mirrors to rotate the polarisation, the losses will be a lot less.

I messed around for ages with mirrors, but once I got a halfwave plate i've never looked back. Those extra few milliwats that you'll save really do make a difference

Cheers

Jem
Jeffrey Weil Tue Oct 18, 2011 6:04 pm #5
Hello John,

Jem is right. If your going to get serious this is a must have item. You'll also need to build or buy something to hold and rotate it.

Please do not glue it. If your building something I know it's much easier but one day you'll regret it.

Johnfp Wed Oct 19, 2011 5:20 am #6
Thanks all, but I am on an extreemely tight budget. My oldest son started college this year and the frigging price of books alone costs a fortune. Not to mention his insurance for his own car (and a list of other things as long as my arm).

So, I already incorporated the two mirror polarization technique. As I stated I used the first transfer mirror and only had to add one additional mirror. For me, that is well worth the savings. So last night I went outside and made about 15 trips carring as many stones as possible. I tried to get ones that were roughtly 3 - 5 inches thick. Everywhere there was not a magnetic mount optic I placed a stone. Probably about 300lbs or so I guess. Everyone was out of the house. Seriously after about 15 minutes after putting the additional weight on the table, and banging and shaking the table to settle it, my fringes looked fantastic. I litterally had a good 5 minutes with no fringe movement. WOOOO HOOOO. I watched the fringes for about 3 hours noting all that disrupted them (not constantly for 3 hours, I had my laptop down there and played Runescape while I was checking them).

Helicopter flying overhead...very very bad...fringes disappeared for the whole time I could hear the hellcopter.

Sump pump (comes on for about 20 seconds every 10 minutes or so - just a little dimming of fringes but no movement

Dehumidifyer - must be off

Activity in house - bad, going to have to make hologram when no one is home or they are sleeping.

My road off my driveway - I dont thing this affect it too much. Out of all the trucks I heard (can't hear cars) only one made a jiggle. Probably because there is a stream between the road and my house and the change from ground to waster soaked ground back to dry ground dampens the vibrations. I'm a good 100 yards or so off that road

But every once in a while for no reason which I can tell I get a very violent jiggle in the fringes for about 2 or so seconds. I can only predict without certainty that I may be a vehicle on the road behind my house (still a good 100 yards away). No stream in between and it is uphill from my house.

I did see a slight bit of creap, about a fringe in the three hours but I take that to be the stones still settling.

All in all, I have to say, as long as no one is home, I should be able to make good holograms. More testing and watching tonight as the set up would have settle a day.

Also, this may sound strange ( I guess that happens to you when you are sitting in the dark for hours watching interference fringes) but for the first time in my life I actually saw the sinusoidal shape of the fringes. For real. The fringes were about 1/2 inch wide for constr and 1/2 inch wide for destructive running vertically. I could actually see, or invision how the fringe is in 3 dimensions. Basically like a bunch of parallel cylindrical lenses. It was quite a revelation to see (or imagine) the actual lens shape of the fringes. A true understanding of the shape of the "lenses" can help a lot with mechanical thinking of fringe capturing, developing, processing...etc. (No, no drugs were involved)

Tonight - buy that green LED chrismas light with green globy thingys attached. It's the last piece I need. Possibly holograms this weekend. I feel like such a beginner again. But it's ok. It's the challenge I think I like only second to seeing the final hologram.

Ok, made a hologram. If you were here now I would kiss you as I am jumping around the house, said man 25 times and really just cannot get over myself. hehe

I want to thank all that help contribute such that my first hologram was a success.

Exposure time based on my LaserCheck. I doubled the exposure and got really, almost too much burn in, sweeet. I was reading 4uW total both sides of plate. Doubled wast 8uW. Divided that into the upper limit Slavich recommends (80uJ). Thus 10 seconds. I exposed for 20 (easier to get a hologam and dial down energy then too little energy and nothhing to way to dim to evaluate).

Processed JD2 as directed.

Hologram came out reddish orange and really bright.

I used no pinholes just to insure i have a hologram. Dirty of course but not real bad.

One mistake, thus a post again in the beginner section, I looked at the train H1 hologram on the object side and did not compensate for the acutal hologam being on the reference side of the H1 trans to H2 reflection. Needless to say my poor hologram is upside down. It is image planed (the train was mastered at about a 80 degree normal to the plate. Thus the final had the front end stinking out 1" and the back end behind the hologram about 1".

I will post some pics when I get batteries for my camera, darn it.
