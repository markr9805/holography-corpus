I forget the guys name but he had a holographic candy company for a while. Chocolates with rainbow holos and some lollipops with stuff inside. The problem was the mix, they had to make the chocolates in a certain way so the tiny surface relief pattern would last through the expected non refrigerated conditions it would encounter.

How good is a holo chocolate if the hotel can't leave a piece on your pillow without it fading before you checked in?

The holo lasted with the mix.....but tasted like crap! No one wanted chocolates that were not edible. The company folded.

Here's what to do if you want to make your own sugar holograms. They look pretty good too, but they attract dust.

Take an embossed hologram. It has to be un-laminated and have no pressure sensitive adhesive on the back. You have to have access to the back side where the surface relief pattern is. Mix up a saturated solution of sugar and water. Heat up the water and mix in as much sugar as it'll take.

Using only the sugar/water stick the hologram to a piece of glass. Black glass is best, paint the back. Roll all the extra water from under the embossed hologram with a hand roller. Have the back side of the hologram, the side with the relief, towards the glass. Let is set for a few days so all the water can evaporate away. This is going to take some time as it's covered up by the plastic hologram. It might take over a week. You'll have to experiment.

After it's dried peel off the embossed sheet and you will be left with a really nice rainbow hologram on glass. But remember, all the dust that falls on it over time will stick.

Bruno Tue Jan 03, 2012 7:17 am #10
> Martin: Bruno wrote:The Mexican paper abstract said they're effectively creating index modulation inside the sugar. It's not a surface hologram.Are you sure?

Well... This is what the abstract says:

> "Computer holograms made with sugar crystals are reported. This material is well known as a good sweetener; the sugar from sugar cane or sugar beet (sucrose). These sweetener can be applied as honey “water and diluted sugar” easily on any substrate such as plastics or glasses without critical conditions for developed process. This step corresponds only to the cured sucrose as a photopolymer process. The maximum absorption spectra is localized at UV region λ=240 nm. We record with lithographic techniques some gratings, showing a good diffraction efficiency around 45%. This material has good resolution to make diffraction gratings. These properties are attractive because they open the possibility to make phase holograms on candies.Mainly the phase modulation is by refraction index."

The question therefore is not whether I'm sure, but whether they are . I'm a bit slow so I only realised later that they're not actually trying to record holograms but to print the fringes directly. So it must indeed be a surface hologram. I found the fulltext of another paper ([http://144.206.159.178/FT/CONF/16411998/16412027.pdf](http://144.206.159.178/FT/CONF/16411998/16412027.pdf)) co-authored by one of same people. It's an embarrassing read. In an attempt to sensitize their material they add blue food colouring, then they still expose their material at 240nm and complain that somehow things haven't improved.

At any rate it was a pleasant waste of time
