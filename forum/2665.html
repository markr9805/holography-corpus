[quote:615dd0594e]What I would like to know is whether the lens below would be suitable for expanding the beams on a table of this size.[/quote:615dd0594e]

Difficult to say unless you knew what you wanted to do with the lens. Assuming you want to illuminate an object 15 cm wide. Then, if we assume that the aperture of the lens is 6mm (fast lens!) in diamter and the lens has a focal length of -6. This would mean that a collimated beam of diameter equal to the lens aperture would diverge from a point 6mm behind the lens into a half angle of 26 odd degrees ((inv tan)(3/6)). Thus to cover an object of diameter 15 cm, you'd need to place the lens about 7cm away. If the beam is not the same as the lens aperture, then the angle of divergence of the beam, theta, depends on the beam diameter, b (in mm):

theta = (inv tan)(b/12)

and to cover an object of diameter 15cm, the distance to the object from the lens, d(in cm) is:

d=7.5/tan(theta) - 0.6

The .6 is to take into account the fact that the point of divergence of the beam is 6 mm behind the lens.

Putting in some numbers, if your beam diameter is 1mm, then

theta = 4.7 degrees

d = 89.4 cm

This is about 35 inches, or almost the full length of your table.

Dinesh Fri Aug 13, 2004 1:08 pm #5
3mm seems reasonable for the diameter. The easiest way to tell is to expose some film, develop it and measure the black spot. You don't need to use holographic film or a holographic setup, since you're simply exposing the raw beam. Remeber that the beam is gaussian so the developed spot won't abruptly end but will fall off. You need to determine where the beams "ends".

To increase the illumination you might try putting two of them together. The combined focal length will be 3mm so for the same beam diameter you'd get a greater divergence.
