MichaelH Mon Apr 17, 2006 8:57 am #2
I'm still generally self-hardening clay, non-hardening and plaster. They all have their advantages and disadvantages. I tend to use plaster for larger areas that will be covered (such as grassy or snowy areas) and while I have used self-hardening clay for large uncovered areas I'll probably use non-hardening next time since those tend to crack while drying unless the clay is fairly think (and heavy).

I've never used fired clay just because I don't have access to a kiln.

I've used un-hardened sculpey twice and it worked out fine as a subject. It doesn't move at all but you have to be careful while handling it as it's fairly soft.
Joe Farina Mon Apr 17, 2006 11:50 am #3
In all seriousness, Colin, I'd suggest using anything and everything that works.

The only caveat is that, if you're doing this professionally, and suggest to your client that you'll be using something other than plaster or epoxy putty, they may balk, because they won't be inclined to take any chances.

As we see from posts on this forum, there seems to be almost no limit on the kind of materials that can be used. It really depends on the length of the exposure, and of course environmental conditions which could cause the object to expand/contract, warp, move, etc.
