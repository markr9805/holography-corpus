In our early days of holography we used to have a small air-cooled Siemens Argon laser that delivered some 10mW at 488nm. The heating effect on the optical table was quite substantial - so we discarded its use for holography. That laser then remained to be reserved for “disco” effects only.

Wasted, I should have said, since much later we discovered the laser could be used to make good DCG contact copies from silver halide H2 holograms (TEA shifted Agfa films). Unfortunately, we were never able to test that extensively because soon after the laser was dead.

So in short, I would definitely consider making contact copies (into DCG and maybe soon, into photopolymer). Moreover, silver halide emulsions have been greatly improved over the last decade. Hence blue reconstructions have become virtually devoid of noise...

Ed Wesly Tue Apr 17, 2007 5:58 pm #9
There was one rather rare air-cooled argon laser that had an etalon, manufactured by ILT, I forgot what that stood for, out of Salt Lake City, I think. It was just one of their high power tubes in an extended cavity, with an etalon.

What they did for heat and stability was have a clothes dryer type hose with a fan on the end, so the vibration is off the table and the wind is away from the optics. I forget the power on it, I might have the brochure somewhere in the archives, but it wasn't more than about 50 mW. It was built in the early '90's.

You could tune it throughout the usual Argon lines, 515, 488, 476 and 458. But the one major problem was in their mirror/prism holder. If you turned a screw a little too far, you could snap it off.

It would be cool to find one of these units, since other tubes currently available might be adapted to fit into the cavity.

I would save my money and not buy either or the Argons mentiioned above, as you might not get any decent coherence, and with a tube sealed with mirrors there is no way that you could attempt to build it into a cavity with etalon.
