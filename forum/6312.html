eric343 Fri Jun 22, 2007 7:50 pm #4
I worked in Mike Bove's group last summer.

The holography-related work is exactly what Tech Review describes: a smaller, more compact, higher resolution, higher bandwidth holo-video display driven by off-the-shelf graphics cards instead of dedicated hardware. It's based on a custom-designed and built-in-house acousto-optic modulator that uses traveling waves (or was it surface waves) in a wafer of lithium niobate (IIRC). Higher bandwidth and easier to produce than the 2 custom AOMs that the Mark II used. (they were the largest such crystals ever made at the time, expensive as hell)

The Holographic Imaging book is based on Steve Benton's old files and/or stuff he was working on when he died.

There is no traditional holography going on in the Media Lab anymore that I know of. I believe Steve Benton's lab --used last year for holovideo research and a creative holography class -- has been disassembled and the space given over to another research group. I believe there is holography research going on at MIT's mechanical engineering department, involving CCDs as the imaging medium or something.

It's a shame that there are no classes in holography at MIT! Where else would there be research into the imaging aspect?

It might not be well-known on this forum, but the previous president of MIT was a holographer! Charles Vest wrote the book, Holographic Interferometry, and was a regular at the Lake Forest College Workshops in the '80's, then he became a Dean or a Provost at the University of Michigan in Ann Arbor, then became the President of MIT until he retired. Maybe if he was still there holography might still thrive!

Also, Erich, is there still a floor dedicated to Doc Edgerton? We made a pulsed ruby portrait of him at Holicon circa 1986 and I got the opportunity to present it to him. My recollection is that he had a whole floor of a wing of a building as a lab where students could make their own photographic strobes, forming the tubes, building the circuits and using them in a studio that had a rifle at one end and bunting at the other to catch the bullet so you could recreate your own making applesauce photo! (That's was Edgerton called that famous picture of the bullet piercing the apple, as it looks like a clean hole in the photo but Doc said the apple ended up all over the room!) Needless to say, this class filled immediately![/img]
