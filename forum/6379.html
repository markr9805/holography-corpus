Hans Wed Aug 08, 2007 6:49 am #1
One problem I always have with laser my diodes is that with the protective window, I get ugly artefacts in the beam. Without the protective window, the lifetime of the diode seems to be reduced greatly. I guess this is due to condensation and dust settling on the facets of the diode.

Would it be possible to fill up the diode can with polyester after removing the protective glass? Or would the polyester dissolve parts of the p-n crystal or connections inside the can while it is still liquid. If this would work, it would be great because it might even be possible to polish a lens shape to the part of the polyester that faces the air side of this setup.

Just to give my two cents, I would remove the window, and keep the diode in a closed or covered container with a little desiccant (when you're not actually exposing a hologram), if you suspect that dust and/or humidity is reducing the lifetime of the diode. (At Jeff's suggestion, I've found that Vaseline works well as a sealant.)

You could always try the polyester with a cheap diode, though. By the way, my can opener is still for sale if you need one However, if the resin doesn't cause damage to the diode, it would still need to be "perfect" in terms of clarity and surface finish, if you need a flawless beam. I think this would be quite difficult. But if you like trying difficult things like I do, you might want to have a look at "Cutting and Polishing Optical and Electronic Materials," Second Edtion, by G. W. Fynn and W. J. A. Powell (1988).
Tom B. Thu Aug 09, 2007 3:28 am #3
One possibility would be to make a small mount for the diode - a machined block with a drilled diode mounting hole and a way of mounting a larger optical flat window at Brewster's angle to the diode. The window removal and diode remounting etc. would have to be done under ultra-clean conditions, but afterwards the external surface of the window would be much easier to clean.

I would keep the original window. I guess it's AR-coated on both sides (no back-reflections).

Hans Thu Aug 09, 2007 5:46 pm #5
The windows on the HL6512 are not anti reflection coated as far as I know because they don't have the characteristic blue reflection that is typical for these coatings.

All the diodes I have tried give the same pattern on the expanded beam (no lens), no matter how much cleaning. Removing the window (I do have the can opener), cleans up the beam perfectly, but reduces the lifetime of the diode considerably.
