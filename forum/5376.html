> MichaelH: Kaveh wrote:It was more of a social event for me.Ahhh, gossip. So, dish some dirt!

OK. Not much dirt, but let's see what I remember.

Wendy McLean is Research Technician at Smart Holograms in Cambridge:

[http://www.smartholograms.com/](http://www.smartholograms.com/)

This is an offshoot of Cambridge University, with Prof Chris Lowe involved. I met him there when I went to meet Jeff Blyth (who can just be seen in one of the photos in the shots, peering at a hologram). I got the impression Prof Lowe is a seriously sharp guy. And the Smart Holograms are really clever too. I can't remember the details but it's an impressive application of holography.

Mike Anderson has been involved in holography since the 60's.

Geoff Rob works with Rob Munday at Spatial Imaging:

[http://www.holograms.co.uk/](http://www.holograms.co.uk/)

Apparently their holography course, run by Patrick Boyd, is doing well:

[http://www.3d-print.com/courses.html](http://www.3d-print.com/courses.html)

I met a guy called Pim Giebels, who I last met 26 years ago at Imperial College. He walked in 'off the street', and said he made holograms and wanted to try some there. He had some impressive holograms from the early days working with Lloyd Cross at the SF School of Holography. It was just when I was starting, and we made some holograms and had a show, open to the public which was really well attended.

There were no safety rules at that time regarding lasers, or at least we hadn't heard of them. I remember two water cooled Argon Ion lasers were put at eye level, as were the transmission holograms, and the beams were split with diffraction gratings to illuminate the holograms - ouch!

Being a phillistine, I can only comment on Pearl's holograms from a technical standpoint. The actually hologram is the yellow or green part that you see, mostly floating in front of the film. The other colors are photographic and 3D. But it was quite effective and eye catching.

Hope some useful info there.
