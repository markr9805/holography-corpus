ron olson Fri Oct 06, 2006 3:34 pm #6
John thank you for the favorable comment - I agree with your observation regarding the contest. My point was to be realistic in targeting the competition to the predominant hardware in use. For me, imaging dogs brings up a completely different set of problems than imaging (for example) tanks of fish or a snow covered tree-top. If you're imaging a ceramic dog or a ceramic whale, it's pretty much the same thing except you go to a different garage sale, art gallery or model maker as a necessary first step. Still lifes are obviously an extremely vital component within art and can be every bit as dramatic as images of living subjects. My own piece "Buddha" is one of my most popular and "Protea with Anthuriums" is one of my personal favorites. I feel that the broader category allows more latitude for creativity without the somewhat dubious sub-category "dogs". I believe Michael's intention is to urge the creation of new works and opening the category to the broader definition could invite recycled pieces (like my own "Rosie" imaged almost 10 years ago). "New Still-Lifes" would seem to better address the goals of the contest and the strengths of the technique as practiced by the vast majority of forum contributors.

I would add that my only regret in not being allowed into the competition is that I don't have a chance to finish third and win Frank DeFreitas' CD.
