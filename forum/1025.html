Hello,

what are reasonable possibilities for reference beam collimation?

1) Classical spherical / parabolic mirror. Good optical properties, but heavy and fragile, expensive. Takes lot of table space and requires careful planning of a setup. Good for overhead reference beam.

2) Spherical lens. Quite good optical properties, also heavy and fragile, even more expensive than mirrors in larger sizes. Sometimes takes less space on the table than a mirror.

3) Home made liquid lens as described in e.g. Practical Holography by Graham Saxby. Does anyone have experience using them?

4) No collimation at all, just use weak lens in the spatial filter and very long reference beam path. Not possible to create converging wave (i.e. real conjugate beam). Takes lot of space, even when folded using planar mirrors.

5) ???

Is it e.g. possible to use a Fresnel lens? Is there any other trick how to use affordable equipment to make collimated reference for a 8" x 10" plate?

Petr
BobH Fri Feb 13, 2015 12:24 am #2
Your list is a great representation of best to worst, regardless of the justifications (or most often practical limitations) that move us down the list. Best realism comes with perfect matching of the reference and reconstruction beams. That says parabolic mirror or nice big lens for an "H1" master, and off-axis spherical mirror or a whoppin' huge lens for an "H2" reflection transfer.

Everyone I know who tried Fresnel lens got nasty patterns all over the plate.
