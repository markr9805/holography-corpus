Yes that really seems to be right Danny when it comes to reusing UNUSED holographic plates. When you think about it, the maximum size of grain is likely to be a lot smaller than typical gelatin strands/ molecules . Unless it is extremely hard , gelatin has inherent elasticity to readily close up and “forget” that it once housed nanograins of silver bromide. After all did’nt those DCG pioneers at Bell Telephone start by using fixed- out KODAK 694F plates?

However I tried to re-use the gelatin from fixed- out old processed silver halide plates which were from holographic abortions (which I was good at making), I found that the results on using them in both the silver diffusion system and DCG were really bad.

----Not surprising really since the holographic exposure effects probably were imprinted permanently onto the gelatin due to factors such as localized hardening

around the developing exposed grains , especially if you are using pyrogallol developers or hardening from dichromate based bleach processes. (Even without using those particular methods , results were still bad.) The gelatin film was unevenly scarred for life!

Actually slightly diverging from this topic I have been thinking a lot in recent years about the way we always take it for granted that the silver halide based fringe structure will pretty much always stay in place and not drift about inside the wet gelatin during processing and thus mess up any delicate fringe structure. I guess it

must be sufficiently well locked in place onto some groups along the gelatin chains always.

I am concerned about this because I have over a number of years been using the silver diffusion system to put reflection holograms or gratings into all sorts of polymers so that our biotech group could use them as sensors. In general these too always kept the silver grains in their right place inspite of the grains having less choices to lock onto compared to gelatin. However there was one polymer that was very much an exception to the rule and proved how lucky we are that the silver does normally stay in place . In this polymer it never did, I could make crude photo images in it but never holographic ones in it, the spatial integrity of the fringe structure was always ruined through a lack of anchorage, there was nothing the grains could latch onto. This very remarkable polymer had marvellous properties we wanted to use such as swelling up hugely in the most hydrophobic liquids like gasoline

, . ---It cost me 3 years work before I finally cracked a way of making it stay in place.

---The patent will be published before long so I can then say more about it.
