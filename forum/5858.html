dcgman Thu Nov 02, 2006 5:08 pm #2
Since no-one else has had a go at this, I’ll kick off discussions:

According to this site, [http://www.astr.ua.edu/keel/techniques/eye.html](http://www.astr.ua.edu/keel/techniques/eye.html)

the eye’s resolution is 3 arcseconds, which is roughly 1 milliradian (mrad).

The equation representing the fringe spacing (X) recorded in a hologram is

X=m/Sin(t)........1

m: recording wavelength, t:angle between the reference and object beam.

When recording a transmission hologram of a diffuse object, t varies over a large range. In order to keep things simple, assume that the average or median angle is 45o. Also, assume the recording wavelength is 532nm. Plugging these values into the above equation yields X=752nm.

Rewrite eq 1:

m=XSin(t).........2

differentiate wrt t

dm/dt=XCos(t)..........3

dm=dt XCos(t).......4

We know X=752nm, q=45o, and dt (eye resolution) is 1 mrad. Substituting these into eq. 4 gives

dm= 0.5nm

So your playback laser has to be no broader than 0.5nm

This figure will apply to an ideal thin transmission hologram.

Notice that the depth of the scene doesn’t seem to come into this analysis- which means it probably needs correction!!

I have tried (and encourage the use of) LED's for lighting abstract transmissions. They are very effective and can be stacked linearly (on a track) to produce red, green and blue images staggered in space.
