edelbrp Tue Sep 20, 2005 4:39 pm #2
Monohydrate refers to the amount of water present in the crystals. If something is Anhydrous, it has no water.

Somebody correct me if I'm wrong, but I don't think you should see any difference between using different hyrdates of the same thing since they get dissolved in water anyway. But, you may need to weigh out the chemicals differently if the recipe calls for, say, an anhydrous and you've got something else. (The extra water will make the chemical weigh more.)

That said, the "Sodium Bisulphate" you are using may actually be a Monohydrate. Ask your supplier.

More info on hydrates:

Paulos Wed Sep 21, 2005 5:19 am #3
As posted by edelbrp, you should use more "Sodium Bisulphate Monohydrate".

Since the Molar mass of Sodium Bisulphate Monohydrate is 138,

while the molar mass of the (anydrous) Sodium Bisulphate (NaHSO4) is 120,

simply multiply by the factor 1.15 (138/120=1.15)

(e.g. use 11.5 g Sodium Bisulphate Monohydrate instead of 10.0 g Sodium Bisulphate).
