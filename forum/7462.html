How do we create efficient holograms in this region? The semiconductor industry has been using photoresists now since Schotky made his transistor; you'd think someone came up with a U/V holographic film by now with very high diffraction efficiencies.

There is film that responds well to UV (but when developed, develops in the visible-blue end); there must be some way for such film to diffract the light that exposed it.

(Fuji RTP 64)

Gelatin film can expand (picture taken under green will develop red; picture taken under blue develops green, etc.), but is it possible to reverse this dynamic process in film; that is, to make film shrink upon development? If this is so, a picture taken under blue (450 nm or so) can diffract much narrower than blue (lower than 450).

Ed Wesly Mon Aug 04, 2008 11:55 am #5
The big stumbling block to UV holograms (and photographs for that matter) using silver halide materials is that the gelatin binder is quite opaque in the UV. Photo-resists might be usable, but if a hologram were recorded in the UV, to see the image it must be viewed with visible light.
