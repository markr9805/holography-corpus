Colin Kaminski Tue Nov 08, 2005 5:01 am #2
The stability requirements of reflection holograms are much higher because the fringe spacing is much smaller. You probably have the film held at the bottom and the top is waving in the breeze. Try to minimize vibrations and hold the film more stable.

A common method is to touch the plate to the object to make it more stable.
JohnFP Tue Nov 08, 2005 10:52 am #3
As they say, a picture is worth a thousand words. Can you take a photo of what you are getting for the reflection and post it here? As Colin stated a good way to keep movement down is to touch the plate to the object. Actaully, if you want as a test, lay the plate horizonally right on the object if it is possible.

Concerning viewing the real image on a transmission. It is actually hard to find sometimes. Take the plate and put it in back in your original set up so that you can see the virtual image while looking through the plate. Then flip the plate with respect to the reference beam. That is, if you have an overhead reference beam flip the plate top to bottom. If you have a side reference beam flip the plate side to side. Then try to get as close as you can to the plate to find the real image BUT....PLEASE BE CARFUL NOT TO GET THE LASER LIGHT INTO YOUR EYES. Another technique it to set the plate up as descibed around (flipped around) then get a piece of white paper and hold it on the opposite side of the reference beam. Move it closer and further away from the plate. You should see parts of the image focused on the plate.

Good luck and happy holographing.
