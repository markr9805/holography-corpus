JohnFP Thu Dec 01, 2005 8:22 pm #2
I was going to wait to reply as I don't want to be considered as a DCG hog here but since you are exposing tonight I thought I might give my two cents.

I am not going to comment on anything you have done except I think you will be ok. Emulsion mixtures look good but 250ml for a few 2.5 x 2.5's? I usually cook 100 ml for 10 - 5x7's.

Lay the plate right on the pendant and do a contact copy to so that you can work on processing variables. Gosh knows there are enough of them. Make sure the you turn the pendant over, pseudo facing the coated plate.

I found with Kodak Rapid fixer mix to a 7:1 dilution. I was working with 4:1 and had not so good results.

Water soak at room temp is fine.

Strait to 100% is fine and should yield a nice broadband holo considering gel hardness (many parameters dictate this). What bloom gel did you start with. If it was Knox I belive it to be about 215 or so. Which if great. When you put is in the alcohol have a white spotlight to view the hologram in the alcohol. Keep the alcohol moving and I rotate the tray as I am never shure which way is the reference angle. You should actually start to see the hologram in the alcohol. As soon as you see it keep it in there for another 10 - 15 seconds then take out and blow dry. For some holograms this has taken seconds up to a couple of minutes. After about 2 minutes just blow dry whether you see the holo or not.

I have not tried thermal hardening. With 24 hour old plates I think the Kodak Rapid Fixer with Hardener will work well.

After the hologram is totally dried through all processes you can measure it with a micrometer pretty acurately. But I would not worry about that with the formula and temperatures of the emulsion you are using and heating the plate you are going to be nicely thin enough.

Hope some of this help and encourages you.

Feel free to call me tonight anytime in between exposures to go over what you got and what can help. I will send you a Private Message with my home phone. I love talking holography.

John.

JohnFP Fri Dec 02, 2005 1:44 pm #19
Yes, I have seen all kinds of presubbing techniques. But again, I have never had a problem with emulsion lifting off plate.

I soak the plates new or used in the Hydrochloric Acid over night.

I put on disposable medical rubber gloves, no lube and no powder.

I then take them out and over the Hydrochloric Acid container scrub them with a plastic wool (used for the Teflon coated non stick pans) and put them in a tray with water.

I then rinse and scrub them with the scrubby under running water and put them in a second tray of water.

I then rinse them again under running water rubbing them all over with my hands with are covered with the gloves and put in a rack standing vertically.

After all are in the rack and before all water drops are evaporated I use one paper towel in my left hand to hold the plate and use another fresh paper towel to wipe each side of the plate. You can hear when they are clean and all the water is gone. I then lay then out on a surface coverd with paper towels. I use Brawney Pick a Size (1/2 sheets).

Then I take some fresh half sheet and cut them in half. When I am ready to coat a plate I take a 1/4 sheet and fold it up. I then grab a plate and hold it ready to be coated. I take the folded sheet at make a single pass down the glass to remove any possible dust specks. I then coat and and use the same folded 1/4 sheet to wipe the bottom edge in my viel coat method.
