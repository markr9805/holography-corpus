Ed Wesly Sun Feb 04, 2007 4:13 pm #6
I met Walter Spierings on my first trip to the Museum of Holography the summer he was their Artist-in-Residence and witnessed arguments that how could this new Pyrochrome process be good for both reflection and transmission holograms. Conventional wisdom at that point was that they were two entirely different beasts. (Walter's final project was a multi-color rainbow of the famous optical illusion, Penroses' Impossible Triangle, in holographic form, processed with this formula!) (Also his famous microscope holo that graced holo-boutiques across the globe used it too!)

The shrinkage in transmission holograms changes the reference angle, but there always is some replay, and image distortions due to emulsion changes aren't always that noticeable.

If anyone ever runs into a supply of some obsolete high speed holographic materials, either Agfa 10E75, 10E56, or Kodak SO-253 Film, Type 131 plates, use Pyrochrome on them and get bright images with lower noise than if they were developed, fixed and bleached.
