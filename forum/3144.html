edelbrp Tue Feb 22, 2005 5:03 am #2
Linkman! You are posting like crazy! :')

Green lasers are out there, including some cheaper pointers, but they are almost useless for making holograms because they have a very short coherence length, and worse, serious mode-hopping problems.

I think the cheapest way to get into green right now is a used Coherent 315M, like from MrGizmos. They run about $1,000, more if sold with a optics platform. He often has offerings on eBay, so look there.

That said, that's much different than making a green hologram. You can use some sugar or TEA to preswell emulsion that will create a green hologram after you develop it. All using a regular red laser diode.

BobH Wed Feb 23, 2005 2:13 am #4
TEA is short for triethanolamine, a substance that swells the emulsion. You soak the recording material in it for a few minutes, let it dry, clean off the back (of the film or plate), then expose and process as usual. The TEA dissolves out of the material in the developer. Because the material was swollen durring exposure, the resulting hologram reconstructs in a shorter wavelength than that used to record it. Saxby probably covers the technique as "pseudocolor" reflection holography.

edelbrp Wed Feb 23, 2005 5:57 am #8
> linkman_11: What do you mean sugar, does that stand for anything, or is it just a cup of sugar water? If so, do you dip it into the sugar water before or after exposure.

Good questions.

Just regular white sugar from the kitchen. Dissolve 15 grams in 100ml of water (experiment with concentrations) and set your film (like PFG-03M) in it (in the dark!) for a few minutes. Then, remove and squeegee off the excess (carefully! the emusion is soft) and try to clean off the glass-side of the plate so it is clear like a window and no sugar residue. Now, dry it (again in the dark! do everything in the dark).

This artificially pre-swells the emulsion making it a little bigger than normal. When you expose it, it captures the interference fringes which shrink when the sugar is dissolved back out. That makes the hologram color shift to shorter wavelengths: R->Y->G->B

That's a great benefit since human eyesight is much more sensitive to yellow or green than to red.

You can do the opposite and do post-swelling of emulsion after development to shift the color the other direction. In this case, sugar or TEA probably aren't good choices since they degrade over time. ([here's more on this](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1995))

[Take a look at this thread started by Mr Bruza.](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=1488) It has a lot of great info.

-Phil

tpark Thu Feb 24, 2005 9:28 pm #11
I've only used TEA with BB-640 - I used a mix of dilute TEA and a bit of photoflow. I soak the plate for a minute, tip it sideways, and put it in a box with a fan which I specially made for drying plates. I've squeegeed the plates, but it seems that if you're careful about not leaving puddles on the plate it works fine, and you don't get squeegee scratches on the plate.

After exposure, I rinse the plate before dumping it in the developer. I've gotten some nice green holograms this way.

I've not tried TEA with PFG-03M, but as far as I can tell, squeegeeing PFG-03M usually leads to big scratches on an otherwise perfect hologram
