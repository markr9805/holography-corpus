Hello, friends,

I am back once again with some dumb ideas, and would appreciate some experienced opinions on whether this particular stinker is magnetically sound...

My latest money-saving idea for building a holography table worth having around is a slight variation on Frank DeFreitas' holography table he demonstrates building in a Youtube video: cinder blocks/carpet/MDF/carpet/inner tubes/carpet/MDF/concrete pavers/carpet/MDF...

His table looks simple-enough for even my slack-jawed self to build, but my goal is a magnetic table. My idea is to, instead of simply painting the top sheet of MDF black and letting gravity do the mounting, instead thoroughly epoxy a sheet of 20 or 18 ga sheet metal to the top layer of fibreboard.

I will carefully clean the sheet metal beforehand (that stuff usually comes pretty liberally oiled), and ensure a perfect mating of flat areas by first painting the epoxy on the fibreboard with a brush, then applying the sheet metal and using a firm rubber roller to work bubbles and high spots out from between.

My basic question is this: Will the relatively thin layer of sheet metal (now made one with the MDF) be a substantial-enough mass of steel for magnets to effectively grip to?

In my magnet browsing online I came across a photo of someone's hand pinned to a refridgerator by a powerful magnet - a fridge is mostly styrofoam, wrapped in sheet metal....

Granted, this query is not strictlyholography-related, but I'm hoping some of you fellers out there may have some experiences in magnetism that you may be willing to lend...?

Many thanks and much respect

References: [http://youtube.com/watch?v=TiJ7XvlFmuo](http://youtube.com/watch?v=TiJ7XvlFmuo)
Colin Kaminski Wed Jul 23, 2008 2:45 pm #2
No problem. I would grind the glue side of the sheet metal with a 80 grit disk first. Also, make sure you have a magnetic steel before you buy it. Just bring a magnet with you. Some stainless steel is not magnetic.
