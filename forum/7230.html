zipsister Sun Mar 23, 2008 9:46 am #14
Hi Julian,

Just successfully made some diffraction gratings with the single diverging beam of the 80mW diode laser. I split off part of the beam using two mirrors that effectively increased this diverted beam by 100cm thereby giving a coherence length of at least 100cm (at least three or four times your normal coherence of a HE-NE laser). The laser diode part number is Hitachi HL-6525MG (658nm@80mW) plus the suitable power adapter was $US25. A few things to remember with reflection holograms and coherence:

With single beam reflection holograms the object has to be up close and personal with the plate; the further the object is or its parts are from the plate the less sharp it is no matter how much coherence or what laser you have. This is the advantage with split beam transfer holograms or even single beam focussed holograms; you get to focus the image at the plate, and with a narrow 'letterbox' slit included somewhere in the set-up or simply by using a narrow master hologram image to project, then you can get greater image depth and sharpness. But even here a larger coherence is only useful if recording a large object in the master stage, and as I said before you realistically use powerful lasers to light up distant parts of the object sufficiently for the correct light ratio. The bottom line is that this diode and power supply is cheap and works with good coherence.

Colin Kaminski Tue Apr 01, 2008 1:53 pm #23
As you approach the edge of your coherence length you will see the object dim, then disappear. So, the coherence length or an-unstabilized HeNe is around the tube length. I always quote 6" but I have not measured this one as I am just using it for small single beams at the moment.

The problem with beam ratios is completely separate. If there is not enough light reaching the plate from the back of the scene you will not form fringes.
