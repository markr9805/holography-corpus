Writing about the Rita hologram reminded me about how I was reminiscing with my fellow survivor from Gallery 1134, known to some of you as the Chicago Museum of Holography, about a contraption that we vaguely remember at the Chicago Museum of Science and Industry.

There was a Kodak exhibit there, with a large photo-mural, Carousel Slide Projectors that you push the buttons on to view a slide show, and an interesting device that had a hologram in it.

It had to have been big, as an S-P 125 was in it, mounted at the bottom of a table that was tilted at an angle so that viewers of all heights had a clear line of sight to a window on the opposite side of the laser. There were two mechanical arms; one had an assortment of glassware, and this arm would swing the glassware into position behind the viewing window. Then the objects would move away, and the other arm with the hologram swung into postion, and got illuminated by the laser.

I remember the first time I saw it the laser was that kind of gun metal blue of early S-P production. Then later on, in the '80's, that laser was replaced by a white later production model.

I am guessing that this was not the only one of these devices out in the field. The glassware and hologram looked similar to some pictures in Howard Smith's Principles of Holography. (He worked at Kodak, also.) And of course Kodak made holographic recording materials way back when, so they would have been happy to show off some results!

Has anyone seen a similar device, or know the history of this doodad?
