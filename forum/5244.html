Since I'm new to dcg, I figure trying all methods I can at first... so far I've

noticed 2 basic ways of processing.

1. exposure, kodak rapid fix/hardner, water soak, ISO baths.....

2. exposure, thermal harden, water soak, ISO baths....

the thermal hardening method comes from slavich's web site on processing

PFG-04, (I got a box a few days ago, and just did some tests comparing with home made dcg)

my results can be found here... [http://www.andylesniak.com/holography/gallery.html](http://www.andylesniak.com/holography/gallery.html)

basic setup

1. exposure in contactcopy configuration using 315M @ 100mw / 532nm

2. exposure 10sec

3. bake in oven for 45min @ 100deg/c (time based on slavich chart)

4. water bath for 3min (room temp)

5. 100% ISO for 2min @ 30deg/c (also tried 50deg/c to try for more broadband)

I did the test using PFG-04 and my Homemade dcg.

still trying to digest what is going on, as well as what test to run next.

anybody have any experience thermal hardening dcg?

or ideas on what's happening on a chemical/physical level?

Differences I've noticed (see webpage pics) / random thoughts.

HomeDCG looks burnt after thermal harden / slavich looks brownish but washes out in water bath, vs homedcg won't wash out.

thought by going straight to 100 ISO would create more bandwidth as with chemical hardening/ still got very narrowband using thermal harden

I need to get much better at coating plates / I hurried (too excited to shoot...)

things to ponder

relationship of initial hardness of gelatin to thermal cook time.

relation of cook time to reconstruction bandwidth.

all of the above to wash time and ISO time/temp...

I plan on doing the same test either tonight or tomorrow but with

chemical hardening to compare.

Well done Andy! You have tackled this pretty well . I would just make one little criticism , you should use a water-bath when you make your gelatin solution, otherwise you can create little pockets of over-heated damaged gelatin particularly as you appear to be using an ordinary beer glass, you are perhaps lucky you have not thermally cracked it yet! An ordinary aluminium bowl is good as it does not affect your magnetic stirrer and distributes heat well enough around your glass.

I am really amazed at your results with only 10 seconds exposure even though you have a powerful 532 nm source. The science literature on the absorption spectrum of the dichromate ion is considered as utterly negligible. around 532nm .

I have run the dichromate spectrum myself this week and found the absorption indistinguishable from the background level. I am mystified how such photosensitivity is coming about. ..

Over 50 years ago , scientists carefully weighed and measured the wing spans of bumble bees and calculated they just should not be able to fly , but the bees did’nt know this. You and others who are doing this in the green without the aid of dyes are todays equivalent of those bumble bees!

andyl Mon Dec 12, 2005 5:38 pm #8
ok here are the results of my latest test...

I haven't performed the chemical test yet (started at midnight on friday > 4am)

I will post pictures soon as my camera is on loan for a few days. but will do

the best I can to describe what is happening.

The Parameters

1. Exposure Testing

Tested on PFG-04 and HomeDCG

5 - 10 - 20 - 40 sec exposures

Thermal Harden 45min at 100deg/c (based on slavich chart)

Cool to room temp

Water bath for 3min

100 % ISO for 3min @ room temp (aprox 22deg/c)

Quick dry with heat gun

Results (round 1)

PFG-04

5 sec plate - dim image / red orange in color

10 sec plate - dim image (slightly brighter) / red orange in color

20 sec plate - ok brightness image / red orange color

40 sec plate - ok brightness not much better than 20sec

HomeDCG

same results as above except image was green color.

None of these came out as bright as my first pfg-04 test, the only difference that I did on the previous attempt is to have the ISO at 40-50deg/c so I tried to re-process with ISO at 50deg/c. I re-soaked

in water for 3min then into ISO... the brightness was at least twice as

bright, no change in color, but slightly more fogging of the plate.

2. Hardening Time Test

after looking at the exposure tests, the 20sec appeared brightnest, as the

40 sec was showing some signs of burn-in. so the above test was repeated, using 20sec exposures, and ranging the hardening time as

15 - 30 - 60 min (didn't use 45 since that was the time on the first test)

PFG-04 Results

15min harden (20sec exp) - opaque white plate / no sign of hologram

30min harden (20sec exp) - slightly less opaque plate / no sign of hologram

60min harden (20sec exp) - decent bright holo / slight fogging on plate

HomeDCG results looked almost identical to the 45min harden tests

with the exception that there was noticably more fogging on the shorten

harden times.

I will try to post pictures soon, I might re-do the last testing, since it was

late and I was a bit tired, so I might of screwed somthing up, I was really

perplexd on having the first few plates turn completely opaque. It appears

that the gelatin is very temp sensitive to the ISO, and I'm thinking it's proportional to the degree of hardness. .. more as I digest all this.
