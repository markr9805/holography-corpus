Ed Wesly Wed Mar 19, 2008 4:39 pm #2
Optical Society of America is much older, probably from the beginning of the 20th century. SPIE, which stands for Society for Photo-Instrumentation Engineers is a product of the early '70's.

I used to belong to both, then dropped down to just SPIE, and now I don't think I will renew as I have not been getting anything out of their journals, which pretty much has gone all on-line. How can you read a monitor when you're on the toilet?

Kind of the way that the two groups are different is that OSA is allied with the American Physical Society, so it's more scientific, while SPIE is more for engineers. I used to enjoy going to the SPIE conferences, back when I had somebody to pay for them, and the SPIE Holography Working Group Newsletter comes out so irregularly that it doesn't make any difference.

There are papers on wierd applications of holography and esoteric recording materials, but the last thing that I got out of any of these journals that translated into me making a better hologram was in 1984, with the classic paper by Cook and Ward with their introduction of the CWC2 developer (which is better known as JD-2 and -3 developer here on the forum. Sorry to say TJ did not invent that formula, he was just shrewd enough to market the best!)
favalora Wed Mar 19, 2008 4:58 pm #3
Hi -

I am a member of both the SPIE and OSA. I was also a member of the IEEE, but dropped it, since I didn't attend the networking meetings & didn't get much enjoyment out of IEEE Spectrum.

I have a different observation than Ed's - the journals for OSA and SPIE that I subscribe to are in print. (That is, I can pick electronic or print.) For whatever reason, I prefer print. At the moment, I'm getting Applied Optics, Optical Engineering, and perhaps a few other things; the journals which are more "engineering" than "science." I also get Optics Letters, but that's definitely more "science" than "engineering."

If you are interested in applied holography, perhaps you should consider looking at or buying the annual SPIE Practical Holography conference proceedings. If you go to [http://SPIE.org](http://SPIE.org), click "publications," and search for "practical holography" you'll find it. And, of course, I need to plug the 3-D display conference, [SPIE Stereoscopic Displays & Applications](http://www.stereoscopic.org).

Membership feels expensive for a private researcher & is geared for those whose employers can pick up the tab.

-g

ps I will now duck out of the way for the flame-balls aimed at me for the "science versus engineering" thing.
