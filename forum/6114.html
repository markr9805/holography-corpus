Joe Farina Wed Mar 14, 2007 6:26 pm #2
You can't use an etalon unless you have access to the lasing cavity. So I wouldn't attempt it with a 532nm laser.

My advice is to save your money, and buy a 315, which has excellent power and proven holographic performace. There is another type of 532nm laser, as I recall made by Uniphase, and called the "microgreen" series or something like that. Supposedly, these are good for holography (has anyone on this forum tried one?). These are lower-powered (and cheaper) than a 315, but I still think the 315 is the best choice for power/performace vs. cost. Of couse, there is the Verdi, but the price is out of the ballpark. There is another type of 532nm laser made by Coherent, (is it called the "532" series?, I forget) that has a ring cavity and excellent coherence, but they must be extremely scarce on the surplus market, since I don't ever recall seeing one for sale.

As far as I know, these four laser groups (315, Microgreen, Verdi, and 532) are the only 532nm lasers which are good for holography. By "good" I mean pretty close to 100% reliability. Are there any others?

Ron Michael Wed Mar 14, 2007 11:30 pm #4
Without going too deep for you on this.

I agree with Joe, you will have difficulty with etalons also better known as Fabry Perot interferometers. First they have to be designed correctly and kept aligned and temperature tuned and held fairly constant temp. An example is to use two kinematic mounts and high concave reflectors to make a FPI. If they are not designed right they will allow multiple modes to pass anyway. You will lose alot of power and this is why when used inside of a laser cavity you have the gain of the laser also as a mode selector as well and the higher number of round trips of photons allows you to get by with a lower finesse (reflectivity) etalon. If you do use an external mode cleaner just remember you will lose a lot of power and your results may prove disappointing unless several are used with different spectral ranges. NOT an easy task but can be doable.

Junior Mon Apr 02, 2007 10:45 am #8
Hello!

An etalon can be used for longitudinal mode selection inside or outside of the cavity. Inside the cavity it supresses the other modes. Outside of the cavity an etalon is just a very expensive filter which selects one mode but costs you about 90% of your output. Then you could buy a HeNe laser as well.

The problem with the cheap DPSS modules is not coherence length, it is thermal instability, because these modules are not fitted with appropiate TEC coolers. An etalon does not fix this problem.

If you really want to start with 532 nm, try to get a second hand Compass 315.
