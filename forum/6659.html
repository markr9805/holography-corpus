Hello everyone

It's been a while since I've logged in but I have been busy, nontheless. What came to me was in the G307 method, the big problem was the glycerin in the plate getting oxidized by the dichromate. So I made a few plates with a mix of gelatin ,glycerin and some eosin (to hopefully sensitize the plate to green),mold coat and fan dry. When I want to use the plates I soak them in a 2% solution of potassium dichromate for 3-4 min,rinse in DIwater bath for 10 sec or so wipe off the back side of the plate and fan dry. So in 20 min ,you have a plate ready to go.

The nice thing is you can coat in daylight.![Image](http://farm3.static.flickr.com/2190/2190288748_8cc38faeef_m.jpg)

this was shot with a 1day old plate

12 min exposure w/20mw micro green

3min wash 25degree dip and 3 min in 70% and 99% IPA

![Image](http://farm3.static.flickr.com/2172/2190289068_7880507f6a_m.jpg)

this was shot on an 5 day old plate stored in a box in the basement with the same exposure & processing parameters as above

Now this is very interesting.--Well done. I had already in an earlier post today scrubbed out a footnote which mentioned that unlike MB dye you could use the dye erythrosin with acid dichromate . I scrubbed it because it invited the question of how much light is absorbed by the dye and how much is directly absorbed by the dichromate and the only point of using dye would be that it could allow less dichromate to be used and I did not have the answer on how much less dichromate you could use. But here you have gone and done almost exactly what I needed to know but using the sister dye eosin .

[Eosin ,erythrosin, are anionic dyes which tolerate acid dichromate but MB and Safranine 'O' are cationic dyes which don't and they preciptate.]

So a key question is how does your idea of using eosin with a just 2 % potassium dichromate bath compare photosensitivity-wise to using the G307 without dye and a 6% pot Di ? Your system after a 5 day delay is almost certainly better dark-reaction-wise. I do hope you will find the time to find more answers.

Hi Hans

Here is what I used:

96ml DIwater

12g gelatin

1.2g glycerin

5ml 0.4% eosin solution

What I wanted was sort of a "freeze dried plate" where everything but the sensitizer was in place,so I wouldn't have any dark reaction or needing to keep them in the refrigerator. Once you sensitize a couple plates, you warm up the laser and by the time they're dry you can start right up.

No, the Kdi and the di soaks do not wash out the eosin, in fact it still remains after processing(I may need less)

When I followed the standard mbdcg recipe using eosine,I got very poor results, with 20+min for a faint faint image. I also tried Rhodamin 6g and Acridine Orange in the mbdcg formula with the same bad results. All dyes were a 0.4% solution, general not microscopic purity

The eosine I have was a VERY old bottle I bought off ebay ;figure 4oz for 10 dollars-not bad.

the label said esoin (aniline red) if that helps .

I guess next would be to try plates with and without the eosine,to see if that helps or hurts;

the last time I tried the G307method, it just didn't work for me. Also try combining the eosin and the Kdi in one bath, but now the tint makes it easier to spot a bad plate

My money was on the acridine orange plates to work better, since it was a better sensitzer for green,maybe like eosine it needs to work in an acid medium like straight dichromate

Hope this helps

rob
