Hans Wed Dec 13, 2006 12:47 am #2
I often develop on the floor of my garage. I just put the trays in order on the floor and dip the plates in sequence. I don't worry much about dust during developing because I usually dry the plates in the house where there is less dust.

If you use MBDCG as your material, there should be no problem with long delays between exposing and processing. I am not so sure it is possible with silver halide material though. Here is a quote from the slavich web site about their [PFG-01](http://www.slavich.com/technical.htm):

> Latensification stabilizes and enhances the latent image formed by the holographic exposure. If required, chemical processing may be done with significant delay after latensification (~8 hours).
Tony Wed Dec 13, 2006 1:19 am #3
I've delayed development by a few hours with no apparent ill effects, on silver emulsions like BB-640 and PFG-01. I expose holograms in a middle school class, then take them home for processing.

MichaelH Wed Dec 13, 2006 4:04 pm #5
In the past I'd delayed development by an hour or so with no apparent ill effects. At the time I was using Kodak film.

As I understand it, latent image fading is material dependent. What film are you using?

In either case I'd suggest just setting up a static processing area and developing in your basement. You don't really need running water for silver-based processing and can pretty easily get away with using tubs of water for rinsing. You can always take the hologram up to a bathroom for a final rinse. You really only need to worry about dust when the film is drying or you're running a squeegee across it. Take the film up to a bathroom and put a clean box over it to keep dust out.
