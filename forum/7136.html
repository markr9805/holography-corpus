I have a variable circular ND filter I had used as a beam splitter and at numerous occasions had punched little irregularities in the metallic coating, in the order of 100-200mW at 514nm. In the end I switched to waveplates and pol. cubes, which has numerous advantages.

I set the attenuator up at a 45degree angle to reflect the beam 90 degrees. The attenuator was about 6 inches from my argon ion laser. wler Thu Feb 07, 2008 12:31 pm #11
I guess much depends on the coating, in fact there are several types. One thing I noticed, once I got suspicious, that the wheel I got absorbs quite a lot rather than reflects, which is bad since I wanted to use it as a variable splitter (and who has laser power to waste, eh?). I measured it once carefully, and I don't remember the result and the conditions any more, but roughly the losses were like 40-50% (adding reflected and transmitted powers). This means that a large portion of the power is absorbed in a small region, in the order of 1mm^2 within a very thin film, so no surprise that this causes damage. When running at high powers, like 250mW, I noticed even a moving pattern on the wall from reflections of the splitter.... that was the end of its use by me

And apart from the damage, also thermal expansion effects can be harmful for holography. So my few cents would be to avoid all absorbing/dissipating optics.

I noticed surprisingly large losses in plenty of other things too, like cube splitters and metallic mirrors. In a mildly complicated setup one can easily loose 50% of the light in this way. Best thing is using AR coated waveplates and dielectric cubes, this can give just a few % losses.
