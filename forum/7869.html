Colin Kaminski Thu Jun 11, 2009 7:12 pm #8
> Dutchelm05: Ahhh that is very clever, thanks ColinCan you explain how the still works? Do you heat the IPA and the evaporated stuff us pure IPA?

There are three ways I have distilled. One is to take advantage of the different boiling points of different liquids. One is to take advantage of different freezing points of different liquids. The third takes advantage of the different vapor pressures of different liquids.

In the freezing method you simply need to get it cold enough to freeze the water. I know for beer that happens at about 28F. If you remove the ice then you are "Jacking" the alcohol. I should mention that for beverage manufactures all three methods require a distillers license.

If you try the salt idea let me know how it works.
Tom B. Fri Jun 12, 2009 6:11 am #9
The problem with distilling IPA/water is that the two liquids form an azeotrope (the combination has a lower boiling point than either) so it

is not possible to get pure IPA from distillation. The ratio you end up with is 12.6% water, 87.3% IPA. For ethanol/water it is better: 4% water, 96% ethanol.

See [http://www.solvent--recycling.com/azeotrope_1.html](http://www.solvent--recycling.com/azeotrope_1.html)
