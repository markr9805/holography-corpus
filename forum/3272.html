With all the discussion on the Beam Shaper Optic thread ( [http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2168](http://holographyforum.org/phpBB2/viewtopic.php?t=2168) ), I was wondering who on the forum, beside Dinesh, has 1) successfully made an HOE other than a grating, and 2) successfully used that HOE for it's intended purpose.

I am still pretty new to the whole world of holography and I can only relate to the poor diffraction efficiencies I see in my own holograms. Granted, I only shoot in sliver, not DCG, so I'd expect a lower D.E., even if I could make it "perfect." I also understand from Dinesh's prev. posts that it takes a different(more technical) mindset to do HOEs.

But, I keep thinking if I ever made an HOE, say a lens or mirror with a specific focal length, I would need 10-100 times more laser power just to use it in a holography setup

Both of these questions are coming from the amatuers stand-point:

1) Is DCG our only hope with respect to "usable" HOEs?

2) In the long run, is the effort better spent just finding a way to do things with OTC lenses and mirrors, etc?

All the papers do make HOE's sound so fun and cool to do, but it's hard to see the real-world usage and practical limits...
BobH Fri Mar 25, 2005 6:57 pm #2
I make HOEs all the time. The most recent HOE I made for use in a pictorial hologram set-up was on a 30x40cm silver-halide plate using a 28mW He-Ne laser. One beam was at Brewster's Angle and collimated (off a tilted 17.5" parabolic mirror), and the other was close to normal incidence and rapidly divergent (from a 40X microscope objective in a spatial filter with 25micron pinhole and a double concave lens glued to its front end). The resulting Reflection HOE was used to rapidly converge the reference beam for an Image-planed Reflection transfer set-up.

There is nothing special about HOEs. They simply must be as clean and uniform as possible. Their specifications regarding aberrations, stability, cosmetics and performance all depend upon the application.
Dinesh Fri Mar 25, 2005 10:56 pm #3
As Bob says, it all depends on the application. In Bob's case, the application was display holography. In this case, you don't need high efficencies. The HOE's I used to make were for military and scientific uses so the efficency needed to be pretty high. In my case, there were additional factors like the spec for bandwidth and photopic/scotopic efficencies. Making a HOE for an application like Bob's is very different to, say, making one for a HUD in a fighter aircraft. A HOE I made for NASA for an orbiting telescope for instance, did require a lot of calculation while a HOE I made for a solar collector required almost no calculation.

If you simply wanted to make a HOE for a table setup in display, you can do it in Silver. However, I would say that you only need to do this if either the HOE carried out a function that would replace an expensive optical component or that the corresponding optical function was complex or impossible. For a simple mirror or lens, the conventional optical stuff is probably better. If only because a silver hologram will never get as efficent as a piece of glass and glass can be cleaned relatively easily.

If you want to make one just for the heck of it, come down to the lab and we'll set it up!
Ed Wesly Sat Mar 26, 2005 1:45 am #4
I have made a variety of HOE's in a variety of materials. The most fun ones were elements of essentially dedicated spectroscopes for a manufacturer of pharmaceuticals. The ones where we could get away with murder were for the glitter that's in the "Sally Hansen Magical Nail Makeup". The worst was for something called Holodar, a system for checking air turbulence. It was a four channel reflection grating, with an off-axis reference and an on-axis collimated beam with a certain kind of fringe system superimposed on it. We shot a bunch of 30 by 40 cm Agfa Holotest 8E75HD with a Watt of 780 nm from a Ti:Sapphire, and the plates still had some sensitivity up there!

The sky's the limit with HOE's. For examples of other things that can be done, see for instance "HOE for Holography" T. Jeong, E. Wesly, Proceedings of the First International Congress on Holography, Varna, Bulgaria,1989. A rather obscure SPIE volume, don't know the number, and I am not so sure where I have the paper!

There's no reason not to try to have fun making some in AgX; just be careful with processing to keep the noise down (like using a reversal bleach instead of rehalogenating) if you are looking through them.
