JohnFP Wed Apr 27, 2005 7:08 pm #2
Single beam reflection?

What diameter is beam spread at plate?

Number wise I am coming up with about 2 seconds for a beam spread of 7 inches in diamter at plate for single beam, not counting any losses.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------

For a film requiring 80 uJ per cm^2 = .08mJ per cm^2

7” beam = 248 cm^2

Laser putting out 10mw = 10mJ per second

10mJ per second /248 cm^2 = .0403mJ per cm^2 per second

.08mJ per cm^2/.0403mJ per cm^2 per second = 2 seconds

---------------------------------------------------------------------------------

How close is this to your calculations?

This is purely calculational and be patient for others that actually work with that material to advise you. I DO NOT WORK WITH SILVER. As I have been told, what is mentioned in the specs may not be practical.

MichaelH Wed Apr 27, 2005 8:01 pm #4
> alesnick: i've done a lot of work with diodes at 658nm on PFG-03

Which diodes? How much power?

> alesnick: so my instincts on exposure time are a bit off for my new 10mW 632.8 HeNe -- any thoughts for a 4 x 5 exposure on PFG-01

Is that the measured power on the laser or what is printed on the label? Depending on the age of the tube the power could be way off from what it's spec'd to put out. If it's the printed power, is that minimum or maximum?

If this is an old tube and you don't have a way to measure the output, you can't safely assume that the laser is still putting out 10mW and plug that into an equation to get exposure times. You're better off doing some exposure strips or making/purchasing a meter and comparing your new lasers output with your old. The comparison won't be 1:1 but it will be close enough.

> alesnick: I tried the equation, but I feel like i may have it wrong -- the only HeNe exposures i've ever done were with AGFA 2.5" plates and a 5mW HeNe a few years back.

Which equations are you referring to. If you'll link or post them here, we can check them.

> alesnick: Also, there will be some losses since i'm working with film sandwiched in glass.

True but if you're doing typical display work (shooting between 40-60 degrees reference) those losses won't matter that much as far as exposure time goes.
