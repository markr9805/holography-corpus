Spatial Imaging An established holography studio of some 15 years has teamed up with award winning holographer/ artist Patrick Boyd to start classes teaching holography at their studios in Richmond, London.

The courses will initially be aimed at beginners and will last for 5 days.

During this time attendees will learn the basics of holography. the theory and will learn how to make a variety of types of hologram including pulsed holography. The primary aim of this course is to provide students with sufficient practical & technical knowledge to develop their own interest in whatever direction they choose.

The style of the teaching will definitely be “hands on” with an emphasis on learning by doing. Apart from a basic intro to each type of hologram, any theory needed will be handled in parallel to the teaching.

The courses are planned to start in June and will run monthly. It is envisaged that there should be about 5 students per class. It is anticipated that a large percentage of students will be from abroad. Advanced & specialist courses will also be available at a later date.

Patrick Boyd has an MA in holography from the Royal College of Art and has won many awards including a Fulbright Fellowship and exhibited in many countries worldwide.

Spatial Imaging is a pioneer in the field of holography with expertise in many holographic processes. They are responsible for many world famous works and recently were a major component in making a holographic portrait of HRH Queen Elizabeth 2nd.

I read about the course in Ian Lancaster's "Holography News". It sounds a lot like the course I took under Edwina and Dave at Richmond. Althoug, of course, in 1981 we didn't do pulse.

By the way, Triple Take in San Diego ( [http://www.triple-take.com/](http://www.triple-take.com/) ) also do courses. They're only 2 days and we don't cover pulse. We cover theory and basic holography. The students go home with an H1, a Denisyuk and an image-planed reflection of their own model that they've made. We also do courses in DCG. Mostly it's couple of hours of theory and the rest "on the table". However, in the last course we had a student who was absolutely fascinated by light propagation theories. I ended up explaining complex refractive indices! Joy took the rest of the class to the table and he was happy sitting with me in our conference room learning about photons and waves!
