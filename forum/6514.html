JohnFP Wed Nov 14, 2007 1:32 pm #2
I am surprised you did not get a response on this yet. I don't have the math but I will explain what I think and others can chime it with the math hopefully. I am sure it can't be that difficult.

Well, let's take a look at this. A lot has to be considered here. Are the beams, ref and object, collimated and normal to plate? With a tranmisison this would be impossible. But if they are close enought we can look a the quick sumary below and negate the fact that they are not both exactly normal.

With both beam collimated and normal to plate, if there is no movement we get a good hologram. If the object moves 1/2 wavelength then we get constructive fringes where their was destructive fringes and destructive fringes where their was contructive. No hologram. If the object moves 1/4 wavelength then we get a dim hologram as there is less contrast beween contructive and destruct fringes.

But the problem with this simple example is the taking into account if either the beams are not collimated or if one or either of the beams are not perfectly on axis. Because then at that 1/2 wave movement of the object there may be a place on the plate the has the contructive where the destructive is and visa vera (total even average of light), but move down the plate some and the movement is only 1/4 wavlength with respect to reference, down the plate further and only 1/8 movement, down the plate further and we catch up on the next wave and the constructives are still were the contructives were without the movement and visa vera. So a really nice fringe at this spot down the plate, even though the object moved. This would give us a hologam with movement bands on it. Even if the object moved a few wavelenths, we could have this same scenerio but we would get more movement bands on it. So as we go the other way, less then a 1/2 wave movement and at about 1/4 wave movement, we start approaching a weakness of creating the fringes. That spot down the plate that allows the catch up to the next wave and allows a constructive fringe where the original contructive fringe was, never happens. All constructive fringes are dimmed to some degree. This results in a dim hologram and then as we get less and less movement, the fringes get more contrasty (less relative movement of the constructive fringe) to a bright hologram.

So I expained it the best I can and maybe someone here can post the exact formula's to allow you to calculate that number based on your rate of divergence of the two beam and their angle with respect to one another. It could be possible you are off by 1/4 to 1/8 of a wave and not really notice it in the hologam.

You may want to actually set your geometry up such that you get dark banding fringes on your object, then using the forumula with your divergence rates, angles and speed of rotation you can calcuate exactly the duration by counting the number of fringes on your object.
