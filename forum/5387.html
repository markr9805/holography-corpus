Today I made a H2 copy of a laserstransmissionhologram:

![Image](http://4c-s.org/v-web/gallery/albums/album16/dragon.jpg)

What I don't understand, is the color of the hologram. I used PFG-01 pretreated with 2% TEA. So the image should be between yellow and green.

Hi Matthias.

I can go as far as telling you that this effect is caused by what is described in Graham Saxby’s “Practical Holography” as ‘chirped fringes”

which means the fringe widths on the top surface differ from those lower down. The standard reply to explaining the cause is as Colin indicated…. processing technique.

But in your case I would hazard a guess that it may be more unusual.

as you indicate yourself.

I think you may actually have got your H2 object beam too strong (!) compared to the reference beam. You may have got “overmodulation” .

jeff
MichaelH Sun Feb 19, 2006 11:47 pm #6
I've had the same thing happen to me as Jeff describes. When the object light is very much brighter than the reference light. Check your light ratios. I'm betting you got them backwards so that the ref beam is much weaker than the object light.
