Greetings all,

One of my attractions to DCG is its high Diffraction Efficiency. I know there has been a lot of work and discussion on making DCG brighter. As a make holograms I look at them and wonder where are they relative to the ideal (not theoretical) brightness. I sent a hologram to John P once who determined it was 70% of what it could be. But that got me thinking if there was an easy way to quantify Diffraction Efficiency.

There are several reasons way I ask, one is I would like to know personally how bright my own stuff is. So if I say I changed the exposure, it was from 70% efficient to 75% I can say wow that was good.

But almost as important, as I read about experiments that others are doing and what sort of improvements are made. For example does during post exposure and storing the film in a dark place for 5 minutes vs. 10 minutes does it affect the Diffraction Efficiency by 1% or 10%?

Perhaps these are all rhetorical questions without answers, but as a beginner, I wonder what variables results in the most change in brightness. Photos cannot communicate this nor can words it seems. I also know the object and light source play a role.

So to boil my rambling down to a question, it there an easy way to quantify Diffraction Efficiency?
BobH Wed Nov 07, 2007 11:06 pm #2
I'd recommend making holograms of a mirror instead of a diffuse object. Then, it will be easy to measure DE and shrinkage while experimenting with film, chemistry or process. Going back to objects when you've found good results will only mean changing beam ratio or other fine tuning.

JohnFP Thu Nov 08, 2007 12:01 pm #5
I have never done it but usually DE can be measured by measuring the thru light. This is usually done with another laser but for DCG that is broadband a white light world be preferable, so I will add to Bob's suggestion;

Do as Bob stated and try this also. You could use both of these with minimal change in set up and compare to see how close each final measuremet is to one another. Bob, what do you think of the below technique?

Point the spotlight to a light meter (a photocell connected to a voltmeter will do as the measurements are only relative) a few feet away. Then place the hologam between the spotlight and meter perpendicular to the light. The fall off of the meter at perpendicular will be the scatter and noise and milkiness of the hologram. Then adjust (tilt or rotate) the hologram such that the hologram is the brightest visually. The meter should fall again. The difference from the perpendicular to optimal view angle should be the DE of the hologram. You want the difference between no hologram in set up to perpendicular to be the smallest and the difference between the hologram being perpendicular and then at the correct veiwing angle to be the geatest. It is said that DCG holograms can approach 97% DE. Remember, this is for a reflection.

For transmissions the process is much easier.

I may do the same thing as this is a great idea, and measure a few of mine. Boy Tony, I like your style and thinking!!!
JohnFP Thu Nov 08, 2007 12:40 pm #6
Let me say that the only way to get a very high efficiency DCG reflection hologram is to increase the object lighting.

Either by make the DCG reflection from a master or

Adding additional lighting from the spillover light in a SBR or

Make a hologam of a mirror which will reflect most of the light without randomly polarizing the object light.
Joe Farina Thu Nov 08, 2007 2:28 pm #7
As for the DCG holograms I've made thus far, I didn't bother to cover the edges of the glass, so I got those familiar bright bands due to total internal reflection. The first bands, near the edges of the plate, are very bright, then they rapidly get dimmer towards the center. (I don't know if it makes any difference, but my emulsion was facing up.)

So, I assume the brightest of those bands (and they are dazzlingly bright) are near the limit of diffraction efficiency as far as beam ratio is concerned, because it should be pretty close to 1:1. Thus far, those bands are almost invariably very bright, even when the image is dim, so this reinforces what John said about the importance of object lighting. But during my last tests, I got some amazingly bright single-beam Denisyuk holograms. They are similar in brightness to an image-planed broadband DCG hologram that I have by August Muth. (These are the ones mentioned previously, that seemed to have a blue stain on the emulsion.)
BobH Thu Nov 08, 2007 3:01 pm #8
Measuring the extinction of light transmitted through the hologram is good, but you have to account for surface reflections, absorption of material and stain from processing, scatter, and higher orders. If using a laser, the angle, spectral bandwidth and color of the hologram will affect the measurement. Measuring extinction efficiency to indicate diffraction efficiency is best done with a spectrometer and white light with the hologram mounted on a rotation stage to optimize the angle of incidence.
