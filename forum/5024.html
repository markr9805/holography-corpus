Has anyone tried to store a page of information in a 1mm x 1mm square using holography. That is, take a page of infomation, lets say a complete web page from a computer. Have this page output to a tranmission LCD screen. Split your laser. Have one go through the screen capture the image and have it projected on a diffusing screen. The image on the diffusing screen will be the object beam. Then have the reference beam only the diameter of the non expanded laser beam. Set this up in a tranmission configuration. Make the hologram. Then when a raw (same size as reference) beam hits that part of the plate from the pseudo side the page of information would be projected on a wall, focused at the distance the diffusing screen was from the plate.

Obviously the next step it to put over 600 pages in one square inch.

Then this one inch square hologram is mounted on the front of a small laser pointer which has a static x-y tension mount. Anytime a page is needed the hologram in the mount can be slide to the correct corrosponding page and projected on the wall. Somewhat like microfiche but without the bulky projector.

My real question is, is there a lens needed between the diffusing plate and the emulsion in the recording? If no lens is used then the page is going to have a specific focus distance in the replay. If a lens is used will the replay hologram have a wider depth of field such that the distance from the hologram to the wall would be more fogiving?

Ed Wesly Fri Sep 02, 2005 1:36 pm #4
I saw the Chinese system and wrote an article on it for holosphere. It's pretty much the idea that you suggested. To add more info, they had several reference angles, so that the same locale on the plate could have different images flying out at different angles in reconstruction. The demo plate I saw had the Chinese character and the English word at the same place, at 90 degrees to each other. (I'm pretty sure, this was almost 20 years ago!)

They had their own ultra fine grain Soviet style emulsion that they were selling, and the recommended developing was something like 15 minutes in Kodak D-76 diluted 1:41. The plates were covered with lots of colloidal red dots. TJ probably has them in his collection.

Need a nice diffuser behind the transparency or LCD screen, and no lens is necessary between the image and the holo-plate. It's like some of the earliest pre-laser pre-three-dimensional object holograms of Leith and Upatnieks, a diffusely backlit transparency with an off-axis reference beam, but smaller.

Since the real image will be projected with an undiverged laser beam, there shouldn't be too much of a focussing problem. There will be plenty of depth of field, as you are interrogating the hologram at a relatively high f/number. However, resolution becomes an issue, as the information can quickly get lost in speckle at high f/numbers!

I hope that I won't be breaking your heart by telling you that all this was thought up before. But this was one of those holographic solutions of the classical age of the '60's, where it was predicted that the whole Library of Congress could be stored using in four filing cabinets holographically! (I'm still waiting!) And don't forget that HAL, the renegade computer of 2001, had a holographic memory, that idea probably based on those kinds of news tidbits! (Really is worthwhile to check out '60's magazine articles on holography!)

As usual, if anyone would like a copy of the holosphere article, I could probably scan it and e-mail it if requested.
