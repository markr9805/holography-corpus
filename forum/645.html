It's been pretty quiet on this forum lately, so I thought I'd post something. I've been working on an apparatus to make a hologram of a small passage in a cave. What I'm trying to do is have the laser, shutter and beamsplitter as one unit and another unit consisting of the plate holder, reference support and object illuminating diffuser with the two units connected together with two fibers. In my earlier fiber work I just shot the laser straight into the fiber which is okay for a low power reference beam but for higher powers the ferrule of the fiber sometimes gets burned while alingning it. I spent a lot of this weekend trying to make something to launch the beam into the fiber with a ball lens. It seems like a simple idea but nothing is ever simple.

Oh, yes. About being quiet. I got laser and other parts as a gift, and I promised keeping posting the results on the forum. But had bad luck with controller (this part is solved by using my own one instead), and with JDSU laser head. Tried everything: changing diode (although original diode working good), tried adjusting all what I can adjust, and only one thing is adjustable: KTP crystal. No more than 2 mW out in green.

Spent a lot of time and effort to bring to life this laser head, but seems that 1064 nm cavity is misaligned. And, there is big problem; OC mirror with its holder is glued over three holes where Allen screwdriver (I noticed torx screwdriver may fit too) should has free access. If I remove (unglue) this mirror, then whole adjusting will be nightmare because OC mirror should be displaced far away.

If I move KTP crystal, cavity goes into some resonant mode, but then KTP crystal is at bad phase matching angle. Not only that; it shows all modes ranging from TEM00 to TEM09 and higher, where TEM03 is somewhat stronger than other modes, but TEM00 is the weaker one.

By moving pump diode, getting somewhat better result, but not close enough. Tried to make FAC lens. Pulling out one borosilicate glass into fiber, cca. 0.8 mm wide, cut and polished one side. After gluing close to the emitter, I am getting corrected fast axis, but slow axis remains uncorrected. Situation doesn't change much, except this time TEMxy mode is spread in bit different orientations. Power is the same, no more than 2 mW in green.

Aside that, made lot of mess in my lab. Built laminar flow hood, using two fans (one 20W, and additional one of 40 W pushing through first one to get maximum air flow). Now is time to disassemble one gramophone, rise up speed from 45 to some 90 or 100 RPM, make holder for it, make some safelight and everything else in such claustrophobic space inside hood. Not easy task compressing everything in. I am so close to making nice and clear emulsion, but laser part and 'camera' part waiting for improved design (multi-beam setup, one referent beam, and at least two object beams).

I have also plan to use 6 um or 9 um single mode fibers, and perhaps will get into same trouble aligning it.

Only option is to use improvised microscope method;

Three element lens (I have no ball lens), put close to the fiber, and then with telescope looking to the barrel of the fiber end. Once lens/fiber are adjusted, then remove telescope (one 'eye' of the binocular), and replace it with laser at the same position where telescope was. This way, I may get crude alignment. Fine alignment will be done after with decreased power (to avoid burning ferrule).

This is one option. Second option is to use two separate platforms (it will be good to use single one, but have no material for that), design first one for three CW lasers and one pulsed (not even close to finish, need crystalizing and polishing KDP crystals). For green, will use 808 nm pump diode mounted onto separate holder with collimating lens, vanadate/KTP hybrid crystal on another holder, both temperature controlled (to avoid mode hopping). Adjust the best temperature points of both; pumping diode and hybrid crystal in order to get maximum coherence length.

If coherence length will be poor, then my plan is to made second platform with set of mirrors (actually mirrors on both platforms) so that path length of the reference beam and light that come from the object is the same or at least close.

The problem here is that two separate platform will vibrate differently when some outdoor vibration hit the room. Fringe locking is one option, but not sure how to deal with three colors (RGB) if one day decide to make panchromatic holograms (AgX or DCG). Since all three colors will be send over single beam splitter for reference beam, I can't move this mirror (or other parts) for all three colors. Only option it so change optical path of the object beam(s).

Pulsed laser has no any problems with fringe locking. Only problem is partially damaged passive Q-switch, which gives me non-uniform gaussian profile (wavefront distortion). Also, train of pulses are unpredictable - don't know whether this Q-switch permit some amount of light to re-circulate so that each next high peak power pulse are in the same phase with previous one. Even I am not sure that this is important at all. If not, then each pulse will make interference fringes on the holo-plates on it's own. I may chose low pump level so that just one pulse is produced, but then I am not sure whether it will be enough for DCG (for AgX is maybe okay). Maximum input energy allowed for this laser is 20 J (earlier I am forcing over 50 Joules, and that partially bake passive Q-switch). With 20 J input, 1% getting out at 1064 nm (that is about 200 mJ with non-Q-switched laser), maybe 0.1% per pulse inside train of pulses (maybe 20 mJ per one pulse).

What else... I have also problem with stove - aside fly ash, it pull in dust into room when fire. That issue can be solved by temporary using electrical heater when doing emulsion and holograms (here in Croatia electricity is very expensive). One crude filter doing its job nicely, but this stove ruin whole effort faster than filter can clean air.

One more thing: bull's eye filter. Multiple choice: or making B/W film mask, or coat some metal onto glass plate and insert in beam path of the reference beam (object beams are not important). Second choice is bit better, but require careful planing: each laser will has its own bulls eye mask, down to 1 mm wide (depend of laser beam output width).

Final cleaning will be done with spatial filter/pinhole. There may be problem too, if I use single element lens (achromatic problem, having three different focal points).

I am hopping get first hologram in about few days.

At first, I will chose the 'faster' option, just to get back on the track. Currently having only 532 nm laser (will try get up to 70 mW green), soon will arrive 450 nm and 650 nm laser diodes (cca 50 mW). Even for precise temperature adjusting - missing temp. sensors (it will arrive soon, I hope). Without sensors, laser may has unpredictable coherence length behavior and mode hopping.

More later...
