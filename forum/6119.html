An idea soon to be posted on [http://www.Holovision.TV](http://www.Holovision.TV)

Holograms are much different than drawn art. No matter how realistic the painting or sketch, it just doesn’t jump out like the dynamic movement of a hologram.

That may all change soon. Recent advances in microelectronics and displays have lead the way to electronic paper. Electronic paper should not be confused with digital paper, which requires a digital pen to input data. Electronic paper is presently twice the thickness as “real” paper but slightly less flexible. E-paper is flexible enough to make roll-up digital displays and may soon provide print media the ability to add video images.

One possible future is that E-paper will merge with computer-generated holograms. The pixels in E-paper are seen by reflecting light rather than having backlighting. Nano-scale organic transistors may take the role as interference patterns. Within years, E-ink may essentially become hologrammatic.

DrOutlier Fri Mar 23, 2007 3:00 am #5
8) The idea is on YouTube, Revver and Netscaoe video news. Hopefully by this weekend it'll also be on my site. That's about as open as I can make it. The idea is also simple enough: There's no backlighting so the information displayed on a piece of electronic paper is seen by reflected light. Computer-generated fringes should cause interference patterns.

The prototyping kit from eink.com costs about $3000, So Team Holovision probably won't submit one to the next holographyforum contest. We can only afford the slightly-advanced shoebox setup. I am sure it will be attempted soon. The only problem is that the research direction is mostly toward displays. It's one thing to store computer-generated fringes. Without a standard way of holographically recording real events and efficient broadcasting of said information we are just performing an old trick in a new way. Compounded by the average holographer's bias that holographic television will require the brute force of microprocessors, this technique will take a long time to reach full maturity.
