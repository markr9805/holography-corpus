Hey Dutchy,

You want two 1/2 wave plates and one POLARIZING cube beam splitter. Here is the reason.

A polarizing cube reflects S waves and transmits P waves. So if your laser is outputting P waves you will get no reflection. So unless you want to rotate your laser up to 90 degrees in very small increments, you need a 1/2 wave plate to rotate the polarization of the beam before it enters the cube. This is acutally (the 1/2 wave plate) the optic that is turned to adjust the ratio of light. When it is rotated such that your beam polarization is at 45 degrees (half way between S and P), you will have half the light reflected and half transmitted. Now for an H1 to H2 copy, as we know, polarization has to be in the same direction. If they are perpendicular to one another (as they will be after a polarizing cube) then you will get no interference at all. So now, after the beam splitter, in one of the paths (reflected or transmitted) you need another 1/2 wave plate such that you can rotate the polarization of that beam to match the polarization of the other beam.

This is an expensive set up and unless you are really really serious about holography, I would just buy a variable density Beam Splitter.

But make sure if you use this you get a mount that is dead tight, or as you rotate it, you reflected beam will walk. I think that is the biggest advantage of a cube and wave plates, the beams stay dead on when you tweak ratios. Great for production but not really necessary for hobbiest.

I have been using a cheap surplus variable beam attenuator but have to deal with the ghost beam. You could always get a cheap variable attenuator, and index matching glue a thick piece of glass on it such that the ghost beam if separated far enough away from the primary beam soas you can block it off. About 10% loss with glass.

JohnFP Wed Feb 25, 2009 8:50 pm #7
Gosh for years I used two antireflective plate beam splitters from Edmuds, very cheap, a 30r/70t, 50r/50t and a thick piece of glass (10r/90t). I would set up my talble with the 30/70 and see what the ratios were always using the cleaner transmitted beam for the reference beam in an H1 and the object beam in an H2. If I needed more transmitted light I would switch to the glass 10/90 or less transmitted ligtht I would switch to the 50/50. To really fine tweak the set up, I would either move the spatial filter in or out a bit, or add a/few piece(s) of glass in one of the beams to down it a smidge.

A little labor intensive but much much cheaper. At under $100 for each of the BS from Edmunds I was set for years. I cut each one into 2 pieces thus I had 4 beam splitter for about $50 each. Glass price was negligible.

What I also did was glue each of the three types on a single gimbal next to each other sticking out the top. Then to switch I would just move the mount over to pass through the one I wanted. If you look in this photo you can see what I am talking about. It is the 2nd optic from the right. You can see the three BS glued to and sticking out the top of the gimbal. This set up only required a slight gimbal adjustment to get the reflected beam back to where it was after the entire mount needed to be move to switch to a differt BS.
