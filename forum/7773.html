...and also cannot find a tips and tricks section any more, so I'll post my hot new tip here:

Make a superferometer! This is my silly name for what I slapped together here. I've just gotten my new table up, and have been doing some basic interferometer testing (straightforward Michelson), and certainly got a sense of what (and it's not much) hassles my fringes...

- but -

A basic interferometer only tells the secrets of the beam splitter and the two mirrors in use. The Superferometer I built uses all the optics to be used in my imaging.

Do this: Remove your objective lens and pinhole from spatial filter. Aim your collimating mirror straight back the way it came. By the time it passes back through your beam splitter, it'll be rather large, but works just fine. Now turn your object beam back on itself. Make sure you are using all of the relay mirrors that your imaging setup will be.

A little fine tweaking and you can watch your two holography beams fringe out. It will likely be much more sensitive than a regular interferometer. Vibration will be very obvious and suddenly air currents you might not have noticed before will be effecting your setup in a very visible way.

Potentially useful in troubleshooting and actually kinda fun to build
