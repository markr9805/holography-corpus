I was wondering if there's a commercial component that allows a single lens to be installed in a "microscope objective" type of mounting.

The problem with microscope objectives, in my experience, is that they are really lossy. The Edmund mounted singlets look much better, the only problem is that the lower limit on focal length on these is 5.5mm, which I think corresponds roughly to a 40X objective. I'm looking for more beam expansion than this.

If this kind of mounting doesn't exist, maybe I'll try to build one myself.
JohnFP Thu Feb 07, 2008 12:20 pm #2
Joe, quite a while ago I wanted to do the same thing. I took an old damaged objective and cut the threads off just above the threads after removing the optics. I then simply epoxied the singlet right to the cut part of the objective (after sanding and smoothing). It is one of my favorite optics as it is always easily cleanable. Also, it was to satisfy the need for a 2-3x objective.
Joe Farina Thu Feb 07, 2008 12:52 pm #3
Thanks, John, that seems like the best way to go, just buy a cheap used objective, cut it (with a hacksaw?) and attach a metal disk with the lens.

Joe Farina Thu Feb 07, 2008 4:59 pm #6
Hey Tony, yes I wanted a single lens (mounted in objective threads) with very short focal length, maybe equivalent to the 60X to 100X range. I have quite a few lenses that I'm experimenting with. This is for another purpose right now, as I'm trying to change the diameters and divergence of my green and blue beams by using a two or three lens combination (i.e., a telescope or zoom telescope). I've found that it's hard to visualize what's happening with a laser beam interacting with two or three lenses, so I'm doing it in a water tank with a small amount of red watercolor paint added (for use with a HeNe) that really shows what's happening. It's pretty interesting, and I would recommend (to people who are interested) taking a 10-gallon aquarium and filling it with water (with a tiny dab of watercolor paint so that the rays will show up very clearly in the water). Then the lenses can be placed under the water, and the effects can be seen very clearly.

What I'm trying to do is get red, green, and blue beams through an aperture which is about 8 feet away from the point where the beams are combined originally. The "aperture" being the point where I intend to place the spatial filter. So, not only do the diameters of the beams need to be the same (say for example 2mm) but the divergence of each needs to be pretty similar if the spread beam is to be uniform white (I think). It's a rather thorny problem, becasue I'm dealing with 3 beams that have different characteristics. The HeNe has the least divergence, the MG has the most (plus it's elliptical and maybe astigmatic?) and the 315 is in between. Plus, according to information on the Wiki, the beams at the aperture should not have exactly the same diameter, so this needs to be capable of fine adjustments.

I'm trying to simplify things be "matching" the green and blue beams to the HeNe. I don't intend to use any beam-shaping optics with the HeNe at all, because it has almost ideal beam quality, and becasue red is the most valuable color (unless someone has a Krypton laser ) and I don't want to waste any with intermediate optics.
