Joe Farina Tue Jan 30, 2007 1:30 pm #10
> JohnFP: MBDCG can work with high power red lasers.I think this may be a little misleading. The MBDCG can also be used with low power red lasers if the hologram is small.

Yes, John is quite right, MBDCG holograms can be made with low-power lasers, and I've done it myself with a 5mW HeNe.

As long as Denisyuk holograms are made in a small format, say for example 1 X 1, or 2 X 2 inches, then some very nice holograms should be possible. The exposure times will be long however, 5 minutes probably for the 1 X 1's, and maybe as long as 10 to 15 minutes for the 2 X 2's. (These are just rough approximations.)

Now if you consider the beautiful holograms that August Muth has been able to make in this small format, and combine this with some of the stunning work shown on this forum with MBDCG, it's pretty exciting, I must say. All that is needed is a meaure of dedication (to do a lot of trial-and-error work). The laser must not mode-hop, but at these low power levels, I guess that's less of a concern (for the smaller laser diodes). Longer wavelength laser diodes will probably be an advantage also, as MBDCG appears to be more sensitive to slightly longer wavelengths.

If anyone is reluctant to make small holograms, let me say this: Size is immaterial. If something is small, you move close. If something is large, you move further away. End of story.

Joe Farina Tue Jan 30, 2007 8:10 pm #12
On the subject of "small and large," it is true however that a sculpture can be either too large or too small, but that is really the fault of the sculptor. For example, if a near-sighted sculptor makes something while looking as close to it as possible, including fine details, and then shows it to a far-sighted person who cannot focus nearly so close, then those details will be lost.

Small sculpture often has a certain appeal not shared by large sculpture. And from a sculptor's point of view, smaller works typically require less time, because shapes, textures, details, etc., need only to be suggested when they are in a small format. When size goes up, other complications arise also.

Sculpture can be as small as you wish, as long as it's not a struggle to look at. Just because something is big enough to break your toe if it falls on you doesn't make it any better.

Michael and Colin, I surely hope neither of you take my opinion as any type of disrespect or attack. It is after all just my opinion. I simply feel strongly about dicouraging, yes even beginnners, about home made DCG or MBDCG emulsions. I guess it is a matter of what we are familliar with. I am comfortable with DCG and know how to make an object to plate interface stable whereas you may be famillar with the sandwiching technique required by film. A novice has to master one or both. So.......

I remember when I first started I started with film. Not knowing much at the time I struggled and it wasn't until 10 or so attempts that I first got an image, dim, but an image. Now, as soon as I switched to glass plates I had much better sucess. Home made plates, being made on glass, are naturally more stable.

So is making MBDCG on glass with 10 minute exposures harder then doing 3 second exposures on sandwiched film??? Not sure, but again, a single beam reflection with the plate lying right on the object negates most of the long exposure difficulties but will not negate film movement.
