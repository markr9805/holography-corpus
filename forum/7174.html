Kris Meerlo and I experimented today with a homemade dry pohotopolymer formula. Tansmission gratings were easily visible and at first we thought that the resoultion was too low for reflection work. But after the fifth try at a Denisyuk Kris noticed that quite suddenly the third hologram started showing a reflection image as well as the transmission image that was visible from the start. At the moment the results are a little too dim to make photo's but as soon as we have better results, we will publish. We did not invent the formula our selves, but used a formula from an old Chinese article. It contains PVA+Acrylamide+Acrylic Acid+TEA+MB. I will send the article to those interested. Send me a pm.
Hans Mon Feb 25, 2008 9:47 am #2
I am trying to figure out why reflection holograms only show up in the polymer some minutes after exposure and then slowly disappear again. Could this be because it takes much more time for the monomers to migrate from the unexposed fringes towards the exposed fringes than it takes for the exposed fringes to polymerize?

Here's the complete recipe of the polymer for those who want to experiment:

- 100ml DI water (Cold temperature)

- 5gram PVA (Needs to be 9000 Molecular weight or lower and near 100% hydrolized)

- Give the PVA some time (perhaps an hour) to swell in the cold water.

- Heat the mix to about 90C. PVA will dissolve quickly now.

- Cool the mix to room temperature again.

- Add 2,4gram of Acrylamide. It will dissolve quite easily.

- Add 0.45gram of Methylene Bisacrylamide.

- Add 2ml of Acrylic Acid.

- Add 0.2ml of TEA.

- Add 1ml of a solution of 0.4% Methylene Blue in water.

- Spread a few ml on a clean horizontal piece of glass until the liquid just touches the edges. The glass does not to be pre-treated because there is no processing required after exposure.

- Wait for the emulsion to dry.

- Expose. It seems to need a lot of light. We have not determined optimum exposure times yet.

Diffraction efficiency is really low for reflection work. I guess that the resolution of this mix is barely touching what is required for pretty Denisyuks.

But... Imagine our great surprise and immense pleasure when suddenly images started showing in the holograms. It was like seeing a Polaroid picture for the first time again.

I now want to try to further develop this mix. If anyone has any suggestions for modifications, I will be happy to try them and post the results on the forum.

Hey Hans, let's think about this. If indeed the polimer shrinks, then with a transmission in which the fringes are nearly perpendicular to the plate, this is not much of a problem. The exposed areas shrink and right next to them the unexposed areas do not shrink. I would imagine that if the emulsion is too think, you may start to see the shrinking areas pull closer together all the fringes as they shrink resulting in a blue shift. But this can be many wavelengths thick

With a reflection hologram in which the fringes are nearly parallel to the plate, when the shrinking occures in the constructive fringes it draws closer together all fringes during exposure unil the constructive fringe is now where the destructive fringe was. So I would imagine you would want a very very short, possibly pulsed exposure if your emulsion is too thick or a very thin emulsion.

So with that, have you tried a Denisyk with an emulsion thickness of only a couple of wavelengths? You could use the quick and rapid spin method of the old resist technique to get a couple/few micron thickness emulsion.
