is it possible to use a multiline (6 lines) argon laser to make holograms?

Beam is TEM00 with polarization ratio of 500:1 and total output power of 130mW (JDS sealed tube).
JohnFP Fri Nov 04, 2005 12:41 pm #2
While some will say no, I say yes with a couple of stipulations.

I made a hologram with all lines from my Argon Ion water cooled laser. I used the broadband mirrors and no prism. I also used an etalon for coherence length. I know the laser was putting out more then one line every with the etalon in place as I tested the "all lines" with an interferometer and looked at the lines after an external prism. The only problem I could see with the laser you are trying with is coherence length and line width. You could alway feed the laser with all lines through an interferometer and see what you get. Here are some photos.

Scroll about half way down the page under Newest Holograms and click on Multi Colored Paints and Multi Colored Paints Broadband Process. These are holograms made using all lines. Then under CLICK ON IMAGE TO ENLARGE, click on both Multi Color Fringes. These are the fringes I was getting will running the muliline laser through a Michaelson interferometer. You can see the 514 and 488 lines independently in the interferometer fringes.

[http://www.sketchys.com/Holograms/myholograms.htm](http://www.sketchys.com/Holograms/myholograms.htm)

Aagain try looking at all the lines through an interferometer. At first start with each leg of the interferometer exacly the same length. Then graduall increase the length of one leg till you don't get fringes any more. This will also give you an idea of how deep an object you can use.

Good luck!!!
Ed Wesly Fri Nov 04, 2005 4:14 pm #3
It sounds like this laser is one of those air-cooled Argons where the mirrors are sealed to the tube from the description above. This may be the downfall of using it, as there is no way of putting an etalon in it.

There really is not much of a chance to get a hologram with a water cooled argon without an etalon. These shorter tubes like the one above may not be building up too many modes. Some sort of test needs to be done to check its coherence, a Fabry-Perot Spectrum Analyzer is best, then a Michelson.

I know of only one air-cooled argon with etalon, produced byt ILT in the early '90's. We had a loaner at the Art Institute and got decent blue and green images on Ilford plates. But this laser is unique. I am not so sure if this company even exists, or was bought up, who knows.

There is no doubt that you can get holos when the laser is running multi-line as has been discussed here before, it's just in this case I don't give it a high possiblity of success in this case due to lack of coherence.

I make holograms all the time with both small and large frame argon lasers running without etalons in them. They are small, and HOEs, but they're still holograms! It's just that the coherence length is short, maybe a couple of inches for the small frame, and an inch or so for the Sabre.

wler Sun Nov 06, 2005 11:08 am #7
I made a few holograms with argon lasers without etalon at first (one air cooled, the other water cooled) - the coherence length was in both cases about an inch (getting better at low power and worse at high power - I had to tune down the air cooled one from 30 to less than 10mW). This requires a very careful matching of path lengths. Moreover the air drafts and vibrations from an air-cooled argon head make life very tough, best is to put it into another room and shine it through a small hole.
