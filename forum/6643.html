Yes I brought this matter up in my paper at the 2006 Symposium in Wales . ( [http://www.holographer.org/](http://www.holographer.org/)

Basically Colin the G307 system for green light needs acidic dichromate with as high a concentration as possible without getting crystallization whereas

the "panchromatic" plan with MB needs alkaline "dichromate" which means actually Chromate salt in really low concentration because it is now not supposed to absorb any green or red light itself but to act as a rejuvenator of light bleached dye and in so doing it gets reduced to CrIII and thus crosslinks the gelatin. You cannot use the MB dye with acidic dichromate unfortunately so you need a green absorbing dye which is photobleachable just like MB under mildly alkaline conditions using chromate ------ the dye I found is Safranine 'O' .

Erythrosin works too on its own but may be less compatible with MB .

Jeff

Danny Bee Tue Jan 22, 2008 12:22 am #14
here is a Panchromatic DCG formula

i recived this from Hans

Here are the details given in the paper:

1. Soak 2g of French inert gelatin in 40ml deionized water at 25C for 12 hours.

2. Heat the suspension in a thermostatic water bath to about 45C, then keep the temperature and stir the mixed solution slowly for 15 minutes.

3. Add 2.5ml of 0.5% potassium chromate solution while stirring at 45C for 2 minutes.

4. Add 0.6ml of 25% TMG solution while stirring at 45C for 2 minutes.

5. Adjust the pH value to 9.18 with TMG or acetic acid solution at 45C.

6. Add 0.3ml of 0.4% MB solution to the suspension while stirring at 45C for 5 minutes, then add 0.3ml of 0.2% R6G solution to the suspension while stirring at 45C for 5 minutes.

7. Pipette out 8ml of the mixed solution and spread it over 8X24cm2 leveled optical glass, keep it in dark and dry in horizontal position at 25C for 24 hours.

Processing:

a) Soak in F5 solution at 25C for 1 minute

b) Wash holographic plate in running water at 25C for 30 seconds

c) Swell holographic plate in warm water at 31C for 1.5 minutes

d) Dehydrate in 60% isopropyl alcohol bath at 25C for 1 minute

e) Dehydrate in 90% isopropyl alcohol bath at 25C for 1 minute

f) Dehydrate in 100% isopropyl alcohol bath at 25C for 2 minutes

g) Dry hologram rapidly with flowing hot air

My note: I assume the "F5 solution" is Kodak F-5 Hardening Fixer, which according to one of my books, contains sodium thiosulfate 240g, sodium sulfite 15g, acetic acid 28% 48ml, boric acid 7.5g, potassium alum, dodecahydrate 15g, water to make 1 liter.

Hans Tue Jan 22, 2008 2:26 pm #20
You are right about the F5 formula. It works nice wonders on MBDCG in that it clears the MB out of the plate very quickly and gives the gelatin a hardening bias. The hardening bias allows higher swelling bath temperatures, decreases milkiness, and reduces chances of patches of different colour in the hologram.

The disadvantage is that a 100ml bath is exhausted after only two plates. I found that giving the plate a uniform exposure of one seventh the normal exposure time, followed by a longer water wash (to remove all traces of MB and TMG) gives similar results.

I don't know if the F-5 would be required for panchromatic DCG, where Rhodamine 6G is used for the green sensitivity.
