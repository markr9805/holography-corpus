JohnFP Thu Feb 24, 2005 11:50 am #13
Well, then why use a sandwich structure at all? I think the key is to have the entire table move as one solid, rigid structure in the presence of any movement at all. Thus if a vibration was propogating from ground from the East the East leg would transfer the vibration first to the table. If the table was not rigid this would cause a wave(s) to travel down the table from the East leg to the West leg, the table itself would be the wave. If the table is rigid the entire table would move but not as a wave but a stiff board riding that wave. All components on the table would remain fixed relative to one another. Weight (mass) causes a dampening of that wave. More mass, more wave energy is needed to move that mass a specific distance.

I think so.It is the same question as "Do we need to glue the edge of the cans together in a can table?" If you imagine the top plate moving in an oposite direction from the bottom plate (shear) then having cans glued together on their sides will make them less likely to tip over. Honey comb is not so extreme of an example but I think some glue would be in order. Microbaloons are cheap and easy to work with.
