For panchromatic DCG (Zhu et al., also see the excellent video from the symposium in China provided by Kaveh), Kodak F5 Hardening Fixer is used during processing (after exposure and just before swelling in water and dehydrating in alcohol).

Hans has used this fixer to good effect with MBDCG, and posted his results on the forum. He felt that the good properties were the result of 1) the fixer clearing out residual MB, and 2) some hardening which helped to produce cleaner and more uniform results.

I'm getting ready to try the F5 fixer, but it has a number of components (sodium thiosulfate, sodium sulfate, acetic acid, and potassium alum). As we've discussed on this forum at length, it would be nice to know what's happening in a fixing/hardening step, and standard fixer formulas (which are complicated, like the one above) make this difficult. But since that's what the authors specify, I will try it first.

If the purpose of the F5 is simply to clear dye, and provide a little extra hardening, maybe we can make use of the research that has been done on the forum.

Was there ever a conclusion reached about the benefit of using a simpler mixture to fix/harden DCG? I'm not up to speed on the subject of fixer, since I've used the light-bulb method to harden DCG (regular DCG, however, not dye-sensitized DCG).

Regarding the clearing of dye, there was a substance mentioned by Jeff Blyth (in an Applied Optics paper) for this purpose: a 1% solution of sodium dithionite (which is also said to clear residual chromate from the layer). Maybe this could be combined with some kind of hardener to replace the F5 fixer?
Joe Farina Fri Sep 04, 2009 1:21 pm #2
correction: the F5 fixer contains: sodium thiosulfate, sodium sulfite, acetic acid, boric acid, potassium alum.
JohnFP Fri Sep 04, 2009 6:27 pm #3
I used a Sodium Metabisulfite (for final reducing) and Alum (for hardening) for "fixing" DCG and it worked fine. In 500ml I disolved 2.5g SM and 5g Alum. It seemed a little strong ( it just worked much faster then the Kodak Rapid Fixer). But as told by Jeff B., those are the only two things needed for DCG processing. If you try it, start with what I did and then dilute and let us know what final strength seemed fine.

You can find the ingredients to Kodak Rapid Fixer here in its MSDS.
