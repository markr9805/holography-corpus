wler Sun Feb 20, 2005 9:49 am #4
Hmm. if it's really that dim, it may be broken indeed. Laser diodes

can die very easily in an instant, and typically they turn into an

expensive LED. So one thing you may try is to find out whether it

still produces coherent light at all or not - just look carefully

whether you can see those granular "speckles" that are typical for

coherent light. If not, then the LD is surely gone. In the one

instance where I killed a LD it went exactly this way: strong

coherent emission at first, then dim LED-like output.

At any rate, be careful not to look straight into a potential 30mW

laser - Murphy's law almost guarantees there is a loose contact

that closes exactly in the moment you look in.... ;=)

One of the problems with trying to sell a laser diode kit without laser diode is that laser diodes as they come from the factory have a wide variation in output power versus drive current and in the response of the photodetector. The only way to safely and reliably set the output power is with a separately purchased laser power meter which costs maybe a couple hundred dollars. Colin Kaminski Mon Feb 21, 2005 11:39 am #6
I am no expert on electronics by any means but I'll chime in because this is something I have thought about a little. I have not looked at the D&S circuit for a long time. But here is how it looks to me.

The way I would adjust the power is to change the value of the resistors R5 and R6 in the D&S circuit. They go between pins 7 and 8 (brigaded) to pin 3 on the IR3C07 chip. R5 and R6 are installed in series making the resistance R5+R6. The formula for calculating it is 1.95V/Im=R. Where Im is the Monitor current of the particular diode. The diode used is the HL6501MG.

[http://www.thorlabs.com/ProductDetail.c ... uct_ID=872](http://www.thorlabs.com/ProductDetail.cfm?DID=6&Objectgroup_ID=7&Product_ID=872)

Im ranges from .05 to 1.5 ma! Which would range between 39 ohms and 1.3 ohms. Typical is .3ma or 6.5 ohms. The D&S values are 4.7+3.3=7 ohms. Now it is easy to see how this could be a problem if you had a different value for Im in your particular diode.

A trim pot would allow one to adjust the output current. I don't know what the best strategy for achieving this is. A quick search shows a 25 ohm pot at [http://www.digikey.com](http://www.digikey.com). One would need to adjust the pot until you get about 28mw measured on a light meter (Frank's value of a working D&S). (I don't know which way you would turn the pot to have minimum brightness. I would guess higher resistance would make the diode brighter. Going the wrong way will make the diode pop so make sure you know.)

From here you would need to measure the mode hopping of the laser and adjust the output power slightly to tweak the laser frequency into a stable zone. Since Frank says most of his kits work straight out of the box, I would guess the spaces in between the hops is fairly large. A photodiode and a high gain amp to a speaker is the simplest method. The final test is to shoot a hologram. Or one could build a $5 Fabry-Perot interferometer from Laser Sam's site.

Here is the IR3C07 data sheet. It was sent to me by another D&S owner. It is from a laser book and not from Sharp. I have not been able to locate the original Sharp data sheet nor do I know what book this one is from.

[http://www.holographyforum.org/files/ir3c07.pdf](http://www.holographyforum.org/files/ir3c07.pdf)

Photodiode Monitor Current.

Noise from construction differences in my circuit. I used a breadboard and not the connectors he supplied. Perhaps I made a ground loop or an antenna or something. I ended up going on to other lasers before I made the mod I suggest here.

JohnFP Thu Feb 24, 2005 12:25 pm #15
As they say a picture is worth a thousand words. Can you post a photo of the hologram?

> lyons: Only tiny parts are visible.

As you move your head right to left does the visible and not visible parts move across the plate or across the object continuously? If the former your plate moved if the latter your object moved. If the visible parts do not move continuously per say but come into view at one angle then disapear at other angles it may be that your lighter is too shiney and is relfecting the light away from the plate except at the angles where you can see it. Brass would not be a good object to start with. Think of the lighter surface, being shiney, as a mirror but with different facets. The light hitting the lighter is reflected with the same properties as a mirror, that is only at a particular angle. The different facets of the light reflect the light to different spots on the plate and only there will you get a hologram. In diffuse object, like a lighter painted flat white, each facet of the light will not reflect the light like a mirror but diffuse it to the entire plate. Also with shiney objects you need to worry about polarization. Polarization is maintained when reflecting off shiney objects whereas a diffuse object will randomly polarize the reflected light and you will not have to worry about it as much (except for woodgrain).

Hope this helps! Again, can you post a photo?
