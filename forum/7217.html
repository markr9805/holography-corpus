Hi everyone,

I set up the first Holography studio at The London College of Printing in 1979 which was then used by Adrian Lines. I then later set up my own studio in my town of Hastings, England. I haven't made a hologram for 20 years. What has changed in this time. I see solid state lasers are really cheap compared with the one I had. I had a NEC He Ne 25 Mw and it cost £6,000 ( approx.$12000). I have recently discovered a website from someone that says that you can now make decent holograms with a 10 mw laser pointer, is that true. Can you recommend a low cost laser, as I do not have a lot of money at present and i would like to start making holograms again. Also do Agfa still make films and plates?
JohnFP Mon Mar 17, 2008 2:13 pm #2
Welcome to our forum Julian.

> I have recently discovered a website from someone that says that you can now make decent holograms with a 10 mw laser pointer, is that true. Can you recommend a low cost laser, as I do not have a lot of money at present and i would like to start making holograms again.


Well, if you design a good table, low power laser only results in longer exposures, that's all. I don't think the "over-the-counter" silver halide is grossly more sensitive then it used to be. On the other had, if you want to dable in MBDCG (Methel-Blue Dichromated Gelatin) then simple reflection holograms can be made with low power red pointers.

[http://www.holokits.com/holography_kit.htm](http://www.holokits.com/holography_kit.htm)

Diode Holographer Fri Mar 21, 2008 8:38 am #6
Speaking from personal experience I can say that the 10mW Aixis laser FAR out performs the Integraf laser. And its a third of the price. The Aixis laser has great polarization, stable output (powered by D cells) and mine has a coherence length of at least 9" maybe a foot or more. For the price, I'm just not sure you can beat this laser.

If you go with the Infiniter I would power it with D cells to provide a more reliable output.
ron olson Mon Mar 24, 2008 7:56 pm #7
Just a quick response - Agfa continues (has never ceased) to make green sensitive holographic plates and as far as I can tell they will continue in production under the product tag "Millimask" for some time. Unfortunately, they only make one size: 16.5" x 12.5" sold in boxes of (12) plates ~1/16"thick. In more than 15 years, I have returned 2 plates for defects out of several hundred.

I would say that the most significant change is that holography (as everything) has moved into digital direction. Now there is possible to make digital image capture in one place and perform digital hologram printing in another place - like in photography.
