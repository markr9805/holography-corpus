A while ago I pointed to Slavich's "Russian" prices: it looks like the prices in Russia are a fraction of what we pay here. Moreover, there's a green sensitive emulsion which is said to be virtually the same as VRP. Sergey Vorobyev writes ([http://www.holography.ru/les14eng.htm](http://www.holography.ru/les14eng.htm)):

Besides VRP photographic plates, "Slavich" manufactures cheaper plates FPR (see right fig.). They are also sensitized for green color, and they have the same sensitivity and resolution as the VRP plates, but they have some worse mechanical properties - strength and temperature of deformation of the emulsion layer. These characteristics have no critical importance for hologram recording, so using of the FPR plates is preferable.

Comments re Fuji film:

First, it seems odd to me that the folks on this forum are so narrow-minded that they think that Fuji produced this film exclusively for the display holography market. That volume wouldn't justify the cost of development, production, and marketing for the film. I think we have lost sight of the fact that there must be a larger, commercial or industrial application for this film, i.e. technical holography, which has larger volume to justify the business investment.

Second, if you're introducing a new product, you're never going to start the price out lower than the competition and try to raise it later! You can always lower the price! Better to test the market at a high price and reduce it later.

I wonder if the observations posted here about pricing might be USA-centric? For example, Ultimate is much less expensive in France than in the USA. Slavich is probably cheaper in Russia than in the USA. Fuji may be cheaper than anything else in Japan, or other market regions around the world.

My advice to holographers on the 'learning curve' (myself included) is to regard the cost of film as the least expensive component of the exercise. By far, the best thing that happened to me in terms of improving technique, was when I was able to buy a 50m roll of 8E75 film (70mm wide) off eBay for $50. I cut it into 5" strips for test shots. That works out to 13 cents a piece. I know this was a really lucky buy, but when it's gone (and it will be soon!), I'm going to bite the bullet and switch back to PFG-01 film for test shots. When I consider the cost of lasers, optics, tables, as well as the value of my own free time, the film becomes a small portion of the expense.

Of course, when I get everything working right, I switch to BB for the 'Money Shot'. There is definitely a huge difference between PFG/Agfa (and apparently Fuji) and BB or Ultimate (if you can get unfogged Ultimate). When I consider the time invested in making a display hologram, I can't understand why anyone would not be willing to pay more for a far superior end result. It makes such a huge difference hanging on the wall that I'm going back and re-shooting some of my earlier PFG/Agfa projects in BB, just for the improvement in brightness and clarity.
