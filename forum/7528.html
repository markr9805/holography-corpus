Joe Farina Sat Sep 20, 2008 1:45 pm #4
Thanks for posting the talks from the conference, Kaveh. I think everyone should check it out.

If any of the people involved in this conference are reading this, I have something important to say. Actually two things:

First, the idea of "holography in the modern museum" is an exciting one, and shows great promise.

Second, and most important: don't blow this opportunity. I mean, the idea of holography and museums should be focused on, exactly why it is beneficial to museums, what it can accomplish for them, and how to go about it. There's no need to talk about aerosol sprays, plankton, or any of the other "wonders" of holography. Holography has become fragmented into a trillion different little categories, which has the effect of turning holographers into scatterbrains. Fit holography into the needs of museums, and don't try to sell them some "magic." We are way beyond that. Actually, the initial description of the conference was a good one, at least the first part, and it gave some compelling reasons why holography should have a place in the modern museum. Please run with that, and don't get distracted by any other nonsense. I could see all that extraneous stuff creeping in (holography's "past") after reading the titles of most of the presentations (which I didn't bother to listen to).
