Colin Kaminski Thu Mar 24, 2005 11:09 pm #2
There are lots of problems with 3-D moving media. One is the large amount of information involved. The other is point of view. There are many solutions to all of these problems but none have solved them all well enough to become a standard.

You will want to look a Liti holographics site as they can take a few seconds of your animation and turn it into a hologram for you. These are actually stereograms that contain a few hundred images. Some search terms are multiplexed hologram, holographic stereogram, autostereoscopic. There are many 3-D technologies out there. Dinesh is up on many of them. One time I went to his house and we made a 3-D video of he and I brewing.

You will also want to look at MIT media labs web site for there holovideo project.

If you think of one of the problems, to start, if we made a hologram the size of a movie screen and fillled an audience, the experience would be different for people sitting in different seats. On the left side of the theater you would see one profile of Gene Harlow and in the middle you would see her head on.

Now making a very long film of a stereoscopic hologram would be actually pretty easy, in film budget terms. If we wanted to make a 6x8 foot feature length movie and had a 20 million dollar budget it would not be terribly difficult. The technology of motion control developed for the first Star Wars film is equal in complexity.

Well, there is a start you are at the beginning of a long road. Welcome aboard!

BobH Fri Mar 25, 2005 3:25 am #4
A few years ago, I worked on a system in which the screen is a hologram optical element and regular video projectors were the source. There is one projector for each eye, so a single viewer sees 3-D without the need for any glasses. The HOE could easily be made for up to six viewers, each having allowable head movement of approximately +/- 3" horizontally and vertically.

The system could theoretically be used with 50 projectors (if they were small enough) so that viewers sitting anywhere along a horizontal line in front of it would see an autostereoscopic image3-D image, dependant upon their perspective view. In the first case, two cameras were needed for recording the scenes. In the second, 50 cameras would be needed.

The big problem with any 3-D system is the availability of adequate and appropriate content along with the release of the system. I can make a 3-D TV, what value does it give to those watching soap operas, newstainment, advertisements, censored movies, or infomercials?
