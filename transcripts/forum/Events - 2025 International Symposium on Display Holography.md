Here is something of interest for every member of this forum! All the participants at this event have the opportunity to make a presentation or demonstration focusing on their interest in wavefront reconstruction. Like the 2024 Symposium there will be an Art Marketplace - Collector's Corner where all attendees may exhibit and show their wares and artwork in the Pop Up Holography Gallery. In 2024 over 200 attendees' holograms were on display in that space, plus an additional 75+ holograms on display in the permanent Dr. Tung Jeong Memorial Gallery space.

![LaserLitTable24Uncapt.jpg](https://holographyforum.org/forum/download/file.php?id=7944)

Dr. Tung Jeong initiated this enterprise in 1982 as a social gathering for the free exchange of ideas across all aspects of the medium; scientific, technical, commercial and artistic. There is no better place for the community of active holographers from hobbyists to artists to experts to meet up and learn from each other to create a renaissance for the medium. Details about the upcoming conference are at [https://isdh2025.org](https://isdh2025.org). Witness the camaraderie from last summer at [https://isdh2024.org](https://isdh2024.org). Registration begins on April 1st, 2025.

Is the future of holography rainbow-colored? Symposia such as this upcoming one can generate plans for the future.

For years the establishment of archives and artist in residence labs and professional holography organizations have been bandied about on social media, but nothing happens. ## Ed Wesly — 2025-05-08 04:17 PM

More interesting stuff posted at ISDH 2025!

What would you like to see hands-on and in person at ISDH 2025, or at any symposium in the future? Suggest a demonstration either to [info@isdh2025.org](mailto:info@isdh2025.org), or in the comments section below, and maybe your wish will come true! Because of the malleable format of the symposium based upon the free exchange of information, and the participation of holographers at all skill levels, plus the hands-on legacy of the Lake Forest College Holography Workshops, the presentations can be tailored to the audience!

A vibration isolation table and other optical equipment is available to the conference (courtesy of the Lake Forest College Physics Department) to set up hands-on demonstrations of typical holo laboratory operations (within reason) to be viewed in reality, not on-line! So if you have a topic that you are curious about, let us know, and certainly there will be someone here who has the expertise to show how it's done! Or if your presentation requires something more than just a PowerPoint, we shall see if we can pull together the resources for a more hands-on experience!

## Din — 2025-06-06 04:01 PM

Joy just showed me his post on LinkedIn about making a hologram of a black hole collapse using the Oppenheimer-Snyder model. As background, Einstein first proposed, then discarded, the idea of a grvaitational collapse into a singularity in a 1939 paper. A few months later, Oppenheimer and his student Hartland Snyder studied Einstein's paper and published a paper that showed that a sufficiently massive star could collapse into a gravitation singularity ("On Continued Gravitational Contraction" Oppenheimer, J. R. and Snyder, H, Physical Review 56 (1939))

The metric they developed is the Oppenheimer-Snyder metric:

ds² = -dτ² +A²{(dR²/[1-2MR²/R(b)²R(+)]) + R²dω²}

If Dr. Hocking does bring up the aforesaid metric, then strictly speaking, he's not using mathematics to demonstrate holography, he's just using a Non-Minkowskian metric on Riemannian space-time. So display holographers can rest easy that there's still no mathematics in holography.
